Getting in
Ulaanbaatar is the international gateway. Dalanzadgad, Khovd, Ulaangom, Moron and Choibalsan take domestic flights, and each one saves several days of driving.
Getting around
Fly from Ulaanbaatar to a provincial capital and arrange ground transport there. Many areas have no road at all, so a four wheel drive with a driver is the practical way to move, and most visitors go with a Mongolian birding guide or an operator.

When to Go

Mongolia has one birding season and it is short. Winter averages minus 20 to minus 30 C and drops to minus 50 in the Gobi, the western lakes and parts of the Khangai, and almost every bird leaves. The prime window is late April to mid-June and late August to early October, and the middle of each period is the most productive. Rain arrives in June, July and August, and even then the wettest of the five stations below takes 285 mm a year.

Spring passage and the breeding season

April to June

The country thaws from south to north and Siberian migrants cross it while the resident breeders take up territory.

Go for Breeding birds are in full plumage and they sing all day, the cranes are on their marshes, and a late blizzard can put down a large fall of migrants.

Trade off Lake Hovsgol is often still frozen into mid-June, the high Khangai meadows clear late, and dust storms cross the Gobi on winds that reach 140 km/h.

Typical daily average
  • Ulaanbaatar: -6 to 23 °C / 21 to 73 °F
  • Khovd: -2 to 24 °C / 28 to 75 °F
  • Dalanzadgad: -1 to 26 °C / 30 to 79 °F

High summer

July and August

The rain arrives, the steppe is at its greenest and the Gobi is at its hottest.

Go for Breeding birds are feeding young, the roads are dry outside the storms, and autumn passage starts to build at Airag Nuur late in August.

Trade off Song has stopped, the Gobi reaches 40 to 50 C, and July is the wettest month in the north.

Typical daily average
  • Ulaanbaatar: 9 to 24 °C / 48 to 75 °F
  • Khovd: 11 to 25 °C / 52 to 77 °F
  • Dalanzadgad: 14 to 28 °C / 57 to 82 °F

Autumn passage and the long freeze

September to March

Passage runs hard through September and the first week of October, then the country empties and freezes for five months.

Go for The crane congregations peak, Little Curlew crosses the eastern steppe, and the oasis towns fill with songbirds working south.

Trade off The window shuts in early October. From November to March the lakes are ice, the temperature averages minus 20 to minus 30 C, and only about a ninth of the list remains.

Typical daily average

Ulaanbaatar, the Tuul valley and Bogd Khan

Gallery forest, gravel ponds and a forested massif, all within half an hour of the capital

Ulaanbaatar stands at the southern edge of the Siberian taiga and the Tuul River runs through it. Copses and thickets follow the river, and the ones near Songino hold Azure Tit, White-crowned Penduline Tit and Daurian Jackdaw, with eastern Siberian migrants among them in spring and autumn. Gravel ponds fed by the Tuul lie 23 km west of the centre and a network of raised dykes crosses them, so waders, waterfowl and grassland birds are all in reach of one walk. Hundreds of Ruddy Shelducks gather on the ponds in late summer. Bogd Khan rises immediately south of the city and carries more than 150 recorded species.

  • Habitat: Gallery forest and thickets follow the Tuul River. Bogd Khan carries coniferous and deciduous forest, scrub, steppe and boulder fields at its highest points, and it is the southernmost reach of the Hentii mountain taiga.
  • When: Late April to mid-June and late August to early October carry the migration through the river copses. Redpoll and the two rosefinches appear on Bogd Khan in winter.
  • Getting there: The Songino copses and the gravel ponds sit on the Tuul about 23 km west of the city centre, and raised dykes cross the ponds. Bogd Khan begins immediately south of the city.
  • Base yourself in: Ulaanbaatar
  • All year: Black Kite, Fork-tailed Swift, Azure Tit, White-crowned Penduline Tit, Red-billed Chough, Daurian Jackdaw, Yellow-breasted Bunting, Amur Falcon, Eurasian Three-toed Woodpecker, Black Woodpecker, Hill Pigeon, Blyth's Pipit, Richard's Pipit, Olive-backed Pipit, Thick-billed Warbler, Dusky Warbler, Brown Shrike, Pine Bunting, Ruddy Shelduck, Daurian Partridge, Azure-winged Magpie
  • In winter: Redpoll, Long-tailed Rosefinch, Pallas's Rosefinch
  • National park: Bogd Khan Uul Strictly Protected Area
  • Stops:
    • : River copses and gravel ponds on the Tuul about 23 km west of the city centre. Raised dykes cross the ponds and hundreds of Ruddy Shelducks gather on them in late summer.
    • : A forested massif of 41,651 ha immediately south of the city, protected since 1778 and possibly the oldest nature reserve in the world. More than 150 bird species have been recorded on it.
    • : The provincial town at the southern foot of Bogd Khan, and the usual way in to the forest from that side.
  • Also covers: Bogd Khan Uul Strictly Protected Area, Nalaikh, Songino, Ulaanbaatar, Zuunmod

Gorkhi-Terelj and the Hentii mountains

Taiga, granite tors and riverside poplar an hour north-east of Ulaanbaatar

Gorkhi-Terelj covers 293,168 ha of the southern Hentii range and it backs onto the Khan Khentii Strictly Protected Area, which adds 1.2 million ha of taiga behind it. Conifer and mixed forest, granite outcrops, rich meadows, small streams under broadleaved woodland and mature poplar along the Tuul all sit within a short drive of each other. The bird list resembles Bogd Khan's and runs a good deal longer. Black-billed Capercaillie is the difficult bird, and Siberian Rubythroat, Red-flanked Bluetail and Taiga Flycatcher are the ones most visitors meet.

  • Habitat: Coniferous and mixed forest, rocky outcrops, rich meadows and small streams under broadleaved woodland, with mature riparian poplar forest along the Tuul River.
  • When: Most of the forest birds are summer visitors and they are in place from May to September. Late April to mid-June gives the song and the passage together.
  • Getting there: A paved road runs 54 km north-east from Ulaanbaatar to the park.
  • Base yourself in: Terelj
  • Look for: Eastern Buzzard, Black-billed Capercaillie, Hazel Grouse, Ural Owl, Black Woodpecker, Eurasian Three-toed Woodpecker, Siberian Jay, Northern Nutcracker, Siberian Rubythroat, Red-flanked Bluetail, Daurian Redstart, Chinese Bush Warbler, Dark-sided Flycatcher, Taiga Flycatcher, Red-throated Thrush, Pine Bunting, Black Stork, Swinhoe's Snipe, Siberian Blue Robin, Long-tailed Rosefinch, Meadow Bunting

    • Gorkhi-Terelj National Park, The nearest large block of taiga to the capital, on the paved road north-east. Granite tors stand above larch forest and the Tuul runs through poplar woodland at the bottom of the valley.

Hustai National Park

Natural steppe, montane forest and a riverside strip on the Tuul, 95 km west of the capital

Hustai covers 50,600 ha of steppe hills west of Ulaanbaatar and it is where Przewalski's Horse was returned to the wild after the species died out outside captivity in the late 1960s. The drive out crosses open steppe and Upland Buzzard, Cinereous Vulture, Demoiselle Crane and a run of larks and wheatears are all on it. The park itself holds three habitats at once: natural steppe on the hills, montane forest in the valleys and a riparian strip along the Tuul. Daurian Partridge, Black Stork, three eagles, Amur and Saker Falcons, Mongolian and Asian Short-toed Larks and Meadow Bunting all breed here.

  • Habitat: Natural steppe, montane forest and riparian habitat along the Tuul River, on rolling hills west of the capital.
  • When: The breeding birds hold territory from May to September, which is when the eagles and both falcons are on show.
  • Getting there: The main paved road runs west out of Ulaanbaatar and the signposted dirt access road into the park leaves it about 95 km from the capital.
  • Base yourself in: Hustai
  • Look for: Daurian Partridge, Black Stork, Golden Eagle, Steppe Eagle, Booted Eagle, Amur Falcon, Saker Falcon, Pied Wheatear, Isabelline Wheatear, Mongolian Lark, Asian Short-toed Lark, Meadow Bunting, Upland Buzzard, Cinereous Vulture, Demoiselle Crane
  • National park:
    • Khustain Nuruu National Park, A block of steppe hills and wooded valleys off the main road west, and the reintroduction site for the takhi. The park shop sells a photographic guide to its birds.
  • Also covers: Argalant, Khustain Nuruu National Park, Lun
  • Also watch for: Przewalski's Horse, the takhi, was reintroduced here after the species became extinct in the wild in the late 1960s, and Hustai is best known for it.

Gun Galuut and the upper Kherlen

Marsh, a chain of small lakes and a rocky range in rolling steppe, two and a half hours east of the capital

Gun Galuut is a reserve of 20,000 ha east of Ulaanbaatar where a river valley and a mountain range stand in rolling steppe. Whooper Swan and White-naped Crane nest in the marshes beside the ger camp. The chain of small lakes behind them carries grebes, waterfowl, gulls and terns on migration, and Asian Dowitcher and Spotted Redshank are among the waders that stop. Willow copses along the river hold White-crowned Penduline Tit and migrant warblers. Argali Sheep live on the rocky hills above.

  • Habitat: Marshes, a chain of small lakes and riparian willow copses along a river valley, with rolling steppe and a rocky mountain range around them.
  • When: Whooper Swan and White-naped Crane nest here through the summer. The grebes, waders, gulls and terns are passage birds and they come through in spring and autumn.
  • Getting there: The reserve lies 130 km east of Ulaanbaatar and the drive takes about two and a half hours.
  • Base yourself in: Gun Galuut
  • All year: Whooper Swan, White-naped Crane, White-crowned Penduline Tit, Demoiselle Crane, Ruddy Shelduck
  • On passage: Asian Dowitcher, Spotted Redshank, Black-tailed Godwit, Bar-tailed Godwit, Horned Grebe, Pallas's Gull, Common Tern, White-winged Tern
  • National park:
    • Gun-Galuut Nature Reserve, A newly established reserve on the upper Kherlen, with a river valley and a mountain range inside rolling steppe. The marshes by the ger camp are where the nesting swans and cranes are.
  • Stops:
    • : The mining town on the paved road east from Ulaanbaatar, and the last fuel and supplies before the reserve.
  • Also covers: Baganuur, Bayandelger, Gun Galuut, Gun-Galuut Nature Reserve
  • Also watch for: A small population of Argali Sheep lives in the rocky hills around the reserve.

Ogii Nuur, Kharkhorin and the Orkhon valley

A freshwater steppe lake and the country's largest arable district, on the road to the old Mongol capital

Ogii Nuur is a freshwater steppe lake in the Orkhon valley and one of the more accessible wetlands in the country. White-tailed Eagle and Pallas's Fish Eagle work it, Swan Goose, Bar-headed Goose and Whooper Swan use it, and White-naped, Hooded and Siberian Cranes have all been recorded on it. Kharkhorin, the Mongol capital of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, lies south-west along the same valley. The farmland around it is the largest arable area in Mongolia and up to 7,500 Demoiselle Cranes gather there in September. Eurasian Eagle-Owl and Blyth's Pipit have both bred in the buildings of the Erdene Zuu monastery.

  • Habitat: A freshwater steppe lake in a broad river valley, with the largest block of arable farmland in Mongolia around Kharkhorin to the south-west.
  • When: September brings up to 7,500 Demoiselle Cranes to the Kharkhorin farmland. The lake works from the thaw in April to the freeze in late autumn.
  • Getting there: Ogii Nuur lies about 400 km west of Ulaanbaatar and Kharkhorin about 370 km south-west of it, and the two sit close enough to combine on one trip.
  • Base yourself in: Kharkhorin
  • All year: White-tailed Eagle, Pallas's Fish Eagle, Swan Goose, Bar-headed Goose, Whooper Swan, Demoiselle Crane, Eurasian Eagle-Owl, Blyth's Pipit, Ruddy Shelduck, Mongolian Gull
  • On passage: White-naped Crane, Hooded Crane, Siberian Crane
  • National park: Khogno Khan National Park
  • Stops:
    • : A rich freshwater lake in open steppe, and the easiest large wetland to reach from the capital. Both fish eagles work it and three species of crane have been recorded.
    • : The site of Karakorum, the Mongol capital of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries. The cropland around the town draws the September crane congregation.
    • : The walled Buddhist monastery at Kharkhorin. Eurasian Eagle-Owl and Blyth's Pipit have both nested in its buildings.

The Khangai mountains and Uliastai

Forest steppe, larch hillsides and subalpine meadow up to 4,021 m

The Khangai is one of the five major ranges in Mongolia and its highest peak, Otgontenger, reaches 4,021 m. Forest steppe covers the lower ground, larch holds the north-facing slopes and alpine meadow lies above both. Near the town of Uliastai the little-known White-throated Bush Chat breeds in subalpine meadow beside streams between 2,430 m and 2,600 m, in country with gorges, rocky outcrops and scattered boulders. Altai Accentor shares that ground with it. Mongolia is the only country the bush chat is known to breed in.

  • Habitat: Forest steppe at lower elevations, larch forest on north-facing slopes, and alpine meadow above, with gorges, rocky outcrops and scattered boulders on the high ground.
  • When: White-throated Bush Chat holds its subalpine breeding ground through the summer. It winters in northern India and Nepal, so it is absent for the rest of the year.
  • Base yourself in: Uliastai

  • National parks: Otgontenger Strictly Protected Area, Khangai Nuruu National Park, Tarvagatai Nuruu National Park, Khorgo-Terkhiin Tsagaan Nuur National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The provincial capital under the western Khangai, and the town the White-throated Bush Chat meadows lie above.

Lake Hovsgol and the northern taiga

A 137 km rift lake at 1,645 m, ringed by larch forest, lagoons and alpine barrens

Lake Hovsgol lies at the northernmost point of Mongolia at 1,645 m. It is 137 km long and about 250 m deep, a rift lake that drains north-east into Lake Baikal 200 km away, and it has seen very little development. The national park around it covers 838,070 ha and more than 250 bird species have been recorded in its combination of open water, lagoon, wet meadow, mature larch forest and alpine barren. In early to mid-June the main lake is often still frozen and waterbirds pack into the shoreline lagoons and the leads in the melting ice. This is one of the few places outside Siberia where Black-throated and Red-throated Thrushes breed alongside each other.

  • Habitat: A deep rift lake with fringing lagoons and wet meadows, mature larch forest on the slopes around it, and alpine barrens at the highest elevations.
  • When: Early to mid-June concentrates waterbirds in the shoreline lagoons while the main lake is still frozen. The forest birds hold territory through the summer.
  • Getting there: The best birding is on the southern half of the western shore and paved and gravel roads reach it. The Alagtsar River valley on the eastern shore is harder to get to.
  • Base yourself in: Hatgal
  • All year: Black-billed Capercaillie, Common Hoopoe, Oriental Cuckoo, Tree Pipit, Olive-backed Pipit, Eurasian Nuthatch, Dark-sided Flycatcher, Taiga Flycatcher, Brambling, Red Crossbill, Pine Bunting, Swinhoe's Snipe, Red-throated Thrush, Black-throated Thrush, Black Woodpecker, Siberian Jay
  • In the breeding season: Black-throated Loon, Horned Grebe, Whooper Swan, Bar-headed Goose, Ruddy Shelduck, White-winged Scoter, Common Goldeneye, Northern Lapwing, Common Redshank, Long-toed Stint, Mongolian Gull, Black-headed Gull, Common Tern, White-winged Tern, White-tailed Eagle, Citrine Wagtail
  • National park: Khuvsgul National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The village at the southern tip of Lake Hovsgol and the way in to the western shore, which is the half of the lake with the road and the best birding.
    • : A shallow lake in a grassy valley about 40 km from Moron on the road up to Hovsgol. Waterfowl use the water and larks and pipits fill the steppe around it.

Uvs Nuur and the north-western lakes

Shallow saline water of 771,000 ha, the biggest sheet of water in the country

Uvs Nuur covers 771,000 ha and is the largest lake in Mongolia by surface area. It is shallow and saline, and sand dunes and snow-capped mountains stand around it. Waterfowl, waders and both wetland and steppe songbirds use it in numbers. Swan Goose, Red-crested Pochard, Black Stork and Pallas's Gull are the birds of special interest here, a colony of Eurasian Spoonbills nests on the lake, and Relict Gull has been recorded. Ulaangom stands close to the southern shore, so a visitor can reach the water from the town, and several days here run on naturally into the Great Lakes Depression to the south.

  • Habitat: A shallow saline lake ringed by sand dunes and snow-capped mountains, with marshy margins and open steppe around it.
  • Getting there: The lake shore is reached from Ulaangom, the provincial capital, which sits close to its southern side.
  • Base yourself in: Ulaangom
  • Look for: Swan Goose, Red-crested Pochard, Eurasian Spoonbill, Black Stork, Pallas's Gull, Relict Gull, Whooper Swan, Bar-headed Goose, Ruddy Shelduck, Demoiselle Crane, Mongolian Gull, Isabelline Wheatear, Asian Short-toed Lark

The Great Lakes Depression

Five very large lakes, some fresh and some saline, and the biggest reedbeds in eastern Asia

The Great Lakes Depression holds Khyargas, Airag, Khar-Us, Khar and Dorgon, which run from 143 to 1,852 square kilometres and from fresh to saline. Khar-Us Nuur National Park covers 850,272 ha and carries some of the largest reedbeds in Central and Eastern Asia. Eurasian Bittern, Bearded Reedling, three reed warblers, the white-headed race of Yellow Wagtail and the easternmost White-headed Duck population in the world all live in them. Airag may be the richest wetland in the country. Tens of thousands of birds stop there in late summer and early autumn, waders feed on exposed mud at the mouth of the Zavkhan River, and White-tailed Eagles and Pallas's Fish Eagles gather in dozens on the waterfowl.

  • Habitat: Large fresh and saline lakes in a closed basin, with extensive reedbeds at Khar-Us, exposed mud at low water and a river delta at the mouth of the Zavkhan.
  • When: Late summer and early autumn bring tens of thousands of birds to Airag and the two fish eagles gather then. The waders feed on exposed mud when the water is low.
  • Base yourself in: Khovd
  • All year: White-headed Duck, Eurasian Bittern, Bearded Reedling, Paddyfield Warbler, Great Reed Warbler, Blyth's Reed Warbler, Common Reed Bunting, Western Yellow Wagtail, Bar-headed Goose, Pallas's Gull, White-winged Tern, Whiskered Tern, Little Tern, Caspian Tern, Gull-billed Tern, Black-tailed Godwit, Eurasian Spoonbill, Dalmatian Pelican, Asian Houbara
  • On passage: Greater Sand Plover, Broad-billed Sandpiper, Sharp-tailed Sandpiper, Asian Dowitcher, Red-crested Pochard, Eurasian Wigeon, Green-winged Teal, White-tailed Eagle, Pallas's Fish Eagle
  • National parks: Khar Us Nuur National Park, Khan-Khokhi Khyargas Mountain National Park

The Valley of the Lakes

Brackish Gobi lakes in a broad valley between the Khangai and the Gobi-Altai

A line of brackish lakes lies in a broad valley north of the Gobi-Altai range and south of the Khangai. Boontsagaan, Orog, Taatsyn Tsagaan, Holboolj and Ulaan Nuur are the main ones. On migration they hold birdlife on the scale of the Great Lakes Depression, and they are the most reliable places in Mongolia for Relict Gull, which is one of the rarest gulls in the world and turns up unpredictably. The lake-dotted steppe and desert between them carries Oriental Plover, Pallas's Sandgrouse, Mongolian Ground Jay, Mongolian Lark and Pere David's Snowfinch.

  • Habitat: Brackish lakes in a broad valley between two mountain ranges, with lake-dotted steppe and desert on the ground between them.
  • When: Migration periods bring the biggest numbers to the lakes. Relict Gull is unpredictable everywhere and these are the most reliable sites for it.
  • Getting there: A 480 km track runs south from Khujirt to Boontsagaan Nuur, and Orog Nuur lies a further 235 km east of Boontsagaan.
  • Base yourself in: Bayankhongor

Gobi Gurvansaikhan and Dalanzadgad

Desert ranges, ravines and dune fields at the northern edge of the driest Gobi, with an oasis town beside them

Gobi Gurvansaikhan is the largest national park in Mongolia at 2,694,737 ha. It sits north-west of Dalanzadgad and runs more than 380 km from east to west. Its ranges stand 2,200 to 2,600 m above the plains, and rocky and sandy desert, arid steppe, precipitous cliffs, ravines, juniper patches, saltpans and oases all sit inside the one park. Over 240 bird species occur here. Yolyn Am, the Valley of the Bearded Vulture, is the stop every Gobi trip makes: Cinereous, Himalayan and Bearded Vultures leave their roosts at mid-morning and fill the sky above the gorge. Dalanzadgad is a green island in dry country, and its leafy avenue and the poplar windbreaks at Bayandalai fill with warblers, chats, thrushes and flycatchers on passage.

  • Habitat: Rocky and sandy desert plains, arid steppe, precipitous cliffs and ravines, patches of juniper, saltpans and oases, with ranges rising 2,200 to 2,600 m above the surrounding plains.
  • When: Migrant songbirds concentrate in the town poplars and the fields on passage. The vultures leave their roosts at mid-morning and work the plains from Yolyn Am for the rest of the day.
  • Getting there: Most visitors reach the Gobi by air from Ulaanbaatar to Dalanzadgad and the park begins north-west of the town. Desert travel needs expedition logistics and is arranged through an operator.
  • Base yourself in: Dalanzadgad
  • Look for: Bearded Vulture, Cinereous Vulture, Himalayan Vulture, Altai Snowcock, Wallcreeper, Black Redstart, Güldenstädt's Redstart, Alpine Accentor, Brown Accentor, Kozlov's Accentor, Mongolian Finch, White-winged Snowfinch, Himalayan Beautiful Rosefinch, Godlewski's Bunting, Asian Houbara, Pallas's Sandgrouse, Oriental Plover, Mongolian Ground Jay, Asian Desert Warbler, Desert Wheatear, Saxaul Sparrow, Chukar Partridge, Hill Pigeon, Eurasian Crag Martin
  • National park: Gobi Gurvansaikhan National Park
  • Stops:
    • : A narrow gorge in the Gobi-Altai named for the Bearded Vulture. Three vulture species roost on the walls and leave at mid-morning, and Altai Snowcock and Wallcreeper are on the rock above.
    • : The Gobi's airport town. The leafy central avenue and the poplar windbreaks in the fields at Bayandalai fill with migrant songbirds, and a wetland with hides for birdwatchers lies east of the town.
    • : The red sandstone Flaming Cliffs, where the first known dinosaur eggs were found in the 1920s. Saxaul Sparrow lives in the drought-resistant saxaul bushes around them.

  • Also covers: Bayandalai, Bayanzag, Dalanzadgad, Gobi Gurvansaikhan National Park, Khongoryn Els, Yolyn Am
  • Also watch for: Argali Sheep and Asiatic Ibex are both seen in Yolyn Am, and a few birdwatchers have glimpsed Snow Leopard there.

The eastern steppe and Dornod

The largest undisturbed grassland on earth, with lakes, reedbeds and river drainages across it

Dornod province holds the largest relatively undisturbed grassland ecosystem in the world, and Mongolian Gazelle herds of tens of thousands still cross it. Great Bustard is resident on the grass, thousands of Pacific Golden Plovers move north over it, and Little Curlew passes in large numbers in autumn. Choibalsan is another oasis of poplars, and spring migration fills them with Taiga Flycatchers, Siberian Blue Robins and the occasional White-throated Rock Thrush. The Herlen River and the Tashgain Tavan lakes sit at the western edge of several East Asian ranges, so Oriental Pratincole, Chinese Pond Heron, Reed Parrotbill and Marsh Grassbird all turn up on them. Numrug, in the remote and uninhabited far eastern tip of the country, covers 311,205 ha of the westernmost Khyangan range, and Mandarin Duck, Yellow-rumped Flycatcher and Black-naped Oriole reach the western limit of their breeding ranges in its riparian forest.

  • Habitat: Tall, lush grassland across most of the province, with lakes, reedbeds and river drainages along the Herlen and at Tashgain Tavan Nuur.
  • When: Spring migration fills the Choibalsan poplars with songbirds. Pacific Golden Plover moves north in thousands and Little Curlew is most numerous in autumn.
  • Getting there: Choibalsan lies 600 km east of Ulaanbaatar and fairly good roads reach it in a day and a half. Numrug in the far eastern tip sits on the border and a special permit is required.
  • Base yourself in: Choibalsan
  • All year: Great Bustard, Oriental Pratincole, Chinese Pond Heron, Reed Parrotbill, Marsh Grassbird, Swan Goose, Mandarin Duck, Yellow-rumped Flycatcher, Black-naped Oriole, Mongolian Lark, Demoiselle Crane, Amur Falcon
  • On passage: Pacific Golden Plover, Little Curlew, Taiga Flycatcher, Siberian Blue Robin, White-throated Rock Thrush, Far Eastern Curlew, Red-necked Stint
  • National parks: Numrug National Park, Dornod Mongol Strictly Protected Area
  • Stops:
    • : The provincial capital on the Herlen River, 600 km east of the capital. Its poplars act as an island of green and spring migration crowds them with songbirds.
    • : The large freshwater lake on the Chinese border in the far east of Mongolia. Red-necked Stints tracked by satellite from Victoria in Australia reach it, and so do the Herlen and Ulz river birds.
  • Also covers: Buir Nuur, Choibalsan, Dornod Mongol Strictly Protected Area, Khalkhgol, Matad, Numrug National Park
  • Also watch for: Mongolian Gazelle herds numbering tens of thousands breed on the eastern steppe and migrate across it, the largest concentration of hoofed animals outside Africa.

Mongol Daguur and the north-eastern river valleys

Daurian steppe and three river valleys holding the densest White-naped Crane nesting in the world

The Mongol Daguur Strictly Protected Area covers 103,016 ha of Daurian steppe in the north-east. Six species of crane have been recorded on it. Common and Demoiselle Cranes breed, White-naped Crane is a common breeder, Hooded Crane is common on passage and most numerous in autumn, and Red-crowned and Siberian Cranes both appear rarely in summer. The Ulz River runs through the southern part of the reserve, and the Ulz together with the nearby Onon and Khurkh rivers supports one of the largest breeding populations of White-naped Crane in the world.

  • Habitat: Daurian steppe with river valleys, marshes and wet meadows cut through it, along the Ulz, the Onon and the Khurkh.
  • When: White-naped and Demoiselle Cranes breed here through the summer. Hooded Crane is a passage bird and it is most numerous in autumn.
  • Base yourself in: Binder
  • All year: White-naped Crane, Demoiselle Crane, Common Crane, Swan Goose, Whooper Swan, Great Bustard, Saker Falcon, Upland Buzzard, Mongolian Lark, Pallas's Fish Eagle
  • On passage: Hooded Crane, Siberian Crane, Red-crowned Crane, Asian Dowitcher
  • National parks: Mongol Daguur Strictly Protected Area, Onon-Balj National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The soum centre on the Ulz River at the northern end of Mongol Daguur, and the way in to the crane marshes on that river.
    • : A wide river valley of marsh and wet meadow that carries part of the world's largest breeding population of White-naped Crane.
    • : The soum centre on the Onon River, and the usual base for the Khurkh and Onon crane valleys.
    • : A larch-forested corner of the upper Onon on the Russian border, where taiga birds meet the Daurian steppe.
  • Also covers: Binder, Chuluunkhoroot, Dadal, Dashbalbar, Khurkh River valley, Mongol Daguur Strictly Protected Area, Norovlin, Onon-Balj National Park

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Mongolia?

Late April to mid-June and late August to early October, and the middle of each period is the strongest. Winter averages minus 20 to minus 30 C and most of the avifauna leaves, so there is one short season rather than a choice of them. A late blizzard in spring or an early one in autumn is worth welcoming, because both tend to produce big falls of migrants. The three seasons.

How do you get around Mongolia?

Fly from Ulaanbaatar to a provincial capital and arrange ground transport from there. The country is 2,400 km across, many areas have no road at all, and a four wheel drive with a driver is the practical arrangement. Most visiting birders travel with a Mongolian guide or an international operator.

How many bird species live in Mongolia?

The national field guide documents 417 regularly occurring species plus 86 vagrants, which is 503 recorded in all. Only about 11 per cent of them are permanent residents. Everything else either breeds here and winters south, passes through between Siberia and southern Asia, or arrives from the Arctic for the winter. Every Mongolian species.

Are there any birds found only in Mongolia?

No species breeds in Mongolia and nowhere else, and the draw is a different one. Mongolia holds the core breeding population of several birds, so it may be the centre of origin for Upland Buzzard, Saker Falcon, Demoiselle Crane, White-naped Crane, Mongolian Lark, Pere David's Snowfinch and Kozlov's Accentor. White-throated Bush Chat is known as a breeding bird from Mongolia alone.

Where do you see cranes in Mongolia?

Six species have been recorded in the Mongol Daguur Strictly Protected Area in the north-east. The Ulz River runs through it, and the Ulz with the nearby Onon and Khurkh rivers carries one of the largest breeding populations of White-naped Crane in the world. Gun Galuut, two and a half hours east of Ulaanbaatar, has nesting White-naped Crane and Whooper Swan in the marshes beside its ger camp. Where to bird.

Where do you stay in the Mongolian countryside?

In a ger camp, which is the standard countryside lodging and comes in a range of prices. The better camps have a lodge building with hot showers and a dining room. Ulaanbaatar itself carries the full range of city hotels.

How long does a Mongolia birding trip take?

Ten days covers the Gobi, Hovsgol or the western lakes properly. Three weeks crosses the taiga, the steppe and the desert in one overland loop, which is what the long operator itineraries run. Five days around Ulaanbaatar already reaches four habitats. The seven routes.

What is the Gobi actually like?

It is a cold, high desert, the northernmost of its kind in the world. The floor lies between 914 m and 1,524 m and the Gobi-Altai peaks rise to 2,400 m above it. Summer can reach 50 C and winter drops to minus 40 C with wind off Siberia. Most of the surface is hard-pan or gravel rather than sand, and the region averages 195 mm of rain a year.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
