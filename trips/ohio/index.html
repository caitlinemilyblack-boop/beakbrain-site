Atlas / Ohio

Birding in Ohio

Warblers moving north stop at Lake Erie rather than cross it, and the last cover before the water is a strip of beach woodland with a boardwalk through it, where the birds are at eye level and too tired to care.

At a glance

Best months
  • The first three weeks of May for the Lake Erie warbler passage, late April to June for the unglaciated forest
  • December to February for the owl roosts and lakefront gulls
Regions
Magee Marsh and the Lake Erie marshesSandusky Bay and the islandsThe Cleveland lakefrontThe Hocking Hills and the Wayne forestKilldeer Plains and the reclaimed prairie
Routes
The lake and the hills7 daysThe Magee Marsh fortnight5 daysThe Lake Erie islands4 daysKilldeer Plains in winter4 days
Getting in
Cleveland (CLE) for the lakefront and the islands, Detroit or Toledo for Magee Marsh, and Columbus (CMH) for the hills and the prairie
Getting around
A hire car, plus ferries to the Lake Erie islands. Oak Harbor to Logan is 200 miles on fast roads

Birds that decide the trip

Blackburnian Warbler on the boardwalk

A canopy bird almost everywhere else feeds at head height on the Magee Marsh boardwalk in May, because it has just crossed several hundred miles and needs to eat before the lake.

When The first three weeks of May, on the boardwalk.

Where Magee Marsh and the western Lake Erie shore

Connecticut Warbler in the beach ridge

One of the hardest warblers to see anywhere becomes a reasonable expectation here in the third week of May, walking on the ground under the boardwalk rails.

When The third week of May, on the ground in low cover.

Where Magee Marsh and the western Lake Erie shore

Cerulean Warbler in the unglaciated hills

It sings from the top of tall mature hardwood, and the ridges of Shawnee State Forest hold one of the better populations left in the eastern states.

When May to July, from the canopy on ridge roads.

Where The Hocking Hills and the Wayne forest

Short-eared Owl over the reclaimed prairie

It hunts by daylight on winter afternoons and quarters the grass on long floppy wings. Killdeer Plains is the traditional place in the state to watch that.

When December to February, from mid-afternoon.

Where Killdeer Plains and the glaciated prairie

Prothonotary Warbler in the marsh timber

It nests in holes over standing water, and the drowned timber in the Lake Erie marshes gives it exactly that within sight of the boardwalk crowds.

When May to July, over standing water in the marshes.

Where Magee Marsh and the western Lake Erie shore, Sandusky Bay and the islands

Black Tern over the diked marsh

A marsh tern that has declined sharply across the Great Lakes, still breeding on the diked coastal wetlands here where the vegetation is managed for it.

When May to July, over the diked marsh units.

Where Sandusky Bay and the islands, Magee Marsh and the western Lake Erie shore

Long-eared Owl in a conifer block

It roosts communally in dense planted pine through the winter and sits absolutely still, so the birds are found by looking for pellets and whitewash under the trees rather than by looking up.

When December to February, in planted conifer.

Where Killdeer Plains and the glaciated prairie

Kentucky Warbler in the gorge understorey

It skulks in dense understorey on damp slopes and sings a rolling song from cover, and the shaded sandstone gorges in the south-east are full of the habitat it wants.

When May to July, in dense understorey on damp slopes.

Where The Hocking Hills and the Wayne forest

When to Go

One fortnight dominates the Ohio year. The second and third weeks of May put a whole warbler migration onto a lake shore in daylight at close range, and the rest of the calendar is a good deal quieter, though the winter owls and the unglaciated forest both stand on their own.

The warbler fortnight

The first three weeks of May

Northbound warblers pile up on the Lake Erie shore and feed at eye level.

Go for The Magee Marsh boardwalk at dawn, Ottawa refuge, and the islands for the same birds with fewer people.

Trade off The boardwalk car park fills before first light and accommodation books out a year ahead.

The breeding season

Late April to June

The unglaciated south-east holds Cerulean, Kentucky and Worm-eating Warblers in full song.

Go for Shawnee State Forest and the Wayne, and Killdeer Plains for the grassland birds.

Trade off The lake shore has emptied and the hills are busy at weekends.

Typical daily average

The autumn passage

September and October

The lakefront parks take southbound birds and the shorebird fields draw down.

Go for The Cleveland lakefront and Headlands Beach, and Funk Bottoms for waders.

Trade off It is far more spread out than spring and the marshes are hunted.

Typical daily average

The owl winter

December to February

Short-eared Owls hunt the reclaimed prairie at dusk and Long-eared Owls roost in the conifer blocks.

Go for Killdeer Plains at dusk, and the Cleveland lakefront for gulls and sea duck.

Trade off The lake shore is frozen and most of the state is quiet.

Magee Marsh and the Lake Erie marshes

A boardwalk through a strip of beach woodland that thirty thousand people visit for two weeks in May

Warblers moving north through Ohio reach Lake Erie and stop rather than cross it, and the beach ridge woodland at Magee Marsh is the last cover before the water. A boardwalk runs through it, the birds are at eye level and they are exhausted enough to ignore people entirely.

  • Habitat: Beach ridge woodland of hackberry and cottonwood on a sand bar, diked coastal marsh, wet meadow and mudflat, and the open water of the lake.
  • When: The first three weeks of May, and it is worth being specific: the second week is usually the peak.
  • Getting there: Detroit or Cleveland are the airports and Oak Harbor the base. The boardwalk is free and open all year, the car park fills before dawn in May, and the marshes are hunted in autumn.
  • Base yourself in: Oak Harbor
  • Look for: Blackburnian Warbler, Bay-breasted Warbler, Cape May Warbler, Magnolia Warbler, Chestnut-sided Warbler, Black-throated Blue Warbler, Connecticut Warbler, Mourning Warbler, Golden-winged Warbler, Prothonotary Warbler, Black Tern, Bald Eagle
  • Parks and reserves: Metzger Marsh (Protected area), Erie State Game Area (Protected area), Toussaint Creek Wildlife Area (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Erie State Game Area, Magee Marsh Wildlife Area, Maumee Bay, Metzger Marsh Wildlife Area, Ottawa National Wildlife Refuge, Toussaint Creek Wildlife Area
  • Also watch for: The marshes hold the eastern fox snake and the state has one of the densest wintering Bald Eagle populations on the Great Lakes.

Sandusky Bay and the islands

Limestone islands in the western basin, taking the same migration with far fewer people

The Lake Erie islands sit in the shallowest, warmest part of the lake and take the same warbler passage as the mainland shore, with a fraction of the crowd. Cedar Point refuge on the bay is closed to the public and its marshes hold the breeding waterbirds.

  • Habitat: Limestone island with alvar and second-growth woodland, diked coastal marsh, shallow warm bay, and open lake water.
  • When: Early to mid-May for the passage, and November to March for the diving ducks on the bay and around the islands.
  • Getting there: Cleveland is the airport, an hour east, and Sandusky the base. Ferries run to Kelleys and South Bass from Marblehead and Catawba, and Cedar Point refuge is closed to visitors.
  • Base yourself in: Sandusky
  • Look for: Prothonotary Warbler, Cerulean Warbler, Black Tern, Common Tern, American Bittern, Least Bittern, King Rail, Marsh Wren, Yellow-headed Blackbird, Canvasback, Redhead, Common Loon
  • Parks and reserves: Cedar Point National Wildlife Refuge, South Bass Island State Park (State or provincial park), Resthaven Wildlife Area (Protected area), Maumee Bay State Park (State or provincial park)
  • Also covers: Cedar Point National Wildlife Refuge, Kelleys Island, Maumee Bay State Park, Resthaven Wildlife Area, Sandusky Bay, South Bass Island State Park
  • Also watch for: The islands hold the Lake Erie water snake, brought back from endangered status, and the bay carries a major walleye fishery.

The Cleveland lakefront

City parks on a lake shore, catching migrants that will not cross open water

Birds moving along the southern shore of Lake Erie run into a city, and the parks on the lakefront are what they drop into. Headlands Beach is the largest natural sand beach in the state and Mentor Marsh behind it is a drowned river mouth with a boardwalk.

  • Habitat: Sand beach and dune, drowned river-mouth marsh, second-growth lakeshore woodland, breakwater and harbour, and open lake water.
  • When: Early May and September to October for the passage, and December to March for gulls, sea duck and owls.
  • Getting there: Cleveland is the airport and the base, and all three sites are inside the metropolitan area with free parking and public transport nearby.
  • Base yourself in: Cleveland
  • Look for: Snowy Owl, Long-eared Owl, Northern Saw-whet Owl, Bonaparte's Gull, Iceland Gull, Glaucous Gull, Long-tailed Duck, Red-necked Grebe, Purple Sandpiper, Golden-winged Warbler, Philadelphia Vireo, Marsh Wren
  • Parks and reserves: Headlands Beach State Park (State or provincial park), Lakeshore Reservation (Protected area), Holden Arboretum (Protected area), Hell Hollow Wilderness Area (Protected area), Lake Erie Bluffs (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Headlands Beach State Park, Hell Hollow Wilderness Area, Holden Arboretum, Lake Erie Bluffs, Lakeshore Metropark, Lakeshore Reservation, Mentor Marsh
  • Also watch for: Coyote are established across the metroparks and the lakefront holds a large winter gull roost.

The Hocking Hills and the Wayne forest

Sandstone gorges and hemlock in the unglaciated corner of the state

The ice sheet stopped short of south-eastern Ohio, so the hills there were never flattened and the sandstone gorges hold hemlock and cool, damp forest that the rest of the state has none of. The breeding warbler list follows from that.

  • Habitat: Sandstone gorge and rock shelter with hemlock, mixed oak and hickory on the ridges, mature beech and maple in the coves, and reclaimed mine land on the margins.
  • When: Late April to June for the breeding birds. The state park gorges are busy at weekends from May onwards.
  • Getting there: Columbus is the airport, an hour north-west, and Logan the base. Hocking Hills has paid car parks and busy trails, and the Wayne and Shawnee forest roads are quieter and free.
  • Base yourself in: Logan
  • Look for: Cerulean Warbler, Worm-eating Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Hooded Warbler, Louisiana Waterthrush, Acadian Flycatcher, Wood Thrush, Yellow-throated Warbler, Scarlet Tanager, Barred Owl, Ruffed Grouse, Wild Turkey
  • Parks and reserves: Shawnee State Forest (Forest or grassland), Hocking Hills State Park (State or provincial park), Wayne National Forest - Athens Unit (Forest or grassland), Wayne National Forest - Ironton Unit (Forest or grassland), Shawnee State Forest Wilderness Area (Forest or grassland)
  • Also covers: Hocking Hills State Park, Shawnee State Forest Wilderness Area, Wayne National Forest - Athens Unit, Wayne National Forest - Ironton Unit
  • Also watch for: Black bear have moved back into the hills from the east, and the gorges hold rare ferns and mosses left from the glacial period.

Killdeer Plains and the reclaimed prairie

Reclaimed wet prairie in the flattest part of Ohio, with a winter owl roost

The centre of the state was a wet prairie that was drained for farming, and these wildlife areas are the pieces put back. Killdeer Plains is known for its wintering Short-eared Owls and its conifer roost of Long-eared Owls, and Funk Bottoms floods for shorebirds in spring.

  • Habitat: Reclaimed wet prairie and sedge meadow, shallow flooded impoundment and mudflat, planted conifer blocks, and cropland between.
  • When: December to February for the owl roosts, March and April for the flooded shorebird fields, and June for the grassland breeders.
  • Getting there: Columbus is the airport, an hour south, and Marion the base. All three are state wildlife areas with gravel roads and free access, and the owl roosts are watched from the road rather than entered.
  • Base yourself in: Marion
  • Look for: Short-eared Owl, Long-eared Owl, Northern Harrier, Bald Eagle, Sandhill Crane, American Golden Plover, Pectoral Sandpiper, Wilson's Phalarope, Sedge Wren, Henslow's Sparrow, Bobolink, Dickcissel
  • Parks and reserves: Big Island Wildlife Area (Protected area), Killdeer Plains Wildlife Area (Protected area), Foundation Lake (Protected area), Myers Woods (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Big Island Wildlife Area, Foundation Lake, Funk Bottoms Wildlife Area, Killdeer Plains Wildlife Area, Myers Woods
  • Also watch for: The wildlife areas hold the state's largest reintroduced trumpeter swan flock and a large white-tailed deer population.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Ohio?

The second week of May, without much argument. The Lake Erie shore holds a whole warbler migration at close range for about a fortnight, and nothing else in the state comes near it. Book accommodation a year ahead if you want to be near Magee Marsh.

Why do the warblers stop at Magee Marsh?

They are moving north and Lake Erie is in the way. Birds that arrive at the shore wait for good conditions before crossing, and the beach ridge woodland at Magee is the last cover before the water, so they concentrate in it and feed at eye level.

Is the boardwalk too crowded?

It genuinely is busy in the second week of May, with thousands of people a day. The birds do not seem to mind, but if you do, the Lake Erie islands take the same migration with a fraction of the visitors, and the Ottawa refuge drive is quieter too.

Where do I go outside May?

The unglaciated south-east for breeding warblers from late April to June, and Killdeer Plains from December to February for Short-eared and Long-eared Owls. The Cleveland lakefront holds gulls and sea duck through the same winter months.

What does unglaciated mean here?

The ice sheet stopped short of south-eastern Ohio, so the hills there were never flattened. That corner has sandstone gorges, hemlock, rock shelters and cool damp forest, and a breeding bird list the rest of the state does not have.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
