Ponta Delgada (PDL) on São Miguel takes most international flights, with Terceira (TER) as the second gateway. Both connect to Lisbon in about two and a half hours.

Inter-island flights and ferries, and the weather cancels both. A hire car covers each island, and Corvo is small enough to walk.

When to Go

Seabird summer

April to September

Cory’s Shearwater is everywhere and calls over the cliffs after dark. The storm petrels are on the Graciosa islets and the boats out of Horta and Madalena run every day the weather allows.

Go for Monteiro’s Storm Petrel at the islets, and the terns on the Santa Maria and Terceira colonies.

Trade off The Nearctic vagrants have not arrived, and this is the busiest half of the year for accommodation.

Typical daily average
  • Ponta Delgada: 15 to 24 °C / 59 to 75 °F
  • Horta: 15 to 23 °C / 59 to 73 °F

The vagrant month

October

Westerlies across the Atlantic put American passerines and waders onto Corvo and Flores, and Cabo da Praia fills with Nearctic waders.

Go for Corvo, which is the single reason many birders come to the archipelago at all.

Trade off Corvo has very few beds and they book a year ahead. The inter-island flights are cancelled by weather more often than not.

Typical daily average
  • Ponta Delgada: 16 to 22 °C / 61 to 72 °F
  • Corvo: 15 to 21 °C / 59 to 70 °F

The quiet half

November to March

Wet, windy and mild. The seabirds have gone and the resident landbirds carry the island.

Go for Azores Bullfinch in the Priolo forest with the whole reserve to yourself.

Trade off The boats mostly stay in and the inter-island flights are unreliable.

Typical daily average
  • Ponta Delgada: 12 to 18 °C / 54 to 64 °F
  • Horta: 12 to 17 °C / 54 to 63 °F

The Azores End to End

Ponta Delgada (PDL), Furnas 3 nights, Santa Maria 2 nights, Terceira 2 nights, Graciosa islets 2 nights, Faial 3 nights, Corvo 3 nights, Ponta Delgada (PDL) out

Driving: about 13 hours behind the wheel across the whole route, roughly 420 km.

Six islands on the inter-island flights, which is as much of the archipelago as one trip sensibly holds. Late September into October is the version that gets both the seabirds and the vagrants.

São Miguel and the Priolo forest

The largest island, and the only place the Azores Bullfinch has ever lived

The Priolo, or Azores Bullfinch, holds a few hundred hectares of native laurel forest at the eastern end of São Miguel and nowhere else on earth. The crater lakes and the Furnas valley are the rest of the island’s birding.

  • Habitat: Native laurel forest and cloud heath on the eastern massif, with crater lakes, hot springs and pasture across the rest of the island.
  • When: The bullfinch is resident and easiest from May to September, when the forest is open and the birds are feeding young.
  • Getting there: Ponta Delgada takes the international flights and the island is worked by hire car in two days. The Priolo forest is reached from the Nordeste road, and SPEA runs an environmental centre with marked trails.
  • Base yourself in: Ponta Delgada or Furnas
  • Look for: Azores Bullfinch, Azores Chaffinch, Atlantic Canary, Common Buzzard, Grey Wagtail, Eurasian Blackcap
  • Parks and reserves: Área Protegida para a Gestão de Habitats do Pico da Vara (protected area)
  • Stops:
    • : The archipelago’s main town and airport. Every trip starts here and the harbour takes gulls and terns.
    • : A hot spring village in a crater at the eastern end of the island, and the base for the Priolo forest above it.
    • : The crater lake below Furnas village. The reedy margins hold moorhen and the woodland around it holds the passerines.
  • Also covers: Área Protegida para a Gestão de Habitats do Pico da Vara, Lagoa das Furnas, Ponta Delgada, São Miguel

Terceira and the Cabo da Praia pool

A worked-out quarry pool on the east coast, and the best wader trap in the archipelago

Cabo da Praia is a flooded quarry beside the sea that pulls in Nearctic waders every autumn. Terceira also has the second airport and the inter-island flights.

  • Habitat: A shallow brackish quarry pool open to the tide, with pasture, laurel remnant and crater ground inland.
  • When: August to November is the wader window. The pool is worth checking on any autumn day.
  • Getting there: Terceira has the archipelago’s second international airport and is the hub for inter-island flights. The Cabo da Praia pool sits beside the road near Praia da Vitória and is watched from the bank.
  • Base yourself in: Praia da Vitória or Angra do Heroísmo
  • Look for: Semipalmated Plover, White-rumped Sandpiper, Common Tern, Roseate Tern, Yellow-legged Gull, Ruddy Turnstone
  • Parks and reserves: Zona de Protecção Especial da Ilhéu das Cabras (special protection area)
  • Stops:
    • : The central island with the second international airport, and the base for the inter-island flights. The Cabo da Praia quarry pool on its east coast is the archipelago’s wader trap.
  • Also covers: Terceira, Zona de Protecção Especial da Ilhéu das Cabras

Graciosa and the storm-petrel islets

Three rocks off one island, and the entire world population of Monteiro’s Storm Petrel

Monteiro’s Storm Petrel was described in 1998 and breeds on the Graciosa islets and nowhere else. The islets were cleared of rodents and the birds are seen from a boat rather than landed on.

  • Habitat: Bare basalt islets off a low volcanic island, with pasture and vineyard on Graciosa itself.
  • When: Monteiro’s Storm Petrel is at the islets from April to October, with the hot-season breeders on the colony through the summer.
  • Getting there: Graciosa is reached by inter-island flight or ferry. The islets of Praia, Baixo and Baleia are worked from a boat out of Praia, and landing is controlled.
  • Base yourself in: Santa Cruz da Graciosa
  • Look for: Monteiro's Storm Petrel, Band-rumped Storm Petrel, Cory's Shearwater, Common Tern, Roseate Tern
  • Parks and reserves: Reserva Natural do Ilhéu da Praia (nature reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : Small offshore islets cleared of rodents. Monteiro’s Storm Petrel breeds here and nowhere else in the world.
  • Also covers: Graciosa islets of Praia, Baixo and Baleia, Reserva Natural do Ilhéu da Praia

Pico, Faial and the channel

A deep channel between two islands, and the boats that work it

The channel between Pico and Faial is where the whale and seabird boats run, and the offshore banks beyond it hold the shearwaters and the deep-water species.

  • Habitat: Deep ocean channel and offshore bank, with laurel forest and lava field on Pico and caldera heath on Faial.
  • When: The boats run from April to October. Cory’s Shearwater is everywhere through the summer and calls over the cliffs after dark.
  • Getting there: Both islands take inter-island flights and a ferry crosses the channel in half an hour. The seabird boats leave Horta and Madalena and are weather dependent.
  • Base yourself in: Horta or Madalena
  • Look for: Cory's Shearwater, Great Shearwater, Sooty Shearwater, Barolo Shearwater, Band-rumped Storm Petrel, Common Tern
  • Parks and reserves: Parque Natural do Faial (natural park)
  • Stops:
    • : The volcano island, and the highest ground in Portugal. Its channel with Faial is where the whale and seabird boats work.
    • : The island across the channel from Pico, with the Horta marina and the Capelinhos volcanic point for seabird passage.
  • Also covers: Parque Natural do Faial, Pico, the Bank of Fortune pelagic grounds
  • Also watch for: Sperm whales are resident in the deep water off Pico and Faial and the channel is one of the most reliable places in the world to see them. Blue and fin whales pass through on spring migration.

Flores and Corvo

The two western islands, a third of the way to Newfoundland and the best Nearctic landfall in Europe

Corvo in October is the reason many birders come to the Azores at all. It is seventeen square kilometres of crater and pasture and it takes American passerines and waders every autumn.

  • Habitat: A small crater island of pasture, stone walls and a single caldera, with Flores across the channel carrying laurel forest and waterfalls.
  • When: October is the month. The whole point of Corvo is the Nearctic vagrant arrival on the westerlies.
  • Getting there: Flores takes the inter-island flight and a small plane or ferry crosses to Corvo. Accommodation on Corvo is limited and books out for October long in advance.
  • Base yourself in: Vila do Corvo or Santa Cruz das Flores
  • Look for: Grey Catbird, Red-eyed Vireo, Black-and-white Warbler, Blackpoll Warbler, Semipalmated Plover, Buff-breasted Sandpiper
  • Parks and reserves: Reserva da Biosfera das Flores (biosphere reserve)
  • Stops:

Santa Maria and the south

The oldest and driest island, and the archipelago’s southern outpost

Santa Maria is warmer and drier than the rest and holds the Azores’ only regular breeding Common Tern colonies on its islets, with Cory’s Shearwater on the cliffs.

  • Habitat: Dry pasture and vineyard on the oldest island in the chain, with sea cliffs and offshore islets.
  • When: April to October, while the terns and shearwaters are on the colonies.
  • Getting there: An inter-island flight reaches Vila do Porto. The island is small and a hire car covers it in a day.
  • Base yourself in: Vila do Porto
  • Look for: Cory's Shearwater, Common Tern, Roseate Tern, Yellow-legged Gull, Atlantic Canary
  • Parks and reserves: Parque Natural de Santa Maria (natural park)
  • Stops:
    • : The oldest and driest island, at the southern end of the chain. Its cliffs and islets hold Cory’s Shearwater and the tern colonies.
  • Also covers: Parque Natural de Santa Maria

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
