Best months

Routes
The Humboldt Coast6 daysThe Manu Road8 daysTambopata and the Clay Licks9 daysThe Northern Circuit12 daysManu, Cusco and the Sacred Valley16 days
Getting around
Lima is the hub and almost every route opens with a domestic flight. The Manu road and the northern circuit are long drives; Tambopata and Iquitos are reached by boat.

When to Go

Peru runs two calendars at once and they oppose each other. The Andes and the Amazon are dry and at their best from May to September. The coast spends those same months under the garua, a low sea fog that holds from May to October, and is clear and warm from December to April.

Peak

May to September

The dry season across the Andes and both stretches of Amazon. Roads hold, forest trails are walkable, and the clay licks run to schedule.

Go for The Manu road end to end, macaws at the Tambopata licks, the Marvellous Spatuletail at Pomacochas, and the longest trip list of the year.

Trade off The highest prices and the fullest lodges, and The Lima Coast and the Humboldt Current spends the whole of it under fog.

Typical daily average Cusco -1 to 19 °C / 30 to 66 °F, The Amazon 18 to 31 °C / 64 to 88 °F, Lima 13 to 21 °C / 55 to 70 °F

Shoulder

October and November

The highland rains return gradually while the coast comes out of the fog, so the two halves of the country swap places.

Go for Andean birding that still mostly works, a bright coast, and the rivers beginning to rise into the flooded forest.

Trade off Afternoon rain in the mountains, and some of the higher passes turn unreliable late in November.

Typical daily average Cusco 4 to 20 °C / 39 to 68 °F, The Amazon 21 to 32 °C / 70 to 90 °F, Lima 14 to 23 °C / 57 to 73 °F

Green season

December to April

Andean rain at its heaviest, high water in the Amazon, and the clearest, warmest weather of the year on the coast.

Go for Flooded forest reachable by boat, breeding Andean birds in song, wintering shorebirds at Paracas, and the lowest rates of the year.

Trade off Landslides close stretches of the Manu road, February closes the Inca Trail completely, and highland travel needs slack in the schedule.

Typical daily average Cusco 4 to 18 °C / 39 to 64 °F, The Amazon 21 to 31 °C / 70 to 88 °F, Lima 17 to 27 °C / 63 to 81 °F

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Peru for birding?

May to September is the dry season across the Andes and both stretches of Amazon, and it is when almost every operator runs. The coast works the other way round and is clearest from December to April, because the garua fog sits over Lima from May to October. The three seasons, and the month each headline bird is about.

How many bird species does Peru have?

Peru is second only to Colombia, with a national list in the high 1,800s, and the count above is the number of those with a card in the BeakBrain guide rather than a figure typed in by hand. Roughly a hundred of them occur in Peru and nowhere else.

Where do you go in Peru for the best birding?

The Manu road is the single most productive stretch in the country, dropping from 4,000 m of puna to Amazon lowland in a day, and nine of the thirty-four published Peruvian itineraries are built on it. The northern circuit from Chiclayo to Tarapoto holds the highest endemic count. The eleven regions.

Peru or Ecuador for a first South American birding trip?

Ecuador packs a similar altitudinal gradient into much shorter drives, so a first trip with limited time goes further there. Peru rewards the longer trip: a bigger list, the Manu road, the Marañón endemics and the Humboldt coast, and none of those pair down neatly into a week. The eleven regions.

Can you see jaguars in Peru?

Yes, most reliably from a boat on the rivers and oxbow lakes of Tambopata and Manu, where animals come to the banks in the dry season from June to September. Sightings stay a matter of luck, and the lodges that run daily river transects report the most.

What other wildlife is there besides birds?

Giant otter on the oxbow lakes, thirteen primate species on the Manu road, sea lions and Humboldt Penguins on the Ballestas, vicuna on the puna, and spectacled bear in the northern cloud forest. Each region card names what is about alongside its birds.

Are there eco lodges in Peru?

There are, and the Amazon concessions are the strongest of them: several are community-owned or run under agreements with the neighbouring communities. Peru has no national certification register of its own, so every property here comes from the supplier archive and carries a chip only where an international scheme lists it. The regions and what each holds.

Can you travel lodge to lodge in Peru?

Along the Manu road and on the Tambopata that is exactly how it works: a chain of lodges at descending altitudes, each a few hours from the next by road or boat, with the transfers included. The northern circuit is more of a driving route between towns.

How long do you need for a birding trip to Peru?

A week reaches one thing well, either the coast or the lower Manu road. Nine to twelve days covers Tambopata or the whole northern circuit. Sixteen takes the southern route from Lima through the Sacred Valley and down the Manu road to the Madre de Dios. Five routes.

Do you need to worry about altitude?

Cusco sits at 3,400 m and the top of the Manu road passes 4,000 m, so two nights acclimatising before any high pass is the standard advice. The Sacred Valley at 2,800 m is a gentler place to spend them than Cusco itself.

Is Peru good for a family trip?

The Amazon lodges suit it best: short boat trips, canopy towers, and wildlife that shows without a long walk. The coast at Paracas works for a shorter trip with no altitude at all. The Manu road is a long drive on rough surfaces and asks more of everyone.

What does responsible travel look like in Peru?

The clearest version is the community-held reserve: Chaparri is owned by the Santa Catalina de Chongoyape community, the Tambopata concessions run on agreements with Infierno, and the Marvelous Spatuletail feeders at Pomacochas exist because ECOAN pays for the habitat around them. Choosing those puts the money where the birds are.

Can you combine birding with Machu Picchu?

Easily, and the sixteen-day route above does it. The train line up the Urubamba runs through good forest, Aguas Calientes has Andean Cock-of-the-rock along the river, and the sanctuary itself holds Inca Wren and Green-and-white Hummingbird. The Inca Trail closes for the whole of February.

Which Amazon do you choose, Iquitos or Tambopata?

Tambopata is an hour from Cusco and then a boat, and it has the clay licks. Iquitos is reached only by air and works from a riverboat, which puts you on water the whole time and reaches the flooded forest at high water from December to April. They hold different birds and both are worth a trip.

Do you need a guide?

For the Amazon lodges a guide comes with the stay. For the Manu road and the northern circuit a driver who knows the stops saves days, and Peruvian guiding is largely independent and regional rather than run through a few big names. The operators publishing itineraries here.
