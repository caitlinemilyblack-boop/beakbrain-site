At a glance

Best months
  • August to October for song and firm roads
  • November to April for the migrants and the fullest marshes
Regions
Nyika National ParkVwaza Marsh Wildlife ReserveThe Viphya Plateau and Nkhata BayDzalanyama and LilongweLiwonde and the southern lake shoreThe Zomba PlateauMount Mulanje and the Thyolo forestsLengwe and the lower Shire
Routes
The north: Dzalanyama, Nyika and Vwaza13 daysThe south: Mulanje, Zomba and the Shire10 daysThe Shire and the southern lake7 days
Getting in
Lilongwe takes most international flights and serves the north, and Blantyre serves the southern highlands and the Shire.
Getting around
The main road runs the length of the country on good tar and covers long distances quickly. The unsurfaced access roads to the best sites are the difficulty, and a 4WD is often essential on them during the rains.

When to Go

The Malawian year runs wet from November to April, cool and dry from May to August and hot and dry in September and October. Bird activity peaks in the rains, and so does the difficulty of the roads to the best sites. The cool dry months give the easiest travelling and the quietest forest.

Warm and wet

November to April

Bird activity is at its peak and the migrants are in, and the roads to several of the best sites are at their worst.

Go for Breeding montane specials on the plateau, Palearctic and intra-African migrants in the southern thicket, and the marshes at their fullest.

Trade off The lowlands are hot, and a 4WD becomes essential on the unsurfaced roads to Nyika, Dzalanyama and Lengwe.

Typical daily average

Cool and dry

May to August

Daytime weather is pleasant and the road conditions are the best of the year.

Go for Miombo foraging flocks, which are most frequent in these months, and easy driving to every region.

Trade off The migrants have gone, forest birds are much less vocal, and nights on the high plateau are very cold.

Typical daily average

Hot and dry

September to October

Many resident birds are breeding and the roads are still firm, in the hottest weeks of the year.

Go for Green-headed Oriole at its noisiest on the southern massifs, and miombo species calling before the rains.

Trade off It is uncomfortably hot in the Shire lowlands and water is scarce away from the river and the lake.

Nyika National Park

Rolling montane grassland at 2,000 m, with forest patches and miombo on the foothills

Nyika is a high plateau of rolling grassland dotted with patches of Afromontane forest, and miombo, the broad-leaved woodland that covers much of the interior, wraps its foothills. Roads reach most of the birding, which makes it one of the easiest montane areas to visit in south-central Africa. Wattled Crane and Denham's Bustard walk the open grassland, and Sharpe's Akalat attends ant columns inside the forest.

  • Habitat: Rolling montane grassland with bracken, protea savanna and bogs sits at about 2,000 m, broken by small forest patches and by larger blocks of Afromontane forest. Hagenia woodland and a series of small dams surround the plateau headquarters, and miombo woodland covers the foothills below.
  • When: May to October for firm roads and pleasant days, and January to April for the greatest bird activity at the cost of the surface. Blue Swallow is on the plateau from mid September to March, and nights are cold from May to August.
  • Getting there: The road is tarred as far as Rumphi, and 53 km of gravel runs on from there to the western entrance gate. A high-clearance vehicle is advisable for the 110 km from that gate to the plateau headquarters, and a 4WD is usually essential from January to April, when sections become impassable. An airstrip serves the headquarters.
  • Base yourself in: Rumphi
  • All year: Sharpe's Akalat, White-chested Alethe, Fülleborn's Boubou, Scarlet-tufted Sunbird, Ludwig's Double-collared Sunbird, Chapin's Apalis, Wattled Crane, Denham's Bustard, Red-winged Francolin, Bar-tailed Trogon, Moustached Tinkerbird, Black-lored Cisticola, Montane Widowbird, Olive-flanked Ground Robin, Brown Parisoma, Mountain Yellow Warbler
  • In the breeding season: Blue Swallow, Angola Swallow, Slender-billed Starling, Scarlet-tufted Sunbird
  • On passage: Pallid Harrier, Montagu's Harrier, Scarce Swift
  • In winter: Fülleborn's Boubou, Brown Parisoma, Hildebrandt's Spurfowl, African Hill Babbler
  • National park: Nyika National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The plateau headquarters at 2,300 m, with small dams, Hagenia woodland and a marsh below the dam wall. Blue Swallow feeds over the marsh from mid September to March and Montane Widowbird sits in the sedges at the dam edges.
    • : A natural lake 8 km west of the headquarters. Wattled Crane is looked for here and on the airstrip nearby.
    • : The last town on tar before the plateau, and the junction for the reserve to the west. The miombo just inside the park's western gate, 53 km beyond, holds foraging flocks with Whyte's Barbet, African Spotted Creeper and Arnot's Chat.
  • Also covers: Chelinda, Lake Kaulime, Rumphi
  • Also watch for: Roan Antelope live here at one of the highest densities anywhere, and Eland, Reedbuck and Burchell's Zebra are common on the grassland. Leopard are numerous and are seen from time to time.

Vwaza Marsh Wildlife Reserve

Pans and marshes below miombo hills, with mopane at its northern limit

Vwaza is a lowland reserve west of the Nyika plateau, where a low range of miombo hills falls to plains of mopane woodland, thorn thicket and a wide network of pans. Babbling Starling moves through the tallest woodland in noisy flocks of up to sixty, and Chestnut-backed Sparrow-Weaver sits quietly in the same trees. Game gathers at the pan inside the main gate.

  • Habitat: A low range of miombo-clad hills runs along the eastern edge. The plains to the west hold mopane woodland at its northern limit, thorny thickets and an extensive network of pans and marshes.
  • When: Year round. The pans concentrate birds and game as the dry season advances from June to November.
  • Getting there: Follow the road towards the plateau from Rumphi and turn off after 9 km, and the reserve gate lies 19 km further on. The reserve can also be entered at its north-eastern corner, and the track to that entrance is in poor condition.
  • Base yourself in: Kazuni
  • All year: Babbling Starling, Chestnut-backed Sparrow-Weaver, Racket-tailed Roller, Miombo Pied Barbet, Arnot's Chat, Miombo Scrub Robin, Böhm's Flycatcher, Meyer's Parrot, Grey Crowned Crane, Dickinson's Kestrel, Collared Palm Thrush, Cabanis's Bunting, Rufous-bellied Tit, Broad-tailed Paradise Whydah, Senegal Coucal, Collared Pratincole
  • In the breeding season: Grey Crowned Crane, Collared Pratincole, Chestnut-backed Sparrow-Weaver
  • On passage: Collared Pratincole, Pennant-winged Nightjar, European Roller
  • In winter: Babbling Starling, Miombo Tit, Miombo Scrub Robin, Racket-tailed Roller
  • National park: Vwaza Marsh Wildlife Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The main entrance in the south-eastern corner, on a pan with abundant game. Grey Crowned Crane and Collared Pratincole come to the water, and the 33 km road that runs north from here along the eastern edge is the stretch that holds Babbling Starling and Chestnut-backed Sparrow-Weaver in tall miombo.
  • Also covers: Kazuni
  • Also watch for: Elephant, Buffalo, Sable, Roan, Puku and Hippopotamus all use the pans, and the camp at the main gate overlooks one of them.

The Viphya Plateau and Nkhata Bay

Pine plantations and forest remnants on a highland spine, with lowland forest by the water beneath

The southern Viphya is a sprawling plateau of grassland, granite hills, pine plantations and remnant Afromontane forest, and the main road north crosses it. Scaly Spurfowl lives in the understorey of the neglected plantations and occurs nowhere else in the country. On the lake shore below, small forest reserves near Nkhata Bay hold East Coast Akalat, which calls at dawn and dusk.

  • Habitat: A mosaic of montane grassland, granite hills, pine plantations and remnant Afromontane forest covers the plateau, with broad grassy hollows between the ridges. The lake shore below carries patches of lowland evergreen forest behind the beaches.
  • When: Year round. Forest birds are most vocal from August to October, and the montane forest is quiet in the coldest weeks from June to August. White-headed Saw-wing crosses the main road from October to April.
  • Getting there: The tarred main road from the capital to Mzuzu crosses the plateau, and sand tracks lead east from it into the plantations and forest. Nkhata Bay lies 50 km east of Mzuzu on tar, and the forest reserves sit beside the roads north and south of the town. A 4WD is needed for the track to the peak above the northern Viphya.
  • Base yourself in: Mzuzu
  • All year: Scaly Spurfowl, East Coast Akalat, Bertram's Weaver, Fülleborn's Boubou, Moustached Tinkerbird, Olive Woodpecker, Olive-flanked Ground Robin, White-eyed Slaty Flycatcher, Yellow-crowned Canary, Blue Swallow, Green Malkoha, Eastern Nicator, Terrestrial Brownbul, Mountain Illadopsis, Oriole Finch, Sharpe's Akalat, Evergreen Forest Warbler, Narina Trogon, Grey-olive Greenbul
  • In the breeding season: Blue Swallow, Yellow-mantled Widowbird, Bertram's Weaver
  • On passage: White-headed Saw-wing, Broad-billed Roller, European Honey Buzzard
  • In winter: Scaly Spurfowl, Fan-tailed Grassbird, Trilling Cisticola, Red-capped Crombec
  • Parks and reserves: Kaning'ina Forest Reserve, Lunyangwa Forest Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The forestry village on the plateau. The tracks east of it run through neglected pine where Scaly Spurfowl is easier to see than in its own forest.
    • : A sand track leaves the main road for 10.6 km and ends at a broad grassy hollow ringed by low miombo and forest. Blue Swallow feeds over the adjacent grassland and Fan-tailed Grassbird sits in the rank growth.
    • Chintheche : The quieter lake shore south of Nkhata Bay, with Eastern Golden Weaver in the lakeside reeds and White-fronted Plover on the beaches.
    • Nkhata Bay : The lakeside town. Two small forest reserves within 15 km of it are the easiest places in the country for East Coast Akalat.
  • Also covers: Chikangawa, Chintheche, Kaning'ina Forest Reserve, Lunyangwa Forest Reserve, Luwawa, Mzuzu, Nkhata Bay

Dzalanyama and Lilongwe

A hundred thousand hectares of miombo woodland an hour and a half from the capital

Dzalanyama protects a vast tract of largely pristine miombo along a low range of border hills 58 km from the capital, and the miombo birding here is as good as any in the region. Stierling's Woodpecker is easier here than anywhere else in its range. Olive-headed Weaver hangs from the old-man's-beard lichen and Souza's Shrike travels in trios inside the same foraging flocks.

  • Habitat: Miombo woodland covers most of the reserve, with rocky hills, numerous grassy hollows and small patches of riparian forest inside it. A strip of riverine forest lines the Lingadzi River in the capital, and rocky miombo and small forest patches surround the hills near Dedza.
  • When: Year round. Foraging flocks are most frequent in the middle of the year, and many species are most vocal from August to October, just before the rains. The access road is occasionally impassable during the rains.
  • Getting there: The road runs south west out of the capital, turns to gravel and passes 24 km through villages and farmland to the reserve gate. Allow at least an hour and a half, and expect a poor surface during the rains. Visitors sign a book at the gate, and the best woodland lies 20 km further along the road inside.
  • Base yourself in: Lilongwe
  • All year: Stierling's Woodpecker, Olive-headed Weaver, Souza's Shrike, Shelley's Sunbird, Pale-billed Hornbill, Miombo Pied Barbet, Whyte's Barbet, Boulder Chat, Miombo Scrub Robin, Red-capped Crombec, Yellow-bellied Hyliota, Wood Pipit, Orange-winged Pytilia, Lesser Seedcracker, African Finfoot, African Broadbill, Anchieta's Sunbird, Miombo Tit, African Spotted Creeper, Miombo Rock Thrush
  • In the breeding season: Broad-tailed Paradise Whydah, Yellow-mantled Widowbird, Red-headed Weaver
  • On passage: Pennant-winged Nightjar, Broad-billed Roller, Thick-billed Cuckoo
  • In winter: Olive-headed Weaver, Shelley's Sunbird, Souza's Shrike, Miombo Tit
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Dzalanyama Forest Reserve, The reserve itself. Drive slowly, stop for any foraging flock and follow it on foot, because the woodland is silent between parties and full of birds inside them.
  • Stops:
    • Lilongwe : The capital and the base for the reserve. A 150 hectare nature sanctuary lines the Lingadzi River in the middle of the city, and African Finfoot, African Broadbill and Half-collared Kingfisher all use its riverside trail.
    • Dedza : The town below a range of rocky miombo hills on the road south. Boulder Chat, Miombo Rock Thrush and Souza's Shrike all occur within a short walk of the hills.
  • Also covers: Dedza, Dzalanyama Forest Reserve, Lilongwe
  • Also watch for: The riverine forest in the capital holds Vervet Monkey along the trail, and the rocky hills at Dedza carry Rock Hyrax on the cliffs.

Liwonde and the southern lake shore

The Shire River below the lake, with mopane, ilala palm savanna and flood plain

Liwonde stretches along the Shire River where it drains the lake, in a low and relatively arid stretch of the southern Rift. Brown-breasted Barbet comes to the riverine figs and Lilian's Lovebird flies to water through the mopane early in the morning. The resort villages up the lake shore add Böhm's Bee-eater and Olive Bee-eater in the lakeside thickets.

  • Habitat: Mopane woodland, river banks and riverine thicket, flood plain and ilala palm savanna line the Shire, with miombo on the hill slopes. The lake shore to the north carries sandy beaches, lakeside thicket and marshy ground behind the villages.
  • When: Birding is good year round. Brown-breasted Barbet is hardest during the dry season, when the riverine figs carry no fruit. Olive Bee-eater passes the lake shore mainly from late August to early October, and Dwarf Bittern arrives on the marshes from November to April.
  • Getting there: The park gate lies about 7 km east of Liwonde town. Turn off the main road beyond the Shire bridge and follow the national park signs. A boat trip on the Shire is the way to reach the river birds, and a ferry crosses to the camp on the far bank. Cape Maclear and Monkey Bay sit at the southern end of the lake on tarred roads from Mangochi.
  • Base yourself in: Liwonde
  • All year: Brown-breasted Barbet, Lilian's Lovebird, Böhm's Bee-eater, White-backed Night Heron, Western Banded Snake Eagle, Racket-tailed Roller, Collared Palm Thrush, Livingstone's Flycatcher, Senegal Lapwing, Meves's Starling, Southern Ground Hornbill, Brown-headed Parrot, Arnot's Chat, African Skimmer, Rufous-bellied Heron, African Pygmy Goose, Allen's Gallinule, Black Coucal, Olive Bee-eater, Eastern Golden Weaver, Bennett's Woodpecker, Mosque Swallow

  • In winter: Lilian's Lovebird, Meves's Starling, Racket-tailed Roller, African Skimmer
  • National parks: Liwonde National Park, Lake Malawi National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The town on the Shire bridge and the way in to the park. The lodges on the northern outskirts share many of the flood plain birds, with Allen's Gallinule and Black Coucal in the marsh.
    • : The village on the road east of the river, 14 km from the bank opposite the park camp. It is the way in for anyone reaching the park without a vehicle.
    • Senga Bay : A lake shore village with Böhm's Bee-eater and Livingstone's Flycatcher in the thickets. The marshy ground just south of it holds Rufous-bellied Heron, African Pygmy Goose and Lesser Jacana.

The Zomba Plateau

A forested plateau above the eastern Rift wall, with pine, montane forest and miombo below

Zomba is a rolling plateau that rises above the eastern wall of the Rift, heavily planted with pine and still holding several accessible patches of montane forest. The forest remnants at the foot of the escarpment behind Zomba town are the convenient place to search for White-winged Apalis, which calls from the roadside trees. Thyolo Alethe attends ant columns in the forest by the dam higher up.

  • Habitat: Patches of Afromontane forest are interspersed with grassland and pine plantations on the plateau, and fine miombo woodland lines the roads lower down. A dam and a river with a waterfall run through the forest on the plateau itself.
  • When: Birding is good year round. The plateau roads become extremely muddy during the rains from December to March and can defeat a 2WD vehicle. Forest birds call most from August to October.
  • Getting there: Steep, narrow roads climb the southern slope above Zomba town, and a 4WD is usually unnecessary outside the rains. Turn north off the main road through the town to reach the plateau. Care is needed on the switchbacks whenever they are wet.
  • Base yourself in: Zomba
  • All year: White-winged Apalis, Yellow-throated Apalis, Thyolo Alethe, Malawi Batis, White-eared Barbet, Forest Double-collared Sunbird, Southern Citril, Evergreen Forest Warbler, Livingstone's Turaco, Olive-headed Greenbul, Black-headed Apalis, Yellow-throated Woodland Warbler, Olive Bushshrike, Red-faced Crimsonwing, Green Twinspot, Bertram's Weaver, Orange Ground Thrush, Lemon Dove, Half-collared Kingfisher, Mountain Wagtail, African Wood Owl, Speckle-throated Woodpecker
  • In the breeding season: Bertram's Weaver, Malawi Batis, White-eared Barbet

Mount Mulanje and the Thyolo forests

A mile-high massif above tea estates, with forest in the wet valleys

Mulanje rises almost vertically from a plain at 700 m to a plateau at about 2,000 m, and a green girdle of tea estates circles its base. The wet valleys carry a gradual transition from lowland through submontane to montane forest, and that is where the forest specials live. Thyolo Alethe, Green-headed Oriole and White-winged Apalis all occur on this massif and in the estate forests on Mount Thyolo to the north west.

  • Habitat: Grassland and forest cover the plateau, with bare rock and bracken slopes between them. The drier valleys carry miombo and riverine forest, and the wetter ones run from lowland forest up to montane forest. Patches of Afromontane and riparian forest survive on the tea estates around Mount Thyolo and on the slopes of Mount Soche.
  • When: Access is possible year round. May to July brings the Chiperoni, a drizzling mist that can cover the mountains for days, and it may freeze at night from May to August. The heaviest rains fall from December to March. Green-headed Oriole is easiest in September and October, when it is at its noisiest.
  • Getting there: The gorge on the wet eastern flank can be visited on a day trip from the tea estate road that runs to the border, and permission and directions come from the estate office. Trekking routes start at Likabula, and huts along the way give shelter. Permission from estate management is needed before entering the forests on Mount Thyolo, and Mount Soche is walked from a village above the city in about an hour.
  • Base yourself in: Mulanje
  • All year: Thyolo Alethe, Green-headed Oriole, White-winged Apalis, Yellow-throated Apalis, Bar-tailed Trogon, Malawi Batis, Green Barbet, White-eared Barbet, Silvery-cheeked Hornbill, Black-fronted Bushshrike, Spotted Ground Thrush, Orange Ground Thrush, Olive-flanked Ground Robin, Stripe-cheeked Greenbul, Yellow-streaked Greenbul, Grey-olive Greenbul, Bertram's Weaver, Scarce Swift, Blue Swallow, Cinnamon Bracken Warbler, Square-tailed Drongo, Red-throated Twinspot, African Broadbill, Grey Cuckooshrike, Trumpeter Hornbill
  • In the breeding season: Green-headed Oriole, Scarce Swift, Bertram's Weaver
  • On passage: Blue Swallow, Scarce Swift, Barred Long-tailed Cuckoo
  • In winter: Thyolo Alethe, Spotted Ground Thrush, Olive-flanked Ground Robin, Green Barbet
  • Parks and reserves: Mulanje Mountain Forest Reserve
  • Stops:
    • Mulanje : The town below the massif and the base for a trek. The wet gorge on the eastern flank is the wettest place in the country and the richest forest birding on the mountain.
    • : The village at the forest station where the walking routes up the mountain begin. The drier valley here carries miombo and riverine forest below the plateau.
    • Thyolo : The town among the tea estates north west of the massif. Green-headed Oriole and Green Barbet hold the estate forest patches, and both are found by call.
    • Blantyre : The southern city and the arrival point for this half of the country. Three highways radiate from it and every site in this region lies within an hour of one of them.
    • : A 1,530 m peak on the southern edge of the city, walked from a village below in about an hour. Forest fragments below the flat-topped ridge hold Spotted Ground Thrush and White-winged Apalis.
  • Also covers: Blantyre, Likhubula, Mount Soche, Mulanje Mountain Forest Reserve, Thyolo
  • Also watch for: Samango Monkey and Blue Duiker live in the wetter forest, and the massif carries a chameleon and a group of orchids found on very few other mountains.

Lengwe and the lower Shire

Hot, low-lying thicket on the Shire plain, strong for migrants

Lengwe is a hot, low park on the Shire plain, dominated by dense thicket and particularly good for Palearctic and intra-African migrants. Böhm's Bee-eater hawks insects from the vegetation along the river inside the gate, and Thick-billed Cuckoo lays its eggs in the nests of Retz's Helmetshrike in the more open woodland to the south. Lemon-breasted Canary lives in the palm country on the sugar estate nearby.

  • Habitat: Dense dry thicket dominates the park, with riparian thicket along the river inside the gate and more open woodland on the southerly roads. Sugar cane and ilala palm cover much of the plain outside.
  • When: November to April brings the migrants that make this park worth the drive, and road conditions during those rains can be unreliable. May to October gives easier driving and a quieter list.
  • Getting there: The park is about two hours from Blantyre. The signposted turn off west from the main road lies 20 km south of the Shire bridge at Chikwawa, and the gate is 10 km further on through sugar cane.
  • Base yourself in: Chikwawa
  • All year: Thick-billed Cuckoo, Böhm's Spinetail, Mottled Spinetail, Narina Trogon, Böhm's Bee-eater, Livingstone's Flycatcher, Grey Sunbird, Retz's Helmetshrike, Rudd's Apalis, Lemon-breasted Canary, Racket-tailed Roller, Bearded Scrub Robin, Eastern Nicator, Terrestrial Brownbul
  • In the breeding season: Böhm's Bee-eater, Retz's Helmetshrike, Livingstone's Flycatcher
  • On passage: Thick-billed Cuckoo, European Roller, Broad-billed Roller
  • In winter: Rudd's Apalis, Grey Sunbird, Terrestrial Brownbul, Racket-tailed Roller
  • National park: Lengwe National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The town at the Shire bridge on the road south, and the turn for the park lies 20 km beyond it.
    • : The sugar estate village south of the park. The small game park on the estate is the local site for Lemon-breasted Canary.
  • Also covers: Chikwawa, Lengwe National Park, Nchalo
  • Also watch for: Nyala live in the dense thicket and are the reason the thicket is protected, and they come to the water holes near the hides in the dry months.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Malawi?

August to October is the strongest window. The roads are still firm, Green-headed Oriole calls all day on Mount Thyolo, and the miombo birds sing before the rains break. November to April brings the migrants in and fills the marshes, and it also turns the unsurfaced approaches to Nyika, Dzalanyama and Lengwe into 4WD ground. May to August gives the easiest driving of the year and the quietest forest. The three seasons.

What do you need to enter Malawi?

Every visitor needs a visa. British passport holders can buy one on arrival or apply for an e-visa on the Malawi Immigration website first, and the passport has to have at least six months left on it from the day of arrival. A yellow fever certificate is required from anyone arriving from a country listed as a transmission risk. Arriving passengers also complete a Traveller Health Check form, which the Ministry of Health publishes online and by QR code at the airport.

Is malaria a risk in Malawi?

The CDC puts malaria transmission across the whole country and recommends that every traveller take prescription antimalarials. Plasmodium falciparum is the main parasite and chloroquine resistance is present, so ask a doctor which drug suits you. The FCDO also warns that tap water may be unsafe outside the towns and that cholera cases rise in the rains.

Can you drive yourself around Malawi?

Yes for the distances between regions. The main road runs the length of the country on good tar, and a hire car covers Lilongwe to Blantyre comfortably. The access roads are the difficulty: a 4WD is usually essential for the 110 km from the western Nyika gate up to Chelinda from January to April, and for the roads into Dzalanyama and Lengwe in the rains. Fuel shortages are frequent, so fill up for the leg ahead, and the FCDO advises against driving after dark.

How safe is Malawi for a birding trip?

The FCDO advises against travel to no part of the country. It records mugging and bag snatching, most thefts from visitors around the main bus stations, and a rise in break-ins in Lilongwe, Blantyre and Limbe. A group of British tourists were assaulted and robbed in their hotel in Mulanje in 2025. Anyone climbing Mount Mulanje should ask the Mountain Club of Malawi for current security advice before setting out.

How long does a Malawi birding trip take?

Ten days from Blantyre covers the southern half: Mulanje, the Thyolo tea estates, Zomba, Liwonde and the lower Shire. Thirteen days from Lilongwe covers the north through Dzalanyama, the Viphya and four nights on Nyika. A week reaches the lake shore and Liwonde and leaves the highlands out. The routes.

Which birds is Malawi the place for?

Yellow-throated Apalis lives here and nowhere else, on the Zomba and Mulanje massifs. Thyolo Alethe holds a handful of southern forest patches in this country and one massif over the border. Nyika carries the montane grassland and forest birds of the south-central African highlands with roads through most of them, and Dzalanyama holds Stierling’s Woodpecker and Olive-headed Weaver in miombo an hour and a half from the capital. Every Malawian species.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
