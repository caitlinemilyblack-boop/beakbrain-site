Birds that decide the trip

Hyacinth Macaws on the Transpantaneira

The largest flying parrot on earth and the bird the Pantanal is sold on. It nests in manduvi trees along the road, and the fazendas at the Pocone end keep known nests in view through the season.

When All year, and it nests from July to November.

Where Pocone, Pixaim

Jabiru, the bird the region is named for

The tallest flying bird in South America, and the Pantanal's own emblem: tuiuiu is the name every local sign uses. Nests are enormous stick platforms in isolated trees, visible from the road for months.

When All year, and it nests from the start of the dry season.

Where Pocone, Porto Cercado

Agami Heron on the Cuiaba channels

A heron that spends its life inside overhanging bank vegetation and is missed on most Neotropical trips. The jaguar boats work exactly that habitat at exactly that range, which is why Porto Jofre turns it into a routine sighting.

When Dry season, when the boats are running.

Where Porto Jofre

Sunbittern on a shrinking bank

A bird with no close relatives and a wing pattern it opens only when alarmed. It works exposed mud and shingle, so it is easiest exactly when the water is lowest.

When June to October, on falling water.

Where Porto Jofre, Caceres

Greater Rhea on the open ground

South America's largest bird, and the Nhecolandia and the Chapada cerrado are where the Pantanal shows it. Males run the nest and the chicks, so a bird with a dozen striped young behind it is a father.

When All year, chicks from September.

Where Aquidauana, Chapada dos Guimaraes

Horned Sungem on the escarpment

A cerrado hummingbird with two iridescent horns and almost no reason to come down to a feeder. Chapada dos Guimaraes is the accessible place for it, on flowering scrub between the sandstone cliffs.

When August to November, on the cerrado flowering.

Where Chapada dos Guimaraes

Red-and-green Macaws in a sinkhole

The Buraco das Araras is a hole 100 m deep and 500 m round with macaws nesting on the walls, so the birds are watched from above rather than from below. No other macaw site works like it.

When All year, and it nests from July to December.

Where Jardim, Bonito

Bare-faced Curassow on the road

A large ground bird that is hunted out across most of its range and walks the verges here. Both the Transpantaneira and the Estrada Parque hold it, which says more about those two roads than about the bird.

When All year, easiest early.

Where Pocone, Miranda

When to Go

One calendar, run by water rather than by rain falling where you are standing. The flood peaks in March because the water arriving then fell upstream weeks earlier, and it falls back through the dry season until October. The escarpment at Chapada dos Guimaraes runs several degrees cooler than the floodplain all year.

Peak

June to October

The dry season. The flood falls back into the channels, the fish concentrate with it, and everything that eats fish concentrates too.

Go for Jaguars on the Cuiaba river banks from July, the wading bird concentrations at their densest, and every Transpantaneira bridge pool holding something.

Trade off The busiest and dearest months at Porto Jofre, and nights in June and July drop to 15 C on the open floodplain.

Typical daily average
  • Pocone: 15 to 33 °C / 59 to 91 °F
  • Chapada: 12 to 30 °C / 54 to 86 °F
  • Corumba: 17 to 33 °C / 63 to 91 °F

Shoulder

April, May and November

The turn of the water, either falling or beginning to rise, with the road passable and the lodges quieter.

Go for April and May for a full wetland still in flood and much lower prices; November for the first storms and the start of the breeding build-up.

Trade off Jaguar viewing is far less reliable at either end, and November afternoons are already thundery.

Typical daily average
  • Pocone: 18 to 33 °C / 64 to 91 °F
  • Chapada: 15 to 29 °C / 59 to 84 °F
  • Corumba: 19 to 33 °C / 66 to 91 °F

Quiet

December to March

The wet season. Around 215 mm falls at Pocone in January, much of the floodplain is under water and parts of the Transpantaneira flood out.

Go for Breeding plumage, full rookeries, and the greenest landscape of the year, for anyone who will take the weather.

Trade off Roads close without notice, boat access replaces driving in places, and jaguar trips largely stop.

Typical daily average
  • Pocone: 21 to 32 °C / 70 to 90 °F
  • Chapada: 18 to 29 °C / 64 to 84 °F
  • Corumba: 23 to 33 °C / 73 to 91 °F

The Transpantaneira and Pocone

A raised dirt road with 122 wooden bridges, running 147 km into the wetland from Pocone

Almost every published Pantanal itinerary is this road. It leaves Pocone on an embankment above the floodplain and runs south for 147 km, and because the water is on both sides of it the birds are at eye level from a vehicle for hours at a stretch. The lodges are set back on their own fazendas, which is where the walking is: Piuval, Pouso Alegre and the Pixaim river crossing between them cover grassland, gallery woodland and open marsh.

  • Habitat: The Transpantaneira is a dead-end road running 148 km south from Pocone to Porto Jofre through extensive forest and open water. Birding from the road alone can produce over a hundred species in a day.
  • When: Best from May to November. Hyacinth Macaw, Jabiru and Bare-faced Curassow stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Pocone lies about 93 km south of Cuiaba, and the Transpantaneira proper begins some 16 km beyond the town. Markers count up from that point, so directions along the road are given as a distance from its start.
  • Look for: Hyacinth Macaw, Jabiru, Bare-faced Curassow, Chestnut-bellied Guan, Great Rufous Woodcreeper, Mato Grosso Antbird, Nacunda Nighthawk, Great Potoo
  • Stops:
    • : The first 30 km, where the road climbs onto its embankment and the roadside pools begin. Bird it slowly from the vehicle at dawn.
    • Pousada Piuval : The first fazenda on the road, 10 km from Pocone. It has cerrado edge as well as marsh, and a Hyacinth Macaw roost.
    • Pouso Alegre : 8,000 hectares of savanna grassland and woodland patches, 6 km off the road down its own track. It is the northern Pantanal's most reliable ground for Giant Anteater and tapir.
    • : The bridge at the halfway point. Boat trips run upstream and down from the two lodges either side of it.
    • Araras Eco Lodge boardwalk : A raised walkway and tower over marsh behind the lodge at km 32. It is open to its own guests.
  • Also covers: Araras Eco Lodge boardwalk, Pousada Piuval, Pouso Alegre, Rio Pixaim crossing, Transpantaneira, the Pocone end
  • Also watch for: Giant Anteater and Brazilian Tapir are the two the fazendas are watched for, and Pouso Alegre's 8,000 hectares of grassland is the usual place for both. Capybara, Marsh Deer and Crab-eating Fox are constant, and Giant Otter holds on the Pixaim.

Porto Jofre and the Cuiaba River

The end of the road, and the most reliable place on earth to watch a wild jaguar

The Transpantaneira stops at the Cuiaba River and the birding beyond it is done by boat. The channels of the Encontro das Aguas state park hold a jaguar density found nowhere else, and the same boats pass kingfishers, Sunbittern, Agami Heron and Black Skimmer colonies on the sandbars. Many trips end here and some go no further.

  • Habitat: The road ends at Porto Jofre at km 132. The open water around km 111 is known as Campo Jofre and carries the largest concentrations of waterbirds on the route.
  • When: Best from June to October. Sunbittern, Black Skimmer and Large-billed Tern stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Hyacinth Macaw roosts along the road, and again on the last stretch into Porto Jofre.
  • Look for: Agami Heron, Sunbittern, Black Skimmer, Large-billed Tern, Black-collared Hawk, Pied Plover, Jabiru, Hyacinth Macaw
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Encontro das Aguas State Park, Where the Cuiaba, Piquiri and Tres Irmaos rivers meet, 108,000 hectares of channel and island. Only a boat from Porto Jofre gets you in.
  • Stops:
    • : The settlement at the road head: a hotel, a campsite and a boat ramp. Every jaguar trip leaves from here.
    • : The quieter of the two main channels. Search it for Giant Otter and for the smaller herons along the bank.
    • : The narrowest of the three. The boats spend most of a jaguar morning on it.
  • Also covers: Encontro das Aguas State Park, Porto Jofre, Rio Piquiri, Rio Tres Irmaos
  • Also watch for: Jaguar is the reason the boats run, and the cats here are habituated to them rather than baited. Giant Otter families work the same channels, and Yacare Caiman line every bank in numbers that are hard to take in.

Chapada dos Guimaraes

Sandstone escarpment and cerrado above Cuiaba, an hour from the airport and 700 m higher

The Pantanal's gateway airport sits at the foot of an escarpment, and the itineraries that fly into Cuiaba almost all spend a day or two on top of it before dropping into the wetland. The national park is 32,630 hectares of cerrado scrub between sandstone cliffs with gallery forest in the valleys, and its list of more than 250 species shares almost nothing with the marsh below.

  • Habitat: The chapada stands at the western edge of the central plateau. Campo and Amazonian gallery forest surround the Veu de Noiva waterfall, and it holds a set of birds the Pantanal below it lacks.
  • When: Best from April to November. Horned Sungem, Collared Crescentchest and Coal-crested Finch are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: The park is 68 km northeast of Cuiaba.
  • Look for: Horned Sungem, Collared Crescentchest, Coal-crested Finch, White-eared Puffbird, Chapada Flycatcher, Red-legged Seriema, Greater Rhea, Band-tailed Manakin
  • National park:
    • Chapada dos Guimaraes National Park, 32,630 hectares of cerrado and sandstone in the municipalities of Cuiaba and Chapada dos Guimaraes. More than 250 species are recorded.
  • Stops:
    • : The park's 86 m waterfall and its viewpoint. Swifts cross the gorge at dawn and the trail in gives cerrado birding.
    • : A gallery-forest valley off the Cuiaba road. Search it for the forest birds the open cerrado lacks.
    • : The cliff-edge viewpoint above the Cuiaba plain. Raptors ride the updraught through the middle of the day.
  • Also covers: Chapada dos Guimaraes National Park, Cuiaba, Mirante do Centro Geodesico, Vale da Bencao, Veu de Noiva
  • Also watch for: Maned Wolf works the open cerrado on the plateau and is occasionally seen at dusk on the park roads. Brown Capuchin and Black-tufted Marmoset are in the gallery forest, and the cliffs hold a resident colony of Neotropical bats that leave at dusk from the Casa de Pedra.

Porto Cercado, the SESC reserve and Barao de Melgaco

The Pantanal's largest private reserve, east of the Transpantaneira

East of the Transpantaneira and reached on a different road out of Pocone, this is the quieter half of the northern Pantanal. The private nature reserve SESC Pantanal covers 106,000 hectares between the Cuiaba and Sao Lourenco rivers, is a UNESCO Pantanal Biosphere Reserve core zone and a Ramsar site, and holds sixty per cent of the bird species recorded in the whole Upper Paraguay basin. The reserve is in Barao de Melgaco and its hotel is at Porto Cercado on the Cuiaba, which is why this region carries both.

  • Habitat: Seasonally flooded grassland, gallery forest along the Cuiaba river and cordilheiras, the low wooded ridges that stay dry, on the northern edge of the Pantanal.
  • When: Good all year. Jabiru, Buff-necked Ibis and Yellow-billed Cardinal stay through the year.
  • Getting there: BR-070 from Cuiaba to Pocone, then the Estrada Parque Porto Cercado, about 140 km and two hours with the dirt section; the Transpantaneira runs south from Pocone.
  • Look for: Jabiru, Plumbeous Ibis, Buff-necked Ibis, Southern Screamer, Blue-throated Piping Guan, Golden-collared Macaw, Yellow-billed Cardinal, Snail Kite
  • Parks and reserves:
    • private nature reserve SESC Pantanal, 106,000 hectares between the Cuiaba and Sao Lourenco rivers, the largest private reserve in Brazil. It has its own lodge, towers and boat trips.
  • Stops:
    • : The river town the reserve and the boat trips are reached from, two hours south-east of Cuiaba.
    • : The eastern boundary river. Bird it by boat for kingfishers, skimmers and the bank-nesting terns.
    • : The Cuiaba river landing at the end of the Pocone road. The SESC hotel sits here and most boat trips on this side leave from it.
  • Visitor centres: private nature reserve SESC Pantanal visitor centre
  • Also covers: Barao de Melgaco, Pirizal, Porto Cercado, private nature reserve SESC Pantanal visitor centre, Rio Sao Lourenco, Santo Antonio de Leverger
  • Also watch for: Marsh Deer stand out of the water across the seasonal lakes here, and the reserve's own surveys carry Brazilian Tapir, Giant Anteater and Giant Otter. The colonial waterbird rookeries on the reserve are the reason the Ramsar listing exists.

Caceres and the upper Paraguay

The river town at the head of the Paraguay, and the way onto the water in the far north-west

Caceres sits where the Paraguay river becomes navigable, and the wetland below it is the least visited part of the northern Pantanal. The Taiama Ecological Station is a river island reserve with one of the highest jaguar densities recorded anywhere, and the boat trips out of town reach it and the channels around it. No operator schedules it, which is its appeal.

  • Habitat: The River Paraguay and its floodplain in the northwest Pantanal, slow water that bursts its banks between November and March and leaves only the ancient forested dunes dry.
  • When: The Rio Paraguay rises and floods the Pantanal from October to March. The water is at its lowest in September, when masses of waterbirds throng the pools that remain.
  • Getting there: The Serra das Araras Ecological Station lies northeast of Caceres and southwest of Cuiaba, and Blue-eyed Ground-Dove has been recorded there. Birds like those of the Pantanal proper occur on the ranch country about 60 km southwest of Caceres.
  • Look for: Jabiru, Large-billed Tern, Black Skimmer, Sunbittern, Black-collared Hawk, Green Ibis, Hyacinth Macaw, Chestnut-eared Aracari
  • Stops:
    • : The river port at the navigable head of the Paraguay, 220 km west of Cuiaba. It is the base for everything below it.
    • Taiama Ecological Station : A federal reserve on an island in the Paraguay. It is closed to general visits and watched from the surrounding channels.
    • : The main channel. Work it downstream by boat for terns, skimmers and the bank-side herons.
  • Also covers: Porto Esperidiao, Rio Paraguai at Caceres, Taiama Ecological Station
  • Also watch for: Jaguar density on Taiama is among the highest measured, and the station's long-running study is where much of what is known about Pantanal jaguars comes from. Giant Otter and Marsh Deer are both regular on the channels.

The Estrada Parque and Miranda

A 116 km dirt causeway through the southern wetland, between Miranda and the Paraguay

The southern equivalent of the Transpantaneira, and older: the Estrada Parque was declared an area of special tourist interest in 1993 and runs 116 km on sections of the MS-184 and MS-228 through Corumba, Miranda and Ladario. It crosses the Abobral and Miranda rivers on wooden bridges of its own, and the fazenda lodges along it work grassland, gallery forest and permanent marsh from one base.

  • Habitat: Seasonally flooded plain along the River Miranda, with cerrado woodland standing on the raised capoes and cordilheiras between the wet ground.
  • When: Good all year. Jabiru, Hyacinth Macaw and Bare-faced Curassow are here in every month.
  • Getting there: An unpaved road, the MS184, leaves the BR262 at Buraco das Piranhas 100 km northwest of Miranda, runs 45 km north and carries on as the MS228 about 75 km west to Corumba. Passo do Lontra on the River Miranda is 8 km in from the BR262. Campo Grande is the airport, with Miranda 210 km west on the BR262, about two and three quarter hours. A 4x4 is essential outside the April to October dry season and sensible during it.
  • Look for: Jabiru, Hyacinth Macaw, Bare-faced Curassow, Toco Toucan, Wattled Jacana, Plumbeous Ibis, Great Rufous Woodcreeper, White-lored Spinetail
  • Stops:
    • : 116 km of raised dirt road across the southern floodplain. Bird it slowly from a vehicle with stops at every bridge. OpenStreetMap carries no line for the road, so the coordinate here is its midpoint at the Miranda river crossing.
    • : The crossing of the Miranda river partway along the road, with lodges either side and boat trips from both.
    • : The smaller of the two river crossings, and the usual place on this road for Giant Otter.
    • : The town at the eastern end, on the Campo Grande to Corumba road. Most southern routes start here.
  • Also covers: Corumba, Estrada Parque Pantanal, Miranda, Passo do Lontra, Rio Abobral
  • Also watch for: Capybara and Yacare Caiman line the causeway pools. Giant Otter is regular on the Abobral, and this is the part of the Pantanal where Ocelot is most often picked up on a night drive.

Aquidauana and the Nhecolandia

Cattle fazendas and a landscape of thousands of saline and freshwater lakes

The Nhecolandia is the strangest ground in the Pantanal. A plain holds thousands of shallow lakes, some fresh and some saline. They sit side by side on sand ridges left by an old river fan. It is cattle country, and you watch it from fazendas down long private tracks off the Rio Negro road. The saline lakes hold wader concentrations the rest of the wetland does not.

  • Habitat: Brackish salinas lying alongside rounded freshwater baias, separated by cerrado forest on the raised capoes, which is the landscape that defines Nhecolandia. The lakes hold wildfowl, waders and capybara in numbers that draw jaguars.
  • When: Good all year. Greater Rhea, Red-legged Seriema and Jabiru are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Campo Grande is the arrival point, by domestic flight or long-distance bus, with Aquidauana 140 km west on the BR262, about two hours. No road here connects the fazendas the way the Transpantaneira does in Mato Grosso, so they are reached one at a time and most visitors stay several days at one. Transfers are usually arranged through the lodge, and a 4x4 is essential outside the April to October dry season.
  • Look for: Greater Rhea, Red-legged Seriema, Jabiru, Hyacinth Macaw, Nacunda Nighthawk, Southern Screamer, Yellow-billed Cardinal, Toco Toucan
  • Parks and reserves: private nature reserve Fazenda Rio Negro (private reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : The town on the eastern edge, 140 km from Campo Grande. It is the way into the northern Nhecolandia fazendas.
    • : The Nhecolandia locality the best-known fazendas here are addressed from. It lies 130 km down mostly unmade road from Aquidauana.
  • Also covers: Aquidauana, private nature reserve Fazenda Rio Negro
  • Also watch for: Giant Anteater is more often seen on this open ground than anywhere else in the Pantanal, and the fazendas here carry Marsh Deer, Pampas Deer and Giant Armadillo on their own camera surveys.

Corumba and the Serra do Amolar

Limestone hills rising out of the flood on the Bolivian border, reached only by boat

Corumba is the Pantanal's only real city and the port for the far west. The Serra do Amolar rises out of the floodplain north of it in a line of forested limestone hills reached by navigating the Paraguay and its tributaries, and the birding there is a genuine mix: wetland species on the water and hill-forest species a few hundred metres above it. The Pantanal Matogrossense National Park sits at the far end of the same run.

  • Habitat: Corumba sits at the southern end of the Pantanal, where there is less forest and less standing water than along the Transpantaneira in the north. The 403 km of country between Campo Grande and Corumba carries Greater Rhea, Hyacinth and Golden-collared Macaws, Great Rufous Woodcreeper and Rufous Cachalote along with many of the birds of the northern Pantanal.
  • When: The Rio Paraguay rises and floods the Pantanal from October to March. The water is at its lowest in September, when masses of waterbirds throng the pools that remain.
  • Getting there: The Pantanal can be reached from Corumba at its southern end, although coming in this way is harder than from the north. The road running the 403 km east to Campo Grande is the line to bird, and there are fazendas along it that take guests.
  • Look for: Hyacinth Macaw, Jabiru, Blue-throated Piping Guan, Chestnut-eared Aracari, Great Potoo, Blaze-winged Parakeet, Sunbittern, Black-collared Hawk
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Azure Gallinule, Band-tailed Antbird
  • National park:
    • Pantanal Matogrossense National Park, 135,000 hectares at the confluence of the Cuiaba and Paraguay rivers. Only a boat gets in, and there is no road access at all.
  • Stops:
    • : The river city on the Bolivian border, with an airport. Everything upriver departs from here.
    • Serra do Amolar: A line of forested limestone hills standing out of the floodplain. A boat reaches them, and you watch from the water and from the reserve trails.
    • : The naval town next to Corumba. The river gauge that measures the whole Pantanal flood has been read here since 1900.
  • Also covers: Corumba, Ladario, Pantanal Matogrossense National Park, Serra do Amolar
  • Also watch for: Jaguar, Giant Otter and Lowland Tapir are all recorded on the Amolar reserves, and the hills carry Brown Capuchin and Black-and-gold Howler that the open floodplain does not.

Bonito and the Serra da Bodoquena

Clear limestone rivers and dry forest on the southern rim, three hours from Campo Grande

The limestone range on the Pantanal's southern rim carries dry forest rather than marsh, and it is the one part of the Pantanal you can bird on foot all day. The Serra da Bodoquena National Park holds more than 300 species, and half an hour from it the Buraco das Araras sinkhole is a 100 m deep, 500 m round hole in the cerrado with Red-and-green Macaws nesting on its walls and 169 species recorded around it.

  • Habitat: Limestone hills where cerrado, Pantanal and Atlantic forest meet, with clear spring-fed rivers cutting through, and both Chaco Owl and Blaze-winged Parakeet on the list.
  • When: Good all year. Red-and-green Macaw, Turquoise-fronted Amazon and Toco Toucan stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Bodoquena town is about 70 km from Bonito and is the way into the national park; most of the private reserves are booked through the Bonito operators, who provide the guide.
  • Look for: Red-and-green Macaw, Turquoise-fronted Amazon, Toco Toucan, Chestnut-eared Aracari, Blue-crowned Trogon, Rufous-tailed Jacamar, Peach-fronted Parakeet, Bare-faced Curassow
  • National park:
    • Serra da Bodoquena National Park, Limestone range and dry forest with more than 300 bird species. Bonito is the nearest town.
  • Stops:
    • Buraco das Araras : A sandstone sinkhole 500 m round and 100 m deep, 53 km from Bonito in the municipality of Jardim. Red-and-green Macaws nest on the walls, Turquoise-fronted Amazons circle the rim, and 169 species are recorded. Visits are booked only.
    • : The town the region is reached from, 300 km south-west of Campo Grande. Every site around it runs a booked-access system.
    • : The clear limestone river through the town's own reserves. Bird it from the bank for kingfishers, Sunbittern and Amazon Kingfisher.
  • Also covers: Bonito, Buraco das Araras, Jardim, Rio Formoso, Serra da Bodoquena National Park
  • Also watch for: The clear rivers around Bonito are snorkelled for the fish rather than the birds, and they are the reason the town exists. Brown Brocket, Coati and Black-and-gold Howler are all regular in the Bodoquena forest.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in the Pantanal?

June to October. The flood falls back into the channels through those months, fish concentrate, and everything that eats fish concentrates with them. July onwards is also when jaguar viewing at Porto Jofre becomes reliable. April and May are the quiet secret: the wetland is still full, the weather has settled and the lodges are half empty. The three seasons.

Northern or southern Pantanal?

The northern Pantanal, which you reach from Cuiaba down the Transpantaneira, has the jaguars and most of the traffic. The southern half runs from Campo Grande through Miranda and the Estrada Parque. It is quieter and cheaper, and it has the Nhecolandia lake country and the Bodoquena limestone that the north has nothing like. A first trip almost always goes north.

Do I actually see a jaguar?

On a boat-based trip out of Porto Jofre in the dry season, yes, and usually more than one. The cats along those channels are used to boats rather than baited, and the density there is unmatched anywhere in the Americas. Away from that stretch of river a jaguar is a lucky accident, the same as anywhere else.

Which part of Brazil should a first trip go to?

Nobody books Brazil. The Pantanal, the Amazon, the Atlantic Forest and the cerrado, the dry savanna of the interior, with the caatinga thorn scrub of the northeast beside it, are different months, different airports and almost entirely different birds. The wetland is the easiest to bird and the best first trip: the birds sit in the open along one road.

Do I need a guide?

For the Transpantaneira and the Estrada Parque, no: both are public roads and the birding is done from them. For Porto Jofre and for anything upriver from Corumba or Caceres you need a boat and the person driving it, and for the fazenda properties you need to be staying there. Most people book a lodge package that includes both.

How does the Pantanal compare with the Amazon for birding?

The Pantanal has fewer species and shows them far more easily: open water, open grassland and a road on an embankment mean birds at eye level for hours. The Amazon has three times the list and hides most of it in canopy. Many operators sell the two together for exactly that reason.

Is there anywhere to stay that is certified?

Very little. Almost nothing in Brazil meets the certification rule this site applies. What the Pantanal has instead is working fazendas that are themselves the habitat, listed here as birding lodges rather than certified ones. How a certification is judged.

What about the fires?

The Pantanal burned very badly in the 2020 season and again in 2024, and September in a dry year can be smoky enough to spoil photography. It is worth checking conditions close to departure rather than assuming, and it is a real argument for the June and July end of the season.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
