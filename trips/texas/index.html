Birding in Texas

More birds have been recorded in Texas than in any other state, because the Mexican border, the coast of the Gulf of Mexico and the western mountains all end here.

Birds that decide the trip

Golden-cheeked Warbler on the Balcones escarpment

The Golden-cheeked Warbler breeds in Texas and nowhere else on earth, in ashe juniper and oak woodland along the Balcones escarpment. Balcones Canyonlands was established for it, and refuge staff run guided walks while the birds hold territory.

When Mid-March to mid-June, while the birds are on their breeding ground in the juniper brakes.

Where Balcones Canyonlands National Wildlife Refuge, Lost Maples State Natural Area, Pedernales Falls State Park

Colima Warbler in the Chisos Mountains

The Chisos Mountains hold the only Colima Warblers in the United States, in the oak and maple woodland near the top. Reaching them means the climb from the Chisos Basin to Boot Canyon, which is a long day on foot and the reason the bird is a Texas pilgrimage.

When April into summer, once the birds are back on their breeding ground high in the Chisos.

Where Chisos Basin, Big Bend National Park

Whooping Crane at Aransas

The only self-sustaining wild flock of Whooping Crane in the world winters on the salt marshes at Aransas. Boats out of Rockport and Port Aransas reach the birds at the water, and the refuge tower looks over the same marsh from land.

When Late October to early April, while the flock is on the wintering grounds.

Where Aransas National Wildlife Refuge, Rockport

Green Jay in the Lower Rio Grande Valley

Green Jay reaches the United States only in the last few Texas counties of the Rio Grande, and it is common wherever the riparian forest survives. Santa Ana and Bentsen both hold it, and it comes readily to the feeding stations at the World Birding Center sites.

When All year, with the feeders busiest from November through the winter.

Where Santa Ana National Wildlife Refuge, Bentsen-Rio Grande Valley State Park

Attwater’s prairie chickens on the coastal prairie

The Attwater’s race of the Greater Prairie Chicken survives on its own refuge west of Houston and almost nowhere else. Regular tours to the booming grounds ended as the population fell, and the festival weekend in April is the way in.

When The second weekend of April, when the refuge festival runs guided tours.

Where Attwater Prairie Chicken National Wildlife Refuge

Black-capped Vireo in the shin oak

Black-capped Vireo holds the shin oak thickets on the plateau divides, and the Kerr Wildlife Management Area manages its habitat deliberately. Around six hundred birders a season come for it, and visitors check in at the area office first.

When April to August, with the birds arriving a month after the warblers.

Where Kerr Wildlife Management Area, Devils River State Natural Area, Lost Maples State Natural Area

Ferruginous Pygmy Owl on the South Texas ranches

The brushland on the King and Kenedy ranches carries Ferruginous Pygmy Owl in numbers the public land further south cannot match. Both ranches are visited on organised tours, and the owls call best in the first hour of daylight.

When All year, and easiest early on a spring morning while the birds are calling.

Where King Ranch, Kenedy Ranch

The Hazel Bazemore hawk flight

The hawk watch on the bluff above the Nueces River counts one of the largest raptor flights in North America, and Broad-winged Hawk makes up most of it. The count runs from mid-August to mid-November and peaks in the second half of September.

When Mid-to-late September for the peak, with hawks moving from mid-August to mid-November.

Where Hazel Bazemore County Park, Corpus Christi

When to Go

Texas runs about a thousand kilometres in each direction and its birding year runs at different speeds across that. The coast holds birds all winter while the Panhandle freezes, the hill country compresses its whole season into spring, and the Chisos stay worth the climb into the summer heat.

Spring passage

Late March to mid-May

Migration crosses the Gulf of Mexico and makes landfall on the upper Texas coast while the hill country endemics take up their breeding ground.

Go for High Island and Sabine Woods for a fallout, the Balcones escarpment for Golden-cheeked Warbler, and the plateau for Black-capped Vireo from April.

Trade off The coastal woods depend on the weather, so a calm week can be slow and a north wind can fill the same trees in an hour.

Summer in the mountains

June to August

The lowlands turn hot and the birding moves to the Chisos, the Davis Mountains and the Guadalupes.

Go for Boot Canyon for Colima Warbler, the Davis Mountains for Montezuma Quail and hummingbirds, and the hawk count opens at Hazel Bazemore in mid-August.

Trade off Coastal days are humid and the shorebird flats are at their emptiest in June.

Winter on the coast

November to February

Waterfowl, cranes and shorebirds fill the coast and the playa lakes while the valley holds its residents.

Go for Aransas for the Whooping Crane flock, the Panhandle playas for Sandhill Crane, and Santa Ana and Bentsen for the valley specialities at the feeders.

Trade off The hill country is quiet and the two endemics are absent until March.

The Lower Rio Grande Valley

Green Jay, Plain Chachalaca and Altamira Oriole at the northern limit of their range in Texas

The Rio Grande below Falcon Dam carries a band of riparian forest and Tamaulipan thornscrub that reaches no further north than these four Texas counties, and around thirty of the birds in it occur nowhere else in the United States. Santa Ana and Bentsen hold the richest surviving forest, and the refuge tracts strung along the river between them are being replanted to join the remnants back together.

  • Habitat: Riparian forest and brushland along the river, with Tamaulipan thornscrub inland, oxbow lakes the valley calls resacas, and a remnant sabal palm forest near the mouth.
  • When: Any time of year rewards a visit to the Texas end of the valley. Spring and autumn passage are the strongest, and the first half of November is excellent because the wintering birds have arrived and the chance of a Mexican vagrant stays open. Summer is very hot.
  • Getting there: McAllen, Harlingen and Brownsville all take flights, and US 83 links every site along the river. Santa Ana lies south of Alamo, Bentsen sits west of Mission at the World Birding Center headquarters, and Sabal Palm is reached from Brownsville along Southmost Road.
  • Base yourself in: McAllen
  • Look for: Plain Chachalaca, Green Jay, Altamira Oriole, Audubon's Oriole, Great Kiskadee, Ringed Kingfisher, Green Kingfisher, Least Grebe, Clay-colored Thrush, White-tipped Dove, Olive Sparrow, Long-billed Thrasher, Groove-billed Ani, Northern Beardless Tyrannulet, Hook-billed Kite, Grey Hawk, Buff-bellied Hummingbird, Golden-fronted Woodpecker, Tropical Parula, Ferruginous Pygmy Owl
  • Parks and reserves: Santa Ana National Wildlife Refuge, Bentsen-Rio Grande Valley State Park, Laguna Atascosa National Wildlife Refuge
  • Stops:
    • Santa Ana Willow Lakes : Shallow lakes lie inside the refuge forest, and the loop trail from the visitor centre reaches them. Least Grebe and Black-bellied Whistling-Duck sit on the open water while Green Jay works the edge.
    • : Mown parkland beside the river below Anzalduas Dam, where the short grass draws Sprague’s Pipit in winter and the river brings Ringed Kingfisher past.
    • : A river bank at the end of a village lane upstream of Falcon Dam. Brown Jay and Red-billed Pigeon were long watched from here and the seed feeders still draw Audubon’s Oriole.
    • Estero Llano Grande State Park : A shallow lake, woodland and thornscrub on the edge of Weslaco, and one of the World Birding Center sites. The tropical kingbirds and pauraques are reliable and the boardwalk is level.
  • Visitor centres: Bentsen-Rio Grande Valley State Park, World Birding Center headquarters

The South Texas brush country

Ferruginous Pygmy Owl and Tropical Parula on two ranches larger than some states

Between the valley and the coastal bend lies a block of Tamaulipan brushland held almost entirely in private hands, and the King and Kenedy ranches carry the largest intact stands of it. The birds that reach the valley in a thin riverside strip are widespread here, which is why guided access to the ranches has become part of a Texas trip.

  • Habitat: Tamaulipan brushland running into Gulf coastal prairie, with live oak mottes and windmill tanks.
  • When: Both ranches are visited on organised tours, so the date follows the tour calendar. Spring holds the breeding birds and the ferruginous pygmy owls call best early in the morning.
  • Getting there: The King Ranch entrance sits just west of Kingsville in Kleberg County and the Kenedy Ranch lies near Sarita in Kenedy County, both off US 77 south of Corpus Christi. Access is by booked tour in each case.
  • Base yourself in: Kingsville
  • Look for: Ferruginous Pygmy Owl, Tropical Parula, Northern Beardless Tyrannulet, Botteri's Sparrow, Cassin's Sparrow, Crested Caracara, Harris's Hawk, White-tailed Hawk, Pyrrhuloxia, Olive Sparrow, Long-billed Thrasher, Greater Roadrunner
  • Stops:
    • : The town to sleep in for a ranch tour, and the place the Norias Division trips assemble.
  • Also covers: Kenedy Ranch, King Ranch, Kingsville, Sarita
  • Also watch for: Nilgai introduced from India range across both ranches in numbers, alongside collared peccary and white-tailed deer.

Aransas and Padre Island

The wild Whooping Crane flock winters at Aransas and a hawk watch counts a million raptors above Corpus Christi

The only self-sustaining wild flock of Whooping Crane in the world winters on the salt marshes at Aransas, and the barrier islands south of it carry beach, tidal flat and dune for two hundred miles. Inland of Corpus Christi the Hazel Bazemore hawk watch sits under the funnel that carries Broad-winged Hawk and Mississippi Kite out of the continent.

  • Habitat: Open bay, mudflat, tidal marsh and coastal prairie at Aransas, with oak and redbay thickets on the ridges. The islands hold coastal dunes, oak mottes, salt marsh, brackish lagoon, tidal flat and bare beach.
  • When: Aransas holds the cranes from late October to early April. Padre Island is best from April into the first week of May for trans-Gulf migrants, with productive autumn passage and winter waterfowl. Hazel Bazemore peaks in the second half of September, and hawks pass from mid-August to mid-November.
  • Getting there: Corpus Christi is the airport and the base. Aransas is northeast of Rockport in Refugio County, and boat trips out of Rockport and Port Aransas reach the cranes at the water. Matagorda Island is reached by ferry from Port O’Connor. Hazel Bazemore sits west of Corpus Christi off Farm Road 624.
  • Base yourself in: Rockport
  • Look for: Whooping Crane, Reddish Egret, Roseate Spoonbill, Piping Plover, Wilson's Plover, American Oystercatcher, Long-billed Curlew, Black Skimmer, Least Tern, Mottled Duck, Seaside Sparrow, Broad-winged Hawk, Mississippi Kite, Swallow-tailed Kite, Crested Caracara, White-tailed Hawk
  • Parks and reserves: Aransas National Wildlife Refuge, Padre Island National Seashore
  • Stops:
    • Aransas observation tower : A raised tower over the salt marsh at the end of the refuge loop, and the place to look for cranes from land.
    • Bird Island Basin : A shallow lagoon shore inside the national seashore with a launch and campsite, good for wintering Redhead and for terns over the seagrass.
    • : A pond, riparian strip and Tamaulipan scrub on a bluff above the Nueces River, with the counting platform at the top of the park.
  • Visitor centres: Hazel Bazemore hawk watch, HawkWatch International
  • Also covers: Aransas National Wildlife Refuge, Aransas observation tower, Bird Island Basin, Corpus Christi, Hazel Bazemore County Park, Hazel Bazemore hawk watch, HawkWatch International, Mad Island Marsh, Matagorda Island, Padre Island National Seashore, Port O'Connor, Rockport

The upper Texas coast

A spring fallout at High Island puts thirty warblers into one stand of oaks

The oak and hackberry woods on the salt domes behind the upper Texas coast are the first cover a bird sees after crossing the Gulf of Mexico, and when a north wind meets that arrival the woods fill. High Island and Sabine Woods are those woods, and the marshes and flats around them hold the shorebirds and rails the coast is equally known for.

  • Habitat: Coastal woodland on salt domes, with salt marsh, mudflat, beach and upland behind it, and fresh and brackish marsh with coastal prairie on the refuges.
  • When: Late April is the peak for spring migrants and late September for autumn, and birding is best on the day after stormy weather. Bolivar Flats rewards a visit nearly all year, with few shorebirds present in June. Anahuac suits any season, and the Yellow Rail walks in the wet prairies there are a spring fixture.
  • Getting there: Houston is the airport. High Island sits on State Highway 124 in Chambers County, and Bolivar Flats lies 25 miles down the Bolivar Peninsula from it, or 3.7 miles east on Highway 87 from the Galveston ferry. McFaddin is about 15 miles up the coast from Anahuac, and Sabine Woods sits near Sea Rim State Park in Jefferson County.
  • Base yourself in: Winnie
  • Look for: Yellow Rail, Black Rail, Seaside Sparrow, Nelson's Sparrow, LeConte's Sparrow, Piping Plover, American Oystercatcher, Reddish Egret, Roseate Spoonbill, Mottled Duck, Purple Gallinule, Least Bittern, Prothonotary Warbler, Hooded Warbler, Painted Bunting, Scissor-tailed Flycatcher
  • Parks and reserves: Anahuac National Wildlife Refuge, Sea Rim State Park
  • Stops:
    • Boy Scout Woods : The Houston Audubon sanctuary in the middle of High Island, with boardwalks, a drip and bleacher seating facing the water.
    • : A tower on the east shore of Galveston Bay where the autumn count runs through September and October, and the point concentrates raptors leaving the bay.
    • Bolivar Flats Shorebird Sanctuary : Mudflat and beach behind a five mile jetty, where the flats keep building as sediment drops. Piping Plover winters here in some of its highest numbers.
    • Yellow Rail Prairie, Anahuac : Salty Spartina prairie on the refuge where the spring rail walks are held, and the birds are found close to the ground in the wet grass.

The Brazoria refuges and the coastal prairies

Attwater’s prairie chickens boom on the last coastal prairie west of Houston

Three refuges around Freeport hold salt and freshwater marsh against old growth coastal live oak and native bluestem prairie, and inland of them the Katy Prairie and the Attwater refuge carry what remains of a grassland that once ran unbroken to the coast. The Attwater’s prairie chicken survives on the refuge and almost nowhere else.

  • Habitat: Saline and non-saline prairie, salt and mudflat, fresh and salt marsh, saltwater lakes and old growth coastal live oak forest, with more than 5,000 acres of native bluestem coastal prairie. The Katy Prairie adds creek corridors, depressional wetlands and rice fields.
  • When: November to March for the wintering birds, with spring and autumn passage strong on the live oak tracts. Brazoria opens fully on the first full weekend of each month and on the third weekend from November to April. The Attwater’s Prairie-Chicken Festival falls on the second weekend in April.
  • Getting there: Houston is the airport. The three refuges lie near Freeport on the coast southwest of the city. The Attwater refuge is about 50 miles west of Houston near Eagle Lake, and the Katy Prairie sits just west of the city south of Hockley. Most of the Katy Prairie is private, so viewing is from the public roads, and Bear Creek Park to its southeast is open.
  • Base yourself in: Freeport
  • Look for: Greater Prairie Chicken, Sandhill Crane, Snow Goose, Mottled Duck, Roseate Spoonbill, Wood Stork, White-faced Ibis, Sprague's Pipit, LeConte's Sparrow, Henslow's Sparrow, Crested Caracara, White-tailed Hawk, Prothonotary Warbler, Swallow-tailed Kite
  • Parks and reserves: Brazoria National Wildlife Refuge, Attwater Prairie Chicken National Wildlife Refuge
  • Stops:
    • : Old growth coastal live oak inside the refuge, part of the Columbia Bottomlands, and the cover migrants use on arrival.
    • : Public parkland southeast of the Katy Prairie with prairie and creek habitat, open when the surrounding private land is closed.
  • Also covers: Attwater Prairie Chicken National Wildlife Refuge, Bear Creek Park, Big Boggy National Wildlife Refuge, Brazoria National Wildlife Refuge, Eagle Lake, Freeport, Katy Prairie, San Bernard National Wildlife Refuge, San Bernard oak woodland
  • Also watch for: American alligator is throughout the Texas refuge marshes, and the Columbia Bottomlands hold swamp rabbit in the wet woods.

The Pineywoods and Caddo Lake

Red-cockaded woodpeckers in longleaf pine, and a cypress swamp on the Louisiana line

East Texas belongs to the southern pine forests, and the four national forests in it hold the state’s red-cockaded woodpeckers in stands of longleaf and shortleaf pine. Caddo Lake, on the Louisiana line, is a bald cypress swamp best seen from a boat, and the Big Thicket to the south packs pine savannah, bog and bottomland into one preserve.

  • Habitat: Shortleaf and loblolly pine hardwood woodland, longleaf pine savannah, bottomland hardwood forest, pitcher plant bogs, palmetto flats and riparian corridors. Caddo Lake adds open lake, cypress swamp and marsh.
  • When: Late April to late June for the breeding birds, with the winter months productive again. April and May are the best months in the Big Thicket for migrants and nesting birds. Caddo Lake rewards any season, and summers there are hot with biting insects.
  • Getting there: Houston and Shreveport are the practical airports. Angelina National Forest lies just east of Lufkin and Davy Crockett just west of it, Sam Houston sits north of Houston near Cleveland, Conroe and Huntsville, and W.G. Jones State Forest is near Conroe. Caddo Lake is near Karnack in Marion County close to the state line, and the lake is best seen by canoe or rented boat.
  • Base yourself in: Lufkin
  • Look for: Red-cockaded Woodpecker, Bachman's Sparrow, Brown-headed Nuthatch, Swainson's Warbler, Prothonotary Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Hooded Warbler, Louisiana Waterthrush, Wood Thrush, Pileated Woodpecker, Anhinga, Barred Owl, Chuck-will's-widow, Swallow-tailed Kite
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Big Thicket National Preserve
    • Caddo Lake State Park, Bald cypress hung with Spanish moss stands in the lake shallows on the Texas shore. Canoes are rented in the warmer months and a boardwalk reaches the swamp.
  • Stops:
    • : A recreation area in longleaf pine in Angelina National Forest, with red-cockaded woodpecker clusters marked on the nearby pines.
  • Also covers: Angelina National Forest, Big Thicket National Preserve, Boykin Springs, Caddo Lake State Park, Conroe, Davy Crockett National Forest, Lufkin, Sam Houston National Forest
  • Also watch for: American alligator reaches the Caddo Lake bayous on the Texas side, and the Big Thicket holds four carnivorous plant genera in its bogs.

The Balcones escarpment and the Austin hill country

Golden-cheeked Warbler, the only bird that breeds nowhere but Texas

The Balcones escarpment runs a limestone step across the middle of the state, and the ashe juniper and oak woodland on it is the entire breeding range of the Golden-cheeked Warbler. Balcones Canyonlands was created for that bird and the Black-capped Vireo, and the parks along the escarpment hold both within an hour of Austin.

  • Habitat: Live oak and ashe juniper woodland, post oak juniper woodland, oak savannah, grassland, riparian corridors and deciduous woodland, with cedar brakes and riverine forest on the Pedernales and the Texas Colorado River.
  • When: Mid-March to mid-June for the two endangered Texas breeders, with the first warblers arriving in early to mid-March and the vireos in April. March through June is the window at Colorado Bend.
  • Getting there: Austin is the airport and the base for the Texas hill country. Balcones Canyonlands lies northwest of the city with guided tours run by refuge staff, Pedernales Falls sits in Blanco County northeast of Johnson City, and Colorado Bend is on the Colorado River in San Saba County. Fort Cavazos near Killeen, formerly Fort Hood, is an open installation whose paved roads the public may use, and hiking there is arranged through the Area Access Office. Barton Creek Habitat Preserve is closed to the public and opens through volunteer workdays and Conservancy tours.
  • Base yourself in: Austin
  • Look for: Golden-cheeked Warbler, Black-capped Vireo, Bell's Vireo, Painted Bunting, Scissor-tailed Flycatcher, Yellow-billed Cuckoo, Canyon Wren, Rufous-crowned Sparrow, Lark Sparrow, Ash-throated Flycatcher, Zone-tailed Hawk, Cave Swallow
  • Parks and reserves: Balcones Canyonlands National Wildlife Refuge, Pedernales Falls State Park, Colorado Bend State Park
  • Stops:
    • : A refuge tract on Cow Creek with a canyon rim trail, and the place staff send visitors for Golden-cheeked Warbler in spring.
    • Pedernales Falls State Park bird blind : A screened blind over water and feeders in juniper oak woodland, level and quiet, with Golden-cheeked Warbler in the surrounding brakes from March.
  • Also covers: Austin, Balcones Canyonlands National Wildlife Refuge, Barton Creek, Colorado Bend State Park, Fort Cavazos, Pedernales Falls State Park bird blind, Warbler Vista
  • Also watch for: The Balcones caves hold blind salamanders found in single aquifers, and Mexican free-tailed bats leave the Austin bridge roosts nightly through a Texas summer.

The Edwards Plateau

Bigtooth maple canyons at Lost Maples, and warblers a ranger will point you at

West of the escarpment the plateau breaks into steep canyons, and the bigtooth maples in the sheltered heads of them at Lost Maples are the reason the place has a name. The canyon woodland carries Golden-cheeked Warbler and the shin oak thickets on the divides carry Black-capped Vireo, and both are watched here more reliably than anywhere.

  • Habitat: Oak and juniper woodland with canyons of bigtooth maple, ashe juniper and live oak shin oak thickets, springs, and steep limestone hills with small open grassland. Kickapoo Cavern adds stands of pinyon pine.
  • When: April through June, with the first warblers in early to mid-March and vireos in April. Late March and April is the window at Kickapoo Cavern as the breeders arrive.
  • Getting there: San Antonio is the airport. Lost Maples lies five miles north of Vanderpool in Bandera County, Kerr Wildlife Management Area is 12 miles northwest of Hunt in Kerr County, and Guadalupe River State Park sits north of San Antonio on the Comal and Kendall county line. Visitors to Kerr check in at the management area office first. Honey Creek opens only on the guided walk on Saturday mornings, and Kickapoo Cavern is undeveloped and open to holders of the Texas Conservation Passport.
  • Base yourself in: Kerrville
  • Look for: Golden-cheeked Warbler, Black-capped Vireo, Canyon Wren, Rufous-crowned Sparrow, Zone-tailed Hawk, Common Black Hawk, Green Kingfisher, Vermilion Flycatcher, Cave Swallow, Montezuma Quail, Scaled Quail, Painted Bunting
  • Parks and reserves: Lost Maples State Natural Area, Guadalupe River State Park, Kickapoo Cavern State Park
  • Stops:
    • Lost Maples East Trail : A canyon trail through bigtooth maple and oak juniper woodland where the warblers hold territory, with a pond at the top of the loop.
    • : Managed shin oak and juniper where roughly six hundred birders a season come for the vireo and the warbler. Check in at the office before driving the tracks.
  • Also covers: Guadalupe River State Park, Honey Creek State Natural Area, Kerr Wildlife Management Area, Kerrville, Kickapoo Cavern State Park, Lost Maples East Trail, Lost Maples State Natural Area, Vanderpool
  • Also watch for: The Kickapoo Cavern bat flight leaves the cave mouth on summer evenings, and the plateau streams hold Guadalupe bass, which lives in this drainage and no other.

The Devils River country

Black-capped Vireos on spring fed limestone that takes a reservation to reach

The Devils River runs clear over limestone through a remote corner of Val Verde County, and the live oak and pecan forest along it sits between Chihuahuan desert scrub and Tamaulipan thornscrub. Black-capped Vireo holds the slopes from March to August, and the access is deliberately tight, which is part of why the habitat is intact.

  • Habitat: Riverine forest of live oak and pecan, semidesert grassland, eastern Chihuahuan desert scrubland and Tamaulipan thornscrub, with springs and seeps.
  • When: The vireos are present from March to August, and the river is at its best in spring.
  • Getting there: Del Rio is the nearest town, and the area lies about 45 miles north of it. The state natural area is open by reservation only. Primitive campsites are available and there is no potable water, so ask about facilities when booking.
  • Base yourself in: Del Rio
  • Look for: Black-capped Vireo, Zone-tailed Hawk, Common Black Hawk, Green Kingfisher, Varied Bunting, Black-throated Sparrow, Cassin's Sparrow, Canyon Wren, Vermilion Flycatcher, Scaled Quail
  • Parks and reserves: Devils River State Natural Area
  • Stops:
    • : The town to sleep in and stock up from, since the natural area has no potable water and the drive in is long.
  • Also covers: Del Rio, Devils River State Natural Area, Dolan Falls Preserve
  • Also watch for: Dolan Falls is the largest waterfall by volume in Texas, and the spring runs hold Devils River minnow and Rio Grande cooter.

The Big Bend

Colima Warbler in the Chisos, and the only place in the country it nests

The Rio Grande turns north through Texas desert here and the Chisos Mountains stand out of that desert as an island of oak and conifer. Colima Warbler nests in the Chisos and nowhere else in the United States, and the walk up to it climbs through every band of habitat the park holds. Big Bend Ranch carries the same country upstream with fewer roads.

  • Habitat: River and desert in the lowlands rising to oak woodland and coniferous forest in the Chisos Mountains, with grassland, desert scrub, canyons, riparian woodland, streams and permanent springs on the state park upstream.
  • When: Birding is excellent at any time and probably best on spring and autumn passage. The Colima Warbler is on their breeding ground from April into summer. Big Bend Ranch is best from autumn through spring.
  • Getting there: Midland and El Paso are the airports and both are a long drive away. The national park lies in Brewster County south of Marathon at the Big Bend of the Rio Grande, and the Colima Warbler is reached on the Pinnacles and Boot Canyon trails out of the Chisos Basin. Big Bend Ranch sits on the river just upstream and has few roads and facilities, with a permit required for the interior.
  • Base yourself in: Chisos Basin
  • Look for: Colima Warbler, Lucifer Sheartail, Blue-throated Mountaingem, Painted Whitestart, Mexican Jay, Montezuma Quail, Zone-tailed Hawk, Varied Bunting, Black-chinned Sparrow, Crissal Thrasher, Elf Owl, Common Poorwill, Black-throated Sparrow, Scaled Quail, Greater Roadrunner, Chihuahuan Raven
  • Parks and reserves: Big Bend National Park, Big Bend Ranch State Park
  • Stops:
    • Boot Canyon : Oak and maple woodland stands high in the Chisos, and a long climb from the basin reaches it. The Colima Warbler holds territory along the canyon.
    • Rio Grande Village : Cottonwoods, a pond and a nature trail beside the river at the park’s southeast corner, with Elf Owl in the trees after dark.
    • Cottonwood Campground : A stand of cottonwoods on the river road with no generators allowed, long known for Lucifer Hummingbird and Vermilion Flycatcher.
  • Also covers: Big Bend National Park, Big Bend Ranch State Park, Boot Canyon, Chisos Basin, Cottonwood Campground, Marathon, Rio Grande Village
  • Also watch for: Black bear recolonised the Texas side of the Chisos from Mexico on its own, mountain lion is resident, and Carmen Mountains white-tailed deer occurs on the high ground.

The Davis and Guadalupe Mountains

Montezuma Quail on grassland at five thousand feet, and the highest ground in Texas

North of Big Bend the Davis Mountains rise out of plains grassland into pine, juniper and oak, and the stock tanks and riparian strips in them draw birds the surrounding desert cannot hold. The Guadalupe Mountains carry the highest ground in the state further north against the New Mexico line, with canyon forest cut into desert.

  • Habitat: Plains grassland with high altitude pine, juniper and oak woodland, streams with riparian woodland, marshes and stock tanks. The Guadalupes hold desert, canyon forest, pine forest and riparian woodland on mountains.
  • When: Any time of year rewards a visit, with spring and early summer usually hot and dry. Weather in the Guadalupes turns fast and high winds are common, so carry what you need.
  • Getting there: Midland and El Paso are the airports. The Davis Mountains lie in Jeff Davis County about 100 miles north northwest of Big Bend National Park, with the scenic loop out of Fort Davis reaching most of the high ground. The Guadalupe Mountains sit in Hudspeth and Culberson Counties just south of the New Mexico line, and facilities inside that park are extremely limited.
  • Base yourself in: Fort Davis
  • Look for: Montezuma Quail, Lucifer Sheartail, Blue-throated Mountaingem, Rivoli's Hummingbird, Acorn Woodpecker, Hepatic Tanager, Mexican Jay, Zone-tailed Hawk, Common Black Hawk, Painted Whitestart, Cassin's Sparrow, Chihuahuan Raven, Scaled Quail
  • Parks and reserves: Guadalupe Mountains National Park, Davis Mountains State Park
  • Stops:
    • Davis Mountains State Park bird blind : A water feature and feeders watched from a screened blind in oak juniper woodland, and the easiest place in the range to see Montezuma Quail come to grain.
    • McKittrick Canyon : A spring fed canyon holding maple, oak and pine inside desert mountains, level for the first miles and busiest when the maples turn in late October.
  • Also covers: Davis Mountains State Park bird blind, Fort Davis, Guadalupe Mountains National Park, Marfa, McKittrick Canyon
  • Also watch for: Pronghorn range the Marfa plains below the mountains, and elk were reintroduced to the Guadalupes.

The Panhandle and the high plains

Lesser prairie chickens on shortgrass, and a canyon cut eight hundred feet into the plains

The high plains of the Panhandle carry shortgrass prairie, playa lakes that fill after rain and the canyon country where the Prairie Dog Town Fork of the Red River cuts into the Caprock. Sandhill Crane winters on the playas in large numbers and the shortgrass holds Lesser Prairie Chicken, and this corner of the state holds a list of its own that the rest of Texas never repeats.

  • Habitat: Shortgrass and mixed grass prairie with sandsage and shinnery oak, seasonal playa lakes, reservoirs, and juniper and mesquite canyon breaks below the Caprock escarpment.
  • When: October to February for the wintering cranes and geese on the playas, and April for the prairie chicken leks. Spring and autumn passage are strong at the reservoirs, and summer on the open plains is hot.
  • Getting there: Amarillo and Lubbock are the airports. Palo Duro Canyon lies southeast of Amarillo off State Highway 217, Buffalo Lake is southwest of the city near Umbarger, and Muleshoe is south of the town of Muleshoe on State Highway 214. Lake Meredith sits north of Amarillo around Fritch.
  • Base yourself in: Amarillo
  • Look for: Lesser Prairie Chicken, Sandhill Crane, Snow Goose, Long-billed Curlew, Mountain Plover, Burrowing Owl, Cassin's Sparrow, Scaled Quail, Golden Eagle, Ferruginous Hawk, Rock Wren, Canyon Towhee, Chihuahuan Raven, Scissor-tailed Flycatcher
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Palo Duro Canyon State Park
    • Buffalo Lake National Wildlife Refuge
    • Muleshoe National Wildlife Refuge, Three playa basins on shortgrass prairie that take tens of thousands of wintering Sandhill Crane when the lakes hold water.
  • Stops:
    • Palo Duro Canyon rim : The canyon edge southeast of Amarillo, where juniper and mesquite breaks drop away below the shortgrass and Rock Wren and Canyon Towhee hold the slopes.
  • Also covers: Amarillo, Buffalo Lake National Wildlife Refuge, Lake Meredith, Lubbock, Muleshoe National Wildlife Refuge, Palo Duro Canyon rim, Palo Duro Canyon State Park
  • Also watch for: Black-tailed prairie dog towns remain on the refuge grasslands, and pronghorn range the open plains west of Amarillo.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Texas?

Late April is the answer for a single week in Texas. The migration across the Gulf of Mexico reaches the upper coast at its peak in that fortnight, and Golden-cheeked Warbler and Black-capped Vireo are both singing in the hill country at the same time. Late September repeats the passage alongside the Hazel Bazemore hawk flight. November to February is the season for wintering waterfowl, cranes and the valley specialities.

Where do I go for the Texas specialities?

The Lower Rio Grande Valley holds around thirty birds that reach the United States nowhere else, and Santa Ana and Bentsen are the two richest sites. Golden-cheeked Warbler breeds only in Texas, on the Balcones escarpment. Colima Warbler nests in the Chisos Mountains and nowhere else in the country.

How long does a first trip need?

One week covers the Lower Rio Grande Valley properly, or the upper coast, or the hill country. Twelve days takes the whole coast from the valley to the Louisiana line. Three weeks is what the coast, the valley, the hill country and the Trans-Pecos need on a single trip.

What is a fallout and can I plan for one?

A fallout happens when birds crossing the Gulf of Mexico meet a north wind and drop into the first cover they reach, which on the upper Texas coast is the oak and hackberry woods at High Island and Sabine Woods. It cannot be booked. Late April gives the best odds, and the day after stormy weather is the day to be there.

Do I need a car?

Yes. Texas is a thousand kilometres across in both directions and every birding region here is reached by road. Even within a single region the sites sit an hour or more apart.

Which sites need permission or a booking?

The King and Kenedy ranches are visited on organised tours. Devils River State Natural Area is by reservation only. Honey Creek opens for a guided walk on Saturday mornings, Barton Creek opens through volunteer workdays and Conservancy tours, and Kickapoo Cavern asks for a Texas Conservation Passport.

Where can I see a Whooping Crane?

Aransas National Wildlife Refuge, from late October to early April. It holds the only self-sustaining wild flock in the world. Boats from Rockport and Port Aransas reach the birds at the water, and the refuge tower overlooks the same marsh from land.

Is the Panhandle worth the drive?

In winter, yes. The playa lakes at Muleshoe take tens of thousands of Sandhill Crane once they hold water, and the shortgrass and canyon country north of Lubbock carries a list the rest of the state never repeats.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
