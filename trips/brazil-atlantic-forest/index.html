Birds that decide the trip

Red-billed Curassow at Sooretama

Reduced to a handful of lowland forest blocks in Espirito Santo and southern Bahia, and Sooretama with the Vale reserve beside it is where it is actually seen.

When All year, on forest tracks at first light.

Where Linhares

Cherry-throated Tanager in Espirito Santo

Lost for most of the twentieth century and rediscovered in 1998. A few dozen birds are known, in montane forest fragments, and a trip can be built around finding one.

When All year, and it is a matter of being taken to a known group.

Where Domingos Martins

Black-and-gold Cotinga above Itatiaia

A cotinga of the high Mantiqueira with a call like a distant whistle, heard from the Agulhas Negras road before it is seen. Endemic to the south-east highlands.

When Calling from about September to December.

Where Itatiaia

Bare-throated Bellbird on the coast

A white bird with a bare turquoise throat and a call loud enough to hurt at close range. The Serra do Mar above Ubatuba is where most people hear their first.

When Calling from September to December.

Where Ubatuba

Pink-legged Graveteiro in the cocoa

Described only in 1996, and found in the canopy of cabruca cocoa plantations in southern Bahia, which is a bird discovered in farmland rather than in wilderness.

When All year, in the canopy of shaded cocoa.

Where Camacan

Red-tailed Amazon at Superagui

Confined to the coastal islands and mangrove of Parana and Sao Paulo, and roosting on Superagui in flocks that are counted every year.

When All year, at the evening roost.

Where Guaraquecaba

Swallow-tailed Cotinga at Intervales

A grey and black cotinga with a long forked tail. It moves through the canopy in small parties, and Intervales is the reliable place for it.

When All year, most often in fruiting trees.

Where Ribeirao Grande

Great Dusky Swifts behind the falls

Swifts that roost and nest on the wet rock behind the Iguacu falls and fly through the water to reach it, which is a thing to watch at dusk rather than a bird to tick.

When All year, best in the last hour of light.

When to Go

This forest runs 2,000 km from Bahia to Rio Grande do Sul, so its calendar is a compromise. September to November is spring everywhere along it: the birds sing, the forest breeds and the rain has not yet become an afternoon certainty. The southern winter is dry and comfortable and very quiet indeed.

Peak

September to November

Spring. The forest breeds, the cotingas and bellbirds are calling, and the rain has not yet become an afternoon certainty.

Go for Every displaying bird above, from the bellbirds at Ubatuba to the Long-trained Nightjars at Intervales, and the flowering that brings the hummingbirds in.

Trade off It warms and wets through the period, and by November the afternoon storms have started.

Typical daily average
  • Itatiaia: 12 to 25 °C / 54 to 77 °F
  • Ubatuba: 16 to 27 °C / 61 to 81 °F
  • Cambara do Sul: 8 to 21 °C / 46 to 70 °F

Shoulder

March to August

Autumn and winter. Cool, dry and comfortable, with the birds quieter and the trails easy.

Go for The most comfortable walking of the year and the clearest views from the high ground. Itatiaia's upper road is at its best.

Trade off Song drops away, which makes the forest interior far harder, and July nights at 1,000 m and above get close to freezing.

Typical daily average
  • Itatiaia: 8 to 27 °C / 46 to 81 °F
  • Ubatuba: 13 to 30 °C / 55 to 86 °F
  • Cambara do Sul: 6 to 23 °C / 43 to 73 °F

Quiet

December to February

High summer. Hot, wet and busy, with the whole country on holiday along this coast.

Go for Breeding still in full swing in December, and the forest at its greenest.

Trade off Ubatuba takes 322 mm in January, the coast road fills with holiday traffic, and afternoon storms cost half of every day.

Typical daily average
  • Itatiaia: 16 to 27 °C / 61 to 81 °F
  • Ubatuba: 20 to 30 °C / 68 to 86 °F
  • Cambara do Sul: 13 to 24 °C / 55 to 75 °F

Itatiaia and the Mantiqueira

Brazil's oldest national park, from lowland forest to 2,700 m of high grassland

Itatiaia was declared in 1937 and is the oldest national park in the country. It runs from Atlantic Forest at 700 m to open high grassland above 2,400 m on the Agulhas Negras road, so a single day covers a gradient that would take a week anywhere else. Its list runs to 357 species.

  • Habitat: The 12,000-hectare park protects subtropical and temperate forest with large stands of giant bamboo, and high grassland on the slopes above. Mount Itatiaia rises to 2,787 metres, and the park list runs past 270.
  • When: Good all year. Itatiaia Spinetail, Black-and-gold Cotinga and Brazilian Ruby are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Itatiaia lies 174 km west of Rio and 257 km east of Sao Paulo, in the middle of the Serra do Mar.
  • Look for: Itatiaia Spinetail, Black-and-gold Cotinga, Brazilian Ruby, Green-crowned Plovercrest, Saffron Toucanet, Slaty Bristlefront, Black-capped Piprites, Blue-bellied Parrot
  • National park: Itatiaia National Park
  • Stops:
    • Itatiaia National Park, lower part : The forested lower section. Itatiaia town is the way in, and it has the Tres Picos trail, the Maromba waterfall and the hotel road, which is birded on foot at dawn.
    • : The upper section, off the main highway at Engenheiro Passos. It climbs to open grassland above 2,400 m and gives a different list entirely from the forest below.
    • : The private road above the park hotel. Its feeders and forest edge are where most of the park's hummingbirds are seen.
    • : The town at the park gate on the Rio to Sao Paulo highway, two and a half hours from either city.
  • Also covers: Agulhas Negras road, Engenheiro Passos, Hotel do Ype road, Itatiaia National Park, lower part, Resende
  • Also watch for: Southern Muriqui, the largest primate in the Americas, holds in the lower forest. Brown Howler is constant and the high grassland carries Maned Wolf, which is why the park matters to the cerrado story as well as this one.

Serra dos Orgaos and the Rio highlands

Granite peaks and cloud forest an hour and a half from Rio de Janeiro

The mountains behind Rio hold the most convenient Atlantic Forest in Brazil: Serra dos Orgaos National Park, the much larger Tres Picos State Park beside it and the Guapiacu reserve on the lowland side, all within two hours of an international airport. A week here can be spent at a single lodge.

  • Habitat: A spectacular mountain park of 30,000 hectares that carries more endemics than any other reserve in the country, on forest much like Itatiaia's.
  • When: Good all year. Grey-winged Cotinga, Black-and-gold Cotinga and Frilled Coquette are here in every month.
  • Getting there: The park lies about 55 km north of Rio. Staying inside it needs permission arranged in advance with the park authority, and Teresopolis at the gate has plenty of places to stay.
  • Look for: Grey-winged Cotinga, Black-and-gold Cotinga, Frilled Coquette, Saw-billed Hermit, Hooded Berryeater, Giant Snipe, Black-hooded Antwren, Brassy-breasted Tanager
  • National park:
    • Serra dos Orgaos National Park, Granite peaks above Teresopolis, with the Dedo de Deus over the entrance. Made trails run from 900 m upwards.
  • Stops:
    • : 58,000 hectares next to the national park, and the largest protected Atlantic Forest in Rio de Janeiro state. The Itororo lodge road runs through it.
    • Guapiacu Reserve : A private reserve on the lowland side at Cachoeiras de Macacu. It has restored wetland, its own lodge and a long reforestation record.
    • : The mountain town at the park gate, ninety minutes from Rio. It is the base for the upper forest.
    • : The town on the far side of the range, and the way onto the Macae de Cima forest.
  • Also covers: Cachoeiras de Macacu, Guapiacu Reserve, Guapimirim, Nova Friburgo, Serra dos Orgaos National Park, Teresopolis, Tres Picos State Park
  • Also watch for: Southern Muriqui has been reintroduced on the Guapiacu reserve, and the lowland forest there holds Lowland Tapir again after a long absence. Brown Howler is on every trail.

Ubatuba and the Serra do Mar coast

Lowland forest running straight down to the sea between Rio and Santos

The Serra do Mar drops to the Atlantic here and takes the forest with it, so lowland species are birded within sight of the beach. The Folha Seca road above Ubatuba is the classic morning, and the feeders in the gardens along it are where most people first see the coquettes and hermits. It pairs with Itatiaia in a few hours' drive.

  • Habitat: Ubatuba is an expensive beach resort 367 km south of Rio. Remnant lowland Atlantic forest stands close by and holds very rare and restricted endemics such as Red-eyed Thornbird, Black-hooded Antwren, Slaty Bristlefront and Buff-throated Purpletuft. Mangroves line the coast between Angra dos Reis and Paraty.
  • When: Good all year. Festive Coquette, Saw-billed Hermit and Bare-throated Bellbird are here in every month.
  • Getting there: The mangroves between Angra dos Reis, about 150 km west of Rio, and Paraty, another 100 km on, are worth working. West of Paraty and east of Ubatuba, tracks turn north off the coast road to private fazendas where permission to bird is sometimes given, so ask at the gate. The Serra da Bocaina park lies inland from Paraty and is still little explored by birders.
  • Look for: Festive Coquette, Saw-billed Hermit, Bare-throated Bellbird, Blond-crested Woodpecker, Spotted Bamboowren, Fork-tailed Tody-Tyrant, Buff-throated Purpletuft, Black-hooded Antwren
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Serra do Mar State Park, Picinguaba, The coastal section of the largest protected Atlantic Forest in Sao Paulo state. Mangrove, restinga (the sandy forest behind a beach) and forest trails sit in one place.
  • Stops:
    • : A dirt road climbing off the coast road north of Ubatuba through lowland forest. Private gardens and feeders along it are open by arrangement.
    • : The coastal town the region is reached from, four hours from Sao Paulo and five from Rio.
    • : The colonial port an hour north along the coast, with forest behind it and the Trindade road for lowland species.
  • Also covers: Estrada da Folha Seca, Paraty, Sao Sebastiao, Serra do Mar State Park, Picinguaba, Ubatuba
  • Also watch for: Golden Lion Tamarin is not here, but Buffy-tufted-ear Marmoset is, and the coastal forest carries Brown Howler and Ocelot. The bay holds Bryde's Whale offshore between June and September.

Intervales and the Paranapiacaba

49,000 hectares of continuous forest on a limestone plateau, and the best bird list in the south-east

The Paranapiacaba block is the largest continuous stretch of Atlantic Forest left anywhere, and Intervales State Park sits in the middle of it with lodging inside the park and its own network of forest roads. This is where the antshrikes, the bamboo specialists and the cotingas of the southern Atlantic Forest are actually seen.

  • Habitat: 41,705 hectares of Atlantic Forest on the Serra de Paranapiacaba, running from 60 m to 1,100 m, with more than 400 species recorded.
  • When: Good all year. Crescent-chested Puffbird, Large-tailed Antshrike and White-bearded Antshrike hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: About three hours southwest of Sao Paulo, 270 km, and 280 km from Guarulhos airport.
  • Look for: Swallow-tailed Cotinga, Crescent-chested Puffbird, Large-tailed Antshrike, White-bearded Antshrike, Solitary Tinamou, Black-fronted Piping Guan, Long-trained Nightjar, Buffy-fronted Seedeater
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Intervales State Park, 49,000 hectares with accommodation inside the park. A network of forest roads is birded on foot from before dawn.
    • Carlos Botelho State Park, 37,000 hectares adjoining Intervales, with a state road running through it and a different set of trails.
  • Stops:
    • : The limestone cave park south of Intervales, in the same forest block. A river valley is birded from the road.
    • : The town at the Intervales gate, four hours south-west of Sao Paulo.
  • Also covers: Capao Bonito, Carlos Botelho State Park, Intervales State Park, Iporanga, PETAR, Ribeirao Grande
  • Also watch for: Lowland Tapir and Southern Muriqui both hold in the Paranapiacaba block, and the park's night drives are among the few places in the south-east where Ocelot and Oncilla are both realistic.

Espirito Santo: Sooretama and the Vale reserve

Two reserves side by side, the lowland stronghold of the coastal forest

Almost all the lowland Atlantic Forest north of Rio has gone, and what is left is in Espirito Santo: the Sooretama Biological Reserve and the Vale Natural Reserve beside it hold nearly 50,000 hectares between them. It is the ground for Red-billed Curassow and Cherry-throated Tanager. Santa Teresa in the hills above is where Augusto Ruschi worked and is still the hummingbird site.

  • Habitat: The Sooretama reserve, 50 km north of Linhares and 136 km north of Vitoria, protects lowland Atlantic forest and is one of only three sites for Red-billed Curassow, the world's rarest cracid. Over 300 species have been recorded there, among them unusually large numbers of woodpeckers and cotingas. Nova Lombardia, 9 km north of Santa Teresa and 90 km from Vitoria, protects montane Atlantic forest with over 200 species recorded.
  • When: October to April, with the southern spring in September and October the best of it.
  • Getting there: Both reserves need permission arranged in advance with the federal environment agency. For Sooretama head north out of Linhares on the BR 101 towards Sao Mateus and turn northwest after about 14 km onto a track to the reserve headquarters. Bird the track northwest of the headquarters, the long trail that leads east before you reach it, and the small trail behind. For Nova Lombardia head west out of Santa Teresa towards Colatina and look for an unmarked track climbing a steep hill at the northeast edge of town, turn right there and right again at the fork about 6 km on. Two roads cross the reserve and paths lead east and west near the warden's house.
  • Look for: Red-billed Curassow, Cherry-throated Tanager, White-necked Hawk, Banded Cotinga, Buff-throated Purpletuft, Salvadori's Antwren, Plumbeous Antvireo, Blue-bellied Parrot
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Sooretama Biological Reserve, 24,000 hectares of federal lowland forest. Entry is by permit for research and by arrangement for birding.
  • Stops:
    • Vale Natural Reserve : 23,000 hectares of private reserve adjoining Sooretama. It has its own accommodation, roads and guides, and it is the most accessible lowland Atlantic Forest in Brazil.
    • : The hill town where Augusto Ruschi worked on hummingbirds. The Augusto Ruschi biological reserve and the museum stand above it.
    • : The montane forest south-west of Vitoria where the Cherry-throated Tanager was rediscovered.
  • Also covers: Domingos Martins, Linhares, Santa Teresa, Sooretama Biological Reserve, Vale Natural Reserve
  • Also watch for: Sooretama and Vale together hold Lowland Tapir, White-lipped Peccary and Jaguar, which is what a block of this size still supports and what the fragments around it have lost.

Southern Bahia: Serra Bonita and Una

Shade-grown cocoa below a hilltop reserve on the wet coastal ridge

Southern Bahia holds the wettest and most species-rich Atlantic Forest of all, and much of what survives does so inside cabruca, cocoa grown under the original canopy. Serra Bonita is a private reserve on a ridge above Camacan with its own lodge, and the Una reserve down on the coastal plain is the stronghold of the Golden-headed Lion Tamarin.

  • Habitat: Monte Pascoal National Park, near Porto Seguro roughly halfway between Vitoria and Salvador, protects forest holding a number of spectacular endemics, and much of it is out of reach of anything short of a major expedition. Around Porto Seguro itself the habitat and the birds are much like those at Monte Pascoal and at Sooretama further south, and Banded and White-winged Cotingas are probably easier to see here.
  • When: Good all year. Pink-legged Graveteiro, Banded Cotinga and Bare-throated Bellbird are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Monte Pascoal is entered from the western side of the park, about 32 km north of Itamaraju on the BR 101, and the visitor centre stands a short way inside. The Porto Seguro reserve and the Pau Brasil ecological station lie on the north side of the road some 14 km west of Porto Seguro. Both are company owned, so entry needs arranging in advance, and roadside birding along there is productive once suitable habitat has been found.
  • Look for: Pink-legged Graveteiro, Banded Cotinga, Bare-throated Bellbird, Buff-throated Purpletuft, Red-billed Curassow, White-necked Hawk, Salvadori's Antwren, Plumbeous Antvireo
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Una Biological Reserve, Lowland forest inland of Ilheus and the stronghold of the Golden-headed Lion Tamarin. It has a visitor section and a canopy walkway.
  • Stops:
    • : A private reserve running from 200 m to 950 m on a ridge above Camacan, with a lodge and a research station on the top.
    • : The cocoa town below Serra Bonita, four hours south of Ilheus.
    • : The coastal town with the airport, with the Veracel reserve and mangrove north of it.
  • Also covers: Camacan, Itacare, Porto Seguro, Serra Bonita Reserve, Una Biological Reserve
  • Also watch for: Golden-headed Lion Tamarin is the reason the Una reserve exists and is seen there rather than hoped for. Yellow-breasted Capuchin and Maned Sloth are both southern Bahia specialities and both are in trouble.

Iguacu and western Parana

Semi-deciduous forest in western Parana, either side of a waterfall two and a half kilometres across

Iguacu National Park protects 185,000 hectares of interior Atlantic Forest, a drier semi-deciduous forest with a list of its own. The falls themselves hold Great Dusky Swift roosting on the wet rock behind the water, which happens nowhere else.

  • Habitat: Iguacu falls are the biggest in the world and stand on the border with Argentina. Subtropical forest surrounds them and holds rare birds restricted to south Brazil, northeast Argentina and east Paraguay, among them Black-fronted Piping-Guan, Helmeted Woodpecker and Spotted Bamboowren.
  • When: Good all year. Black-fronted Piping Guan, Solitary Tinamou and Blond-crested Woodpecker are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: The Poco Preto trail, a couple of kilometres from the park entrance near the falls, is the best birding in the park. Walking it needs permission from the park office, which is usually given. The Itaipu dam north of Foz do Iguacu is another good site.
  • Look for: Black-fronted Piping Guan, Solitary Tinamou, Vinaceous-breasted Amazon, Blond-crested Woodpecker, Saffron Toucanet, Bare-throated Bellbird, Toco Toucan, Plush-crested Jay
  • National park:
    • Iguacu National Park, 185,000 hectares of interior Atlantic Forest. The falls walkway is at one end, and the Poco Preto and Bananeiras trails run away from the crowds.
  • Stops:
    • : The city with the airport, at the meeting of Brazil, Argentina and Paraguay. It is the base for both sides of the falls.
    • : A 9 km forest track inside the park, cycled or driven. It is the way to bird Iguacu away from the falls.
  • Also covers: Foz do Iguacu, Iguacu National Park, Poco Preto trail, Sao Miguel do Iguacu
  • Also watch for: Coati work the Brazilian walkways in numbers and are fed by visitors against the park's own signs. Jaguar is present, and the binational monitoring project with Argentina is one of the longest running in the Atlantic Forest.

Parana and Santa Catarina: the southern coast

Mangrove, restinga and araucaria on the coast south of Sao Paulo

The Paranagua bay is the largest estuary in southern Brazil and the forest around it is the last stronghold of the Red-tailed Amazon, which roosts on Superagui island in flocks. Inland, and on south into Santa Catarina, the forest turns to araucaria, the flat-topped conifer that once covered the southern plateau and now covers about two per cent of it.

  • Habitat: Marumbi National Park is a well-forested mountainous park between Sao Joao da Graciosa, 14 km north of Morretes, and the Curitiba to Sao Paulo road, and lowland forest runs along the nearby Morretes to Matinhos road. Immediately north along the same coast, forest still stands from Iguape south to Ariri and on Ilha do Cardoso, which holds the endemic Red-tailed Parrot.
  • When: The tall restinga near Itapoa on the state border works all year, and March to July is when the Parana Pine seeds ripen and Red-spectacled Parrots gather inland in thousands.
  • Getting there: A road crosses Marumbi, which makes access to the park excellent, and several tracks lead into lowland forest off the Morretes to Matinhos road. Morretes makes a good base. Ilha do Cardoso is reached from Cananeia, and the research station on the island is the most reliable place for Red-tailed Parrot.
  • Look for: Red-tailed Amazon, Marsh Antwren, Black-fronted Piping Guan, Vinaceous-breasted Amazon, Saffron Toucanet, Purple-winged Ground Dove, Solitary Tinamou, Long-trained Nightjar
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Band-winged Nightjar, Black-capped Screech Owl
  • National park:
    • Superagui National Park, Island and mangrove at the mouth of Paranagua bay. A boat takes you across, and the Red-tailed Amazon roost is here.
  • Stops:
    • : The town on the northern shore of the bay, with reserves inland of it and the boats to Superagui.
    • : The port city at the head of the bay, ninety minutes from Curitiba, and the way in.
    • : The smaller port on the western arm, with the Cachoeira valley reserve and the mangrove edge behind it.
  • Also covers: Antonina, Guaraquecaba, Joinville, Paranagua, Superagui National Park
  • Also watch for: Superagui holds the last population of the black-faced lion tamarin, described only in 1990. The bay carries Guiana Dolphin year round.

Rio Grande do Sul and the gaucho country

Araucaria forest, canyon and open grassland at the southern end of the forest

At its southern end in Rio Grande do Sul the Atlantic Forest gives way to araucaria and then to open grassland, and the Aparados da Serra canyons are where the two meet. The draw is the grassland list: seedeaters, the Long-tailed Cinclodes and a set of birds that share nothing with the coastal forest.

  • Habitat: Aparados da Serra National Park lies 187 km northeast of Porto Alegre and protects high plateau grassland dotted with marshes and stands of araucaria forest around the spectacular Itaimbezinho canyon.
  • When: Good all year. Vinaceous-breasted Amazon, Red-legged Seriema and Solitary Tinamou hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Head north out of Porto Alegre towards Taquara and Sao Francisco de Paula, then turn north off the RS 020 3 km west of Sao Francisco de Paula onto the road to Canela, which is good birding in its own right. From Sao Francisco de Paula head north towards Tainhas on an excellent birding road, with a track running east 18 km along. Continue past Tainhas and turn east at the Itaimbezinho sign, and a marsh lies east of that road before the park entrance gate. The canyon can be watched from the cafe.
  • Look for: Vinaceous-breasted Amazon, Long-trained Nightjar, Red-legged Seriema, Buffy-fronted Seedeater, Purple-winged Ground Dove, Black-fronted Piping Guan, Saffron Toucanet, Solitary Tinamou
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Biscutate Swift, Black-and-white Monjita
  • National parks:
    • Aparados da Serra National Park, The Itaimbezinho canyon, 700 m deep, with araucaria on the rim and subtropical forest on the floor.
    • Serra Geral National Park, The adjoining park with the Fortaleza canyon, higher and more open. The grassland birds are on the plateau.
  • Stops:
    • : The small town both canyons are reached from, at 1,000 m. It is cold in winter by Brazilian standards.
    • : The araucaria forest town an hour south, with the Pro-Mata reserve and the roadside stands the parrots roost in.
  • Also covers: Aparados da Serra National Park, Cambara do Sul, Sao Francisco de Paula, Serra Geral National Park, Torres
  • Also watch for: Maned Wolf and Pampas Deer are both on the grassland here, and the canyon walls hold a resident population of Vinaceous-breasted Amazon that roost in the araucaria below.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in the Atlantic Forest?

September to November. That is spring the whole length of the forest: the birds sing, the cotingas and bellbirds display, and the rain has not yet become an afternoon certainty. April to July is the alternative, dry and comfortable and very much quieter, which makes the forest interior far harder work. The three seasons.

Where should a first Atlantic Forest trip go?

Itatiaia and Ubatuba, or Itatiaia and the Serra dos Orgaos. Both pairings sit within a few hours of Rio and Sao Paulo and cover lowland forest and high ground in one trip, and they are what most trips use. Intervales is the third and it adds the most new birds.

How much of the forest is left?

Roughly twelve per cent of the original extent, in fragments, and the largest remaining block is the Paranapiacaba around Intervales. That is why the regions above are named for reserves rather than for landscapes: outside them there is often nothing to bird.

Which part of Brazil should a first trip go to?

Nobody books Brazil. The Pantanal, the Amazon, the Atlantic Forest and the cerrado, the dry savanna of the interior, with the caatinga thorn scrub of the northeast beside it, are different months, different airports and almost entirely different birds. The Atlantic Forest holds the greatest concentration of endemics and the easiest logistics of the four, which is why many first trips start here.

Do I need a guide?

For Itatiaia, Ubatuba and Iguacu, no: all three are watched from public roads and trails. For Intervales, Sooretama and Serra Bonita, yes in practice, because access is arranged and the birds are hard. Most operators use the same handful of local guides, who are very good.

How many endemics are there?

The Atlantic Forest holds the largest concentration of endemic birds on the continent, and a good three-week trip along this coast will see sixty or more Brazilian endemics. This site does not print a per-region endemic count, because the range data behind the cards is coded at country level and cannot honestly be cut finer.

What is cabruca?

Cocoa grown under the original forest canopy, which is how southern Bahia has farmed for two centuries. It holds far more birds than any other agriculture in the region and it is the reason a great deal of forest there is still standing, so it matters to the birds even though it is a plantation.

Is there anywhere certified to stay?

Very little. Almost nothing in Brazil meets the certification rule this site applies. The listings here are lodges inside private reserves, several of which restore forest as their main activity, and each card says which. How a certification is judged.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
