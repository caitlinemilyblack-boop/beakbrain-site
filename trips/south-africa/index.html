Getting around
This is the easiest country in Africa to self-drive. Roads are good, Kruger and the national parks take your own car, and the only 4x4 needed is the Sani Pass.

When to Go

South Africa runs two opposite rainfall calendars. The Cape gets its rain in winter and is at its best from November to March; everywhere else gets it in summer, and the bushveld game viewing is at its best in the dry winter from May to September. A trip that wants both is a compromise, and September and October are the fairest one.

Spring

September to November

The best compromise month for a whole-country trip: migrants arriving, grassland larks singing, and the bush still open.

Go for Namaqualand flowers into September, the Highveld larks nesting, and whales still off the south coast into November.

Trade off The interior heats up quickly and the first thunderstorms arrive from October.

Typical daily average The Cape 11 to 24 °C / 52 to 75 °F, Kruger 17 to 32 °C / 63 to 90 °F, The berg 6 to 22 °C / 43 to 72 °F

Dry winter

May to August

The dry season in the north. Water is scarce, the bush is thin, and birds and game concentrate at the waterholes.

Go for The best Kruger and Kgalagadi game viewing of the year, vultures at the Drakensberg hides, and whales arriving from June.

Trade off Cold nights on the highveld and the berg, and the Western Cape is in its wet season.

Typical daily average The Cape 9 to 19 °C / 48 to 66 °F, Kruger 7 to 27 °C / 45 to 81 °F, The berg -1 to 19 °C / 30 to 66 °F

Summer

December to April

Summer rain in the interior, everything breeding, and the Western Cape at its driest and windiest, which is its best.

Go for Palearctic migrants across the country, breeding plumage on the bishops and widowbirds, and the Cape Peninsula at its finest.

Trade off Thick bush and hard game viewing in the north, heat, and the coasts full of local holidaymakers.

Typical daily average The Cape 16 to 26 °C / 61 to 79 °F, Kruger 20 to 31 °C / 68 to 88 °F, The berg 13 to 24 °C / 55 to 75 °F

Semi-desert scrub, and larks that live nowhere else

The Karoo is a great arid basin in the middle of the country, and the Tankwa is its driest corner. The birding is done slowly from a gravel road, and the specialities are small brown birds that are worth every hour of it.

The Garden Route and the Overberg

Coastal forest, fynbos plains and the cranes on them

The southern coast keeps its rainfall all year and grows the only substantial indigenous forest in the country. Inland of it the Overberg wheatfields hold Blue Cranes and bustards in the open, which is the easiest large-bird watching in South Africa.

  • Look for: Knysna Turaco, Knysna Warbler, Blue Crane, Denham’s Bustard, Southern Black Korhaan, Black Harrier

The Eastern Cape and the Amathole

Malaria-free game reserves, coastal thicket and a mistbelt forest parrot

The Eastern Cape is where five biomes meet, and the practical result is malaria-free game reserves within an hour of an airport and Albany thicket that holds its own set of birds. Above them the Amathole mistbelt forest carries the Cape Parrot, the only parrot endemic to South Africa.

  • Look for: Cape Parrot, Knysna Woodpecker, Chorister Robin-Chat, Southern Tchagra, Narina Trogon, Ground Woodpecker, Blue Crane

Alpine grassland at 3,000 m, reached by one road

The Sani Pass climbs from KwaZulu-Natal into Lesotho on a rough track, and it is the only place a vehicle reaches the high Maloti grassland. The birds at the top belong to that grassland and to nowhere else, and Bearded Vultures cross the escarpment daily.

Zululand and the iSimangaliso Wetlands

Sand forest, coastal wetland and the birds that need both

Northern KwaZulu-Natal packs dune forest, sand forest, wetland and thornveld into a short drive, and a set of birds reaches its southern limit here. iSimangaliso runs from the coral reef to the lake and is a World Heritage site for it.

  • Look for: Pink-throated Twinspot, Rudd’s Apalis, Neergaard’s Sunbird, Green Twinspot, Saddle-billed Stork, Southern Ground Hornbill

Two million hectares of bushveld, driven from your own car

Kruger is the size of Wales, has tarred roads and self-catering camps, and you can drive it without a guide. The bird list of the lowveld, the hot low country east of the escarpment, runs past 500, alongside the large mammals the park is better known for.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit South Africa for birding?

September and October are the fairest compromise for a whole-country trip: migrants arriving, grassland larks singing, and the bush still thin enough to see through. The bushveld is best from May to August and the Western Cape from November to March, and those two do not overlap. The three seasons.

How many bird species does South Africa have?

Around 850, with a large set of endemics concentrated in the fynbos, the Karoo, the Highveld grassland and the Drakensberg. The number above counts the species with a card in the BeakBrain guide rather than a figure typed in by hand.

Can you self-drive?

This is the easiest country in Africa for it. Roads are good, Kruger and the national parks take an ordinary car, the rest camps are self-catering, and the only 4x4 above is the Sani Pass, which is done as a day transfer from Underberg. Five routes.

Where do you go birding in South Africa?

The Cape for fynbos endemics and a pelagic that ranks with any in the world, Kruger for bushveld and big game, the Drakensberg for the alpine set, Zululand for sand forest, and the Highveld for the larks. The regions and what each holds.

Can you combine birding and a safari?

They are the same trip here. Kruger holds over 500 bird species alongside the full large-mammal set, and the Kgalagadi has the highest raptor density in southern Africa on top of its lions. Nothing above asks you to choose.

What is the Cape Town pelagic?

A day trip from Simon’s Town or Hout Bay out to the trawling grounds beyond Cape Point, and among the best pelagics in the world. Albatrosses, petrels and shearwaters gather behind the trawlers, and the winter months bring the most species.

Can you see whales and birds together?

From June to November southern right whales calve close inshore along the south coast, and Hermanus and De Hoop are watched from the land. The same coast holds the fynbos and the Overberg cranes, so it is one trip.

What are the Namaqualand flowers?

For a few weeks in August and early September the semi-desert of the northern Cape flowers across whole hillsides. The timing moves with the rain, so it is worth checking before booking, and the birds of the strandveld are there whatever the flowers do.

How long do you need?

Five days does the Western Cape properly. Nine covers Kruger and the escarpment. Twelve takes the eastern endemic centres. Three weeks crosses the country west to east, which is what most operators sell. Five routes.

Do you need a guide?

For the Western Cape and Kruger a car and a good book work. For the Highveld larks and the Zululand sand forest a local guide saves days, and the Wakkerstroom community guides are the established way to find three of the rarest birds in the country.
