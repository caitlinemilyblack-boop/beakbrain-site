Birding in Maine

Maine is the only state where Razorbills breed, and more than seventy per cent of them sit on ledges between Great Wass Island and Cutler Bay, with boreal forest birds reachable at sea level an hour to the west.

Birds that decide the trip

Razorbill on the Down East ledges

Maine is the only state where the species breeds, and more than seventy per cent of that population sits between Great Wass Island and Cutler Bay. Old Mann Island alone held over a hundred pairs in 1997.

When Late May to early August, from a boat or from the Cutler headlands.

Where Down East and the seabird islands

Atlantic Puffin on Machias Seal Island

A licensed landing puts a hide within a few metres of breeding puffins and terns, which is as close as the species can be watched anywhere in the country. Numbers of landings are strictly capped.

When Late June to early August, on the licensed boat landings.

Where Down East and the seabird islands

Bicknell's Thrush on Katahdin

It breeds only in stunted fir on a handful of north-eastern mountains, and Baxter is the main ground for it in the state. Above three thousand feet, on sparsely wooded slopes, and singing mostly at dusk.

When Mid-June to mid-July, above three thousand feet at dawn and dusk.

Where Baxter State Park and Katahdin

Spruce Grouse in the boreal forest

It sits still and lets people walk past, which is why it is missed far more often than it is absent. Acadia has it at sea level and the north woods have it along the haul roads.

When All year, most easily in June on quiet forest tracks.

Where Acadia and Mount Desert Island, The north woods and the upper St. John

Leach's Storm Petrel off the outer islands

It nests in burrows on the outer islands and comes and goes only after dark, so the way to meet one is from a boat over deep water rather than at the colony.

When June to August, from a boat over the cold water offshore.

Where Acadia and Mount Desert Island, Down East and the seabird islands

Canada Jay along the Maine haul roads

It comes to a stopped car rather than the other way round, and the gravel forest roads in the north are where that happens most reliably. It nests in late winter, so pairs are already feeding young in April.

When All year, at road pull-ins in the spruce.

Where The north woods and the upper St. John

American Black Duck on the Maine tidal bay

The Maine bay is one of the more important staging grounds on this coast for the species, alongside thousands of Canada Geese and a long list of dabbling and diving ducks.

When March and April, and again in October and November.

Where Merrymeeting Bay and the midcoast

Arctic Tern on the island colonies

The Maine islands are near the southern edge of its breeding range, and the birds nesting here have come further to get to them than almost any other animal on earth travels in a year.

When Late May to August, on the island colonies.

Where Down East and the seabird islands

When to Go

The Maine season is short and it is worth planning around one thing at a time. The seabird landings run for about six weeks in high summer, the forest breeders sing through June, and the waterfowl come and go in the shoulder months when the coast is empty of visitors.

The waterfowl passage

March and April

Merrymeeting Bay stages thousands of geese and ducks as the ice goes out.

Go for Merrymeeting Bay from the bridges, and the midcoast beaches for the last sea ducks.

Trade off The north is still snowbound and the seabird islands are closed.

Typical daily average

The forest breeding season

June

Twenty warbler species are singing and the boreal birds are at their most vocal.

Go for Baxter for the high slopes, the north woods haul roads, and Acadia for the same list at sea level.

Trade off Blackflies are at their worst and the Baxter gate reservations are gone months in advance.

Typical daily average

The seabird season

Late June to early August

The puffin and tern landings run and shearwater numbers build offshore.

Go for Machias Seal Island, the Cutler ledges, and Petit Manan.

Trade off Boats cancel for swell without much warning, and the coast fills with holiday traffic.

Typical daily average

The autumn return

October and November

Waterfowl come back through the bay and sea ducks build along the coast.

Go for Merrymeeting Bay, Reid and Popham for scoters and Purple Sandpiper.

Trade off The seabird colonies have gone and the north is closing for winter.

Coast, island and north woods

Bangor (BGR), Bar Harbor 2 nights, Jonesport 2 nights, Millinocket 3 nights, Greenville 1 night, back to Bangor

The whole state in early July, which is the only window when the seabird landings and the mountain breeders overlap. Acadia and Mount Desert Island

Spruce, granite and fjord on one island, with a boreal bird list at sea level

Acadia puts Maine mountains of 1,500 feet straight down into the sea, and the cold coast pulls northern forest birds to a height a visitor can walk to. Spruce Grouse, Black-backed Woodpecker and Canada Jay all breed here, along with Leach's Storm-Petrels on the outer islands.

  • Habitat: Coniferous and birch forest, granite mountain to 1,500 feet, rocky coast, freshwater lakes, a fjord at Somes Sound, and offshore island ledges.
  • When: Late May to July for the breeding birds. The park roads fill from July onwards and the loop road runs one way.
  • Getting there: Bangor is the airport, an hour away, and Bar Harbor the base. The Park Loop Road charges an entrance fee, and the Schoodic side across the bay is an hour by road and far quieter.
  • Base yourself in: Bar Harbor
  • Look for: Spruce Grouse, Black-backed Woodpecker, Canada Jay, Boreal Chickadee, Common Loon, Common Eider, Leach's Storm Petrel, Black Guillemot, Bald Eagle, American Woodcock, Northern Saw-whet Owl, Red Crossbill
  • Also covers: Acadia National Park, Schoodic Peninsula
  • Also watch for: Harbour seals haul out on the ledges and the park carries a dense population of white-tailed deer.

Down East and the seabird islands

Ledges that carry most of the country's Razorbills, reached only by boat

The coast from Great Wass Island up to Cutler Bay carries most of the Razorbills in the only state where the species breeds: a hundred pairs on Old Mann Island alone in 1997, with several hundred pairs of Black Guillemot beside them. Machias Seal Island ten miles offshore takes puffins and terns, and the water in between holds storm-petrels in six figures.

  • Habitat: Rocky coast and offshore ledges, low island grassland, open ocean over the cold water of the Gulf of Maine.
  • When: Late June to early August, which is when the puffin and tern landings run and the shearwater numbers build.
  • Getting there: Bangor is the airport and Jonesport or Cutler the base. Machias Seal Island is licensed boat landings only, strictly capped and booked months ahead, and the crossing is often cancelled for swell.
  • Base yourself in: Jonesport
  • Look for: Razorbill, Atlantic Puffin, Black Guillemot, Arctic Tern, Roseate Tern, Common Eider, Wilson's Storm Petrel, Great Shearwater, Sooty Shearwater, Northern Gannet, Red-necked Phalarope, Red Phalarope
  • Also covers: Machias Seal Island, Northeastern Coastal Maine, Petit Manan National Wildlife Refuge
  • Also watch for: Minke and finback whales work the same water as the shearwaters, and grey seals haul out on the outer ledges.

Merrymeeting Bay and the midcoast

Six rivers meeting twenty miles inland, and the biggest waterfowl staging ground in the state

Merrymeeting Bay is a freshwater tidal bay in Maine where six rivers come together well inland of the sea, and it stages thousands of Canada Geese with large numbers of dabbling and diving ducks. The beaches to the south add nesting Piping Plovers and Least Terns on sand that is scarce this far north.

  • Habitat: Freshwater tidal bay with mudflats and wild rice, wet meadow and marsh, second-growth forest and farmland, with sand beach and dune to the south.
  • When: March and April for the northbound waterfowl, October and November for the return, and May to July for the beach nesters.
  • Getting there: Portland is the airport, forty minutes south, and Bath the base. The bay is best watched from the Route 197 bridge and from Swan Island, which is reached by a state ferry with a permit.
  • Base yourself in: Bath
  • Look for: American Black Duck, Canada Goose, Snow Goose, Northern Pintail, Blue-winged Teal, Green-winged Teal, Common Goldeneye, Bald Eagle, Piping Plover, Great Cormorant, Purple Sandpiper, Snow Bunting
  • Also covers: Merrymeeting Bay, Popham Beach State Park, Reid State Park
  • Also watch for: Harbour seals come up the estuary on the tide and sturgeon still run these rivers.

Baxter State Park and Katahdin

The state's stronghold for Bicknell's Thrush, in the sparse fir near the summit

Katahdin is the highest ground in the state and the sparse fir on its upper slopes is the main Maine ground for Bicknell's Thrush. Twenty warbler species breed in the park below it, and the whole area is managed as wilderness rather than as a park with facilities.

  • Habitat: Spruce and fir forest with birch and maple below, sparse krummholz fir on the high slopes, alpine tableland on the summit, ponds and bog.
  • When: Mid-June to mid-July for the breeding birds, which is also the only window when the high slopes are reliably walkable.
  • Getting there: Bangor is the airport and Millinocket the base. Baxter caps daily vehicle entry and the gate reservations go early, the roads inside are gravel and slow, and there is no fuel, food or mobile signal in the park.
  • Base yourself in: Millinocket
  • Look for: Bicknell's Thrush, Blackpoll Warbler, Bay-breasted Warbler, Cape May Warbler, Canada Warbler, Black-throated Blue Warbler, Blackburnian Warbler, Magnolia Warbler, Spruce Grouse, Boreal Chickadee, Yellow-bellied Flycatcher, Olive-sided Flycatcher
  • Also covers: Baxter State Park, Mount Katahdin
  • Also watch for: Moose are common on the pond edges at dawn and dusk, and black bear range through the whole park.

The north woods and the upper St. John

One of the largest unbroken forests left in the east, which is what the interior warblers need

The forest along the upper St. John runs unbroken for a very long way, and that matters because many of the neotropical migrants breeding in it do badly wherever the woods are cut into fragments. It is remote, it is privately owned working forest, and the birding is done from gravel haul roads.

  • Habitat: Unbroken spruce-fir and northern hardwood forest, riparian zones along the river, peat bog and beaver flowage.
  • When: Mid-June to mid-July, when the interior forest breeders are singing. Blackflies are severe through June.
  • Getting there: Bangor is the airport and Greenville the base. Most of the ground is private working forest reached by gated gravel roads with a fee and a signed logbook, and logging trucks have right of way on all of them.
  • Base yourself in: Greenville
  • Look for: Canada Warbler, Bay-breasted Warbler, Cape May Warbler, Blackpoll Warbler, Mourning Warbler, Palm Warbler, Lincoln's Sparrow, Rusty Blackbird, Spruce Grouse, Black-backed Woodpecker, Canada Jay, American Goshawk
  • Also covers: Allagash Wilderness Waterway, Moosehead Lake, Upper St. John River Project
  • Also watch for: Moose are abundant, and the Maine north woods hold lynx in one of very few populations left in the lower states.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Maine?

Late June to early August if the seabird islands are the point, because that is when the landings run and the shearwaters build offshore. June if the boreal forest birds are, because the warblers and thrushes sing then and stop soon after. March to April or October to November for the Merrymeeting Bay waterfowl.

How do I get onto Machias Seal Island?

Only with a licensed operator running from Cutler or Jonesport, and the number of people allowed to land each day is capped. Book months ahead, allow spare days, and expect the crossing to be called off for swell more often than you would like.

Where do I look for Bicknell's Thrush?

Baxter State Park, on the sparsely wooded slopes of Katahdin above three thousand feet, from mid-June to mid-July. It sings mostly at dusk, which means either a long day or a night in the park, and the park caps daily vehicle entry.

Do I need to book Baxter State Park in advance?

Yes. Daily vehicle entry is capped and the reservations go months ahead for the summer. Inside there is no fuel, no food and no mobile signal, and the roads are gravel and slow, so plan the day before you drive in.

Can I see boreal birds without going north?

Yes, and that is what makes Acadia unusual. The cold Maine coast brings Spruce Grouse, Black-backed Woodpecker, Canada Jay and Boreal Chickadee down to sea level on Mount Desert Island, where most places would need a mountain or three hundred miles of latitude to hold them.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
