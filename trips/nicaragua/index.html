Trips / Nicaragua

Birding in Nicaragua

Nicaragua holds the largest rainforest on the Central American isthmus outside Panama, a chain of active volcanoes between two lakes, and the last real stands of Pacific dry forest anywhere on the coast.

At a glance

Best months
December to April for the Pacific side, the volcanoes and the northern highlands, which is most of what a first trip covers. The Caribbean half and the Rio San Juan are wet all year and are least wet in February and March.
Regions
The Rio San Juan and Indio MaizThe volcanoes and the great lakesThe Pacific dry forest and estuariesThe northern highlands: Matagalpa and JinotegaThe Caribbean lowlands and the pine savanna
Routes
Granada and the volcanoes5 daysThe classic circuit8 daysThe Rio San Juan7 daysThe northern highlands6 daysEnd to end14 days
Getting in
Managua (MGA) takes almost all the international flights, and Granada is an hour away on a surfaced road. Internal flights to San Carlos, Bluefields and Puerto Cabezas take about an hour each and save a day or more of road and river.
Getting around
A hired vehicle covers the Pacific side and the northern highlands comfortably, and the main roads are surfaced. The Rio San Juan and the whole Caribbean half have no road network to speak of and are reached by air and boat.

When to Go

Nicaragua runs a dry season on the Pacific side from December to April and a wet one from May to November. The Caribbean half runs no dry season worth the name and takes four times the rain, which is the single most useful thing to know before planning a route across the country.

Peak

December to April

The Pacific dry season. The dry forest at Chacocente is leafless and open, the volcano roads are firm, and the northern highland tracks are passable.

Go for Dry forest birds at their most visible, wintering warblers through the shade coffee, and the clearest weather on the cones.

Trade off The busiest months, and the Caribbean side stays wet throughout.

Green

May to August

The rains reach the Pacific side. Afternoon storms rather than all-day rain, and the dry forest greens over within a fortnight.

Go for Breeding activity everywhere, the lowest rates of the year, and turtle arrivals beginning at Chacocente in July.

Trade off The dry forest closes in and its birds become far harder, and the unsurfaced tracks turn.

Migration

September to November

The wettest stretch on both coasts, and the arrival of the North American migrants down the isthmus.

Go for Autumn passage, raptor movement over the Pacific plain, and the turtle nestings at their peak.

Trade off September and October are the hardest months to travel in, and the Caribbean side can be cut off.

The Rio San Juan and Indio Maiz

The largest block of rainforest on the Central American isthmus outside Panama, on the Costa Rican border and reached only by river

Indio Maiz is over three thousand square kilometres of lowland rainforest and the Rio San Juan runs along its southern edge. Refugio Bartola sits at the junction with the Bartola river and is the usual base, reached by boat down from El Castillo. Great Green Macaw and Agami Heron are both here.

  • Habitat: Lowland tropical rainforest on the Caribbean slope, cut by blackwater tributaries, with river-edge swamp and the San Juan itself running the whole southern boundary.
  • When: February to April is the least wet, and this corner takes rain in every month. River level rather than rainfall decides what is reachable.
  • Getting there: San Carlos is reached by air from Managua in an hour or by road in seven. From there it is a public boat down the San Juan to El Castillo and Bartola, which takes most of a day.
  • Look for: Great Green Macaw, Agami Heron, Sungrebe, King Vulture, Great Curassow, Montezuma Oropendola
  • National parks: Reserva Biologica Indio Maiz, Refugio de Vida Silvestre Rio San Juan
  • Stops:

    • El Castillo, a river village under a Spanish fort, and the last place with rooms before the reserve
    • , a blackwater tributary paddled at first light for herons, kingfishers and Sungrebe
    • , a wetland refuge on the southern shore of Lake Nicaragua, reached by boat from San Carlos marena.gob.ni
  • Also watch for: Baird's tapir and jaguar in the reserve interior, and West Indian manatee in the lower river.

The volcanoes and the great lakes

A chain of active cones between two lakes, with cloud forest on the tallest and a crater lagoon inside one of them

Mombacho and Maderas both hold cloud forest on their upper slopes, small islands of it above the dry Pacific plain. Masaya is a working volcano with a parrot colony nesting in the crater walls, and Ometepe rises from the great lake as two cones joined by an isthmus.

  • Habitat: Cloud forest and elfin woodland on volcanic cones above dry tropical forest, with crater lagoons, lava fields and the shores of two large lakes.
  • When: December to April for the dry season and the clearest cone weather. The cloud forests hold their moisture whatever the calendar says.
  • Getting there: Granada is the base, an hour from Managua on a surfaced road. Mombacho is reached by a park truck up the cobbled access road, and Ometepe by ferry from San Jorge near Rivas.
  • Look for: Yellow-naped Amazon, Turquoise-browed Motmot, White-throated Magpie-Jay, Long-tailed Manakin, Keel-billed Motmot, Crested Guan
  • National parks: Reserva Natural Volcan Mombacho, Parque Nacional Volcan Masaya, Reserva Natural Laguna de Apoyo
  • Stops:

    • , an active crater with a road to the rim, and a parakeet colony that nests in the sulphurous walls marena.gob.ni

The Pacific dry forest and estuaries

Tropical dry forest and mangrove estuary on the Pacific plain, holding the birds of a habitat almost gone from Central America

Chacocente and La Flor protect two of the last stands of Pacific dry forest, a habitat that once ran the length of the isthmus and is now almost gone. The estuaries north of them take waders and terns, and Salinas Grandes is where the shorebird counts are done.

  • Habitat: Deciduous tropical dry forest on a volcanic coastal plain, with mangrove estuary, salt flat and beach behind it.
  • When: December to April, when the dry forest is leafless and the birds are visible in it. The estuary counts peak with the northern winter.
  • Getting there: Managua to Chacocente is three hours south on the Carretera a Rivas and then a rough track. Leon is two hours northwest of the capital and the base for the northern estuaries.
  • Look for: Turquoise-browed Motmot, White-throated Magpie-Jay, Lesser Ground Cuckoo, Yellow-naped Amazon, Long-tailed Manakin, Bare-throated Tiger Heron
  • National parks: Refugio de Vida Silvestre Rio Escalante Chacocente, Reserva Natural Isla Juan Venado, Reserva Natural Volcan Cosiguina
  • Stops:

  • Also watch for: Olive ridley turtles arrive at Chacocente and La Flor in mass nestings between July and January, which is the reason both were protected.

The northern highlands: Matagalpa and Jinotega

Coffee country on the highest ground in the country, with cloud forest reserves above the plantations

The northern highlands are shade coffee and cloud forest, and the reserves above Matagalpa and Jinotega hold the montane birds the rest of the country lacks. Selva Negra has trails through forest and plantation together, which is the easiest introduction to both.

  • Habitat: Montane cloud forest and pine-oak on the central highlands, cut by shade coffee plantations and the reservoirs of the Apanas basin.
  • When: December to April for the roads and the clearest mornings. Wintering North American warblers fill the shade coffee from November.
  • Getting there: Matagalpa is two and a half hours north of Managua on a surfaced road, and Jinotega an hour further over the pass. The reserve tracks above both towns need clearance in the wet.
  • Look for: Resplendent Quetzal, Three-wattled Bellbird, Keel-billed Motmot, Collared Aracari, Crested Guan, Montezuma Oropendola
  • National parks: Reserva Natural Cerro Datanli El Diablo, Reserva Natural Miraflor
  • Stops:

    • , cloud forest above Jinotega, walked in from the coffee farms on its flanks marena.gob.ni
    • , a community-managed reserve of cloud forest, pine and pasture north of Esteli marena.gob.ni
    • , a reservoir above Jinotega taking wintering duck and the country's largest concentration of Snail Kite
  • Also watch for: The shade coffee here is one of the better documented cases of a farmed habitat holding a forest bird community, and the counts are run from Selva Negra.

The Caribbean lowlands and the pine savanna

Lagoon, rainforest and one of the few great tracts of natural Caribbean pine savanna in the Americas

The Caribbean side is a different country: Miskito and Creole rather than Spanish, wet all year, and reached by air or river rather than road. Bluefields and the Pearl Lagoon sit on the southern lowlands, and the pine savanna behind Puerto Cabezas runs north into Honduras.

  • Habitat: Lowland rainforest and swamp forest behind a coast of brackish lagoon and mangrove, giving way north to natural Caribbean pine savanna on white sand.
  • When: February to April is the least wet. This coast takes over four metres of rain a year and there is no dry season in the sense the Pacific side has one.
  • Getting there: Flights from Managua to Bluefields and Puerto Cabezas take about an hour. The road to Bluefields ends at El Rama and the last stretch is by river boat.
  • Look for: Great Green Macaw, Snail Kite, Limpkin, Agami Heron, Bare-throated Tiger Heron, King Vulture
  • National parks: Reserva Natural Cerro Silva, Reserva Biologica Cayos Miskitos
  • Stops:

    • , open Caribbean pine on white sand behind the town, a habitat that occurs almost nowhere else
    • , a reserve and agroforestry centre inland of Pearl Lagoon, reached up the Wawashang river
  • Also watch for: West Indian manatee in the lagoons, and the Miskito Cays marine reserve offshore, which is one of the largest in the Caribbean.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Nicaragua?

December to April for the Pacific side, the volcanoes and the northern highlands, which is most of what a first trip covers. The dry forest is leafless then and its birds are visible in it. The Caribbean half and the Rio San Juan are wet all year and are least wet in February and March.

How many bird species does Nicaragua have?

Around 750 species have been recorded. The country is the widest part of the isthmus and holds both slopes plus two large lakes, so the Caribbean rainforest avifauna and the Pacific dry forest one are both present with the volcanic cloud forests between them.

Is Nicaragua safe to travel for birding?

The birding regions are rural and the reserves are managed by MARENA and by community groups. Check current government travel advice before booking, since the political situation has affected advisories in recent years, and note that the Caribbean coast is reached by air rather than road either way.

Where do I see a quetzal?

Cerro Datanli El Diablo above Jinotega, or the reserves around Matagalpa. This is the northern edge of the population and quetzals are scarcer here than in Costa Rica, so allow more than one morning and go between March and June when pairs are calling.

What is the Rio San Juan trip actually like?

A flight to San Carlos, then public boats down the river to El Castillo and Bartola over most of a day. There is no road. Accommodation is simple, the forest starts at the water's edge, and it is the only way to reach Indio Maiz.

Do I need four-wheel drive?

For Chacocente, the Mombacho access road and the reserve tracks above Matagalpa and Jinotega, yes. The main highways between Managua, Granada, Leon and Rivas are surfaced and a normal car is fine on them.

Is the Pacific dry forest worth a detour?

It is one of the two reasons to come. Tropical dry forest once ran the length of the Pacific isthmus and almost none of it is left, so Chacocente holds a bird community that is genuinely hard to see anywhere else.

Can I combine Nicaragua with Costa Rica?

Easily, and the Rio San Juan is the border for much of its length. The Los Chiles crossing puts you on the Costa Rican side of the same wetland system within an hour of San Carlos, and the two halves of it are counted together.

How wet is the Caribbean side?

Bluefields takes over four metres of rain a year against about a metre and a half in Managua. There is no dry season on that coast in the sense the Pacific plain has one, only a less wet stretch from February to April.

What field guide should I take?

Birds of Central America by Vallely and Dyer covers the whole isthmus in one volume and is the current standard. There is no guide to Nicaragua alone.
