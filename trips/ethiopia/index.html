Birds that decide the trip

Ruspoli's Turaco on the Genale

A green turaco with a white crown that lives in a scatter of juniper woods and fig-lined riverbeds in southern Ethiopia and nowhere else. It was known from one 1892 specimen for ninety years. The fig trees on the Genale tributaries and the juniper at Arero are where people find it.

When The dry season concentrates it on fruiting figs, so December to March is best.

Where Wadera, Arero

Ethiopian Bushcrow around Yabello

A grey and black crow the size of a jay, with bare blue skin around the eye. Its entire world range is about 100 km across in Borana country, and nobody has explained why it stops where it does. It builds bulky stick nests in acacias beside villages and cattle.

When Resident all year and easy to find within its range.

Where Yabello, Mega

White-tailed Swallow over the Borana bush

A small blue and white swallow with white outer tail feathers, confined to the same small block of southern acacia as the bushcrow. It hunts low over open bush and perches on huts and wires in the villages.

When Resident all year, and easiest along the gravel roads to Soda.

Where Yabello, Mega

Spot-breasted Lapwing on Sanetti

A heavy black-crowned lapwing with a spotted breast that lives on wet Afro-alpine grassland above 3,000 m. Sanetti and the Web valley carry it in the open, and it has become scarce on the plateau grassland further north.

When Present all year on the plateau, and the pairs are easiest to see in the June to August wet season.

Where Dinsho, Goba

Harwood's Spurfowl in the Jemma valley

A dark francolin with a red bill and bare red skin round the eye, restricted to the gorges of the Jemma and Blue Nile drainage. It calls from the rocky acacia slopes at first light and goes quiet soon after.

When Dawn from the valley descent, and November to March keeps the gravel firm.

Where Alem Ketema, Debre Libanos

Ankober Serin on the escarpment

A small streaky finch found in 1976 and known from a thin band of cliff and moorland along the eastern escarpment. It feeds among lichens on sheer rock faces and in the fields directly above them.

When Present all year, and cloud often covers the cliffs, so early mornings work best.

Where Ankober, Debre Birhan

Arabian Bustard on the Awash plains

A tall grey and brown bustard of Sahelian dry country, and the Awash region is one of the easiest places in the world to see it. Birds walk the open plains north-east of the national park and the road down from the escarpment.

When Resident all year, and the coolest walking is October to March.

Where Awash, Metahara

Egyptian Plover on the Baro

A grey, black and apricot wader of large sandy rivers, and one of the most wanted birds in Africa. It feeds on the exposed banks and rocks of the Baro at Gambela, and a second population uses the Gibe gorge closer to the capital.

When December to April, when the rivers are low and the sandbanks are exposed.

Where Gambela, Welkite

When to Go

Ethiopia runs two wet seasons. The small rains fall in April and May and the main rains from June to September, heaviest on the highlands. The dry months between them are when the roads work, and November to March carries the Palearctic migrants on top of the resident birds.

The dry months

November to March

The Palearctic winter, and the season nearly every bird tour books. Roads are firm from the northern escarpment to the Somali border.

Go for Full migrant numbers on the Rift lakes, every gravel road passable, and cool clear mornings in the highlands.

Trade off It is the busiest and priciest window, and the southern lowlands are hot by the middle of the day.

Typical daily average
  • Addis Ababa: 9 to 23 °C / 48 to 73 °F
  • Awash: 15 to 32 °C / 59 to 90 °F
  • Yabello: 13 to 27 °C / 55 to 81 °F

The small rains

April to May

Short afternoon storms across the highlands, with the country greening and resident birds starting to breed.

Go for Nesting and song in the forests, mild temperatures, and very few other visitors.

Trade off The Palearctic migrants have gone, and gravel descents into the gorges turn slippery.

Typical daily average
  • Addis Ababa: 11 to 24 °C / 52 to 75 °F
  • Awash: 19 to 34 °C / 66 to 93 °F
  • Yabello: 14 to 24 °C / 57 to 75 °F

The main rains

June to September

The long wet season, when the highlands take most of their rain and the west becomes impossible to move around.

Go for Wildflowers on the Bale moorland from June to July, weavers and bishops in breeding plumage, and green country with the mildest temperatures of the year.

Trade off The Omo valley and the western lowlands are shut, several southern tracks turn to mud, and highland trekking is heavy going.

Typical daily average
  • Addis Ababa: 10 to 22 °C / 50 to 72 °F
  • Awash: 20 to 34 °C / 68 to 93 °F
  • Yabello: 13 to 23 °C / 55 to 73 °F

Addis Ababa and the Highland Forests

Juniper forest, city parkland and two gorges within a morning of the capital

Almost every trip lands at Addis Ababa, and several endemic birds live inside the city and on the hills above it. Menagesha and Entoto hold dry evergreen juniper at about 2,600 m, and the Muger and Gibe gorges cut through the plateau close enough for a day out. A stopover of one or two days already reaches a good share of the highland endemics.

  • Habitat: Dry evergreen juniper forest with scattered Hagenia and olive at about 2,600 m, eucalyptus plantation between the remaining patches, open plateau grassland to the north, and acacia woodland under rocky cliffs in the Muger and Gibe gorges.
  • When: The forests hold their endemics all year. November to March adds Palearctic ducks on the reservoir and wheatears and pipits on the grassland.
  • Getting there: Menagesha lies about an hour west of the capital on the Jimma road, and the last stretch is gravel that can want a 4WD after heavy rain. Entoto sits on the northern edge of the city. Muger Gorge is 42 km north to Chancho and then 29 km west on gravel, and Gibe Gorge is about 150 km south-west on tar.
  • Base yourself in: Addis Ababa
  • Look for: Yellow-fronted Parrot, White-cheeked Turaco, Abyssinian Catbird, Abyssinian Woodpecker, White-winged Cliff Chat, Wattled Ibis, Banded Barbet

    • : A remnant juniper patch on the hills above the city. A signposted road climbs about 3 km through eucalyptus to the churches at the summit.

The Jemma Valley and the Northern Escarpment

Gorge rims and cliff country two hours north of the capital

The plateau north of Addis Ababa breaks into deep gorges and dizzying escarpments, and five endemic birds live here that the southern highlands never hold. Harwood's Spurfowl belongs to the Jemma drainage alone, and Ankober Serin feeds on the cliffs where the escarpment drops towards the Rift. A loop of two to three days takes in Debre Libanos, the Jemma descent and Ankober.

  • Habitat: Montane grassland and cultivation on the plateau, olive and juniper forest around the monastery, sheer rock cliffs and gorges below the rim, and hot acacia thornbush on the valley floor at about 1,400 m.
  • When: Dawn is the only dependable time for Harwood's Spurfowl on the valley slopes. November to March is dry and kindest to the gravel descents.
  • Getting there: Debre Libanos is under two hours north of the capital on tar and then 4.6 km of gravel. The Jemma descent is reached on gravel from either Muka Turi or Debre Birhan, and Ankober lies 130 km from the capital through Debre Birhan. A 4WD is wanted for the escarpment road down to Melka Ghebdu, and conditions on it change with the rains.
  • Base yourself in: Debre Birhan
  • Look for: Harwood's Spurfowl, Ankober Serin, Rüppell's Black Chat, Abyssinian Wheatear, White-billed Starling, Red-billed Pytilia, Yellow-throated Seedeater
  • Stops:
    • : A monastery on the lip of the Jemma valley, with a forest patch beside it and a gorge falling away below.
    • : The pass drops from the plateau to the river on gravel, and the rocky slopes below it hold the endemic spurfowl at first light.
    • : A gap in the mountains beside the tar road north of Debre Birhan, with a sheer cliff over the Awedi river a short walk from the verge.
    • : A village on the escarpment lip at about 3,000 m, and the place the serin is named for.
    • : A stream crossing in hot acacia country below the escarpment, and the most reachable site for Yellow-throated Seedeater.
  • Also covers: Alem Ketema, Ankober, Debre Birhan, Debre Libanos, Gemessa Gedel, Jemma Valley descent, Melka Ghebdu, Muka Turi
  • Also watch for: Gelada troops graze the cliff edges in numbers, and klipspringer stands on the rocks below them.

Awash and the Eastern Lowlands

Acacia savanna, a volcano and a lava lake in the hot north-eastern Rift

Awash sits where the Rift Valley drops into Afar country, and the dry savanna here carries Sahelian birds that reach no other part of Ethiopia. Arabian Bustard walks the open plains in numbers, and Sombre Rock Chat lives on the lava beside Lake Beseka. Three days covers the national park, the plains to the north-east and the eastern road towards Harar.

The Central Rift Valley Lakes

A string of lakes and escarpment forest on the main road south

Every southern trip drives the central Rift Valley, and the lakes strung along it carry the largest waterbird numbers in the country. Ziway, Langano, Abijatta and Hawassa sit within a morning of each other, and the forest left on the escarpments above them holds Yellow-fronted Parrot. From November to March the Palearctic ducks, waders and gulls arrive on top of the residents.

  • Habitat: Open and swampy lake shores with reedbeds and riparian trees, acacia savanna and low wooded cliffs between the lakes, tall fig forest on the south-eastern shore of Langano, and mid-altitude montane forest at Wondo Genet at about 1,800 m.
  • When: November to March brings the Palearctic ducks, waders and gulls onto the lakes. The forest patches are worth walking at any season.
  • Getting there: The lakes lie on the tar road running south from the capital, with Ziway about 160 km down it and Hawassa about 270 km. Gravel spurs of 3 to 20 km run east from the tar to the Langano shores, and the longer of them can want a 4WD.
  • Base yourself in: Lake Langano
  • Look for: Black Crowned Crane, Lesser Flamingo, Yellow-fronted Parrot, Black-winged Lovebird, Lesser Jacana, Abyssinian Ground Thrush, Bat Hawk, African Spotted Creeper
  • Also recorded in the parks here: African Grey Woodpecker, Bare-eyed Thrush
  • National park:
    • Abijatta-Shalla National Park, Two lakes in one park, one saline and shallow and the other deep, and the flamingos gather on the first.

    • : A shallow seasonal pan beside the crater lakes, and the best water in the area for ducks and cranes.

The Bale Mountains and the Sanetti Plateau

Afro-alpine moorland above 4,000 m, and the easiest highland birding in the country

The Bale massif carries the largest block of Afro-alpine moorland in Africa, and a road crosses the Sanetti plateau at over 4,000 m. The open heath up there holds Spot-breasted Lapwing, Wattled Crane and the Ethiopian wolf, and the Hagenia and juniper woodland around Dinsho holds most of the forest endemics. Nearly every endemic bird of the southern highlands lives inside this one park.

  • Habitat: Hagenia and juniper woodland with wet forest around Dinsho at about 3,000 m, tussocky montane streams and short grassland in the Web valley, and Afro-alpine heath with alpine tarns on the Sanetti plateau above 3,800 m.
  • When: The plateau is crossable all year and the wildflowers open from June to July. The Web valley track becomes impassable in the rains.
  • Getting there: Dinsho is about four hours from Shashemene and roughly 400 km from the capital. Goba lies 42 km east of Dinsho, and the Sanetti road climbs from there on a high-clearance 2WD. Park fees are paid at Dinsho before going up. A 4WD is needed for the Web valley, and nights on the plateau are freezing at any season.
  • Base yourself in: Goba
  • Look for: Spot-breasted Lapwing, Rouget's Rail, Abyssinian Catbird, Wattled Crane, Moorland Francolin, Abyssinian Owl, Red-billed Chough, Blue-winged Goose, Abyssinian Longclaw, Thick-billed Raven
  • National park:
    • Bale Mountains National Park, One park covering the woodland, the moorland and the forest on the southern slope.
  • Stops:
    • : The park headquarters, with Hagenia woodland behind it that a short walk covers.
    • Sanetti Plateau: Afro-alpine heath and tarns above 3,800 m, crossed by the highest all-weather road in Africa.
    • : The town at the foot of the Bale escarpment and the start of the plateau climb.
    • : A limestone gorge and cave system east of Goba, with acacia thicket in the valley bottom.
    • : The junction town north of Goba where the eastern road to the caves turns off.
  • Also covers: Bale Mountains National Park, Dinsho, Goba, Robe, Sanetti Plateau, Sof Omar
  • Also watch for: The Ethiopian wolf hunts giant mole rats across open Sanetti moorland, and mountain nyala and Menelik's bushbuck feed in the juniper at Dinsho.

The Harenna Forest and the Genale Descent

Cloud forest on the southern slope of Bale, and 3,000 m of descent to thornbush

South of Sanetti the road drops through 35 km of forest and loses nearly 3,000 m in a single day, which is one of the great descents in Africa. Harenna carries tree heath along its upper margin, Hagenia and Podocarpus cloud forest through the middle and drier open woodland at the bottom. The fig-lined watercourses on the Genale drainage below it are the classic ground for Ruspoli's Turaco.

  • Habitat: Tree heath and lichen-hung St John's wort at about 3,200 m, Hagenia and Podocarpus cloud forest at about 2,400 m, drier open-canopy forest at about 1,800 m, and sandy fig-lined watercourses on the Genale drainage below 1,200 m.
  • When: The turaco is easiest to find in the dry season when the figs draw it in. The forest road is crossable all year with a 4WD.
  • Getting there: The road from Goba to Dolo Mena bisects the forest, and a 4WD is wanted at any season. Camping fees for the forest clearings are paid at the park headquarters before the descent. The Genale crossings lie on the road between Dolo Mena and Negele, and a local guide keeps track of the turacos there.
  • Base yourself in: Dolo Mena
  • Look for: Ruspoli's Turaco, Abyssinian Crimsonwing, African Hill Babbler, Abyssinian Ground Thrush, Brown Parisoma, Sharpe's Starling, White-cheeked Turaco, Abyssinian Woodpecker
  • Stops:
    • Harenna Forest: Wet montane forest on the southern slope of Bale, crossed by the road down to the lowlands.
    • : The lowland town at the bottom of the descent, where the forest gives way to thorn.
    • : A gold-mining town in the western foothills with forest patches on the road either side of it.
    • : A junction on the Negele road, and the northern end of the forest belt that holds the turaco.
  • Also covers: Dolo Mena, Harenna Forest, Kebre Mengist, Shakiso, Wadera
  • Also watch for: Lion and African wild dog live in the Harenna forest, and the Bale monkey feeds in the bamboo along its western edge.

Negele and the Liben Plain

Short grassland and dense thornbush where the southern lowlands begin

The Liben plain outside Negele is a small square of short grassland that holds Archer's Lark, one of the most threatened birds in Africa. The thornbush around it and the tracks running east towards the Somali border carry species that are hard to reach anywhere else on earth. A week is well spent between Negele and Bogol Manyo, and a full day of driving separates them.

  • Habitat: Open short grassland with scattered thornbushes at about 1,600 m on the Liben plain, mixed and whistling-thorn woodland around it, and sparse Acacia and Commiphora savanna with dense riparian thicket falling away to 200 m in the east.
  • When: November to March is dry and easiest on the tracks, and the grassland is at its shortest then. The eastern roads become impassable in the rains.
  • Getting there: Negele is a full day of driving south of the Bale escarpment. Bogol Manyo is a further full day east, and the gravel road to Wachile takes a 2WD in dry weather. Permission from the local authorities and a local guide are needed for the eastern sites, and the security situation there changes, so ask before setting out.
  • Base yourself in: Negele
  • Look for: Archer's Lark, White-crowned Starling, Somali Courser, Salvadori's Seedeater, Juba Weaver, Heuglin's Bustard, White-winged Collared Dove, Philippa's Crombec
  • Stops:
    • Liben Plain: A treeless square of short grassland south-east of Negele, and the only known home of Archer's Lark.
    • : The regional town and the base for the plain, the Genale figs and the eastern roads.
    • : Dense Commiphora thicket on the Dawa drainage, and the link road west towards Yabello.
    • : A hill town east of Negele with acacia country around it that holds Salvadori's Seedeater.
  • Also covers: Filtu, Liben Plain, Negele, Wachile
  • Also watch for: Gerenuk and Salt's dik-dik feed in the thornbush, and camels graze the plain alongside Borana cattle.

Yabello and the Borana Acacia

Tall acacia and Commiphora bush holding two birds found nowhere else on earth

Yabello sits in Borana country in the far south, and two endemic birds live in a range barely 100 km across around it. Ethiopian Bushcrow works the ground beside cattle and settlements, and White-tailed Swallow hunts over the same bush. The juniper at Arero and the stony plains at Soda add most of the remaining southern specialities.

  • Habitat: Tall acacia savanna and lower Acacia and Commiphora thicket at about 1,400 to 1,600 m, pure Commiphora bushland, open stony plains around Soda, rocky hills, and juniper woodland at Arero at about 1,600 m.
  • When: Both endemics are present all year and neither moves. November to March is dry and the gravel is firm.
  • Getting there: Yabello is 565 km south of the capital on tar and takes a long day of driving, so most trips break the run at the Rift Valley lakes. Mega lies 104 km further south on the same tar. The gravel roads east to Soda and Arero want a high-clearance vehicle, and the direct Arero road has been damaged for years.
  • Base yourself in: Yabello
  • Look for: Ethiopian Bushcrow, White-tailed Swallow, Vulturine Guineafowl, Scaly Chatterer, Pringle's Puffback, Short-tailed Lark, Northern Grosbeak-Canary, Ruspoli's Turaco
  • Stops:
    • : The town at the centre of the endemic range, with bushcrow nests in the acacias on its outskirts.
    • : An isolated juniper wood at about 1,600 m east of Yabello, and a northern site for Ruspoli's Turaco.
    • : A hill town 104 km south of Yabello, with arid open plains north and east of it.
    • : A salt crater reached by gravel from the main road, and the stony verges either side hold larks and coursers.
    • : Roadside juniper and Podocarpus forest with wet grassland between the patches, and a good short stop on the drive south.

  • Also watch for: Gerenuk browse on their hind legs along the Soda road, and Guenther's dik-dik and Grant's gazelle use the open bush.

Nechisar and the Southern Omo

Grass plains between two Rift lakes, and the bush country of the lower Omo

Nechisar spreads over a grass plain between Lake Abaya and Lake Chamo, and it is the only place the Nechisar Nightjar has ever been recorded. The park sits beside Arba Minch and drops easily onto a southern circuit. Further south the Omo bush and the riverine forest at Mago hold elephant and buffalo, and the birdlist starts to pick up western species.

  • Habitat: Riparian forest along the two lake shores, grassy plains with sparse thornbush at about 1,200 m on the Nechisar plains, and dense bushland and savanna with swamps and riverine forest along the Mago and Neri rivers.
  • When: October to March is dry and the plains tracks hold up. The Omo valley stays too wet to enter until the last week of June and sometimes later.
  • Getting there: Arba Minch is a long day of driving south of the Rift Valley lakes. Nechisar takes a scout and a high-clearance 4WD at any season, and the rocky ridge between the two lakes is the hardest part of it. Mago is reached from Jinka, and a letter of permission and a high-clearance vehicle are both needed.
  • Base yourself in: Arba Minch
  • Look for: Nechisar Nightjar, Scissor-tailed Kite, Orange River Francolin, Yellow-throated Sandgrouse, Black-bellied Bustard, Star-spotted Nightjar, Northern Masked Weaver, Stripe-breasted Seedeater
  • Also recorded in the parks here: African Pied Wagtail, White-browed Robin-Chat
  • National parks:
    • Nechisar National Park, Grass plains between Lake Abaya and Lake Chamo. A rough ridge road climbs to them from the park gate.
    • Mago National Park, Dense bushland and savanna with riverine forest, and the only Ethiopian ground that still holds African buffalo.
    • Senkelle Swayne's Hartebeest Sanctuary, A small highland grassland reserve south of the Shashemene to Arba Minch road, entered with a scout.
  • Stops:
    • : The town between the two lakes, with groundwater forest on its doorstep.
    • : The town above the Omo valley and the access point for Mago.
    • : A terraced highland town on the junction between the southern acacia and the Omo road.
  • Also covers: Aje, Arba Minch, Jinka, Konso, Mago National Park, Nechisar National Park, Senkelle Swayne's Hartebeest Sanctuary, Turmi, Weito
  • Also watch for: Burchell's zebra and Swayne's hartebeest use the Nechisar plains, and Nile crocodile and hippopotamus lie along the shore of Lake Chamo.

Gambela and the Western Lowlands

Baro river floodplain and deciduous woodland at 560 m, with a Sudanese birdlist

Gambela lies on the Baro river in the western lowlands at about 560 m, and its birds belong to the Sudan and Guinea savanna rather than to the Ethiopian highlands. Egyptian Plover feeds on the river bank and roosts on rocks inside the town itself. Black-headed Gonolek, Yellow-bellied Hyliota and Black-faced Firefinch reach the country here and barely anywhere else.

  • Habitat: Dry deciduous broadleaved woodland punctuated by rocky hillocks, and the riparian floodplain of the Baro river at about 560 m.
  • When: December to April is the dry season and the workable window. Travel outside the town is close to impossible from May to September, and the humidity is heavy even when the rain stops.
  • Getting there: Gambela is two days of driving west of the capital, and an airstrip 13 km south of the town takes internal flights. Vehicles are hard to hire locally, so most visitors send one ahead or move on foot and by bicycle. Check the security situation before travelling outside the town.

Lake Tana and the Simien Mountains

Lake shore, papyrus and a 4,000 m escarpment on the northern route

Bahir Dar sits on the southern shore of Lake Tana where the Blue Nile leaves it, and the wooded lake edge gives easy and varied birding. The Simien escarpment north of Gondar rises past 4,000 m and holds the northern highland endemics along with Walia ibex. This is the route that puts birding alongside the rock churches and the island monasteries.

  • Habitat: Swampy and open lake shore with reedbed and riparian forest at about 1,850 m around Lake Tana, and Afro-alpine grassland, giant lobelia and sheer basalt cliffs on the Simien plateau above 3,000 m.
  • When: October to March is dry and clear on both the lake and the escarpment. Trekking in the mountains is hardest through the July and August rains.
  • Getting there: Bahir Dar is the better part of a day of driving from the capital and has its own airport. Debark is the entry town for the mountains and lies north of Gondar, and the park requires a scout. Most of the escarpment is reached on foot, and the camps sit above 3,200 m.
  • Base yourself in: Bahir Dar

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Ethiopia?

November to March. It is the Palearctic winter, so the migrants sit on top of a resident list already full of endemics, and every gravel road in the south is firm. April to July has fewer birds and better flowers, and the west shuts down in the main rains. The three seasons.

How many birds live only in Ethiopia?

Sixteen species occur inside Ethiopia and nowhere else, and about thirty more are shared only with Eritrea or the Somali interior. Ruspoli's Turaco, Ethiopian Bushcrow, White-tailed Swallow, Archer's Lark and Harwood's Spurfowl are the ones that shape a route.

Can you drive yourself around Ethiopia?

Hiring a 4WD with a driver is the normal way trips run here, and it is what the site guides assume. A driver handles directions, translation and the local permissions several southern sites require, and many roads want high clearance whatever the season.

How long does the endemic circuit take?

Two weeks covers the southern loop through the Rift lakes, Bale, Negele and Yabello and reaches most of the endemics. Adding the Jemma valley, Ankober and Awash to the north takes it to three weeks and adds five more. The four routes.

Which parts of Ethiopia are worth a route?

Eleven regions, from the juniper forest above the capital to the Baro river in the far west. They follow the site list in the standard Ethiopian site guide, and the southern loop through Bale, Negele and Yabello is the one most trips take. Where to bird.

Is a permit needed for the birding sites?

The national parks require a game scout, which is arranged at the gate. Several sites near the eastern and southern borders require permission from local authorities, and asking about security before setting out is part of planning a route there.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
