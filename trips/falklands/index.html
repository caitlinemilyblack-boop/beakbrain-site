When to Go

The breeding season

October to December

The penguins come ashore, the albatrosses settle on their pedestal nests and the elephant seals pup on the southern beaches. Daylight runs to seventeen hours and the ground is at its greenest.

Go for The albatross colonies at West Point and Saunders, and the elephant seal beach on Sea Lion Island.

Trade off Chicks have yet to hatch, so the colonies are quieter than they will be in January.

Typical daily average

Chicks on the beach

January to March

Penguin chicks are out of the nest and the colonies are at their loudest. The king penguins at Volunteer Point hold brown chicks through the whole period and the camp tracks are at their firmest.

Go for Volunteer Point for the king penguins, and Sea Lion Island for all five breeding penguins in one walk.

Trade off This is the busiest quarter for beds on the outer islands and the cruise ships call most often.

Typical daily average

The quiet half

April to September

The seabirds go to sea, the days shorten to seven hours of light and the wind picks up. The resident landbirds and the harbour wildfowl carry the birding through the winter.

Go for The harbour front at Stanley and the ponds on Pebble Island, both open in any month.

Trade off Light aircraft are cancelled by weather more often, and the penguin colonies stand empty.

Where to Bird

Itineraries

7 days

East Falkland and the Kings

Mount Pleasant (MPN), Stanley 2 nights, Volunteer Point day trip, Sea Lion Island 2 nights, Bleaker Island 1 night, Stanley 1 night, Mount Pleasant (MPN) out

The short introduction to the islands. Stanley and Cape Pembroke on foot, the king penguin colony at Volunteer Point by track, then light aircraft south to Sea Lion Island for all five breeding penguins and on to Bleaker Island.

10 days

The Western Islands

Mount Pleasant (MPN), Stanley 1 night, Carcass Island 2 nights, West Point Island 1 night, Saunders Island 3 nights, Pebble Island 2 nights, Stanley 1 night, Mount Pleasant (MPN) out

Built around the albatross colonies of the north west. Light aircraft hop between the small islands, so each leg is short and the weather decides the order. Carcass adds the tussock landbirds and Pebble adds the freshwater wetland.

14 days

Both Main Islands and the Outer Camp

Mount Pleasant (MPN), Stanley 2 nights, Volunteer Point day trip, Port Howard 2 nights, Port Stephens 2 nights, Hill Cove 2 nights, Pebble Island 2 nights, Saunders Island 2 nights, Sea Lion Island 2 nights, Mount Pleasant (MPN) out

The full circuit. It takes in the farm settlements of West Falkland that most short trips skip, with Port Stephens for the wildest coast in the islands and Hill Cove for the only planted woodland. Light aircraft carry the whole route between settlements.

Stanley and Cape Pembroke

The capital on its long harbour, and the low peninsula running east of it

Stanley stands on the north shore of a sheltered harbour on East Falkland, and the shoreline inside the town holds birds within a few minutes of the waterfront. Kelp Goose, Upland Goose, Crested Duck and Falkland Steamer Duck feed along the tide line, and the peninsula that runs east to Cape Pembroke carries heath, ponds and a long sand beach.

  • Habitat: Dwarf shrub heath and white grass over peat, with a sheltered tidal harbour, rocky shoreline and a scatter of freshwater ponds on the peninsula east of the town.
  • When: All year for the harbour birds, with the settled weather and the breeding season running from October to March.
  • Getting there: Stanley lies about an hour by surfaced road from the airport at Mount Pleasant, and the harbour front is walkable from end to end. A track carries on east from the town to Cape Pembroke, which takes a morning on foot.
  • Base yourself in: Stanley
  • Look for: Kelp Goose, Upland Goose, Crested Duck, Falkland Steamer Duck, Dolphin Gull, Blackish Oystercatcher, Long-tailed Meadowlark, Black-chinned Siskin
  • Stops:
    • : The capital and the hub of every route. Geese, steamer ducks and gulls feed along the harbour front inside the town.
    • : The eastern tip of the peninsula beyond the town, with heath, a lighthouse and a long sand beach.
    • : A small bay on the north side of the peninsula where Magellanic Penguins burrow in the tussock above the sand.
  • Also covers: Cape Pembroke, Gypsy Cove, Stanley
  • Also watch for: Commerson's and Peale's dolphins come into the harbour mouth, and sei whales feed in the water offshore through the summer.

Volunteer Point and the north east

A king penguin colony on an open beach, at the end of a long track north of the capital

Volunteer Beach carries the largest King Penguin colony in the Falkland Islands, and the count has climbed from fifteen pairs in 1967 to more than five hundred. Gentoo and Magellanic Penguins breed along the same sand, a warden lives on the site through the summer, and the promontory beyond the beach runs out to Volunteer Point itself.

  • Habitat: A long sand beach backed by dunes and coastal greens, with diddle-dee heath, peat ground and shallow ponds inland of it.
  • When: November to March, when the king penguin chicks stand on the beach and the track is firm enough to cross.
  • Getting there: The site sits at the end of a stretch of open camp track north east of Stanley, and the crossing takes about two hours each way in a four wheel drive. Most visits are day trips out of the capital, and the ground turns soft after heavy rain.
  • Base yourself in: Stanley
  • Look for: King Penguin, Gentoo Penguin, Magellanic Penguin, Magellanic Oystercatcher, Rufous-chested Dotterel, Upland Goose, Ruddy-headed Goose
  • Stops:
    • : The king penguin beach at the end of the promontory, with gentoo and Magellanic colonies on the same stretch of sand.
    • : The farm settlement the track to the point passes through, on a sheltered inlet of the north coast.
    • : The oldest settlement in the islands, on a quiet arm of Berkeley Sound with waders on the mud below it.
  • Also covers: Johnson's Harbour, Port Louis, Volunteer Point
  • Also watch for: A colony of southern fur seals breeds on a stack offshore, and elephant seals haul out on the beach in spring.

Sea Lion and Bleaker islands

Two small southern islands, one of them the only place all five Falkland penguins breed

Sea Lion Island measures little more than a kilometre across at its widest and packs more wildlife into that ground than anywhere else in the archipelago. All five of the penguins that breed in the Falkland Islands nest on it, and the Striated Caracara the islanders call the Johnny Rook hangs around the settlement. Bleaker Island lies a short hop north east, with a sanctuary on its northern end and a sheep farm over the rest.

  • Habitat: Low tussock grass and heath over a flat peat island, with ponds inland, sand beaches on the north shore and a broken rocky coast facing south.
  • When: October to March. The penguins are ashore, the elephant seals pup in October and November, and the sea is at its calmest.
  • Getting there: Light aircraft out of Stanley land on the grass strips of both islands, and the colonies sit within walking distance of each settlement. Two nights on Sea Lion Island covers the ground comfortably.
  • Base yourself in: Sea Lion Island
  • Look for: Gentoo Penguin, Magellanic Penguin, Western Rockhopper Penguin, King Penguin, Macaroni Penguin, Striated Caracara, Imperial Shag, Cobb's Wren, Falkland Steamer Duck
  • Stops:
    • : The southernmost inhabited island, with penguin colonies, striated caracaras and elephant seals all within a walk of the settlement.
    • : A long thin island whose northern end is a sanctuary. Rockhopper, gentoo and Magellanic penguins breed there beside a shag colony.
  • Also covers: Bleaker Island, Sea Lion Island
  • Also watch for: Sea Lion Island holds one of the few southern elephant seal breeding beaches in the islands, and killer whales patrol the shore while the pups are in the shallows.

Carcass and West Point islands

Two small islands off the north west, with a cliff albatross colony and tussock the rodents never reached

West Point Island holds around 2,100 pairs of Black-browed Albatross and 500 pairs of Western Rockhopper Penguin nesting together in a cliff amphitheatre called Devil's Nose, two and a half kilometres on foot from the settlement. Carcass Island across the water has never carried cats, rats or mice, so its small landbirds are abundant and tame, and a gentoo rookery and large Magellanic colonies sit beside the houses.

  • Habitat: Replanted tussock grass plantations, steep cliff faces on the exposed western coasts, and sheltered sand bays with kelp beds close inshore.
  • When: October to March, while the albatrosses and rockhoppers are on their nests above the sea.
  • Getting there: Light aircraft from Stanley reach both islands, and cruise ships put passengers ashore at each of them. The walk out to the albatross colony on West Point crosses open ground and takes about half an hour.
  • Base yourself in: Carcass Island
  • Look for: Black-browed Albatross, Western Rockhopper Penguin, Gentoo Penguin, Magellanic Penguin, Cobb's Wren, Blackish Cinclodes, Black-chinned Siskin, White-bridled Finch
  • Stops:
    • : A rodent free island where the small landbirds feed in the open, with a gentoo rookery and Magellanic burrows around the settlement.
    • : Tussock plantations above a cliff coast, and the mixed albatross and rockhopper colony at the far side of the island.
  • Also covers: Carcass Island, West Point Island
  • Also watch for: Commerson's dolphins run the channel between the two islands, and southern sea lions use the rock stacks below the cliffs.

Saunders Island

A big farmed island in the north west, with seabird cliffs and a sand isthmus called The Neck

Saunders Island carries one of the mixed colonies of Black-browed Albatross and Western Rockhopper Penguin that the western islands are known for, and a small colony of King Penguin has taken hold on it as well. The narrow isthmus called The Neck puts gentoo and Magellanic colonies on one beach, and Port Egmont at the north end was the first British settlement in the islands.

  • Habitat: Grazed white grass and heath over a hilly interior, with seabird cliff ground on the north coast and a low sand isthmus joining two headlands.
  • When: November to February for the albatross and rockhopper colonies, and the king penguins hold the beach all year.
  • Getting there: Light aircraft from Stanley serve the island strip, and vehicles from the settlement carry visitors out to The Neck over rough open ground. The walking between the colonies at The Neck is level and short.
  • Base yourself in: Saunders Island
  • Look for: Black-browed Albatross, Western Rockhopper Penguin, King Penguin, Gentoo Penguin, Magellanic Penguin, Imperial Shag, Rock Shag
  • Stops:
    • : A large working sheep island whose north coast carries albatross and rockhopper colonies on the cliff ground.
    • : The ruined first British settlement at the north end of the island, with shags and steamer ducks on the water below it.
  • Also covers: Port Egmont
  • Also watch for: Southern sea lions lie up on the rocks under the cliffs, and Peale's dolphins pass close to the isthmus.

Pebble Island

Ponds, marsh and a long sand shoreline on a farmed island of wildfowl and penguins

Pebble Island runs east to west off the north coast of West Falkland and holds the most extensive freshwater wetland in the islands. Grebes, swans, coots and dabbling ducks use the ponds behind the settlement all year, and rockhopper and gentoo colonies sit on the shore at the western end. The beaches at that end give the island its name.

  • Habitat: Freshwater ponds and marsh behind a long sand shoreline, with heath and grazed grassland over the ridge that runs the length of the island.
  • When: October to March for the breeding penguins, and the ponds carry wildfowl in every month.
  • Getting there: Light aircraft from Stanley land at the settlement, and a track runs west along the island towards the colonies. The far end is a slow drive over open ground and a full day out.
  • Base yourself in: Pebble Island
  • Look for: Silvery Grebe, White-tufted Grebe, Black-necked Swan, Chiloe Wigeon, Silver Teal, Yellow-billed Teal, Magellanic Snipe, Western Rockhopper Penguin, Gentoo Penguin
  • Stops:
    • : The settlement end of the island, where the ponds behind the houses hold grebes, swans and ducks through the year.
    • : A wide sand bay on the north shore, with wader ground along the tide line and wetland behind it.
  • Also covers: Elephant Bay
  • Also watch for: Southern sea lions breed along the north shore, and Commerson's dolphins come close in along the sand.

The West Falkland settlements

Sheep stations, sheltered sounds and ocean headlands, quieter and harder to reach than the east

West Falkland is the second of the two main islands and its birding sits around a handful of farm settlements. Port Howard is the oldest farm on the island and stands under Mount Maria on a sheltered arm of Falkland Sound. The headlands at Port Stephens face the open South Atlantic, and thousands of rockhopper penguins, shags and other seabirds breed along that exposed coast.

  • Habitat: Rolling heath and white grass over stone runs, with sheltered inlets on the eastern side and high cliff coast facing the open ocean in the south west.
  • When: November to February for the rockhopper colonies, with the raptors and passerines around the settlements all year.
  • Getting there: Falkland Sound separates the two main islands, and light aircraft link the settlements on both sides with Stanley. A vehicle ferry crosses the sound, and the tracks on the western island are slow going in any weather.
  • Base yourself in: Port Howard
  • Look for: Western Rockhopper Penguin, Imperial Shag, Variable Hawk, Cinereous Harrier, Turkey Vulture, Crested Caracara, Upland Goose, Long-tailed Meadowlark
  • Stops:
    • : The oldest farm on the western island, under Mount Maria on a sheltered arm of Falkland Sound.
    • : Rugged headlands in the far south west where rockhopper penguins, shags and other seabirds breed on the exposed coast.
    • : A settlement on the north coast with the only planted woodland in the islands, which draws the small landbirds.
  • Also covers: Hill Cove, Port Howard, Port Stephens
  • Also watch for: Sei whales have become common in the water on both sides of the islands, and southern sea lions use the ledges below the Port Stephens cliffs.

Preparation

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in the Falkland Islands?

October to March. The penguins come ashore and the albatrosses settle on their pedestal nests in October, and the chicks fill the beaches from December to February. The rest of the year belongs to the resident landbirds and the harbour wildfowl, because the seabird colonies stand empty, daylight drops to about seven hours and the weather cancels the light aircraft far more often.

How do you get to the Falkland Islands?

Every international flight lands at Mount Pleasant on East Falkland. The route from southern Chile takes under two hours in the air, and the long service from England crosses the Atlantic with a refuelling stop at Ascension Island and runs to about eighteen hours. Stanley sits an hour east of the airport on a surfaced road. Expedition ships also call here on their way to South Georgia and Antarctica.

How do you travel between the islands?

Light aircraft carry almost every leg, because the road network covers little ground beyond Stanley and the two main islands. Seats are booked by date, and the order of the next day's flying is settled the evening before. Wind and low cloud cancel legs with little warning, so any route that finishes on an outer island needs a spare day at the end. A hire vehicle covers the surfaced roads and the camp tracks on East and West Falkland.

How long do you need in the Falkland Islands?

A week covers East Falkland, which means Stanley and Cape Pembroke on foot, a day out to the king penguins at Volunteer Point and two or three nights on Sea Lion and Bleaker. Ten days reaches the western islands and the albatross colonies at West Point and Saunders. Two weeks takes in the West Falkland settlements that shorter routes skip. Add a travel day at each end and one spare day for weather to whichever length you pick.

Can you visit the Falkland Islands independently, or does it have to be a cruise?

The islands work as a trip of their own. Flights land at Mount Pleasant, light aircraft link the settlements, and the island lodges and farms take bookings direct. A cruise call is the other way in, and it buys half a day ashore at one or two places, which suits a first look and leaves the outer islands for a longer visit.

Where do you stay outside Stanley?

The camp settlements and the outer islands take guests. Port Howard, Hill Cove and Port Stephens on West Falkland, and Carcass, Saunders, Pebble, Bleaker and Sea Lion offshore, all put visitors up in farmhouses and small lodges within a walk of the birds. Each place holds a handful of rooms, so the beds for January and February go a long way ahead.

What are the birds to plan the trip around?

Five penguins breed in the islands and Sea Lion Island holds all five within a walk of the settlement: King Penguin, Gentoo Penguin, Magellanic Penguin, Western Rockhopper Penguin and Macaroni Penguin. The king colony at Volunteer Point has grown from fifteen pairs in 1967 to more than five hundred. Black-browed Albatross fills the cliff colonies at West Point and Saunders. Cobb's Wren and the Falkland Steamer Duck live here and nowhere else.

How close do you get to the colonies?

Close enough to sit down beside one. The albatross colony at West Point lies half an hour on foot from the settlement, and rockhoppers nest between the peat pedestals of the albatross nests. A warden lives at Volunteer Point through the summer, and every site asks visitors to keep to the marked ground and leave the penguins a clear run to the beach. Birds that grew up without ground predators carry on with the day while you watch.

What does the weather do to a plan here?

Wind is the constant and it blows hard in every month. It cancels flights between the islands, it soaks an open beach and it turns the peat tracks soft once rain has been through. Summer temperatures sit around nine degrees at their best, so waterproofs and windproof layers travel in any month. Leave slack in the route, because a week here usually gives up a day to the weather.

How many bird species does the Falkland Islands have?

Around 170 species are on the list for the islands, and most of that length comes from the seabirds offshore and from the South American landbirds that blow east across the water each year. The set that breeds ashore is much shorter, and it is what a visit is built on: the five penguins, the albatross colonies, the shags, the geese and steamer ducks of the harbours, and a handful of open camp landbirds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
