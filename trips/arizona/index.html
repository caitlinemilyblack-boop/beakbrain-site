Birding in Arizona

Arizona holds more Mexican birds than any other state, because a chain of forested mountains stands out of the Sonoran Desert close enough to the border for them to spill north.

Birds that decide the trip

Elegant Trogon in the sky island canyons

The sky islands are the only place in the United States where Elegant Trogon breeds, and Madera Canyon is the classic site. The bird calls like a dog barking and is easier heard than seen in the sycamores.

When Late April to September, while the birds are in the sycamore canyons.

Where Madera Canyon, Chiricahua Mountains

Mexican Chickadee, only in the Chiricahuas

In the United States this bird occurs in the Chiricahuas and one other range and nowhere else. It works the high conifers above Cave Creek, well up the mountain from where most visitors stop.

When All year, in the high conifer forest above the canyon.

Where Chiricahua Mountains

Fifteen hummingbirds at one feeder

The canyon feeders in the Huachucas and Santa Ritas can hold more hummingbird species in a morning than most countries have. The monsoon weeks are the peak, when the desert flowers and the birds come up to the canyons.

When July to September, at the canyon feeders during the monsoon.

Where Ramsey Canyon, Madera Canyon

Ferruginous Pygmy Owl in the saguaro

The Arizona population is small and protected, holding on in saguaro desert and mesquite in the south of the state. It hunts by day, which is the one thing that makes it findable.

When All year, and calling best in spring at first light.

Where Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge

Botteri's Sparrow over the border grassland

A sparrow that is silent and invisible until it sings, and then obvious. It arrives with the monsoon rains in the grasslands around the San Pedro and sings from the tallest stem it can find.

When July to September, once the rains start and the birds sing.

Where Las Cienegas National Conservation Area, San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area

Elf Owl in a woodpecker hole

The smallest owl in the world, about the weight of a golf ball, nesting in old woodpecker holes in saguaro and sycamore. It leaves the hole at dusk and the whole thing is over in a few seconds.

When April to August, at dusk at a known nest hole.

Where Madera Canyon, Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument

Painted Whitestart in the oaks

A warbler that fans a black and white tail and flashes a scarlet belly, common in the canyon oaks all summer. It is the bird that makes a first sky island morning feel like a different country.

When April to September, in oak and pine-oak canyons.

Where Madera Canyon, Chiricahua Mountains

Grey Hawk on the San Pedro

A small tropical hawk that follows cottonwood galleries north into Arizona and hunts lizards along the river. The San Pedro holds one of the highest densities anywhere in the country.

When April to September, along the cottonwood gallery.

Where San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area

When to Go

Arizona has two seasons that matter. April to May brings the breeding birds into the canyons, and the summer monsoon from July flushes the desert green and puts the hummingbirds at their peak. The lowlands are dangerous hot from June to September.

April to May

The Mexican breeders arrive in the sky island canyons and the desert is still comfortable.

Go for Madera and Ramsey for trogon and hummingbirds, the San Pedro cottonwoods, and the desert before the heat.

Trade off The monsoon hummingbird peak is still months away.

The Santa Rita and Huachuca sky islands

Fifteen hummingbird species in one canyon, on mountains standing out of desert

The sky islands are forested ranges rising out of Sonoran Desert, close enough to Mexico that Mexican birds spill north into them and isolated enough that each holds its own list. Madera and Ramsey are the two everyone comes for, and the feeders in both run all summer.

  • Habitat: Sycamore and oak canyon bottoms rising through pine-oak woodland to conifer, standing out of Sonoran Desert scrub on the lower slopes.
  • When: Late April to September. The Mexican breeders arrive in April and the hummingbird numbers peak with the summer monsoon from July, when afternoon storms green the canyons.
  • Getting there: Tucson is the airport. Madera Canyon is fifty minutes south of it on a paved road, and Ramsey Canyon sits above Sierra Vista with a Nature Conservancy preserve and limited parking that fills early.
  • Base yourself in: Green Valley
  • Look for: Elegant Trogon, Rivoli's Hummingbird, Blue-throated Mountaingem, Broad-billed Hummingbird, Violet-crowned Hummingbird, Painted Whitestart, Mexican Jay, Arizona Woodpecker, Greater Pewee, Montezuma Quail, Whiskered Screech Owl, Elf Owl
  • Parks and reserves: Tucson Mountain Park (Protected area), Saguaro National Park - Tucson Mountain District, La Estancia de Tucson (Protected area), Ramsey Canyon Preserve, Tucson Mountain Village (Protected area)
  • Also covers: La Estancia de Tucson, Madera Canyon, Ramsey Canyon Preserve, Saguaro National Park - Tucson Mountain District, Sierra Vista, Tucson Mountain Park, Tucson Mountain Village
  • Also watch for: Coatimundi cross the canyon trails in bands and black bear come down to the fruiting oaks.

The Chiricahuas and Cave Creek

The range that holds the most Mexican birds of any in the country

The Chiricahuas are the largest and least visited of the sky islands, and Cave Creek Canyon on their eastern side is the single most productive canyon in the United States for birds that otherwise belong to Mexico. Portal sits at its mouth.

  • Habitat: A deep canyon of sycamore and cypress below rhyolite cliffs, rising through pine-oak to spruce and fir on the highest ground.
  • When: April to September for the Mexican breeders, with the monsoon weeks from July the busiest. The high conifer is reachable only once the road is open.
  • Getting there: Portal is the base and sits at the canyon mouth. It is four hours from Tucson round the south of the range, or a longer unpaved crossing over the top, and there are no shops nearer than Rodeo.
  • Base yourself in: Portal
  • Look for: Elegant Trogon, Mexican Chickadee, Painted Whitestart, Red-faced Warbler, Mexican Jay, Arizona Woodpecker, Whiskered Screech Owl, Elf Owl, Northern Pygmy Owl, Greater Pewee, Hepatic Tanager, Blue-throated Mountaingem
  • Parks and reserves: Chiricahua National Monument, Coronado National Forest (Forest or grassland), Chiricahua Wilderness (Protected area), Manhattan Claims (Protected area), May Memorial Wildlife Area (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Chiricahua Mountains, Chiricahua National Monument, Chiricahua Wilderness, Coronado National Forest, Manhattan Claims, May Memorial Wildlife Area, Portal
  • Also watch for: The Chiricahua leopard frog survives in the canyon pools and javelina work the lower washes.

The San Pedro and the border grasslands

A cottonwood river through desert, and grassland the sparrows sing over

The San Pedro is one of the last undammed desert rivers in the south-west and its cottonwood gallery carries an extraordinary density of breeding and migrant birds. The grasslands either side of it hold the sparrows and the last masked bobwhite ground.

  • Habitat: Cottonwood and willow gallery along an undammed desert river, with mesquite bosque, sacaton grassland and semi-desert scrub either side.
  • When: April to September. The riparian breeders are in from April and the grassland sparrows only start singing once the monsoon rains arrive in July.
  • Getting there: Sierra Vista is the base. The San Pedro conservation area is entered from Highway 90 at the San Pedro House, Las Cienegas lies north near Sonoita, and Buenos Aires is west of Nogales near Sasabe.
  • Base yourself in: Sierra Vista
  • Look for: Botteri's Sparrow, Cassin's Sparrow, Grasshopper Sparrow, Northern Bobwhite, Grey Hawk, Common Black Hawk, Yellow-billed Cuckoo, Bell's Vireo, Lucy's Warbler, Vermilion Flycatcher, Scaled Quail, Chihuahuan Raven
  • Parks and reserves: San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area (Protected area), Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge, Las Cienegas National Conservation Area (Protected area), Coronado National Forest (Forest or grassland)
  • Also covers: Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge, Coronado National Forest, Las Cienegas National Conservation Area, San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area
  • Also watch for: Pronghorn range the Las Cienegas grassland and beaver have returned to the San Pedro.

The lower Colorado River

Cottonwood and willow on a river that is otherwise all dam and canal

Almost all of the lower Colorado has been straightened, dammed or drained, and the refuges along it hold what is left of the cottonwood and willow gallery. Bill Williams is the best surviving piece and it is loud at dawn.

  • Habitat: Cottonwood and willow gallery, dense marsh and backwater lagoons along the river, with creosote desert immediately behind the green strip.
  • When: November to March. The refuges are dangerously hot from June to September, and the wintering waterbirds are in from late autumn.
  • Getting there: Lake Havasu City is the base for the northern refuges and Yuma for the southern. All four are reached from Highway 95, and Bill Williams has a paved pull-in above the delta.
  • Base yourself in: Lake Havasu City
  • Look for: Ridgway's Rail, Black Rail, Willow Flycatcher, Bell's Vireo, Elf Owl, Gilded Flicker, Abert's Towhee, Lucy's Warbler, Vermilion Flycatcher, Gambel's Quail, Verdin, Phainopepla
  • Parks and reserves: Havasu National Wildlife Refuge, Imperial National Wildlife Refuge, Cibola National Wildlife Refuge, Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge, Warm Springs Wilderness (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge, Cibola National Wildlife Refuge, Havasu National Wildlife Refuge, Imperial National Wildlife Refuge, Warm Springs Wilderness
  • Also watch for: Desert bighorn come down to the river to drink and the backwaters hold razorback sucker.

The western Sonoran Desert

Saguaro forest, and the emptiest country in the state

The far south-west is the hottest and driest corner of Arizona and holds the saguaro desert at its purest. Organ Pipe is the accessible half; Cabeza Prieta behind it is a wilderness that needs a permit and a serious vehicle.

  • Habitat: Saguaro and organ pipe cactus forest on rocky bajadas, with creosote flats, dry washes lined with palo verde and ironwood, and isolated palm-lined springs.
  • When: November to March for comfortable daytime birding. The desert is dangerous in summer heat, and the birds are active only at first light.
  • Getting there: Ajo is the base. Organ Pipe is entered from Highway 85 with a paved scenic loop, and Cabeza Prieta behind it needs a permit and a signed hold-harmless agreement because it adjoins a bombing range, plus a high-clearance vehicle.
  • Base yourself in: Ajo
  • Look for: Gilded Flicker, Ferruginous Pygmy Owl, LeConte's Thrasher, Bendire's Thrasher, Crissal Thrasher, Elf Owl, Gambel's Quail, Verdin, Phainopepla, Greater Roadrunner, Black-throated Sparrow, Costa's Hummingbird
  • Parks and reserves: Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge, Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, Cabeza Prieta Wilderness (Protected area), Organ Pipe Cactus Wilderness (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge, Cabeza Prieta Wilderness, Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, Organ Pipe Cactus Wilderness
  • Also watch for: Desert bighorn and Sonoran pronghorn both hold Cabeza Prieta, and the organ pipe cactus reaches its northern limit here.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Arizona?

April and May for the canyon breeders, or July to September for the monsoon, when afternoon storms green the desert and the hummingbird numbers in the sky island canyons reach their height. The lower Colorado refuges are a winter trip: they are dangerously hot in summer.

Which birds can I see in Arizona and nowhere else in the country?

Elegant Trogon breeds in the sky islands and nowhere else in the United States. Mexican Chickadee occurs in the Chiricahuas and one other range. Arizona Woodpecker, Whiskered Screech Owl, Violet-crowned Hummingbird, Rivoli’s Hummingbird and Painted Whitestart are all effectively Arizonan too.

Madera, Ramsey or Cave Creek?

All three if you can. Madera is the easiest and closest to Tucson, Ramsey has the hummingbird feeders, and Cave Creek in the Chiricahuas holds the most Mexican birds of any canyon in the country. Cave Creek is four hours from Tucson, which is why it needs its own nights.

How hot does it actually get?

The lowlands and the Colorado river refuges regularly pass 45°C from June to September, which is a safety question rather than a comfort one. The canyons are several thousand feet higher and stay workable, which is why the summer birding is all in the mountains.

Do I need a permit for anything?

Cabeza Prieta needs a permit and a signed hold-harmless agreement because it adjoins a bombing range, and it wants a high-clearance vehicle. Every other refuge, canyon and monument in Arizona named here is open access.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
