Getting in
Kochi (COK) for Thattekad and Munnar, Bengaluru (BLR) or Mysuru for Kabini, Goa (GOI) for the Konkan, and Port Blair (IXZ) by flight from Chennai or Kolkata
Getting around
A car and driver on the mainland, and a flight to the Andamans with local taxis once there

Birds that decide the trip

Sri Lanka Frogmouth at Thattekad

A frogmouth of Western Ghats and Sri Lankan forest. It roosts in dense thicket by day and you find it only because a guide knows the tree. Thattekad is where almost everybody sees it.

When All year, at a known day roost.

Where Thattekad

Nilgiri Pipit at Eravikulam

A pipit of high shola grassland above 1,800 m, confined to the southern Western Ghats, and one of very few Indian birds that lives entirely on montane grass. Eravikulam is where it is looked for.

When All year, most vocal in the dry.

Where Munnar

Malabar Trogon in the Ghats forest

A crimson and slate trogon of Western Ghats evergreen forest. It sits motionless in the mid-storey until it drops for an insect. Thattekad and the Goan Ghats camps both hold it.

When All year.

Where Thattekad, Backwoods

Andaman Crake near Port Blair

A crake that lives on these islands and nowhere else. It walks damp forest edges at dawn. It is one of about twenty Andaman endemics and the reason the archipelago has its own itineraries.

When All year, easiest in the dry from December.

Where Port Blair

Nilgiri Flycatcher in the shola

An indigo flycatcher of southern Western Ghats shola forest, in the wooded folds between the grassland tops. Munnar's estate ridges hold it in every patch of remaining shola.

When All year.

Where Munnar

Malabar Pied Hornbill over the Kabini

A large black and white hornbill of riverine and deciduous forest. It crosses the Kabini backwaters in the early morning, and the boat safaris are the way to watch it.

When All year, and it nests from January to April.

Where Kabini

Indian Pitta in the Goan Ghats

A pitta in nine colours that winters in the Western Ghats forests and calls at dawn and dusk from the leaf litter. The Backwoods camp at Tambdi Surla is where most visitors meet their first.

When October to April, when it winters here.

Where Backwoods

Palani Laughingthrush above Munnar

A laughingthrush of the high southern shola, split from the Nilgiri birds, and confined to the hills above 1,500 m in Kerala and Tamil Nadu. It comes to the Eravikulam paths in parties.

When All year.

Where Munnar

When to Go

The mainland south runs on the southwest monsoon, which arrives in Kerala at the start of June and is the reason the Western Ghats exist as a rainforest. The birding season is November to March: dry, warm and when the forest is workable. The Andamans are drier from December to April and wettest in September, which is a different calendar again.

November to March

The dry season, and there is no argument about it here.

Go for Panaji records 0 mm in January and Kabini 3 mm, the Ghats forests are workable, the reserves are open and the Andamans are dry as well.

Trade off It is high season for tourism generally in Kerala and Goa, so the accommodation is priced accordingly.

Thattekad and the Kerala lowlands

A small lowland reserve on the Periyar in Kerala, with an extraordinary list for its size

Salim Ali called Thattekad the richest bird habitat in peninsular India and the twenty-five square kilometres of it still deliver. The guiding is done by a handful of local men who know individual roosts, which is how Sri Lanka Frogmouth and Oriental Bay Owl are found here reliably. Malabar Trogon and Malabar Grey Hornbill are on the same morning.

  • Habitat: Evergreen and semi-evergreen lowland forest on the north bank of the Periyar, with rubber and cardamom around it, holding over 300 species in a small area Salim Ali called the richest bird habitat in peninsular India.
  • When: Good all year. Sri Lanka Frogmouth, Malabar Trogon and Malabar Grey Hornbill are here in every month. Indian Pitta is away from May to September.
  • Getting there: About 71 km from Kochi airport and 48 km from the railway at Aluva, by bus or taxi to Kothamangalam and on to the sanctuary.
  • Look for: Sri Lanka Frogmouth, Malabar Trogon, Malabar Grey Hornbill, Heart-spotted Woodpecker, Malabar Barbet, Indian Pitta, Flame-throated Bulbul, Oriental Bay Owl
  • Parks and reserves: Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary, Thattekad
  • Stops:
    • : The sanctuary on the Periyar, an hour and a half from Kochi. Homestays sit at the gate.
  • Also covers: Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary, Thattekad
  • Also watch for: Indian Giant Squirrel and Nilgiri Langur are both in the reserve, and the Periyar carries Smooth-coated Otter. Thattekad is small enough that the wildlife is incidental to the birds, which is unusual in India.

Munnar, Eravikulam and the high Western Ghats

Tea estates and shola grassland above 1,600 m, holding the endemics of the southern hills

Above Munnar the tea gives way to shola forest in the folds and grassland on the summit ridges, and that mosaic holds the southern Western Ghats endemics. Nilgiri Pipit is on the grassland at Eravikulam, Palani Laughingthrush and Nilgiri Flycatcher in the shola patches, and Nilgiri Wood Pigeon crosses between them.

  • Habitat: Shrubby grassland runs alongside the road up to Rajamalai and carries Nilgiri Tahr with the birds. Roadside scrub 57 km east of Munnar holds the localised Yellow-throated Bulbul.
  • When: Good all year. Nilgiri Pipit, Palani Laughingthrush and Nilgiri Flycatcher stay through the year.
  • Getting there: The road running up to Rajamalai from Munnar gives the birding, and Nilgiri Tahr appear on the same slopes. Roadside scrub 57 km east of Munnar and 13 km west of Bodinayakanur is worth a stop. Periyar lies five hours by road to the south, and its entrance is 2 km outside the small town of Kumilly.
  • Look for: Nilgiri Pipit, Palani Laughingthrush, Nilgiri Flycatcher, Nilgiri Wood Pigeon, Himalayan Shortwing, Black-and-orange Flycatcher, Painted Bush Quail, Malabar Whistling Thrush
  • Parks and reserves: Eravikulam National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The tea town at 1,600 m. Eravikulam is ten kilometres north and shola patches cover every ridge.
  • Also covers: Eravikulam National Park, Munnar
  • Also watch for: Eravikulam holds the largest population of Nilgiri Tahr, a mountain goat down to a couple of thousand animals and habituated enough at the park to walk past visitors. Nilgiri Langur is in the shola, pockets of mountain forest set in grassland, and Indian Elephant crosses the estates.

Nagarhole, Bandipur and the Karnataka forests

Moist deciduous forest and teak in Karnataka on the Kerala and Tamil Nadu borders, and the best big-mammal country in the south

The Nagarhole and Bandipur complex is one continuous forest across three states and it holds the highest elephant density in Asia. The birding is deciduous forest and lake edge: Malabar Pied Hornbill over the Kabini backwaters, Blue-faced Malkoha in the teak, and the raptors over the open grass at Bandipur.

  • Habitat: Dry deciduous forest, evergreen gallery forest and stands of bamboo run through the Nilgiri parks. Five endemics and twelve species of woodpecker live here alongside elephant, dhole, gaur and leopard.
  • When: Nagarhole and Bandipur are worth visiting throughout the year.
  • Getting there: Nagarhole and Bandipur lie 80 km west of Mysore, and Mudumalai is 90 km south of the same city. The bamboo along the river beside the Mysore to Gudalur road is where Grey-headed Bulbul shows, and elephants use it too.
  • Look for: Malabar Pied Hornbill, Blue-faced Malkoha, Malabar Flameback, Indian Scimitar Babbler, White-bellied Woodpecker, Grey Junglefowl, Yellow-throated Bulbul, Brown Fish Owl
  • Parks and reserves: Nagarhole National Park, Bandipur National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The reservoir at the Nagarhole gate, with the lodges on its southern shore.
  • Also covers: Bandipur National Park, Kabini, Nagarhole National Park
  • Also watch for: The Kabini backwaters draw hundreds of elephants in the dry season, which is the largest gathering in Asia. Tiger, Leopard, Dhole and Gaur are all here, and the black leopard photographed at Kabini made the reserve internationally famous.

Goa and the Konkan coast

Paddy, plateau and the northern Western Ghats an hour inland, and the easiest introduction to Indian birding

Goa is where a large share of British birders met India first, and the reason is that a beach hotel, a paddy field and a Western Ghats forest camp are within ninety minutes of each other. The Carambolim lake and the Zuari mangroves hold the waterbirds, and the Backwoods camp at Tambdi Surla puts you in the Ghats forest with Malabar Trogon and Indian Pitta behind the tents.

  • Habitat: Paddies, marshes, mangroves, mud flats, rocky scrub and remnant lowland forest line the coast of Goa, and the foothill forests of the Western Ghats rise an hour or so inland.
  • When: Good all year. Malabar Pied Hornbill, Indian Pitta and Blue-eared Kingfisher stay through the year. Black-capped Kingfisher is away from May to September.
  • Getting there: The best coastal bases are Bambolim, which holds remnant lowland forest behind the shore, and Baga, which has a good spread of habitat close to the beach. The Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary on Chorao Island is reached by ferry from Ribander, and Carambolim Lake lies 2 km east of Old Goa. In the Ghats, Bondla sanctuary is 10 km east of Ponda and Molem sanctuary 28 km east, where the track to Dudhsagar Falls is the best of the walking.
  • Look for: Malabar Pied Hornbill, Indian Pitta, Blue-eared Kingfisher, Malabar Grey Hornbill, White-bellied Blue Flycatcher, Vigors's Sunbird, Nilgiri Wood Pigeon, Black-capped Kingfisher
  • Parks and reserves: Bhagwan Mahavir Wildlife Sanctuary
  • Stops:
    • : The camp in the Ghats forest at the Karnataka border, ninety minutes inland from the coast.
    • : The state capital. Carambolim lake and the Zuari mangroves are within half an hour of it.
  • Also covers: Backwoods, Bhagwan Mahavir Wildlife Sanctuary, Panaji
  • Also watch for: The Zuari mangroves hold Smooth-coated Otter and Mugger, and the Ghats forest above Tambdi Surla carries Indian Giant Squirrel and Gaur. Olive Ridley turtles nest on the Morjim and Galgibaga beaches from November.

The Andaman Islands

An archipelago closer to Myanmar than to India, with a bird list that shares almost nothing with the mainland

The Andamans sit twelve hundred kilometres east of the mainland, and their birds came from Southeast Asia rather than from India. Around twenty species live on these islands and nowhere else, Andaman Crake, Andaman Woodpecker and Andaman Scops Owl among them. A few sites around Port Blair cover the whole set in four or five days.

  • Habitat: Remnant lowland forest covers South Andaman Island, which rises to 800 m. The islands lie far to the east of the Indian coast and much closer to Burma, and their birds belong to southeast Asia.
  • When: Good all year. Andaman Crake, Andaman Woodpecker and Andaman Scops Owl stay through the year.
  • Getting there: South Andaman is the main island open to visiting birders, and Port Blair is reached by air from the Indian mainland. Entry rules for the islands change, so check them when you book. From Port Blair the sites are reached by local transport, taxi or hired scooter. Mount Harriet National Park is the best of them and is reached by boat across the harbour, and a narrow strip of forest at Chiriya Tapu lies 30 km south of the town.
  • Look for: Andaman Crake, Andaman Woodpecker, Andaman Scops Owl, Andaman Serpent Eagle, Andaman Cuckoo-Dove, Andaman Treepie, Andaman Drongo, Narcondam Hornbill
  • Parks and reserves: Mount Harriet National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The capital and airport. Chidiya Tapu, Mount Harriet and Wandoor are all within an hour.
  • Also covers: Mount Harriet National Park, Port Blair
  • Also watch for: The reefs at Wandoor and Havelock are the reason most visitors come and they are excellent. Andaman Wild Pig is endemic to the islands, and Saltwater Crocodile uses the mangrove creeks, which shapes where anybody swims.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to bird southern India?

November to March. Panaji records 0 mm of rain in January and Kabini 3 mm, the Western Ghats forests are workable, the reserves are open and the Andamans are in their dry season at the same time. The southwest monsoon closes the mainland from June. The three seasons.

How sharp is the monsoon here?

Sharp enough to reverse a month. Panaji takes 945 mm in July and 0 mm in January. Forty kilometres separates Thattekad on the windward side of the Ghats, at 3,051 mm a year, from the rain shadow that gives Kabini 1,094 mm, and that gradient is what makes the Western Ghats endemics endemic. When to go, season by season.

What are the Western Ghats endemics?

Around twenty species live along this mountain chain and nowhere else, among them Nilgiri Pipit, Nilgiri Flycatcher, Malabar Grey Hornbill, White-bellied Blue Flycatcher and the Kerala and Palani laughingthrushes. Most are above 1,000 m, which is why a southern trip has to climb. Munnar.

Why is Thattekad so highly rated?

Salim Ali called it the richest bird habitat in peninsular India and twenty-five square kilometres of it still delivers. What makes it work is the guiding: a handful of local men know individual frogmouth and bay owl roosts, and booking one of them is the difference between a good morning and a frustrating one. Thattekad.

Are the Andamans worth the flight?

If you want the endemics, yes, and there is no other way to get them. About twenty species live on those islands and nowhere else, and their nearest relatives are in Myanmar rather than India. Four or five days around Port Blair covers most of the set, and the reefs are a genuine bonus. The Andaman Islands.

Is Goa still a good introduction?

Yes, and for the same reason it always was: a beach hotel, a paddy field and a Western Ghats forest camp are within ninety minutes of each other. It is the softest possible landing in Indian birding and the list from a week there is far longer than people expect. Goa and the Konkan coast.

Do the places to stay in the Western Ghats and the south hold sustainability certification?

Almost none. India has no national scheme and the international ones reach the city hotel chains. The Kabini lodges and the Munnar tea bungalows are among the best wildlife accommodation in India and none of them appears in a register. Certification.

How many bird species does India have?

Around 1,350 recorded, and about eighty live nowhere else, most of them in the Western Ghats or on the Andamans. India is four guides here because those two lists share almost nothing with the Himalaya or the plains. The birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
