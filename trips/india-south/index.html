At a glance

Best months
November to March across the whole page, with January the driest and the Andamans dry at the same time
Regions
Thattekad and the Kerala lowlandsMunnar, Eravikulam and the high Western GhatsNagarhole, Bandipur and the Karnataka forestsGoa and the Konkan coastThe Andaman Islands
Getting in
Kochi (COK) for Thattekad and Munnar, Bengaluru (BLR) or Mysuru for Kabini, Goa (GOI) for the Konkan, and Port Blair (IXZ) by flight from Chennai or Kolkata
Getting around
A car and driver on the mainland, and a flight to the Andamans with local taxis once there

When to Go

The mainland south runs on the southwest monsoon, which arrives in Kerala at the start of June and is the reason the Western Ghats exist as a rainforest. The birding season is November to March: dry, warm and when the forest is workable. The Andamans are drier from December to April and wettest in September, which is a different calendar again.

Peak

November to March

The dry season, and there is no argument about it here.

Go for Panaji records 0 mm in January and Kabini 3 mm, the Ghats forests are workable, the reserves are open and the Andamans are dry as well.

Trade off It is high season for tourism generally in Kerala and Goa, so the accommodation is priced accordingly.

Elephants

March to May

The hot months, when the Kabini backwaters draw the largest elephant gathering in Asia.

Go for Hundreds of elephants come to the shrinking reservoir, which is the single most spectacular wildlife event in southern India.

Trade off Thattekad reaches 32 C, the pre-monsoon storms start in the Ghats, and the forest birding falls off as the heat builds.

Typical daily average Kabini 21 to 34 °C / 70 to 93 °F, Thattekad 24 to 33 °C / 75 to 91 °F, Munnar 15 to 26 °C / 59 to 79 °F

Monsoon

June to September

The southwest monsoon, which is why the Western Ghats are a rainforest.

Go for Very little for a visitor. The Ghats are at their most beautiful and the endemics are breeding, and almost nobody comes.

Trade off Panaji takes 945 mm in July, the reserves restrict access, and the Andamans are wet through September as well.

Typical daily average Thattekad 23 to 29 °C / 73 to 84 °F, Panaji 24 to 29 °C / 75 to 84 °F, Kabini 20 to 28 °C / 68 to 82 °F

Thattekad and the Kerala lowlands

A small lowland reserve on the Periyar in Kerala, with an extraordinary list for its size

Salim Ali called Thattekad the richest bird habitat in peninsular India and the twenty-five square kilometres of it still deliver. The guiding is done by a handful of local men who know individual roosts, which is how Sri Lanka Frogmouth and Oriental Bay Owl are found here reliably. Malabar Trogon and Malabar Grey Hornbill are on the same morning.

  • Look for: Sri Lanka Frogmouth, Malabar Trogon, Malabar Grey Hornbill, Heart-spotted Woodpecker, Malabar Barbet, Indian Pitta, Flame-throated Bulbul, Oriental Bay Owl
  • Stops:
    • , the sanctuary on the Periyar, an hour and a half from Kochi, with homestays at the gate
  • Also watch for: Indian Giant Squirrel and Nilgiri Langur are both in the reserve, and the Periyar carries Smooth-coated Otter. Thattekad is small enough that the wildlife is incidental to the birds, which is unusual in India.

Munnar, Eravikulam and the high Western Ghats

Tea estates and shola grassland above 1,600 m, holding the endemics of the southern hills

Above Munnar the tea gives way to shola forest in the folds and grassland on the summit ridges, and that mosaic holds the southern Western Ghats endemics. Nilgiri Pipit is on the grassland at Eravikulam, Palani Laughingthrush and Nilgiri Flycatcher in the shola patches, and Nilgiri Wood Pigeon crosses between them.

  • Look for: Nilgiri Pipit, Palani Laughingthrush, Nilgiri Flycatcher, Nilgiri Wood Pigeon, Himalayan Shortwing, Black-and-orange Flycatcher, Painted Bush Quail, Malabar Whistling Thrush
  • Stops:
    • , the tea town at 1,600 m, with Eravikulam ten kilometres north and the shola patches on every ridge
  • Also watch for: Eravikulam holds the largest population of Nilgiri Tahr, a mountain goat down to a couple of thousand animals and habituated enough at the park to walk past visitors. Nilgiri Langur is in the shola, pockets of mountain forest set in grassland, and Indian Elephant crosses the estates.

Nagarhole, Bandipur and the Karnataka forests

Moist deciduous forest and teak in Karnataka on the Kerala and Tamil Nadu borders, and the best big-mammal country in the south

The Nagarhole and Bandipur complex is one continuous forest across three states and it holds the highest elephant density in Asia. The birding is deciduous forest and lake edge: Malabar Pied Hornbill over the Kabini backwaters, Blue-faced Malkoha in the teak, and the raptors over the open grass at Bandipur.

  • Look for: Malabar Pied Hornbill, Blue-faced Malkoha, Malabar Flameback, Indian Scimitar Babbler, White-bellied Woodpecker, Grey Junglefowl, Yellow-throated Bulbul, Brown Fish Owl
  • Stops:
    • , the reservoir and the lodges on its southern shore, at the Nagarhole gate
  • Also watch for: The Kabini backwaters draw hundreds of elephants in the dry season, which is the largest gathering in Asia. Tiger, Leopard, Dhole and Gaur are all here, and the black leopard photographed at Kabini made the reserve internationally famous.

Goa and the Konkan coast

Paddy, plateau and the northern Western Ghats an hour inland, and the easiest introduction to Indian birding

Goa is where a large share of British birders met India first, and the reason is that a beach hotel, a paddy field and a Western Ghats forest camp are within ninety minutes of each other. The Carambolim lake and the Zuari mangroves hold the waterbirds, and the Backwoods camp at Tambdi Surla puts you in the Ghats forest with Malabar Trogon and Indian Pitta behind the tents.

  • Look for: Malabar Pied Hornbill, Indian Pitta, Blue-eared Kingfisher, Malabar Grey Hornbill, White-bellied Blue Flycatcher, Vigors's Sunbird, Nilgiri Wood Pigeon, Black-capped Kingfisher
  • Stops:

    • , the state capital, with Carambolim lake and the Zuari mangroves within half an hour of it
  • Also watch for: The Zuari mangroves hold Smooth-coated Otter and Mugger, and the Ghats forest above Tambdi Surla carries Indian Giant Squirrel and Gaur. Olive Ridley turtles nest on the Morjim and Galgibaga beaches from November.

The Andaman Islands

An archipelago closer to Myanmar than to India, with a bird list that shares almost nothing with the mainland

The Andamans sit twelve hundred kilometres east of the mainland and their birds came from Southeast Asia rather than from India. Around twenty species live on these islands and nowhere else, including Andaman Crake, Andaman Woodpecker and Andaman Scops Owl, and the whole set is watched from a few sites around Port Blair in four or five days.

  • Look for: Andaman Crake, Andaman Woodpecker, Andaman Scops Owl, Andaman Serpent Eagle, Andaman Cuckoo-Dove, Andaman Treepie, Andaman Drongo, Narcondam Hornbill
  • Stops:
    • , the capital and airport, with Chidiya Tapu, Mount Harriet and Wandoor all within an hour
  • Also watch for: The reefs at Wandoor and Havelock are the reason most visitors come and they are excellent. Andaman Wild Pig is endemic to the islands, and Saltwater Crocodile uses the mangrove creeks, which shapes where anybody swims.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to bird southern India?

November to March. Panaji records 0 mm of rain in January and Kabini 3 mm, the Western Ghats forests are workable, the reserves are open and the Andamans are in their dry season at the same time. The southwest monsoon closes the mainland from June. The three seasons.

How sharp is the monsoon here?

Sharp enough to reverse a month. Panaji takes 945 mm in July and 0 mm in January. Forty kilometres separates Thattekad on the windward side of the Ghats, at 3,051 mm a year, from the rain shadow that gives Kabini 1,094 mm, and that gradient is what makes the Western Ghats endemics endemic. When to go, season by season.

What are the Western Ghats endemics?

Around twenty species live along this mountain chain and nowhere else, including Nilgiri Pipit, Nilgiri Flycatcher, Malabar Grey Hornbill, White-bellied Blue Flycatcher and the Kerala and Palani laughingthrushes. Most are above 1,000 m, which is why a southern trip has to climb. Munnar.

Why is Thattekad so highly rated?

Salim Ali called it the richest bird habitat in peninsular India and twenty-five square kilometres of it still delivers. What makes it work is the guiding: a handful of local men know individual frogmouth and bay owl roosts, and booking one of them is the difference between a good morning and a frustrating one. Thattekad.

Are the Andamans worth the flight?

If you want the endemics, yes, and there is no other way to get them. About twenty species live on those islands and nowhere else, and their nearest relatives are in Myanmar rather than India. Four or five days around Port Blair covers most of the set, and the reefs are a genuine bonus. The Andaman Islands.

Is Goa still a good introduction?

Yes, and for the same reason it always was: a beach hotel, a paddy field and a Western Ghats forest camp are within ninety minutes of each other. It is the softest possible landing in Indian birding and the list from a week there is far longer than people expect. Goa and the Konkan coast.

Why does this guide carry no certification badges?

Because India has no national scheme and the international ones reach the city hotel chains. The Kabini lodges and the Munnar tea bungalows are among the best wildlife accommodation in India and none of them appears in a register. Certification.

How many bird species does India have?

Around 1,350 recorded, and about eighty live nowhere else, most of them in the Western Ghats or on the Andamans. India is four guides here because those two lists share almost nothing with the Himalaya or the plains. The birds.
