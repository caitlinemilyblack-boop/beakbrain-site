At a glance

Best months
March and April for the Castro Verde bustard leks and the spring steppe, late September into the first week of October for the Sagres raptor passage, and late May to early August for the Madeira and Azores pelagics
Getting around
A hire car on the mainland. Lisbon, Porto and Faro carry the full desks, and the country is small enough to cross in a day
The one thing to know
Castro Verde is a working farmed steppe rather than a reserve, so the birds are on private land seen from public tracks. The LPN programme is what keeps the farming compatible with the bustards

When to Go

The Portuguese year runs on two things: the steppe, which is at its best before the summer burns it, and Sagres, which happens in one fortnight in autumn. The islands run to their own calendar entirely, because the pelagic season is short.

spring

March to May

The season most itineraries run. Bustards display through March and early April before the grass grows tall enough to hide them, and the whole country is in song.

Go for Steppe leks, breeding raptors on the border cliffs, and spring passage through the estuaries.

Trade off Easter lifts prices, and the good Alentejo guesthouses fill their April dates early.

Typical daily average Porto 9 to 18 °C / 48 to 64 °F, Lisboa 11 to 19 °C / 52 to 66 °F, Faro 11 to 21 °C / 52 to 70 °F

summer

June to August

The mainland interior runs hot and the steppe empties after the harvest. This is the island season: the Madeira and Azores pelagics are at their peak.

Go for Zino's Petrel, Monteiro's Storm-petrel and the shearwater colonies.

Trade off Over forty degrees in the Alentejo and Guadiana in high summer. Birding is early morning work.

Typical daily average Porto 15 to 24 °C / 59 to 75 °F, Lisboa 17 to 27 °C / 63 to 81 °F, Faro 18 to 28 °C / 64 to 82 °F

autumn

September to October

Sagres. Raptors funnel into the southwest corner and pile up waiting for the wind, and the seawatching runs off the cape at the same time.

Go for The raptor crossing, seawatching, and wader passage through the Algarve and the estuaries.

Trade off The festival week books out. Reserve accommodation in Sagres and Vila do Bispo well ahead.

Typical daily average Porto 12 to 22 °C / 54 to 72 °F, Lisboa 16 to 24 °C / 61 to 75 °F, Faro 16 to 25 °C / 61 to 77 °F

winter

November to February

Mild, wet in the north, and the estuaries hold their largest wader numbers of the year. The steppe keeps its resident specialities.

Go for Wintering waders and wildfowl, flamingos in the Algarve, Black-winged Kite and the resident eagles.

Trade off Snow closes the top of Serra da Estrela and the northern sierras are genuinely wet.

Typical daily average Porto 6 to 15 °C / 43 to 59 °F, Lisboa 9 to 16 °C / 48 to 61 °F, Faro 9 to 18 °C / 48 to 64 °F

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Portugal?

March and April if you want one answer. Great Bustards are displaying at Castro Verde, the steppe is still green and the border canyons have their raptors on the nest. Late September into early October is the other answer, for the raptor passage at Sagres.

Where do I go for bustards in Portugal?

Castro Verde, in the Alentejo, ninety minutes north of Faro. It is a cereal steppe farmed under an LPN conservation programme and it holds both Great and Little Bustard, along with Montagu's Harrier, Black-bellied Sandgrouse and Lesser Kestrel.

Is Sagres worth a trip on its own?

In the first week of October, yes. Raptors funnel into the southwest corner of Europe and wait for the wind to cross, and Cabranosa and Vila do Bispo give the view. The Sagres Birdwatching Festival runs in the same window.

Do I need a car in Portugal?

For the mainland, yes. The steppe tracks, the canyon viewpoints and the estuary hides are all well away from public transport. Lisbon and Faro both have the full hire desks.

Are Madeira and the Azores part of a Portugal birding trip?

They are sold as their own trips rather than folded into a mainland circuit. Both are island and pelagic destinations with their own endemics, and each takes a week of its own.

What certification should I look for in Portuguese accommodation?

Green Key, which the Portuguese call Chave Verde. It runs at real national scale here, with 446 establishments awarded in the 2026 edition and a public register anyone can search.

Which birds is Portugal best for in Europe?

Black-winged Kite is the standout, more reliable here than anywhere else in Europe. Little Bustard is another, with over ninety-five per cent of the national population on the Alentejo plains, and Zino's Petrel breeds nowhere but Madeira.

Who are the birding institutions in Portugal?

SPEA is the BirdLife partner, founded in 1993 with around three thousand two hundred members. LPN is the oldest conservation body in the country and runs the Castro Verde programme, and ICNF manages the protected areas.
