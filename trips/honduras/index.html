At a glance

Best months
February to May for the country as a whole, and March and April for nesting quetzals in the western cloud forests. The Caribbean slope is wet all year and is treated on its own terms.
Regions
Pico Bonito and the Caribbean lowlandsLake Yojoa and the Santa Barbara massifThe western cloud forests: Celaque and OpatoroLa Tigra and the Tegucigalpa highlandsThe Bay IslandsOlancho and the Agalta pineLa Mosquitia and the Rio Platano
Routes
Pico Bonito and Lake Yojoa6 daysThe classic circuit9 daysThe capital and La Tigra5 daysOlancho and the eastern pine8 daysEnd to end14 days
Getting in
San Pedro Sula (SAP) is the practical entry for the north and west, Tegucigalpa (TGU) for the centre and east, and Roatan (RTB) takes direct flights from the United States. La Ceiba (LCE) links the mainland to the islands and the Mosquitia.
Getting around
A hired vehicle with clearance covers everything on surfaced highways except the unmade tracks into Agalta and the western ranges, and the Mosquitia, which has no road at all and is reached by light aircraft and boat.

When to Go

Honduras runs a dry season from February to May over most of the country and a wet one from June to November, but the Caribbean slope is the exception and takes rain in every month. The interior valleys and the Pacific side are far drier than either.

Peak

February to May

The dry season inland. Roads into Agalta and the western cloud forests are firm, the quetzals are holding territory at Celaque and La Tigra, and the dry valleys are open enough to see into.

Go for Quetzals nesting from March, the Honduran Emerald at Olanchito, and spring migration through the Bay Islands in April.

Trade off The busiest months, and the Caribbean slope stays wet throughout.

Green

June to September

The rains. Afternoon storms rather than all-day rain over most of the country, and the forest is at its loudest.

Go for Breeding activity everywhere, the lowest rates of the year, and the cloud forests at their greenest.

Trade off Unsurfaced roads into Olancho and the Mosquitia become a real question, and the hurricane season is open.

Migration

October to January

The wettest stretch on the Caribbean coast, and the arrival of the North American wintering birds.

Go for Autumn passage runs along the north coast, wintering warblers work the pine and oak, and the Yojoa duck counts fall now.

Trade off October is the peak of the hurricane season and the north coast can be cut off.

Pico Bonito and the Caribbean lowlands

A wall of rainforest rising from sea level to 2,400 metres within twelve kilometres of the coast

Pico Bonito puts every lowland and foothill height band on one mountain and a lodge at the bottom of it. Lovely Cotinga and Keel-billed Motmot are both on the lower trails, and the Caribbean coast below runs migrants along it, and the Cuero y Salado wetland an hour west is worked by boat through mangrove and canals.

  • Habitat: Lowland and premontane rainforest on a steep limestone and schist massif, with mangrove, canal and coastal lagoon at Cuero y Salado below it.
  • When: February to May for the driest weather, and the Caribbean slope is wet in every month. Migrants pour along this coast in April and October.
  • Getting there: La Ceiba is the base, with an airport of its own and a two-hour drive from San Pedro Sula. The lodge road leaves the highway at El Pino, and Cuero y Salado is reached by a narrow-gauge railcar from La Union.
  • Look for: Lovely Cotinga, Keel-billed Motmot, King Vulture, Agami Heron, Sungrebe, White-necked Puffbird
  • National parks: Parque Nacional Pico Bonito, Refugio de Vida Silvestre Cuero y Salado
  • Stops:
    • The Pico Bonito lodge trails : A network of loops from sea level up the lower slope. A canopy tower stands over the ridge.
    • Cuero y Salado : Mangrove, canal and lagoon. A narrow-gauge railcar takes you in, and boats work it for herons and Sungrebe.
    • : A river trail into the national park proper. It climbs to a waterfall through foothill forest.
    • Lancetilla Botanical Garden : A century-old plantation collection outside Tela. Big trees and bamboo give an easy list of lowland birds.
  • Visitor centres: Cuero y Salado Visitor Centre, Salado Barra
  • Also covers: Cuero y Salado Visitor Centre, Salado Barra, El Pino, La Ceiba, La Union, Lancetilla Botanical Garden, Parque Nacional Pico Bonito, Refugio de Vida Silvestre Cuero y Salado, Tela, The Pico Bonito lodge trails, The Rio Zacate trail
  • Also watch for: West Indian manatee in the Cuero y Salado canals, and one of the last Honduran populations of Baird's tapir on the massif above.

Lake Yojoa and the Santa Barbara massif

The largest natural lake in the country, with cloud forest on the mountain above it and the longest bird list in Honduras around it

More species have been recorded around Yojoa than anywhere else in the country, because the lake, its marshes, the pine ridges and the cloud forest on the Santa Barbara massif all sit within a few kilometres. The Panacam trails on the eastern side are the usual way in.

  • Habitat: A karst lake with reed and floating marsh, ringed by pine and broadleaf forest rising to cloud forest on the Santa Barbara and Cerro Azul massifs.
  • When: Year round, and February to May is driest. The lake holds wintering North American ducks and warblers from November.
  • Getting there: Two hours south of San Pedro Sula on the CA-5, and the lake is beside the highway. Panacam is signed at La Guama and the Santa Barbara trails leave from Los Andes.
  • Look for: Keel-billed Motmot, Russet-naped Wood Rail, Collared Aracari, White-necked Puffbird, Crested Guan, Sungrebe
  • National parks: Parque Nacional Cerro Azul Meambar, Parque Nacional Santa Barbara
  • Stops:
    • Panacam : Trails and a lodge on the eastern shore. They climb from the lake into cloud forest.
    • Los Naranjos: An archaeological park on the northern shore, with a boardwalk over the lake marsh.
    • The Santa Barbara cloud forest : The second highest mountain in Honduras. Walk in from Los Andes for the highland endemic set.
  • Visitor centres: Panacam Lodge visitor centre
  • Also covers: La Guama, Los Naranjos, Panacam Lodge visitor centre, Parque Nacional Cerro Azul Meambar, Parque Nacional Santa Barbara, Peña Blanca, The Santa Barbara cloud forest
  • Also watch for: Howler and white-faced monkeys on the Panacam trails, and the lake's own fishery, which is what the roadside restaurants are known for.

The western cloud forests: Celaque and Opatoro

The highest mountain in Honduras, holding the cloud forest birds shared with Guatemala and almost nothing else

Celaque is a block of cloud forest on the highest ground in the country and the trail up it is steep from the first metre. Resplendent Quetzal, Highland Guan and the montane jays are all here, and the western towns below it are the base.

  • Habitat: Montane cloud forest of oak, liquidambar and tree fern above a pine belt, on the highest ground in Honduras at 2,870 metres.
  • When: February to May for the trails, and quetzals call and nest from March. The cloud forest is wet whatever the calendar says.
  • Getting there: Gracias is the base, five hours west of Tegucigalpa or three from San Pedro Sula. The visitor centre is an hour's drive and walk above the town.
  • Look for: Resplendent Quetzal, Highland Guan, Black-throated Jay, Bushy-crested Jay, Three-wattled Bellbird, Crested Guan
  • National park: Parque Nacional Celaque
  • Stops:
    • The Celaque visitor centre trails : The lower loops from the centre at 1,400 metres. They reach cloud forest without the summit climb.
    • : A colonial town below the park. Permits and guides are arranged here.
    • : Pine and oak ridges east of Marcala. They are quieter than Celaque and hold much the same list.
  • Visitor centres: Celaque Visitor Centre, Villa Verde
  • Also covers: Celaque Visitor Centre, Villa Verde, Gracias, La Esperanza, Marcala, Parque Nacional Celaque, Santa Rosa de Copan, The Celaque visitor centre trails, The Opatoro and Guajiquiro ridges
  • Also watch for: Quetzal-following mixed flocks, and a puma population the park is monitored for.

La Tigra and the Tegucigalpa highlands

The first national park declared in Honduras, twenty minutes above the capital in old cloud forest

La Tigra sits directly above Tegucigalpa and is the easiest cloud forest in the country to reach, walked on mining trails cut when the hills were mined for silver. Quetzals hold territory in the upper forest and the whole park fits between flights.

  • Habitat: Montane cloud forest and pine-oak on a granite ridge above the capital, cut by the tunnels and tracks of a nineteenth-century mining concession.
  • When: February to May for the driest walking, and quetzals are holding territory from March. The forest is in cloud most afternoons whatever the season.
  • Getting there: Twenty-two kilometres from Tegucigalpa, an hour by road to the Jutiapa entrance on the eastern side or El Rosario on the west. Both have a visitor centre and both roads need care in the wet.
  • Look for: Resplendent Quetzal, Highland Guan, Black-throated Jay, Bushy-crested Jay, Collared Aracari, King Vulture
  • National park: Parque Nacional La Tigra
  • Stops:
    • El Rosario : The western entrance, in an old mining camp. The Sendero La Esperanza starts here.
    • Jutiapa entrance : The eastern way in, closer to the capital and the shorter drive.
    • : A pine-and-oak town on the road up. Most people stay here rather than in the capital.
  • Visitor centres: La Tigra Visitor Centre, El Rosario
  • Also covers: Jutiapa entrance, La Tigra Visitor Centre, El Rosario, Parque Nacional La Tigra, San Juancito, Tegucigalpa, Valle de Angeles
  • Also watch for: The mining tunnels hold bat roosts, and the park supplies a large share of the capital's water, which is why it was protected in 1980.

The Bay Islands

Three limestone islands off the Caribbean coast, holding an endemic hummingbird found on one of them

Utila, Roatan and Guanaja sit on the same reef system and each holds a slightly different list. The Utila endemic hummingbird is confined to the island's eastern scrub, and the mangrove and iron-shore between the resorts take migrants in numbers each spring.

  • Habitat: Low limestone and volcanic islands with dry scrub, mangrove swamp, iron-shore and coconut, on the southern end of the Mesoamerican reef.
  • When: March to May for spring migration and the driest weather. The endemic hummingbird is resident and easiest early in the morning.
  • Getting there: Flights from La Ceiba to Roatan, Utila and Guanaja take under half an hour, and the Utila and Roatan ferries run from La Ceiba daily.
  • Look for: Magnificent Frigatebird, Yellow-naped Amazon, White-bellied Chachalaca, Turquoise-browed Motmot, Collared Aracari, Royal Tern
  • National park: Parque Nacional Marino Islas de la Bahia
  • Stops:
    • : Low dry scrub past the airstrip. It is the whole world range of the Utila endemic hummingbird.
    • : The forested eastern end, and the hillside botanical garden above Sandy Bay. Both are quieter than the resort strip.
    • : The least developed of the three. Caribbean pine covers the ridge and there are no roads to speak of.
  • Also covers: Coxen Hole, Guanaja, Parque Nacional Marino Islas de la Bahia, Roatan's Port Royal and Carambola, Utila's eastern scrub
  • Also watch for: Whale sharks off Utila between March and April, and the Roatan agouti, which occurs nowhere else.

Olancho and the Agalta pine

Open pine savanna and a cloud forest range above it, in the largest and emptiest department in the country

Olancho is pine country: open savanna with scattered oaks, cut by rivers and backed by the Agalta range, which holds cloud forest above 2,000 metres. The Honduran Emerald's dry valley at Olanchito lies on the way in from the coast.

  • Habitat: Caribbean pine savanna on a wide intermontane plain, with gallery forest along the rivers and montane cloud forest on the Sierra de Agalta above it.
  • When: February to May, when the unsurfaced roads are firm. The dry valleys are best early in the year before the rains green them.
  • Getting there: Catacamas and Juticalpa are three to four hours northeast of Tegucigalpa on the CA-15. Agalta is reached on tracks from Gualaco, and the Olanchito valley from the north coast highway.
  • Look for: Honduran Emerald, Ocellated Turkey, King Vulture, Great Curassow, Crested Guan, Yellow-naped Amazon
  • National parks: Parque Nacional Sierra de Agalta, Refugio de Vida Silvestre Colibri Esmeralda
  • Stops:
    • The Olanchito dry valley : Thorn scrub in the Aguan rain shadow. It is the stronghold of the Honduran Emerald, which lives nowhere else on earth.
    • Sierra de Agalta : Cloud forest above the pine. A track runs in from Gualaco and you walk from the end of it.
    • : Open pine and oak either side of the road, with pullouts to stop at for the savanna species.
  • Also covers: Gualaco, Juticalpa, Parque Nacional Sierra de Agalta, Refugio de Vida Silvestre Colibri Esmeralda, The Catacamas pine savanna, The Olanchito dry valley
  • Also watch for: The Rio Plátano headwaters rise on the far side of Agalta, and the range holds jaguar and Baird's tapir.

La Mosquitia and the Rio Platano

The largest block of rainforest north of the Amazon, reached by light aircraft and river boat and by nothing else

The Rio Platano Biosphere Reserve covers eight thousand square kilometres of rainforest, pine savanna, lagoon and coast, and there are no roads into it. Trips run by boat from Palacios or Brus Laguna and are arranged well in advance, which is why almost nobody goes.

  • Habitat: Lowland rainforest, coastal lagoon and one of the few large tracts of natural Caribbean pine savanna in the Americas, cut by the Platano and Patuca rivers.
  • When: February to May, and the region is wet all year. River levels rather than rainfall decide what is reachable.
  • Getting there: Light aircraft from La Ceiba to Palacios, Brus Laguna or Ahuas, then boat. There is no road, and a trip here is organised rather than driven to.
  • Look for: Great Green Macaw, Scarlet Macaw, Harpy Eagle, King Vulture, Agami Heron, Sungrebe
  • National park: Reserva de la Biosfera Rio Platano
  • Stops:
    • The Rio Platano : The river itself. A boat up from the coast is the only practical way to see the reserve.
    • : A lagoon town with an airstrip. It is the usual staging point for the eastern half.
    • : Open Caribbean pine on white sand behind the lagoons. The habitat occurs almost nowhere else.
  • Also covers: Ahuas, Brus Laguna, Palacios, Puerto Lempira, Reserva de la Biosfera Rio Platano, The Mosquitia pine savanna, The Rio Platano
  • Also watch for: Jaguar, Baird's tapir and West Indian manatee, and the Miskito and Pech communities who hold much of the land.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Honduras?

February to May for the country as a whole: the interior roads are dry, the western cloud forests are at their most workable and the quetzals are holding territory from March. The Caribbean slope is the exception and takes rain in every month, so it is treated on its own terms rather than saved for the dry season.

How many bird species does Honduras have?

Around 770 species have been recorded. The country's position and its height range are the reason: Pico Bonito climbs from sea level to 2,400 metres in twelve kilometres, and Celaque reaches 2,870, so the Caribbean lowland and montane avifaunas are both present within a short drive of each other.

What is the one bird found nowhere else?

The Honduran Emerald, a hummingbird restricted to thorn scrub in the rain shadow of the Aguan valley around Olanchito. A wildlife refuge was declared for it and the surrounding scrub is still being cleared, which makes it the country's conservation emblem as well as its only endemic.

Is Honduras safe to bird independently?

The birding areas are rural and the reserves are managed, and most visiting birders travel with a local guide or a lodge transfer rather than driving alone at night. San Pedro Sula and Tegucigalpa are the parts of the country that carry a reputation, and neither is where the birding is.

Do I need four-wheel drive?

For Agalta, the Opatoro ridges and the tracks above Gracias, yes. The main highways between San Pedro Sula, Yojoa, Tegucigalpa and La Ceiba are surfaced and a normal car is fine on them.

Can I reach the Mosquitia?

Only by light aircraft from La Ceiba and then by boat. There is no road into the Rio Platano Biosphere Reserve and there is not going to be one, which is why it still holds Great Green Macaw and Harpy Eagle. A trip there is organised months ahead rather than driven to.

Where do I see a quetzal?

Celaque above Gracias in the west, or La Tigra above Tegucigalpa, which is twenty minutes from the capital and the easier of the two to fit into a short trip. Both hold territories and both are best from March, when pairs are calling at nest holes.

Are the Bay Islands worth it for birds?

For two things. The Utila endemic hummingbird lives in the island's eastern scrub and nowhere else, and the mangrove and iron-shore on all three islands catch spring migrants crossing the Caribbean. The mainland holds the country list.

How does Honduras compare with Costa Rica?

Fewer visitors, lower prices, a similar height range and a national list within a hundred species of it. What Costa Rica has is infrastructure: more lodges, more guides and more roads. Honduras rewards a trip planned around fewer places and longer stays.

What field guide should I take?

Birds of Central America by Vallely and Dyer covers the whole isthmus in one volume and is the current standard. There is no guide to Honduras alone.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
