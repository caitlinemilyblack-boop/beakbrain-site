At a glance

Best months
February and March, inside a dry window that runs November to April and nothing else
Regions
Kaziranga and the Brahmaputra floodplainEaglenest and the Bugun forestsNameri, Pakke and the Assam foothillsDibru-Saikhowa and upper AssamMishmi Hills and the far east
Getting in
Guwahati (GAU) for Kaziranga and Nameri, Tezpur for the Eaglenest road, Dibrugarh (DIB) for upper Assam and the Mishmi Hills
Getting around
A car and driver throughout, plus an Inner Line or Protected Area Permit for Arunachal Pradesh arranged before arrival

When to Go

The northeast has a long monsoon that runs from April to October, which is far longer than the plains, and a short dry window from November to March that the season is squeezed into. Kaziranga closes for the wet. Arunachal's roads become landslide country. The season is November to April and the best of it is February and March, when the ridge birds start calling.

Peak

February and March

The short window when the floodplain and the ridges are both at their best.

Go for Kaziranga's grass has been cut and burned so the visibility is good, Bengal Florican displays in March, and the Eaglenest birds start calling as the rhododendrons open.

Trade off It is the whole industry's window, so the Eaglenest camps and the Kaziranga lodges book far ahead, and Arunachal permits take time.

The driest months, cold and clear.

Trade off Kaziranga's grass is still high in November and the highest ground on the Mishmi road can be under snow.

Kaziranga and the Brahmaputra floodplain

Elephant grass, wetland and semi-evergreen forest on the Brahmaputra, holding two thirds of the world's one-horned rhinos

Kaziranga is grassland birding done from the back of an elephant or a jeep, and the density of birds is what people remember. Bengal Florican displays over the grass in spring, Greater Adjutant stands in the wet meadows, Swamp Francolin calls from the tall grass and the wetland holds Spot-billed Pelican and Lesser Adjutant together.

  • Habitat: Marsh, grassland and lowland forest cover 470 square kilometres of the Brahmaputra floodplain. White-bellied Heron, Swamp Francolin and Bengal Florican live here alongside good numbers of Indian rhinoceros. Around 200 species is a fair three-day total.
  • When: October to May, while Pallas's Fish Eagle and Black-necked Stork are here. Greater Adjutant, Lesser Adjutant and Swamp Francolin stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Kaziranga sits on the floodplain in Assam.
  • Look for: Bengal Florican, Greater Adjutant, Lesser Adjutant, Swamp Francolin, Spot-billed Pelican, Pallas's Fish Eagle, Black-necked Stork, Great Hornbill
  • Parks and reserves: Kaziranga National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The village on the highway at the central range gate. The lodges and the safari bookings are here.
  • Also covers: Kaziranga National Park, Kohora
  • Also watch for: Two thirds of the world's Greater One-horned Rhinoceros live in this park, and the tiger density is among the highest measured anywhere. Wild Water Buffalo, Eastern Swamp Deer and Asian Elephant are all here in numbers, and Ganges River Dolphin uses the Brahmaputra channels.

Eaglenest and the Bugun forests

An Arunachal ridge road climbing from 500 m to 2,800 m through forest a community owns and protects

Eaglenest is the single richest bird road in India. It runs from the Assam plain to a 2,800 m pass through forest the Bugun community protects, and it produced Bugun Liocichla in 2006, the first new Indian bird species described in more than half a century. Camps at Lama, Bompu and Sessni put you at three altitudes, and the whole trip is a road walked slowly.

  • When: Best from November to May. Bugun Liocichla, Ward's Trogon and Beautiful Nuthatch stay through the year.
  • Getting there: The Eagle's Nest sanctuary lies north of Tezpur, close to the Bhutanese border in west Arunachal Pradesh. Arunachal Pradesh needs an entry permit arranged in advance, and the rules on group size and length of stay change, so check them when you book.
  • Look for: Bugun Liocichla, Ward's Trogon, Beautiful Nuthatch, Fire-tailed Myzornis, Blyth's Tragopan, Rufous-necked Hornbill, Long-billed Wren-Babbler, Purple Cochoa
  • Parks and reserves: Eaglenest Wildlife Sanctuary
  • Stops:
    • Lama Camp: The camp at 2,350 m near the top of the road. The liocichla was described from this site.
    • : The town on the road up from Assam. The permits and the last shops are here.
  • Also covers: Eaglenest Wildlife Sanctuary, Lama Camp, Tenga
  • Also watch for: Red Panda and Asiatic Black Bear both use the higher forest, and Clouded Leopard is on camera traps. The Bugun community reserve is the conservation story here: a village that gave up its own forest rights to protect a bird found nowhere else.

Nameri, Pakke and the Assam foothills

Riverine forest at the foot of the Arunachal hills, with a river to raft down through it

Nameri sits on the Jia Bhoroli where it comes out of the hills, and the birding is done on foot in riverine forest and on a raft down the river. White-winged Duck survives here in one of its last Indian populations, Ibisbill winters on the shingle and Wreathed Hornbill crosses at dusk. Pakke over the Arunachal border is the hornbill reserve, with a nest-protection programme run by local men.

  • When: Best from November to May. White-winged Duck, Ibisbill and Wreathed Hornbill stay through the year.
  • Look for: White-winged Duck, Ibisbill, Wreathed Hornbill, Great Hornbill, Oriental Pied Hornbill, Small Pratincole, Blue-naped Pitta, Sultan Tit
  • Parks and reserves: Nameri National Park, Pakke Tiger Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The reserve on the Jia Bhoroli. The eco-camp sits at Potasali with the raft launch above it.
  • Also covers: Nameri National Park, Pakke Tiger Reserve
  • Also watch for: Asian Elephant crosses the Jia Bhoroli and the raft trip runs through their crossing points. Capped Langur and Hoolock Gibbon are both in the riverine forest, and Tiger uses the reserve without being seen.

Dibru-Saikhowa and upper Assam

Wet grassland and swamp forest where the Brahmaputra is formed, in the far northeast corner

Upper Assam is where the Brahmaputra is made from three rivers, and the wetland that results is enormous. Maguri Beel outside Dibru-Saikhowa is a shallow lake watched from a country boat, and Black-breasted Parrotbill and Marsh Babbler live in its reeds. The grassland behind carries Jerdon's Babbler and Swamp Grass Babbler, and almost nobody sees any of the four.

  • Habitat: The 640-square-kilometre sanctuary and the Bherjan and Podumoni reserves beside it hold marsh, grassland and lowland forest much like Kaziranga's, with about 250 species recorded and White-winged Duck among them.
  • When: Best from October to May. Black-breasted Parrotbill, Marsh Babbler and Jerdon's Babbler are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Staying inside the reserve needs forest department permission, and that has to be arranged in advance.
  • Look for: Black-breasted Parrotbill, Marsh Babbler, Jerdon's Babbler, Swamp Grass Babbler, Slender-billed Babbler, Bengal Florican, Pale-capped Pigeon, Baikal Teal
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Abbott's Babbler, Aberrant Bush Warbler
  • Parks and reserves: Dibru-Saikhowa National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The town in upper Assam. Maguri Beel and the Guijan boat landing are a short drive south.
  • Also covers: Dibru-Saikhowa National Park, Tinsukia
  • Also watch for: Gangetic Dolphin surfaces in the main channels and is watched from the boats at Maguri. Feral Horse, descended from animals abandoned in the Second World War, roams the grassland, and Hoolock Gibbon holds the remaining forest patches.

Mishmi Hills and the far east

The last road in India before Myanmar and Tibet, climbing to 3,000 m through unbroken forest

The Mishmi Hills are the farthest thing from a developed birding destination in India: one road from Roing up to the Mayodia pass at 2,655 m and on towards Anini, with forest on both sides most of the way. Mishmi Wren-Babbler lives here and nowhere else, Sclater's Monal is on the highest ground, and the whole area was only opened to foreign visitors recently.

  • Getting there: The Mishmi Hills sit in Arunachal Pradesh, which needs an entry permit arranged in advance, and the rules on group size and length of stay change, so check them when you book.
  • Look for: Mishmi Wren-Babbler, Sclater's Monal, Blyth's Tragopan, Ward's Trogon, Rusty-bellied Shortwing, Beautiful Sibia, Gould's Shortwing, Slender-billed Scimitar Babbler
  • Parks and reserves: Mehao Wildlife Sanctuary
  • Stops:
    • : The town at the foot of the hills. The permits are checked here and the road up begins.
    • Mayodia: The pass at 2,655 m. There is a guesthouse, and the best of the road runs either side of it.
  • Also covers: Mayodia, Mehao Wildlife Sanctuary, Roing
  • Also watch for: Mishmi Takin holds the high forest and Red Panda the middle altitudes. The Dibang valley is one of the least surveyed places in India and the mammal list is genuinely incomplete.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to bird northeast India?

February and March, inside a dry window that runs November to April. Kaziranga's grass has been cut by February so the visibility is good, Bengal Florican displays in March, and the Eaglenest ridge birds start calling as the rhododendrons open. The monsoon closes the region from May to October. The three seasons.

How long is the wet season?

About six months. Every station here records its driest month in December, between 4 and 22 mm, and its wettest in June or July at 396 to 515 mm. Kaziranga closes from May to October under park rules, so the birding year runs November to April. When to go, season by season.

Do I need a permit for Arunachal Pradesh?

Yes. Indian citizens need an Inner Line Permit and foreign visitors a Protected Area Permit, both arranged in advance through the operator and both taking time. Nothing about Eaglenest or the Mishmi Hills happens without one. Eaglenest.

What is the Bugun Liocichla?

A bird described in 2006 from the Eaglenest ridge, the first new Indian bird species named in more than half a century, and known from a few dozen individuals. The Bugun community whose land it lives on gave up forest rights to protect it and now takes the visitor income, which is the conservation model worth backing. Eaglenest and the Bugun forests.

Is Kaziranga a birding site or a mammal site?

Both, and the grassland birding is the part visitors do not expect. Bengal Florican, Swamp Francolin and Greater Adjutant are all here alongside two thirds of the world's one-horned rhinos, and the elephant-back and jeep formats work for both. Kaziranga.

How hard are the Mishmi Hills?

Hard. One road from Roing to the Mayodia pass at 2,655 m and on towards Anini, a guesthouse at the top, no infrastructure and a permit to get there. It holds Mishmi Wren-Babbler and Sclater's Monal, reachable nowhere else, and the Dibang valley below is one of the least surveyed places in India. Mishmi Hills.

Do the places to stay in Northeast India hold sustainability certification?

None of them. India has no national scheme, and there is almost no hotel accommodation here to certify. What exists instead is the Bugun community reserve arrangement, named here for what it is. Certification.

How many bird species does India have?

Around 1,350 recorded, and the northeast holds a large share of them because it is where the Indian, Himalayan and Indo-Burmese avifaunas meet. India is four guides here for that reason. The birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
