When to Go

The pelagic season

May to September

The boats run and the petrels are at the colonies. This is the only window in which Zino's, Desertas and the storm petrels are all reachable.

Go for Zino's Petrel at the Areeiro colony after dark, and the Funchal pelagics at their best in August and September.

Trade off It is the busiest and hottest half of the year, and the boats fill weeks ahead.

Typical daily average
  • Funchal: 19 to 26 °C / 66 to 79 °F
  • Pico do Areeiro: 9 to 17 °C / 48 to 63 °F

Passage

March, April, October and November

The shoulder months, when the island's vagrant traps do their work and the forest birds are singing.

Go for Porto Santo for whatever has landed, and the laurisilva endemics in full song in spring.

Trade off The pelagic boats run less often and the weather turns them back more.

Typical daily average
  • Funchal: 15 to 21 °C / 59 to 70 °F
  • Pico do Areeiro: 5 to 13 °C / 41 to 55 °F

Winter

December to February

Mild and wet on the north side, and the island is very quiet. The endemics stay put and the seabirds have gone south.

Go for Trocaz Pigeon and Madeira Firecrest without another birder on the levada.

Trade off No pelagics worth the name, and the high ridge is often closed by cloud or ice.

Typical daily average
  • Funchal: 13 to 19 °C / 55 to 66 °F
  • Pico do Areeiro: 2 to 9 °C / 36 to 48 °F

The laurisilva: Ribeiro Frio and Fanal

Laurel forest older than the last ice age, holding two birds that live on this island alone

The laurisilva is a UNESCO World Heritage site and the largest surviving laurel forest on earth. Trocaz Pigeon and Madeira Firecrest both live here and nowhere else, and the levada paths walk straight into the canopy.

  • Habitat: The laurisilva, the laurel forest that once covered the island, survives between 300 and 1,300 metres and is protected inside the Madeira Natural Park. The wet form grows on north-facing slopes and in gorges under a canopy about 25 metres high.
  • When: Trocaz Pigeon nests through the year with a clear peak from February to June, and Madeira Firecrest lays from May to July. Both are easiest in the first hours of light.
  • Getting there: Ribeiro Frio sits on the road over the centre of the island, and the path to the Balcões lookout runs level for about half an hour along a levada (a channel that carries water round the island and doubles as a footpath). Fanal is higher and further west, and its ancient tree heaths often sit in cloud.
  • Base yourself in: Santana or Funchal
  • Look for: Trocaz Pigeon, Madeira Firecrest, Plain Swift, Madeira Chaffinch, Eurasian Sparrowhawk, Grey Wagtail, Common Buzzard
  • Parks and reserves: Parque Natural da Madeira, Laurisilva of Madeira UNESCO World Heritage Site
  • Stops:
    • Ribeiro Frio : A laurel forest reserve on the island's north side, with a levada walk from the road. It is the reliable place for Trocaz Pigeon and Madeira Firecrest.
    • : A level levada path of about half an hour from Ribeiro Frio village. The viewpoint at the end looks over the Ribeira da Metade valley, and pigeons cross below it.
    • : Ancient tree heaths on the high plateau in the northwest, often in cloud. The pasture edge holds Berthelot's Pipit and the forest behind it holds the endemics.
  • Also covers: Balcões, Fanal, Laurisilva forest of Ribeiro Frio, Laurisilva of Madeira UNESCO World Heritage Site, Parque Natural da Madeira
  • Also watch for: The forest is the other thing to look at, with til, vinhático and laurel in the canopy. Madeiran wall lizards run the levada walls and the path edges.

Pico do Areeiro and the high peaks

A road to 1,818 metres, with Europe's rarest seabird nesting on the ledges below it

Zino's Petrel breeds on the Areeiro ledges and nowhere else on earth. The colony is guarded and the birds come in after dark, so the ridge is walked for the setting and the petrel is heard rather than seen.

  • Habitat: Bare volcanic ridge and cliff rise above the treeline, with tree heath on the shoulders below. Grassland and rocky scree cover the ground near the summit, and laurel forest runs up the slopes under it.
  • When: Zino's Petrel lays in May and June and the young fledge in October. The birds call over the ridge after dark. The ridge itself is clearest early, before the cloud builds.
  • Getting there: A paved road climbs to the Pico do Areeiro car park at 1,818 metres. The ridge path on to Pico Ruivo is exposed and takes about three hours each way, and Achada do Teixeira is the easier way to Ruivo from the north. The breeding valleys should only be visited with a local guide.
  • Base yourself in: Funchal
  • Look for: Zino's Petrel, Plain Swift, Berthelot's Pipit, Common Kestrel, Rock Dove, Grey Wagtail
  • Parks and reserves: Maciço Montanhoso Central (protected area)
  • Stops:
    • Pico do Areeiro : The third highest peak on the island and a road runs to the top. It is the accessible ground above the Zino's Petrel colony that nests on the ledges below.
    • Pico Ruivo : The highest point on Madeira at 1,862 metres. The walk in from Achada do Teixeira is the shorter of the two approaches.
  • Also covers: Maciço Montanhoso Central, Pico do Areeiro, Pico Ruivo
  • Also watch for: Madeira has no native land mammal, so the ridge is quiet ground. Madeiran wall lizards bask on the rock right up to the summits, and rats and feral cats are controlled around the petrel ledges.

Ponta de São Lourenço and the east

A treeless volcanic peninsula at the eastern tip, and the driest ground on the island

The peninsula is bare red rock and low scrub, which is where Berthelot's Pipit and Spectacled Warbler live. The marked path runs out along the spine and the sea below it carries shearwaters through the summer.

  • Habitat: Treeless volcanic peninsula of red and ochre rock, with low windclipped scrub and sea cliffs on both sides.
  • When: Migrants reach the peninsula only under the right weather, which is uncommon. Berthelot's Pipit and Rock Sparrow are the birds on any other day.
  • Getting there: A marked path leaves the car park at the end of the road past Caniçal and runs about an hour and a half out to the neck. There is no shade and no water on it.
  • Base yourself in: Machico or Caniçal
  • Look for: Berthelot's Pipit, Rock Sparrow, Spectacled Warbler, Cory's Shearwater, Yellow-legged Gull, Plain Swift
  • Parks and reserves: Reserva Natural Parcial da Ponta de São Lourenço (nature reserve)
  • Stops:
    • Ponta de São Lourenço : A treeless volcanic peninsula at the island's eastern tip, on a marked path. Berthelot's Pipit and Spectacled Warbler hold the low scrub.
    • : The village before the peninsula road ends, and the harbour the Desertas boats leave from.
    • : The town on the east coast beside the airport. Its bay is the first sheltered water on the island and takes gulls and terns.
  • Also covers: Caniçal, Machico, Reserva Natural Parcial da Ponta de São Lourenço
  • Also watch for: Madeiran wall lizards are all over the peninsula and come to the path and the picnic tables. They are the island's only native land reptile.

Funchal and the petrel boats

The harbour every pelagic leaves from, and the reason most birders come to Madeira

The pelagics out of Funchal are the product Madeira is known for. Zino's Petrel, Desertas Petrel and Band-rumped Storm Petrel are all reachable on a day boat, and the Desertas islands lie within the run.

  • Habitat: Deep Atlantic water lies south of the island and the Desertas rise out of it 16 km to the south-east. The group is three barren islands, the highest of them Deserta Grande at 479 metres.
  • When: The pelagic season runs May to September. August and September are when Zino's, Desertas and the storm petrels overlap.
  • Getting there: Boats leave Funchal marina and run south towards the Desertas. The sea there can be very rough, trips are weather dependent and organised landings are irregular. Ponta da Cruz just west of the city is the southernmost point of Madeira and the place to watch from land.
  • Base yourself in: Funchal
  • Look for: Zino's Petrel, Desertas Petrel, Band-rumped Storm Petrel, Bulwer's Petrel, Cory's Shearwater, Manx Shearwater
  • Parks and reserves: Reserva Natural das Ilhas Desertas (nature reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : The harbour the petrel boats leave from. Everything offshore starts here and the trips are booked ahead.
    • The Desertas : A ridge of uninhabited islands south of Madeira, protected as a nature reserve. Desertas Petrel breeds here and the boats run out to the water around them.
  • Also covers: Funchal marina, Reserva Natural das Ilhas Desertas, The Desertas, the Oceanodroma pelagic departure point
  • Also watch for: Short-finned pilot whales and bottlenose dolphins are regular on the pelagic runs, and Mediterranean monk seals hold the Desertas, where the population has recovered under protection.

Porto Santo

A low sand island forty kilometres northeast, dry where Madeira is wet

Porto Santo is flat, sandy and dry, so it holds birds Madeira does not. It is also the archipelago's vagrant trap, and the islets off its coast carry breeding seabirds.

  • Habitat: Porto Santo covers about 50 square kilometres and rises to 517 metres at Pico do Facho. It carries the sandy beaches Madeira itself lacks, thin Euphorbia scrub inland, and rock islets offshore.
  • When: Spring and autumn passage are what the island is for. Nearctic and Palearctic vagrants both turn up.
  • Getting there: A ferry runs from Funchal in about two and a half hours, and there are short flights. The island is small enough to cover by hire car in a day. The pool at Tanque is the largest standing water on it and the one site to check.
  • Base yourself in: Vila Baleira
  • Look for: Berthelot's Pipit, Spectacled Warbler, Cory's Shearwater, Bulwer's Petrel, Yellow-legged Gull
  • Parks and reserves: Reserva Natural do Ilhéu da Cal (nature reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : A low sand island northeast of Madeira, on a ferry from Funchal or a short flight. It is dry where Madeira is wet, and it is where the vagrants land.
  • Also covers: Reserva Natural do Ilhéu da Cal
  • Also watch for: Madeiran wall lizards are common on the dry ground and the walls all over the island. Rabbits have been here since settlers released them in the fifteenth century, and they still graze the slopes.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I go to Madeira?

August and September, when Zino's Petrel, Desertas Petrel and the storm petrels overlap on the pelagic runs. Trocaz Pigeon and Madeira Firecrest hold the laurel forest in every month of the year.

Can I see Zino's Petrel?

At sea, on a day boat out of Funchal between May and September. The breeding ledges below the Areeiro ridge are guarded by the park and the birds only come in after dark, so the colony is heard rather than watched. The ridge path above it is open to anyone.

How should I book the pelagics?

Ahead, from Funchal marina, and book more than one date. The water south of the island can be very rough and the trips are weather dependent, so leave slack in the week.

Where do I see Trocaz Pigeon and Madeira Firecrest?

Ribeiro Frio, on the road over the centre of the island. The level levada path to the Balcões lookout runs about half an hour and both birds are on it, easiest in the first hours of light. Fanal, higher and further west, is the other stand of old laurel forest.

Do I need a hire car?

Yes. Ribeiro Frio, Fanal, the Areeiro road and the Ponta de São Lourenço car park all sit away from a bus route, and the tunnels have made every drive on the island short.

Is Porto Santo worth a day?

In spring and autumn, yes. It is flat, sandy and dry where Madeira is wet, and it is where the vagrants land. A ferry runs from Funchal in about two and a half hours and there are short flights, and the pool at Tanque is the one site to check.

Is Madeira part of a Portugal birding trip?

It is Portuguese and it is a separate trip. Funchal is under two hours from the mainland by air, and the birds people come for, Zino's Petrel, Desertas Petrel, Trocaz Pigeon and Madeira Firecrest, live here and nowhere on it.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
