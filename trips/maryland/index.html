Atlas / Maryland

Birding in Maryland

Maryland puts the densest breeding Bald Eagle population on the Atlantic coast north of Florida on marsh that is drowning under rising water, and boreal bog with a Canadian bird list three hours west of it.

At a glance

Best months
  • November to March for the Chesapeake waterfowl and the Blackwater raptors, late May to July for the highland bogs
  • August to October for the coastal shorebirds

Baltimore (BWI) for most of the state, Salisbury (SBY) for the eastern shore, and Pittsburgh in Pennsylvania for the Allegheny corner
Getting around
A hire car, with the Bay Bridge between the shores. Ocean City to Oakland is 300 miles, which is the whole width of the state

Birds that decide the trip

Bald Eagle at Blackwater

The refuge holds the densest breeding population on the Atlantic coast north of the Carolinas, and the Maryland wildlife drive puts nests and perched birds within easy scope range from a car.

When All year, with the largest numbers from December to February.

Where Blackwater and the eastern shore

Tundra Swan on the Chesapeake

The bay takes a large share of the eastern wintering population. The birds are on open water and flooded fields from November, and they call all night.

When November to March, on open bay and flooded fields.

Where The Chesapeake shores, Blackwater and the eastern shore

Black Rail in the high marsh

The smallest rail in North America and one of the hardest birds on the continent to see. It calls at night from the driest part of the marsh, and the Maryland populations have fallen sharply as that marsh drowns.

When May to July, after dark from high marsh.

Where Blackwater and the eastern shore

Piping Plover on Assateague

It nests on open sand above the tideline on the undeveloped part of the island, which is why the beach sections north and south of the crossing are roped through the summer.

When April to August, on roped beach sections.

Where Assateague and the Atlantic coast

Golden-winged Warbler in the highlands

It needs young scrubby regrowth beside mature forest, which the reverting hill farms in the far west still provide, and it is one of the fastest declining songbirds in the east.

When Late May to July, in scrubby regrowth.

Where The Allegheny highlands

Saltmarsh Sparrow in the drowning marsh

It nests just above ordinary high tides and loses nests to the tides that go higher, which makes it one of the birds most directly threatened by sea level rise anywhere.

When May to August, in high salt marsh.

Where Blackwater and the eastern shore, Assateague and the Atlantic coast

Canada Warbler in the Maryland bogs

A northern breeder holding on in frost pockets at the southern edge of its range, in rhododendron and sphagnum under red spruce.

When Late May to July, in bog and streamside thickets.

Where The Allegheny highlands

Purple Sandpiper on the inlet jetties

It winters on wave-washed rock, which this coast has almost none of except the two stone jetties at the Ocean City inlet, so the birds concentrate there.

When November to April, on the inlet jetties.

Where Assateague and the Atlantic coast

When to Go

Maryland is a winter state on the water and a summer state in the mountains. A million waterfowl use the Chesapeake from November, and the boreal bogs in the far west only come alive from late May. Very little asks you to be in both places at once.

The waterfowl winter

November to March

About a million ducks, geese and swans use the Chesapeake and its marshes.

Go for Blackwater and Eastern Neck, and the Ocean City inlet for sea duck.

Trade off The highland bogs are under snow and the breeding birds have gone.

Typical daily average

The breeding season

Late May to July

The Allegheny bogs in Maryland hold Canada Warbler and Alder Flycatcher, and the marsh sparrows are singing.

Go for Cranesville and Finzel at dawn, and Blackwater for rails and sparrows.

Trade off The coast is between passages and the bay is quiet.

Typical daily average

The autumn shorebirds

August to October

Assateague and the bay flats take the southbound waders and the raptor stream builds.

Go for Assateague and Sinepuxent Bay, and Point Lookout for the passage.

Trade off Hurricane weather can close the seashore roads without warning.

Typical daily average

The spring passage

April and May

Warblers move up the piedmont river valleys and the first marsh breeders arrive.

Go for Patapsco Valley and Patuxent, and Soldiers Delight for the barrens birds.

Trade off The highland bogs are still cold and the waterfowl have gone north.

Coast to mountains

Salisbury (SBY), Berlin 2 nights, Cambridge 2 nights, Laurel 1 night, Oakland 2 nights, out of Pittsburgh (PIT)

An open-jaw run west in the first week of June, from barrier island to boreal bog across three hundred miles. Beach nesters and marsh sparrows first, then the piedmont serpentine barrens, and a finish in tamarack and spruce that belongs four hundred miles north.

  1. 1Blackwater National Wildlife Refuge
  2. 2Fishing Bay Wildlife Management Area
  3. 3Eastern Neck National Wildlife Refuge
  4. 4Sandy Point State Park

5 days

The Chesapeake in winter

Baltimore (BWI), Cambridge 2 nights, Chestertown 2 nights, back to Baltimore

Four days in January on the largest estuary in the country, when it holds about a million waterfowl. The Blackwater wildlife drive at dawn for eagles and geese, Eastern Neck for swans and diving ducks, and the bay bridges and points for whatever is on open water.

Assateague and the Ocean City inlet

Thirty-seven miles of barrier island with wild horses on it and a working inlet at the top

Assateague is undeveloped Maryland barrier island running south from the Ocean City inlet, with beach, dune, salt marsh and loblolly behind. The inlet at the northern end concentrates gulls, terns and sea duck where the tide runs hard, and the jetties let you watch the sea in winter from a car park.

  • Habitat: Barrier beach and dune, salt marsh and tidal flat on the bay side, loblolly pine and myrtle thicket on the older ridges, and a deep tidal inlet with rock jetties.
  • When: May to July for the beach nesters, August to October for the shorebird passage, and December to March for the sea duck and the inlet seawatch.
  • Getting there: Salisbury or Baltimore are the airports and Berlin the base. The seashore charges an entrance fee, the Ocean City jetty is free with a car park beside it, and the beach nesting areas are roped in summer.
  • Base yourself in: Berlin
  • Look for: Piping Plover, Least Tern, American Oystercatcher, Black Skimmer, Royal Tern, Purple Sandpiper, Long-tailed Duck, Surf Scoter, Northern Gannet, Red-throated Loon, Saltmarsh Sparrow, Snowy Owl
  • Also covers: Assateague Island National Seashore, Ocean City Inlet, Sinepuxent Bay
  • Also watch for: The wild horses on the island are the reason most visitors come, and bottlenose dolphins work the inlet in summer.

Blackwater and the Dorchester marshes

Tidal marsh sinking into the bay, with the densest breeding Bald Eagles on the Atlantic coast

Blackwater in Maryland holds the largest breeding Bald Eagle population on the Atlantic coast north of the Carolinas and a reintroduced Delmarva fox squirrel population, on marsh that is disappearing under rising water faster than almost anywhere else. The wildlife drive runs right through it.

  • Habitat: Brackish tidal marsh with open guts and ponds, loblolly pine on the higher ground, farmed impoundment, and drowned forest where the salt has killed the trees.
  • When: November to February for the geese, swans and raptors, and May to July for the marsh sparrows and rails.
  • Getting there: Baltimore or Salisbury are the airports and Cambridge the base. Blackwater has a paid four-mile wildlife drive and a visitor centre, and Deal Island is a free state area on gravel dyke roads.
  • Base yourself in: Cambridge
  • Look for: Bald Eagle, Northern Harrier, Short-eared Owl, Black Rail, Saltmarsh Sparrow, Seaside Sparrow, Glossy Ibis, Snow Goose, Tundra Swan, American Black Duck, Clapper Rail, Least Bittern
  • Also covers: Blackwater National Wildlife Refuge, Deal Island Wildlife Management Area, Fishing Bay Wildlife Management Area
  • Also watch for: The endangered Delmarva fox squirrel is here, along with river otter and the largest concentration of nesting Ospreys on the bay.

Eastern Neck and the Chesapeake points

The largest estuary in the country, holding a million wintering waterfowl

The Chesapeake takes about a million ducks, geese and swans each winter, and the necks and points that stick out into it are where a birder stands to watch them. Point Lookout at the mouth of the Potomac also holds migrants and gives a view of open water.

  • Habitat: Open estuary and tidal river, brackish marsh and tidal flat, sandy point and beach, mixed loblolly and hardwood on the necks, and farmed impoundment.
  • When: November to March for the wintering waterfowl, which is what this region is for, and September and October for the passage at Point Lookout.
  • Getting there: Baltimore is the airport and Chestertown the base for the upper bay. Eastern Neck has a free refuge road and trails, and both state parks charge a small entry fee.
  • Base yourself in: Chestertown
  • Look for: Tundra Swan, Canvasback, Redhead, Greater Scaup, Long-tailed Duck, Common Goldeneye, Bonaparte's Gull, Northern Gannet, Bald Eagle, Osprey, Brown Pelican, Royal Tern
  • Also covers: Eastern Neck National Wildlife Refuge, Point Lookout State Park, Sandy Point State Park
  • Also watch for: The bay carries the largest blue crab fishery in the country and its oyster reefs are slowly being rebuilt.

The piedmont and the Patuxent

Serpentine barrens and river valley forest between two cities

Patuxent is the oldest wildlife research refuge in the country and its Maryland forest and impoundments sit between two cities. Soldiers Delight nearby is serpentine grassland, a rock chemistry that supports a sparse open habitat found in very few places.

  • Habitat: Bottomland and upland hardwood forest, managed impoundment and marsh, serpentine grassland with stunted oak and prairie grasses, and river gorge with rapids.
  • When: Late April to June for the breeding birds, and October and November for the passage and the waterfowl on the impoundments.
  • Getting there: Baltimore and Washington airports are both close and Laurel in Maryland sits between them. Patuxent has free trails and a visitor centre, part of it is closed as active research land, and Soldiers Delight is free with marked paths.
  • Base yourself in: Laurel
  • Look for: Prothonotary Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Hooded Warbler, Louisiana Waterthrush, Acadian Flycatcher, Worm-eating Warbler, Wood Thrush, Scarlet Tanager, Barred Owl, Pileated Woodpecker, Prairie Warbler, Field Sparrow
  • Also covers: Patapsco Valley State Park, Patuxent Research Refuge, Soldiers Delight Natural Environment Area
  • Also watch for: The refuge holds the research flock history of the Whooping Crane reintroduction, and beaver have returned to the Patuxent.

The Allegheny highlands

Boreal bog and hemlock at three thousand feet, with birds from four hundred miles north

The western corner of the state sits above two and a half thousand feet, cold enough for sphagnum bog, tamarack and red spruce. Cranesville and Finzel are frost pockets holding plants and birds left behind when the ice retreated, and the breeding list there belongs in Canada.

  • Habitat: Sphagnum bog with tamarack and red spruce in frost pockets, hemlock along the streams, northern hardwood on the slopes, and high open pasture and reverting farmland.
  • When: Late May to July for the breeding birds. The highlands take heavy snow and the back roads can be difficult from December.
  • Getting there: Pittsburgh in Pennsylvania is the nearest airport, two hours north-west, and Oakland the base. Cranesville is a Nature Conservancy preserve with a boardwalk, and Finzel and Swallow Falls have free or low-cost access.
  • Base yourself in: Oakland
  • Look for: Northern Saw-whet Owl, Golden-winged Warbler, Mourning Warbler, Canada Warbler, Blackburnian Warbler, Magnolia Warbler, Black-throated Blue Warbler, Alder Flycatcher, Winter Wren, Veery, Bobolink, Northern Raven
  • Also covers: Cranesville Swamp, Finzel Swamp, Savage River State Forest, Swallow Falls State Park
  • Also watch for: The bogs hold carnivorous plants and the highlands carry black bear and a reintroduced fisher population.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Maryland?

January, if the Chesapeake is the point: about a million ducks, geese and swans winter on the bay and its marshes, and the Blackwater eagles are at their most numerous. June is the answer for the Allegheny bogs and the marsh sparrows.

Why does Blackwater matter?

It holds the densest breeding Bald Eagle population on the Atlantic coast north of the Carolinas, along with Black Rail and Saltmarsh Sparrow. It is also losing marsh to rising water faster than almost anywhere in the country, so the drowned forest along the drive is the story as much as the birds.

Where do I look for Black Rail?

The drier high marsh at Blackwater and Deal Island, after dark in June, by listening. It is among the hardest birds to actually see in North America, and the Maryland populations have fallen sharply, so a heard bird is the realistic goal.

Is the western corner really different?

Completely. Garrett County in western Maryland sits above two and a half thousand feet with sphagnum bog, tamarack and red spruce in the frost pockets, and the breeding list there includes Canada Warbler, Alder Flycatcher and Mourning Warbler. It is a three-hour drive and a different state entirely.

What is Soldiers Delight?

Serpentine barrens: a rock chemistry that most plants cannot tolerate, so the ground stays open with stunted oak and prairie grasses. Very few places have it, and the bird list there is grassland and scrub species inside the Baltimore suburbs.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
