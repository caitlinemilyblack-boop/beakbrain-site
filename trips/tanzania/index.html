Best months
  • June to October across the whole country
  • October and November for migrants, late January and February for the calving

Getting around
Arusha and Kilimanjaro serve the north, Dar es Salaam the south and the Eastern Arc. The northern circuit is driven; the southern parks and the islands are reached by short flights.

When to Go

The north runs two rainy seasons, the long rains from March to May and the short rains in November. The south runs one, from December to April, which is why the southern circuit is a dry-season trip from June to October. June to October works everywhere, and October brings the most migrants.

Dry season

June to October

The one stretch that works across the whole country. Water dries back to the rivers and everything concentrates on it.

Go for The Serengeti migration in the north, Ruaha and Nyerere at their best, and clear walking in the Eastern Arc.

Trade off The busiest and priciest months, and the northern circuit lodges book far ahead.

The Serengeti and the Western Corridor

Short-grass plain, kopjes and the largest land migration on earth

The Serengeti is 15,000 square kilometres of grassland with rock islands in it, and the wildebeest move round it in a slow annual circuit. The birding is open-country species and raptors on a scale no other park matches.

  • Habitat: The Serengeti is the second largest national park in Tanzania, a vast expanse of plains carrying wildebeest, zebra, gazelle and flat-topped acacias trimmed by giraffe. The short grass plains of the southeast give way to longer grass and more varied country once you pass Naabi Hill Gate on the road to Seronera in the centre. The terrain turns hilly and rocky in the north, and the west is a mosaic of hills, valleys, rivers, ribbons of riverine forest and plains. There is no fence, and animals graze on both sides of the southeastern boundary.
  • When: Good all year. Grey-breasted Spurfowl, Rufous-tailed Weaver and Kori Bustard stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Seronera is 300 km northwest of Arusha and the park opens year-round, though the long rains in April and May make some areas difficult. Seronera is the visitor hub, with maps, a shop and guides for hire, and guides can also be taken at the main gates.
  • Look for: Grey-breasted Spurfowl, Rufous-tailed Weaver, Fischer's Lovebird, Kori Bustard, Secretarybird, Southern Ground Hornbill
  • National parks:
    • Serengeti National Park, Grass plain, acacia woodland and granite kopjes, and the core of an ecosystem shared with the Kenyan Mara.
    • Grumeti Game Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The central valley with the park headquarters. It has permanent water and the highest year-round game density.

Ngorongoro and the Crater Highlands

A collapsed volcano with everything living inside it

The crater is 260 square kilometres of grassland and soda lake enclosed by a 600 m wall, and the animals in it mostly stay in it. The highlands around are farmed forest and moorland with a different bird list from the floor.

  • Habitat: The crater is the largest unbroken caldera in the world. Its rim stands at about 2,200 metres and the floor lies 600 metres below and 20 km across. Gorigor Swamp, Lake Magadi, Lerai Forest and open plain make up that floor. The crater is 3% of the conservation area, which also protects the Crater Highlands and the eastern Serengeti Plain.
  • When: Good all year. Lesser Flamingo, Grey Crowned Crane and Secretarybird hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: The rim is 165 km west of Arusha on good sealed road and open year-round. Descending into the crater needs 4WD, and a private vehicle takes a Conservation Area guide to enter. Hiking elsewhere in the Crater Highlands needs a guide too.
  • Look for: Lesser Flamingo, Grey Crowned Crane, Secretarybird, Kori Bustard, Ashy Starling
  • National park: Ngorongoro Conservation Area
  • Stops:
    • : The caldera floor, entered on one descent road, with Lake Magadi and the Lerai forest inside it.
    • : A smaller caldera north-east with a deep soda lake. You walk down to it, and it is usually empty of people.
    • : The highland farming town below the gate, where most crater trips sleep.
    • : The palaeoanthropology site on the road to the Serengeti, in dry short-grass country.
    • : Montane forest on the crater rim, with Hartlaub's Turaco at the lodges.
    • : Short-grass country inside the Ngorongoro Conservation Area rather than the park. The wildebeest calve here from January to March.

Tarangire, Manyara and the Rift Wall

Baobabs, a shallow soda lake and the wall above it

Tarangire is baobab and swamp along a river that holds the only water for miles in the dry season, so birds and mammals concentrate along it. Manyara below the Rift wall is groundwater forest, soda lake and escarpment in a narrow strip.

  • Habitat: Tarangire sits 30 km from the Rift Valley escarpment but outside the nutrient-rich volcanic belt, so rainfall runs low at 550 mm and much of it is semiarid country dominated by baobab and acacia. Manyara is a shallow alkaline Rift Valley lake 40 km long, and the park is the lake and its narrow shore, where water draining through aquifers at the foot of the Rift wall feeds a groundwater forest.
  • When: Good all year. Yellow-collared Lovebird, Ashy Starling and Rufous-tailed Weaver stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Tarangire is 120 km southwest of Arusha and some of it closes in the rains. Manyara is 120 km west of Arusha and its main park road is all-weather and fine for 2WD.
  • Look for: Yellow-collared Lovebird, Ashy Starling, Rufous-tailed Weaver, Lesser Flamingo, Southern Ground Hornbill
  • Also recorded in the parks here: African Grey Flycatcher, Black Bishop, Black-necked Weaver, Blue-capped Cordon-bleu, Chestnut Sparrow
  • National parks:
    • Tarangire National Park, Baobab and swamp on the Tarangire river, and the dry-season water for a much larger ecosystem.
    • Lake Manyara National Park, Groundwater forest, soda lake and the Rift escarpment in a strip a few kilometres wide.

Arusha and Mount Meru

A montane park on a city edge, and the mountain behind it

Arusha is where every northern trip starts, and the national park on its shoulder has crater lakes, montane forest and a 4,566 m volcano. It makes a first morning worth having before the long drive west.

The Usambaras and the Eastern Arc

Ancient forest islands with birds that evolved in them

The Eastern Arc mountains have been forested for perhaps thirty million years without interruption, which is why so much in them lives nowhere else. The East Usambaras at Amani are the most accessible piece and the richest.

  • Habitat: These mountains stand in northeast Tanzania and divide into the East Usambaras at 1500 m and the West Usambaras at 2250 m, separated by the Lwengera Valley. The forested slopes of both blocks carry many restricted range specialities and four endemics, and Usambara Akalat lives nowhere else. The East Usambaras alone hold eleven montane and five lowland forest reserves, and many of the specialities keep to either the montane ground or the lowland ground, so at least three reserves are worth working to see the full set.
  • When: The northeast is very wet from April to June. The two dry seasons run from January to March and from July to November, and both are the better times to come. Nights in the highlands turn chilly.
  • Getting there: The 1:50000 maps sold by the Lands Office in Dar es Salaam are the surest way to find the sites. Moustached Green-Tinkerbird, Long-billed Tailorbird, Dapple-throat and the Uluguru Violet-backed and Amani Sunbirds are in the Amani forest reserves in the East Usambaras, and Usambara Eagle-Owl is in the lowland Kambai reserve in the same block. Mazumbai forest reserve near Lushoto in the West Usambaras holds Usambara Akalat and Spot-throat, and the University of Morogoro research station there has a good trail. Usambara Weaver has been recorded at Mount Nilo in the Lutindi reserve.
  • Look for: Usambara Weaver, Usambara Akalat, Amani Sunbird, Long-billed Forest Warbler, Sharpe's Akalat
  • National park:
    • Amani Nature Reserve, Submontane forest in the East Usambaras, with a research station and trails from the rest house.
  • Stops:
    • : The West Usambara hill town, cooler and more farmed, with forest patches above it.
    • : A private forest estate near Muheza with a crater lake, and a quieter alternative to Amani.
    • : The junction town on the Tanga road where the Amani turning leaves.
    • : The northern block of East Usambara forest, higher and wetter than Amani.
  • Also covers: Amani Nature Reserve, Korogwe, Lushoto, Magoroto Forest, Muheza, Nilo Nature Reserve

The Udzungwa and Uluguru Mountains

The southern Eastern Arc, and a partridge found in 1991

The Udzungwas are the largest block in the Eastern Arc and the least explored, and a forest partridge new to science was found there in 1991. The Ulugurus above Morogoro are smaller, more threatened and hold their own bushshrike.

  • Habitat: The largest of the eleven Eastern Arc ranges rises sharply from the flats through lowland and submontane forest into montane forest, and tops out near 2,500 metres in short mountain grassland covered with ericas. Newer trails in the drier western section open a contrasting range.
  • When: Migrant birds are in from late September to mid-December, and highland forest birds drop lower to escape the cold from late May to early August.
  • Getting there: The main gate at Mang'ula is 75 km from Mikumi on local transport or a rented vehicle, and entry is also possible from Iringa through Msosa and Udekwa. Every walking trail needs a guide hired at park headquarters. The common birds show best just outside the park along the main road or at headquarters, where the open ground gives visibility.
  • Look for: Udzungwa Forest Partridge, Rufous-winged Sunbird, Iringa Akalat, Uluguru Bushshrike, Loveridge's Sunbird

    • Udzungwa Mountains National Park, The only Tanzanian park with no road through it. Walk in from Mang'ula on a network of trails.

Ruaha and the Southern Circuit

Miombo woodland, and the half of Tanzania the itineraries skip

Ruaha is the largest national park in the country and sits where the East African and Zambezian faunas meet, so it carries miombo birds the northern circuit never sees. No operator schedules it, and the miombo list is the reason to go anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Tanzania for birding?

June to October is the one stretch that works across the whole country. October and November bring the biggest migrant arrival, and late January to February is the calving on the Serengeti plains. April is difficult almost everywhere. The three seasons.

How many bird species does Tanzania have?

Over 1,100, and around thirty of them live in Tanzania and nowhere else, concentrated in the Eastern Arc forests and on Pemba. The number above counts the species with a card in the BeakBrain guide rather than a figure typed in by hand.

What is the Eastern Arc?

A chain of ancient crystalline mountains from the Taita Hills in Kenya down through the Usambaras to the Udzungwas, forested continuously for perhaps thirty million years. That continuity is why so much in them lives nowhere else, and a forest partridge new to science was found there in 1991.

Can you combine birding and a safari?

They are the same trip on the northern circuit. The Serengeti and Ngorongoro hold the open-country birds and raptors alongside the migration, and Tarangire is one of the best raptor sites in East Africa.

When is the wildebeest migration?

It is a year-round circuit rather than an event. Calving on the southern plains is late January to March, the Grumeti crossings are May to July, and the Mara river crossings are July to October. The timing moves with the rain.

Is the southern circuit worth it?

For birds, yes, and no operator schedules it. Ruaha sits where the East African and Zambezian faunas meet, so the miombo species the northern circuit never sees are all in it. The eight regions.

How do you get to Pemba?

A short flight from Zanzibar or Dar es Salaam, or the ferry from Unguja. Four birds live on the island and nowhere else, and all four are findable in a couple of days at Ngezi.

Do you need a guide?

For the parks a driver-guide is the normal arrangement. For the Eastern Arc it is close to necessary: the forests are steep, the trails are not obvious and the birds are found by call.

What other wildlife is there?

The full East African set on the northern circuit, wild dog in the southern miombo, and primates found nowhere else in the Eastern Arc, the Sanje mangabey and the Udzungwa red colobus among them.

How long do you need?

Seven days does the northern circuit. Five covers the Usambaras. Nine takes the southern parks. Sixteen puts the plains and the forests in one trip, which is what makes Tanzania different. Five routes.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
