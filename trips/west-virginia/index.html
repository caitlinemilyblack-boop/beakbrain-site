Birding in West Virginia

West Virginia holds sphagnum bog and stunted spruce at four thousand feet and a thousand-foot gorge cut by one of the oldest rivers on the continent, and the two are two hours apart.

Birds that decide the trip

Mourning Warbler in the Canaan beaver meadow

It skulks in dense wet regrowth and the beaver meadows in the valley floor keep producing exactly that, at close to the southern edge of its breeding range.

When June and July, in wet regrowth.

Where Canaan Valley and Dolly Sods

Cerulean Warbler on the gorge wall

It sings from the tops of tall hardwoods on the gorge sides, and the rim overlooks put a viewer level with a canopy that would otherwise be a hundred feet overhead.

When Late April to July, from the gorge rim overlooks.

Where The New River Gorge, The eastern panhandle ridges

Northern Saw-whet Owl in the high spruce

It breeds in the red spruce and northern hardwood above three thousand feet, at the southern end of a range that mostly lies far to the north, and it is found by listening after dark.

When April to June, after dark in the high forest.

Where Canaan Valley and Dolly Sods, The Monongahela high country

Alder Flycatcher in the valley alder

It sings a burry two-note phrase from alder thickets in wet ground, and the valley floor bogs are among the few places this far south that give it what it needs.

When June and July, from alder thickets.

Where Canaan Valley and Dolly Sods

Swainson's Warbler in the rhododendron

It keeps to dense rhododendron on wet ground and is heard far more than seen, which puts it in the same hollows as birds that belong hundreds of miles north.

When May and June, from thick cover at dawn.

Where The Monongahela high country

Golden Eagle down the ridge

The eastern population passes down the Appalachian ridges late in the season, and the Hanging Rock tower is one of the sites that sees them in early November, after most watchers have gone home.

When Late October to November, on a north-west wind.

Where The eastern panhandle ridges

Prothonotary Warbler on the Ohio islands

It nests in holes over standing water, and the wooded islands and restored floodplain wetlands are what is left of that habitat on a working river.

When April to July, over standing water.

Where The Ohio valley and the western hills

Red Crossbill in the restored spruce

It breeds whenever the cones are right rather than to a calendar, and the red spruce that is being deliberately restored on the high plateau is starting to carry it again.

When All year, in mature red spruce.

Where The Monongahela high country

When to Go

West Virginia is a June state. The high plateau breeders sing through it and the gorge warblers with them, and the one thing worth coming for outside that window is the autumn raptor flight down the eastern ridges.

Spring passage

Late April to May

Migrants move up the river valleys and the gorge warblers arrive.

Go for The New River Gorge rim, and the eastern panhandle ridges.

Trade off The high plateau is still cold and Dolly Sods can be under snow.

Typical daily average

The breeding season

Late May to July

The bogs and spruce hold their northern breeders and the gorge holds its warblers.

Go for Canaan Valley and Dolly Sods, Cranberry Glades, and the gorge overlooks.

Trade off The gorge fills with rafters and climbers and the high roads are gravel.

Typical daily average

The raptor flight

Mid-September to November

The Hanging Rock tower counts the autumn flight down the eastern ridge system.

Go for Hanging Rock on a north-west wind, and the panhandle ridges.

Trade off The breeding birds have gone and the high country is closing.

Typical daily average

The river winter

November to February

Waterfowl and eagles hold the Ohio River islands and the restored wetlands behind them.

Go for The Ohio River refuge and the western wildlife areas.

Trade off The high roads are shut and the forest is silent.

An open-jaw run south in June, from bog and heath barren at four thousand feet down into a thousand-foot gorge. Canaan and Dolly Sods first, then Cranberry Glades and Spruce Knob, and a finish on the New River rim. About 300 miles on slow ridge roads.

Four days in June in the highest valley of its size east of the Rockies. Alder Flycatcher and Mourning Warbler in the beaver meadows, Northern Saw-whet Owl after dark, and heath barren on the plateau above with wind-flagged spruce. Take a coat in any month.

Washington Dulles (IAD), Berkeley Springs 3 nights, back to Dulles

Three days in late September on the last Appalachian ridges before the coastal plain. The Hanging Rock tower counts the flight down Peters Mountain, and the drier oak and shale barrens below hold a warbler list that the high country does not.

Canaan Valley and Dolly Sods

A bog-floored valley at three thousand feet, with heath barrens on the plateau above it

Canaan Valley is the highest valley of its size east of the Rockies, with sphagnum bog and beaver meadow on its floor, and Dolly Sods above it is heath barren and stunted spruce shaped by wind off the Allegheny Front. Both hold birds that belong in Canada.

  • Habitat: Sphagnum bog and beaver meadow on the valley floor, red spruce and northern hardwood on the slopes, heath barren with wind-flagged spruce on the plateau, and sandstone outcrop.
  • When: Late May to July for the breeding birds. Dolly Sods can be cold and foggy at any time and the roads to it are gravel.
  • Getting there: Pittsburgh in Pennsylvania is the nearest airport, three hours north, and Davis the base. The refuge and the wilderness are free, and the Dolly Sods roads are rough gravel that close in winter.
  • Base yourself in: Davis
  • Look for: Northern Saw-whet Owl, Alder Flycatcher, Mourning Warbler, Canada Warbler, Magnolia Warbler, Blackburnian Warbler, Nashville Warbler, Winter Wren, Hermit Thrush, Golden-crowned Kinglet, Northern Raven, American Woodcock
  • Parks and reserves: Canaan Valley National Wildlife Refuge, Dolly Sods Wilderness (Protected area), Blackwater Falls State Park (State or provincial park), Monongahela National Forest - Canaan Mountain (Forest or grassland), Potomac Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge)
  • Also covers: Blackwater Falls State Park, Canaan Valley National Wildlife Refuge, Dolly Sods Wilderness, Monongahela National Forest - Canaan Mountain, Potomac Wildlife Management Area
  • Also watch for: The valley holds snowshoe hare at the southern edge of their range and the plateau carries black bear in numbers.

The Monongahela high country

The highest ground in the state, with a bog at four thousand feet and a red spruce restoration around it

Spruce Knob reaches 4,863 feet, the highest point in the state. The red spruce that once covered this whole plateau was logged off and is now being deliberately restored. Cranberry Glades is a set of open sphagnum bogs with a boardwalk, holding plants that belong hundreds of miles north.

  • Habitat: Red spruce and northern hardwood on the high plateau, open sphagnum bog with cranberry and orchid, rhododendron thickets in the hollows, and sandstone outcrop on the knobs.
  • When: Late May to July for the breeding birds. Spruce Knob is cold and exposed and the summit road is gravel.
  • Getting there: Charleston or Pittsburgh are the airports and Marlinton the base. The forest is free with gravel roads, Cranberry Glades has a free boardwalk with a nearby visitor centre, and Spruce Knob has a road to the summit.
  • Base yourself in: Marlinton
  • Look for: Northern Saw-whet Owl, Canada Warbler, Blackburnian Warbler, Magnolia Warbler, Black-throated Blue Warbler, Mourning Warbler, Swainson's Warbler, Winter Wren, Hermit Thrush, Veery, Ruffed Grouse, Red Crossbill
  • Parks and reserves: Monongahela National Forest - Canaan Mountain (Forest or grassland), Spruce Knob-Seneca Rocks National Recreation Area (National site), Potomac Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Cranberry Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Cranberry Wilderness (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Cranberry Glades Botanical Area, Cranberry Wilderness, Cranberry Wildlife Management Area, Monongahela National Forest - Canaan Mountain, Potomac Wildlife Management Area, Spruce Knob-Seneca Rocks National Recreation Area
  • Also watch for: The spruce restoration is aimed at the Cheat Mountain salamander and the northern flying squirrel, which came off the endangered list because of it.

The New River Gorge

A thousand-foot gorge cut by one of the oldest rivers on the continent

The New River is among the oldest rivers in the world and it has cut a gorge a thousand feet deep through the Appalachian plateau. The forest on those walls is mixed mesophytic, which is the most species-rich temperate forest type anywhere, and the breeding warbler list follows from that.

  • Habitat: Mixed mesophytic forest on steep gorge walls, sandstone cliff and boulder field, rhododendron along the streams, and reclaimed mine benches on the rim.
  • When: Late April to June for the breeding birds. The gorge fills with rafters and climbers from May onwards.
  • Getting there: Charleston is the airport, an hour west, and Fayetteville the base. The park is free with several rim overlooks and a visitor centre, and Babcock charges a small state park fee.
  • Base yourself in: Fayetteville
  • Look for: Cerulean Warbler, Worm-eating Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Hooded Warbler, Louisiana Waterthrush, Acadian Flycatcher, Wood Thrush, Scarlet Tanager, Yellow-throated Warbler, Barred Owl, Peregrine Falcon, Eastern Whip-poor-will
  • Parks and reserves: New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, Babcock State Park (State or provincial park), Bluestone National Scenic River (Protected area), New River Gorge National Preserve, New River Gorge National Park
  • Also covers: Babcock State Park, Bluestone National Scenic River, New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, New River Gorge National Preserve
  • Also watch for: Peregrines were reintroduced to the gorge cliffs and black bear are common through the whole park.

The eastern panhandle ridges

The last Appalachian ridges before the coastal plain, with a hawk watch on one of them

The eastern panhandle is a series of long parallel ridges running north-east, drier and warmer than the high country to the west. The Hanging Rock tower on Peters Mountain counts the autumn raptor flight down that ridge system, and the forest below holds the warmer-climate warblers.

  • Habitat: Oak and hickory on long parallel sandstone ridges, pine and heath on the dry crests, mountain laurel thickets, and shale barrens with plants found in very few places.
  • When: Late April to June for the breeding birds, and mid-September to November for the raptor flight at Hanging Rock.
  • Getting there: Washington Dulles is the airport, ninety minutes east, and Berkeley Springs the base. The wildlife management area is free, the state park charges a small fee, and the Hanging Rock tower is a walk in from a forest road.
  • Base yourself in: Berkeley Springs
  • Look for: Broad-winged Hawk, Sharp-shinned Hawk, Golden Eagle, American Goshawk, Peregrine Falcon, Cerulean Warbler, Worm-eating Warbler, Prairie Warbler, Blue-winged Warbler, Yellow-breasted Chat, Eastern Whip-poor-will, Northern Bobwhite
  • Parks and reserves: Cacapon Resort State Park (State or provincial park), Sleepy Creek Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Jefferson National Forest (Forest or grassland), Potts Creek Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Chesapeake and Ohio Canal National Historical Park (National site)
  • Also covers: Cacapon Resort State Park, Chesapeake and Ohio Canal National Historical Park, Hanging Rock Raptor Observatory, Jefferson National Forest, Potts Creek Wildlife Management Area, Sleepy Creek Wildlife Management Area
  • Also watch for: The ridges hold timber rattlesnake and one of the state's larger black bear populations.

The Ohio valley and the western hills

Islands in a working river, and wetlands rebuilt on the floodplain behind them

The Ohio River refuge protects a chain of islands along nearly four hundred miles of a heavily used river, which is what is left of the bottomland habitat. McClintic and Green Bottom on the floodplain are restored wetlands that hold the state's marsh birds.

  • Habitat: Wooded river islands and sandbar, restored wetland and shallow pond on the floodplain, bottomland hardwood, and old field on the terraces.
  • When: April to July for the breeding marsh birds, and November to February for waterfowl and eagles on the river.
  • Getting there: Charleston or Huntington are the airports and Point Pleasant the base. The island refuge is reached by boat with some units open to landing, and both wildlife areas are free with gravel roads and dykes.
  • Base yourself in: Point Pleasant
  • Look for: Prothonotary Warbler, Least Bittern, American Bittern, Sora, Virginia Rail, Marsh Wren, Wood Duck, Bald Eagle, Osprey, Sedge Wren, Willow Flycatcher, Yellow-breasted Chat
  • Parks and reserves: Ohio River Islands National Wildlife Refuge, McClintic Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Green Bottom Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Ohio River Islands National Wildlife Refuge - Gallipolis Island, Wayne National Forest - Marietta Unit (Forest or grassland)
  • Also covers: Green Bottom Wildlife Management Area, McClintic Wildlife Management Area, Ohio River Islands National Wildlife Refuge - Gallipolis Island, Wayne National Forest - Marietta Unit
  • Also watch for: The islands hold freshwater mussel beds that are among the richest left on the river, and beaver work the restored wetlands.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in West Virginia?

June, when the high plateau bogs and spruce hold their northern breeders and the New River Gorge warblers are singing at the same time. Late September to November is the alternative, for the raptor flight down the eastern ridges.

What is Dolly Sods?

A high West Virginia plateau of heath barren, sphagnum bog and stunted wind-flagged spruce on the Allegheny Front, at about four thousand feet. It looks and sounds like eastern Canada, and the roads to it are rough gravel that close in winter.

Why is the red spruce being replanted?

The plateau was logged off and burned in the early twentieth century and the spruce forest that covered it never came back on its own. The restoration is aimed at the Cheat Mountain salamander and the northern flying squirrel, and the birds are benefiting alongside them.

What makes the New River Gorge forest special?

Mixed mesophytic forest, which is the most species-rich temperate forest type in the world, on the walls of a thousand-foot gorge. That combination of depth, aspect and moisture gives an unusually long breeding warbler list in a small area.

How slow are the roads?

Slower than the map suggests. West Virginia is all ridge and valley, so almost no road goes in a straight line, and the routes to Dolly Sods and Spruce Knob are gravel. Allow half again what a distance looks like it should take.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
