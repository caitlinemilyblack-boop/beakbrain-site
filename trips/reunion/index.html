Birds that decide the trip

Réunion Cuckooshrike on the Roche Écrite

The tuit-tuit is the rarest bird on the island and lives on one massif above Saint-Denis. Rat and cat control along the ridge has taken it back from the edge, and it answers a walker who waits.

When September to December

Where Roche Écrite

Barau’s Petrel over the west coast

The petrel breeds high in the mountains and crosses the west coast at dawn and dusk on its way there. It was described only in 1964 and the whole world population nests on this island.

When October to March

Where Saint-Paul

Réunion Harrier over the cirques

The papangue is the only bird of prey that breeds on Réunion and it quarters the slopes above the cirques. The male turns grey and white with age and the female stays brown.

When All year

Where Cilaos

Réunion Stonechat on the high heath

The tec-tec sits on the scrub at the edge of the lava and follows walkers along the path. It is the easiest of the endemics to find and the one people remember.

When All year

Where Fournaise

Mascarene Paradise Flycatcher at Bébour

The flycatcher works the understorey of the tamarin forest and hovers to take insects off the leaves. The male carries a long tail in the breeding season.

When September to January

Where Bébour

Réunion Olive White-eye in the mountain forest

The olive white-eye keeps to native forest and nectar, which is why it is scarcer than its grey cousin. It is found above about 1,000 metres.

When All year

Where Cilaos

Réunion Grey White-eye everywhere

The grey white-eye lives from the shore to the summit and changes plumage from one valley to the next. It is the bird a visitor sees first.

When All year

Where Saint-Paul

Mascarene Petrel, described from a handful of birds

The Mascarene Petrel nests somewhere in the island’s cliffs and its colony was found only in this century. Most records are of birds grounded by lights.

When November to March

Where Saint-Paul

Mascarene Swiftlet over the ravines

The swiftlet nests in lava tubes and ravine caves and feeds over the forest all day. It is shared with Mauritius and found nowhere else.

When All year

Where Salazie

La Roche Écrite and the Tuit-tuit

The Réunion Cuckooshrike, down to a few dozen pairs on one massif

The Réunion Cuckooshrike, which the island calls the tuit-tuit, holds on in the forest of the Roche Écrite massif above Saint-Denis and nowhere else. SEOR has trapped rats and cats along the ridge for years and the population has answered.

  • Habitat: Wet mountain forest on a basalt massif, tamarin des hauts above the cloud line and mixed native forest below it.
  • When: September to December, when the cuckooshrike calls through the morning.
  • Getting there: Saint-Denis is twenty minutes below and Roland Garros is the airport. The Mamode Camp track leaves the Brûlé road and the walk to the ridge takes half a day.
  • Base yourself in: Saint-Denis
  • All year: Reunion Cuckooshrike, Reunion Bulbul, Reunion Olive White-eye, Reunion Grey White-eye

  • Also watch for: Panther chameleons sit on the roadside branches and the tamarin des hauts is the tree the whole forest is named for.

The cirques: Cilaos, Mafate and Salazie

Three collapsed calderas, and the island’s endemic passerines through all of them

The three cirques are what is left where the Piton des Neiges fell in on itself. Their forest carries the Réunion Stonechat, the Réunion Bulbul and both white-eyes, and the harrier hunts the open slopes above them.

  • Habitat: Mountain forest and heath in three collapsed calderas, with tamarin forest on the high ground and cliffs between them.
  • When: May to October for settled walking weather. The cirques are cool at night all year.
  • Getting there: Roads climb into Cilaos and Salazie from the coast and both take an hour of hairpins. Mafate has no road at all and is walked in or reached by helicopter.

Bébour-Bélouve and the eastern rainforest

Tamarin forest on the windward slope, and the paradise flycatcher in it

The forest between Bélouve and Bébour is the largest block of native woodland left on Réunion and the wettest ground on the island lies under it. The Mascarene Paradise Flycatcher hunts the understorey and the Réunion Bulbul calls from the canopy.

  • Habitat: Tamarin des hauts forest on a high windward plateau, tree fern understorey, and mossy cloud forest along the rim.
  • When: September to December for the breeding birds. Rain is likely in any month here.
  • Getting there: The Bélouve road climbs from Hell-Bourg in Salazie and the Bébour forest road runs on from the Plaine des Palmistes.
  • Base yourself in: La Plaine-des-Palmistes

Piton de la Fournaise and the volcano heath

Bare lava and heath on one of the most active volcanoes on earth

The Piton de la Fournaise erupts most years and the Plaine des Sables in front of it is a desert of ash and lava. The harrier quarters the heath on the way up and the Réunion Stonechat sits on the scrub at the edge of the flows.

  • Habitat: Heath and scrub on old lava, bare ash on the Plaine des Sables, and rainforest on the seaward slope below.
  • When: May to October, when the road to the Pas de Bellecombe is most often clear of cloud.
  • Getting there: The RF5 climbs from Bourg-Murat to the Pas de Bellecombe in an hour. The road closes when the volcano is active.
  • Base yourself in: Bourg-Murat
  • All year: Reunion Stonechat, Reunion Harrier, Mascarene Martin, Reunion Grey White-eye

  • Stops:

  • Also covers: Bourg-Murat, Bras de la Plaine, Parc national de La Réunion, Piton de la Fournaise, Plaine des Sables, Réserve biologique intégrale des Hauts de Saint-Philippe
  • Also watch for: The Plaine des Sables is a red ash desert that film crews use for Mars, and the lava reaches the sea in most eruptions.

The west coast and the Étang de Saint-Paul

The island’s one large wetland, and the petrels passing offshore

The Étang de Saint-Paul is the largest freshwater wetland in the Mascarenes and it holds the island’s herons, moorhens and migrant waders. Barau’s Petrel passes the coast morning and evening in the breeding season on its way to the highland colonies.

  • Habitat: A coastal freshwater marsh behind a shingle bar, reedbed and open water, with dry savanna on the slopes above.
  • When: October to March for Barau’s Petrel offshore. The wetland works all year.
  • Getting there: Saint-Paul is twenty minutes from the airport on the coast road and the reserve has marked paths from the town.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
