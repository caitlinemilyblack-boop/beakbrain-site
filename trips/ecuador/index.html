Getting around
Quito is the hub and no region here is more than a day from it. Mindo is ninety minutes by road and the Amazon is a forty-minute flight to Coca and then a boat.

When to Go

Ecuador sits on the equator, so the year turns on rain rather than on temperature and every altitude keeps its own climate the whole year round. The Andean dry season is June to September. The west slope is misty most mornings whatever the month, which is what makes it a cloud forest.

Peak

June to September

The Andean dry season. Clear mornings on the paramo, the eastern slope roads at their most reliable, and low water in the Amazon.

Go for Condors over Antisana, the full west-slope hummingbird list, and Amazon trails walkable rather than paddled.

Trade off The busiest and priciest months, and the lodges on the Mindo road book furthest ahead.

Typical daily average
  • Quito: 8 to 21 °C / 46 to 70 °F
  • The Amazon: 20 to 30 °C / 68 to 86 °F
  • Mindo: 14 to 24 °C / 57 to 75 °F

Shoulder

October and November

Rain returns to the highlands while the fruiting season starts on the west slope, so the trade is weather against wildlife.

Go for Spectacled bears at Maquipucuna, the antpitta stations running as usual, and fewer people on the roads.

Trade off Afternoon rain most days in the Andes, and the high paramo turns unreliable late in November.

Typical daily average
  • Quito: 9 to 21 °C / 48 to 70 °F
  • The Amazon: 20 to 31 °C / 68 to 88 °F
  • Mindo: 14 to 24 °C / 57 to 75 °F

Green season

December to May

Andean rain at its heaviest, high water in the Amazon, and the warm wet season that makes the Tumbesian dry forest green and full of song.

Go for Tumbesian endemics breeding, flooded forest reachable by canoe, and the lowest rates of the year.

Trade off Rain on both Andean slopes and rougher going on the unpaved roads to Canande and the Nangaritza.

Typical daily average
  • Quito: 9 to 21 °C / 48 to 70 °F
  • The Amazon: 20 to 30 °C / 68 to 86 °F
  • Guayaquil: 21 to 31 °C / 70 to 88 °F

Mindo and the Northwest Slope

Choco cloud forest and hummingbird feeders an hour and a half from the capital

The west slope drops from the Quito ridge into Choco rainforest, and a chain of reserves along it has turned feeders and antpitta stations into the most productive morning of birding in the country. Eleven of the forty-four published Ecuadorian itineraries are built on this road.

Open paramo and glacial lakes above 4,000 m, ninety minutes from Quito

The Choco Lowlands and the North Coast

Wet lowland Choco forest, the rarest habitat block above

Below the Mindo slope the Choco reaches the coast, and almost all of it has gone to oil palm and cattle. What remains is held in a few private reserves, and it carries birds found nowhere on the east slope at all.

  • When: Good all year. Long-wattled Umbrellabird, Rose-faced Parrot and Choco Toucan are here in every month.
  • Look for: Banded Ground Cuckoo, Long-wattled Umbrellabird, Great Green Macaw, Rose-faced Parrot, Choco Toucan, Pale-mandibled Aracari, Scarlet-breasted Dacnis
  • National park: Mache-Chindul Ecological Reserve

Cuenca and the Cajas

Polylepis woodland and paramo lakes above a colonial city

The Cajas sits on the continental divide an hour above Cuenca, a plateau of hundreds of glacial lakes with polylepis woodland in the hollows. The road across it is paved and the birding is done from it.

  • When: Good all year. Violet-throated Metaltail, Tit-like Dacnis and Andean Gull stay through the year.
  • Look for: Violet-throated Metaltail, Tit-like Dacnis, Andean Gull, Andean Teal, Rainbow-bearded Thornbill, Tawny Antpitta

Loja, Podocarpus and Tapichalaca

The southern Andes, where the cordillera drops and the biogeography changes

The Andes fall below 3,000 m here and the wet and dry systems meet across a short distance, which is why the south holds so many birds found in no other part of the country. The Jocotoco Foundation was founded on the discovery of an antpitta at Tapichalaca in 1997.

  • When: Good all year. Jocotoco Antpitta, Bearded Guan and White-breasted Parakeet are on site in all twelve months.
  • Look for: Jocotoco Antpitta, Bearded Guan, White-breasted Parakeet, Neblina Metaltail, Rainbow Starfrontlet, Coppery-chested Jacamar, Grey-breasted Mountain Toucan
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Ash-browed Spinetail, Ash-colored Tapaculo, Ashy-throated Chlorospingus

  • Also watch for: Spectacled bear and mountain tapir in the Podocarpus highlands.

The Cordillera del Condor and the Southeast Foothills

Sandstone tabletop forest on the Peruvian border, and a bird list this country only recently began

The Cordillera del Condor is a separate range east of the main Andes, capped with sandstone and holding plants and birds shared with the Guianan shield rather than with the rest of Ecuador. Access is recent and difficult, and the Orange-throated Tanager is why people come.

  • When: Good all year. Orange-throated Tanager, Coppery-chested Jacamar and Wire-crested Thorntail are here in every month.

The Tumbesian Southwest: Buenaventura and Jorupe

Dry ceibo forest and humid foothills, an endemism centre shared with Peru

The Tumbesian region runs down the coast into Peru and holds more restricted-range birds than anywhere else in Ecuador. Two Jocotoco reserves cover its two halves: humid foothill forest at Buenaventura and dry ceibo woodland at Jorupe.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Ecuador for birding?

June to September is the Andean dry season and the busiest window, with clear paramo mornings and low water in the Amazon. Ecuador sits on the equator, so birding is good all year and the real choice is which weather you would rather have. The three seasons, and what each one is for.

How many bird species does Ecuador have?

Around 1,600 on a country the size of Britain, which is more birds per square kilometre than anywhere else on earth. The number above counts the species with a card in the BeakBrain guide rather than a figure typed in by hand.

Where do you go birding in Ecuador?

The west slope around Mindo is the most productive single road, and most trips are built on it. The Napo reaches the richest lowland forest, the eastern slope lodges cover everything between, and the south holds the endemics. The ten regions.

What are the Amazon jungle lodges in Ecuador like?

They sit on the Napo below Coca. A short flight and then a boat of one to three hours gets you there, and the good ones are built around a canopy tower or walkway, because that is where the birds are. Several are community-owned. The regions and what each holds.

Are there eco lodges in Ecuador?

The strongest are the reserve lodges: places whose bed nights pay for the forest around them. The Jocotoco Foundation runs several, Sani Lodge is owned by the Sani Isla Kichwa community, and the Mindo reserves fund their own antpitta and hummingbird work.

Is Ecuador or Peru the better first Neotropical trip?

Ecuador packs the same altitudinal gradient into far shorter drives, so a first trip with limited time goes further here: Mindo is ninety minutes from the airport and the Amazon is a forty-minute flight. Peru rewards the longer trip with a bigger list. Countries that combine well with this one.

Do you need to worry about altitude?

Quito is at 2,800 m and the Papallacta pass tops 4,100 m, so a night or two in the city before any high paramo day is the standard advice. Every route above can be planned to descend rather than climb after arrival.

What are the antpitta feeding stations?

A farmer near Mindo called Angel Paz found in 2005 that a wild Giant Antpitta would come to worms and a call, and the idea spread. Several reserves now run stations where two to four antpitta species appear at dawn, which turned some of the hardest birds in the Neotropics into a reliable morning.

Can you combine the mainland with the Galapagos?

Easily, and many operators sell it as one trip. They are entirely different: the islands are a boat, a short list and tame birds, while the mainland is a road and 1,600 species. The islands have their own page here.

What other wildlife is there?

Spectacled bears come down to fruiting trees at Maquipucuna from October to December, giant otter and pink river dolphin live on the Napo oxbows, and eleven primate species have been recorded around Yasuni.

Do you need a guide?

For the Amazon lodges a guide comes with the stay, and most run two: a naturalist and a local Kichwa guide. On the Mindo road and the eastern slope a driver who knows the stops saves days, and the antpitta stations need the person who does the calling.

How long do you need?

Five days reaches the Mindo road properly. Eight covers both slopes. Ten adds the Amazon. Eighteen takes the whole mainland from Quito to the Peruvian border. Five routes.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
