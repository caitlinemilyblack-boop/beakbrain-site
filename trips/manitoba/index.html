Birding in Manitoba

Churchill sits where boreal forest, tundra and a salt sea meet, and a road system of about sixty kilometres reaches all three, which is why it is the most visited arctic birding place on earth.

Birds that decide the trip

Ross's Gull at Churchill

A small pink gull that breeds in Siberia and has nested on the tundra ponds here, which is what made Churchill famous. It is not annual and the place is worth the trip anyway.

When June, on the tundra ponds.

Where Churchill and Hudson Bay

Hudsonian Godwit displaying on the tundra

It breeds on the wet tundra here and display-flights over the polygon ponds in June, which is the only time of year the species is easy to watch anywhere.

When June, over the tundra ponds.

Where Churchill and Hudson Bay

Smith's Longspur at the treeline

It breeds in a narrow band where stunted spruce meets tundra, and Churchill is one of very few places where a road reaches that band at all.

When June, in the treeline zone.

Where Churchill and Hudson Bay

Great Grey Owl in the escarpment forest

The provincial bird, hunting voles from low perches at meadow edges in the last hour of light, and Riding Mountain is the reliable place to look.

When All year, at meadow edges at dusk.

Where Riding Mountain and the western uplands

Yellow Rail in the tallgrass swale

It calls like two stones tapped together, only after dark, from wet sedge. The preserve and the Whitewater margins are where the province still has it.

When June, after dark in wet sedge.

Where The Tall Grass Prairie Preserve, Riding Mountain and the western uplands

Snow Goose over Oak Hammock

The autumn passage through the Red River valley runs into the hundreds of thousands, and the birds come off the marsh at first light in a continuous stream.

When October, at dawn and dusk from the dykes.

Where Oak Hammock and the Red River valley

Connecticut Warbler on the shield

It walks rather than hops, sings from high in a tamarack and then vanishes, and the jack pine and muskeg of the eastern shield hold it an hour from the city.

When June, in bog and jack pine at dawn.

Where The eastern shield

Piping Plover on the alkali shore

The northern Great Plains population nests on bare alkali and gravel lake shorelines far from any sea, and Whitewater Lake holds a share of it when the water is right.

When May to July, on bare alkali shoreline.

Where Riding Mountain and the western uplands

When to Go

Manitoba has one date that decides everything: the first three weeks of June at Churchill, when the tundra thaws and the birds arrive at once. The southern half runs a normal prairie calendar from mid-May, and the goose passages are in April and October.

The spring goose passage

April

Snow Geese and Canada Geese move north through the Red River valley marshes.

Go for Oak Hammock Marsh and the Interlake, and Delta Marsh on the big lake.

Trade off Churchill is still frozen and the southern breeders have not arrived.

Typical daily average

The southern breeding season

Mid-May to July

The prairie remnants, the parkland and the eastern shield hold their breeding lists.

Go for The Tall Grass Prairie Preserve, Riding Mountain and Whiteshell.

Trade off Churchill is at its best in a narrow overlapping window and it is expensive.

Typical daily average

The Churchill window

The first three weeks of June

The tundra thaws, the shorebirds display and the sea ice breaks up all in about twenty days.

Go for Churchill: the Cape Merry rocks, the tundra roads and the boreal edge.

Trade off Flights and beds book out a year ahead and the weather can be freezing in June.

Typical daily average

The autumn passage

September and October

Geese come back through the south and Churchill takes late shorebirds and the first white birds.

Go for Oak Hammock and Delta Marsh, and Churchill for the beluga and bear season.

Trade off The southern breeders have gone and the northern days shorten fast.

Churchill and Hudson Bay

Where boreal forest, tundra and a salt sea meet, on sixty kilometres of road

Churchill is the most visited arctic birding place on earth for one reason: three biomes meet there and a short road system reaches all of them. Ross's Gull has nested on the tundra ponds, the estuary holds beluga whales in thousands, and the whole season is about three weeks long.

  • Habitat: Tundra with polygon ponds and rock ridge, stunted spruce at the treeline, salt marsh and tidal flat on the bay, and a large river estuary.
  • When: The first three weeks of June, when the tundra thaws and everything arrives at once. Late July for the beluga and October for the bears.
  • Getting there: There is no road to Churchill. A flight from Winnipeg or the two-night train are the only ways in, vehicles are hired locally, and beds book out a year ahead.
  • Base yourself in: Churchill
  • Look for: Ross's Gull, Pacific Loon, Red-throated Loon, King Eider, Willow Ptarmigan, Rock Ptarmigan, Hudsonian Godwit, Stilt Sandpiper, American Golden Plover, Smith's Longspur, Harris's Sparrow, Snowy Owl
  • Also covers: Cape Merry, Churchill River estuary, Twin Lakes Road
  • Also watch for: Polar bears come ashore as the ice breaks, and the estuary holds several thousand beluga whales through the summer.

Oak Hammock and Delta Marsh

Restored marsh at the bottom of a lake basin, taking hundreds of thousands of geese

Oak Hammock is a small remnant of a very large marsh that was drained for farming and then partly rebuilt, and in autumn it holds geese in the hundreds of thousands. Delta Marsh on Lake Manitoba is one of the largest freshwater coastal marshes in North America and runs a long-standing bird observatory.

  • Habitat: Restored cattail and bulrush marsh with managed cells, a very large freshwater coastal marsh on the lake, ridge forest on the beach bars, and cropland between.
  • When: April and October for the goose passage, and May to July for the marsh breeders.
  • Getting there: Winnipeg is the airport and the base, with Oak Hammock half an hour north. The interpretive centre charges admission and the dyke trails are free, and Delta Marsh is reached on township roads.
  • Base yourself in: Winnipeg
  • Look for: Snow Goose, Canada Goose, Greater White-fronted Goose, Franklin's Gull, Black Tern, Yellow-headed Blackbird, American White Pelican, Western Grebe, Black-necked Grebe, Least Bittern, Sora, Virginia Rail
  • Parks and reserves: Oak Hammock Marsh (Protected area), Delta Marsh Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Winnipeg Beach Provincial Park (State or provincial park), Delta Marsh Wildlife Managemente Area (Protected area), Oak Hammock Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge)
  • Also covers: Delta Marsh Wildlife Management Area, Delta Marsh Wildlife Managemente Area, Netley-Libau Marsh, Oak Hammock Marsh, Oak Hammock Wildlife Management Area, Winnipeg Beach Provincial Park
  • Also watch for: The marsh holds muskrat in numbers that keep it open, and the Delta Marsh observatory has banded there since 1938.

Riding Mountain and the escarpment

An escarpment island of forest above the plains, with a shallow lake and a sand dune field nearby

Riding Mountain stands on the Manitoba Escarpment as an island of boreal and hardwood forest above farmland, and it holds a fenced plains bison herd and a full forest breeding list. Whitewater Lake south of it is a shallow alkaline lake that takes shorebirds when it is low, and Spruce Woods has an active sand dune field.

  • Habitat: Boreal and hardwood forest on an escarpment plateau, fescue grassland and aspen on the slopes, shallow alkaline lake and mudflat, and active sand dunes and mixed-grass prairie at Spruce Woods.
  • When: Mid-May to July for the forest and grassland breeders, and August and September for the Whitewater Lake shorebirds when the water is low.
  • Getting there: Winnipeg is the airport, three hours east, and Brandon the base. The national park charges an entry fee, and Whitewater Lake is watched from municipal grid roads.
  • Base yourself in: Brandon
  • Look for: Great Grey Owl, Barred Owl, Broad-winged Hawk, Black-backed Woodpecker, Canada Jay, Boreal Chickadee, Yellow Rail, Piping Plover, American Avocet, Wilson's Phalarope, Marbled Godwit, LeConte's Sparrow
  • Parks and reserves: Riding Mountain National Park, Spruce Woods Provincial Park (State or provincial park), Whitewater Lake Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Brandon Hills Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge), Douglas Marsh Protected Area
  • Also covers: Brandon Hills Wildlife Management Area, Douglas Marsh Protected Area, Riding Mountain National Park, Spruce Woods Provincial Park, Whitewater Lake Wildlife Management Area
  • Also watch for: Riding Mountain holds a fenced plains bison herd, elk and one of the more studied black bear populations in the province.

The eastern shield

Precambrian rock, jack pine and ten thousand lakes, starting an hour from the city

The Canadian Shield reaches its south-western corner here, and Whiteshell and Nopiming are granite, jack pine and lake within an easy drive of Winnipeg. The boreal breeding list starts abruptly at the rock edge, which makes the change from farmland to shield unusually sharp.

  • Habitat: Granite outcrop and jack pine on Precambrian rock, black spruce muskeg in the hollows, thousands of lakes and rivers, and wild rice beds in the shallows.
  • When: June and early July for the boreal breeders. Blackflies are severe through June.
  • Getting there: Winnipeg is the airport, ninety minutes west, and Pinawa the base. The provincial parks charge a vehicle pass and the access roads are paved to the main lakes and gravel beyond.
  • Base yourself in: Pinawa
  • Look for: Common Loon, Bald Eagle, Osprey, Spruce Grouse, Black-backed Woodpecker, American Three-toed Woodpecker, Canada Jay, Boreal Chickadee, Connecticut Warbler, Cape May Warbler, Bay-breasted Warbler, Northern Saw-whet Owl
  • Parks and reserves: Whiteshell Provincial Park (State or provincial park), Nopiming Provincial Park (State or provincial park), Pinawa Dam Provincial Park (State or provincial park), Grand Beach Provincial Park (State or provincial park), Whitemouth Falls Provincial Park (State or provincial park)
  • Also covers: Grand Beach Provincial Park, Lake Winnipeg east shore, Nopiming Provincial Park, Pinawa Dam Provincial Park, Whitemouth Falls Provincial Park, Whiteshell Provincial Park
  • Also watch for: Wolves and moose hold the shield, and the shallow lakes carry wild rice beds that the rock country would not otherwise support.

The Tall Grass Prairie Preserve

The largest piece of tallgrass prairie left in the country, on ground too wet to plough

Canada had about a million hectares of tallgrass prairie and has a fraction of one per cent of it left, and this preserve in the far south-east is the largest single piece. It survived because the ground is too wet and stony to farm easily, and it holds the country's entire western prairie fringed orchid population.

  • Habitat: Tallgrass prairie on wet stony ground, sedge meadow and wet swale, aspen and oak bluff on the drier ridges, and riverine woodland along the Roseau.
  • When: Late May to July for the grassland breeders, with Yellow Rail calling after dark in June.
  • Getting there: Winnipeg is the airport, ninety minutes north-west, and Vita the base. The preserve is free with marked trails and interpretive stops, and the surrounding grid roads are gravel.
  • Base yourself in: Vita
  • Look for: Sharp-tailed Grouse, Upland Sandpiper, Bobolink, Sedge Wren, LeConte's Sparrow, Nelson's Sparrow, Yellow Rail, Sandhill Crane, Short-eared Owl, Northern Harrier, Marbled Godwit, Golden-winged Warbler
  • Parks and reserves: Rat River Wildlife Management Area (National wildlife refuge)
  • Also covers: Gardenton, Manitoba Tall Grass Prairie Preserve, Rat River Wildlife Management Area, Roseau River, Vita
  • Also watch for: The preserve holds the entire Canadian population of the western prairie fringed orchid and one of the country's few remaining tallgrass insect communities.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Manitoba?

The first three weeks of June, if Churchill is the point: the tundra thaws and the shorebirds, longspurs and loons all arrive within about twenty days. Mid-June works for the southern half at the same time, and October for the goose passage.

How do I get to Churchill?

There is no road. A flight from Winnipeg takes about two and a half hours, and the train takes two nights. Vehicles are hired locally, the road system is about sixty kilometres long, and flights and beds book out a year ahead for June.

Will I see Ross's Gull?

Probably not, and it is still worth going. It has nested on the tundra ponds here and turns up in some years, which is what made Churchill famous, but it is not annual. The godwits, longspurs, eiders and loons are the reliable reason to come.

What is left of the tallgrass prairie?

A fraction of one per cent of what the country had, and the preserve in the far south-east is the largest single piece. It survived because the ground is too wet and stony to plough easily, and it holds the entire Canadian population of the western prairie fringed orchid.

How close is the shield to the city?

About an hour east. The change from farmland to Precambrian granite, jack pine and lake is abrupt, and the boreal breeding list starts at the rock edge, which makes Whiteshell an unusually easy introduction to shield birding.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
