Getting around
Panama City is the hub and the Canal Zone forest starts twenty minutes from the airport. The Darien is a day on the road, and Bocas and Chiriqui are short domestic flights or a long drive.

When to Go

The two coasts run opposite calendars. The Pacific side and the canal forest have a real dry season from December to April; the Caribbean side has no dry season worth the name and its best window is September and October, which is when the Pacific side is wettest.

Peak

December to April

The Pacific dry season. Pipeline Road is walkable, the canal forest is at its easiest, and every northern migrant is in the country.

Go for The full Canal Zone list, El Valle in comfortable weather, and shorebirds massing on the Bay of Panama in March and April.

Trade off The highest prices and the fullest lodges, and the Caribbean side is wet throughout.

Typical daily average
  • The canal 23 to 32 °C / 73 to 90 °F
  • Boquete 14 to 26 °C / 57 to 79 °F
  • Bocas 22 to 30 °C / 72 to 86 °F

Shoulder

September and October

The one window when the Caribbean coast dries out, and the southbound raptor passage crosses the isthmus in numbers hard to believe.

Go for Bocas at its most workable, and hundreds of thousands of Turkey Vultures, Broad-winged and Swainson Hawks over the canal.

Trade off The Pacific side is at its wettest and afternoon rain is the rule there.

Typical daily average
  • The canal 23 to 31 °C / 73 to 88 °F
  • Boquete 14 to 25 °C / 57 to 77 °F
  • Bocas 23 to 30 °C / 73 to 86 °F

Green season

May to August and November

Rain falls on both coasts, breeding runs across the lowlands, and the rates are the lowest of the year.

Go for Birds singing, the forest at its greenest, and humpback whales off Coiba from July.

Trade off Afternoon downpours most days, and the Darien tracks turn difficult.

Typical daily average
  • The canal 23 to 31 °C / 73 to 88 °F
  • Boquete 15 to 25 °C / 59 to 77 °F
  • Bocas 23 to 30 °C / 73 to 86 °F

The Canal Zone: Soberania and Pipeline Road

Lowland rainforest either side of the canal, twenty minutes from the airport

The forest kept as the canal watershed is the most accessible lowland rainforest in the Americas, and Pipeline Road running through it holds a one-day count record that stood for decades. Ten of the twenty-five published Panamanian itineraries are built on it.

  • Habitat: Two national parks stand either side of the canal. They run from dry semi-deciduous forest in Camino de Cruces to much wetter forest at the north end of Soberania, with town sites, grass and scrub, small ponds and marshes, the Rio Chagres and the edges of Lake Gatun between them.
  • When: Good all year. Blue Cotinga, Slaty-tailed Trogon and Keel-billed Toucan stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Most of the birding sites here are within a 30 to 45 minute drive of downtown Panama City. Summit, Old Gamboa Road, Plantation Road, Gamboa and Pipeline Road can all be reached by bus, or more expensively by taxi.
  • Look for: Great Green Macaw, Blue Cotinga, Slaty-tailed Trogon, Keel-billed Toucan, Red-capped Manakin, Golden-headed Manakin, Rufous-vented Ground Cuckoo, Green Shrike-Vireo
  • National parks:
    • Soberania National Park, The canal watershed forest, 22,000 ha, with the road and several trails inside it.
    • Chagres National Park
  • Stops:
    • : A disused service road running 17 km into Soberania. Bird it on foot from the gate at dawn.
    • : The canal village the whole area is reached from, at the mouth of the Chagres.
    • : Dry forest inside Panama City itself, with a canopy walkway. Its morning list is worth the detour.
    • : Caribbean-slope forest on the other side of the canal. It is wetter and gives a different list.
    • : Marsh beside the road at Gamboa. Search it for rails, jacanas and the White-throated Crake.

  • Also watch for: Three sloth species, Geoffroy tamarin and coati along the road, and howler monkeys audible from the tower.

El Valle de Anton and the Central Highlands

A town inside a volcanic crater at 600 m, two hours from the canal

El Valle sits in the flat floor of an extinct crater with forested rim above it, which puts foothill and highland birds within a short drive of a comfortable base. It pairs with the Canal Zone on almost every itinerary that names it.

  • Habitat: A mountain town sitting in the caldera of an extinct volcano, with cloud forest on Cerro Gaital above it. Southern Cocle below is grassland, pasture and savanna, and the coast at Aguadulce holds mangrove and dry scrub.
  • When: Good all year. Tody Motmot, Snowcap and Rufous-crested Coquette hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: El Valle is a two-hour drive from Panama City and the easiest of the highland sites to reach. Much of the area is accessible by paved road or short trails in good condition, though some have steep sections and stairs.
  • Look for: Tody Motmot, Snowcap, Rufous-crested Coquette, Black-crowned Antpitta, Stripe-cheeked Woodpecker, White-tipped Sicklebill
  • National park: Omar Torrijos National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The crater rim above the town, with a trail into cloud forest at 1,100 m.
    • : The road climbing north out of the crater. Bird it from the verge for foothill species.
    • : A hill development east of El Valle whose roads reach 1,000 m of intact forest.
    • : The crater town every route here is based in, with a market and lodges on the floor.
    • : A quieter highland town further west, with foothill forest on the continental divide above it.
    • : The oldest national park in Panama, on a ridge an hour west of the city. miambiente.gob.pa
    • : Continental divide forest above El Cope, where the Caribbean and Pacific slopes meet on one trail. miambiente.gob.pa
  • Also covers: Altos del Maria, Cerro Gaital, El Valle de Anton, La Mesa, Omar Torrijos National Park, Parque Nacional Altos de Campana, Parque Nacional Omar Torrijos, El Cope, Penonome, Santa Fe
  • Also watch for: Golden frogs in the captive breeding centre, and olingo on the night walks above La Mesa.

Bocas del Toro and the Caribbean Coast

Island and mainland lowland forest on the wet Caribbean side

An archipelago and the coast behind it, wetter than anywhere else in the country and holding Caribbean-slope birds that reach nowhere near the canal. Getting about is by boat, which is the point of it.

  • Habitat: Ever-wet evergreen tropical forest on the Atlantic slope, with an archipelago offshore that holds specialities of its own.
  • Getting there: Reached either by driving from Panama City or by flying to Bocas del Toro and exploring from there.
  • Look for: Bare-necked Umbrellabird, Snowcap, Sunbittern, Agami Heron, Black-cheeked Woodpecker, Fiery-billed Aracari
  • National park: Bastimentos Island National Marine Park
  • Stops:
    • : A national marine park island with lowland forest. A boat runs from Bocas town.
    • : The archipelago town everything here starts from, with a small airport.
    • : A Ramsar wetland north of Changuinola, with manatee and mangrove birding by canoe.
    • : The mainland town for the wetlands and the road up towards the Talamanca.
  • Also covers: Almirante, Bastimentos Island National Marine Park, Bocas del Toro, Changuinola, Isla Bastimentos, San San Pond Sak
  • Also watch for: Manatee in the Changuinola wetlands, and three sloth species on the islands.

The Chiriqui Highlands: Boquete and Volcan

Talamanca cloud forest to 3,400 m, and the quetzals in it

The Talamanca range crosses from Costa Rica into Chiriqui and tops out at Volcan Baru, and the highland birds here are shared with the Costa Rican side rather than with the rest of Panama. Coffee towns on both flanks put the forest within a morning.

  • Habitat: The Talamanca massif is shared with Costa Rica and rises to 3,475 m at Volcan Baru, the highest point in the country. Cloud forest covers its flanks, and La Amistad International Park runs along the ridge into Bocas del Toro.
  • When: Good all year. Resplendent Quetzal, Three-wattled Bellbird and Volcano Junco are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Fly to David and drive up, or come by road from Panama City on the Carretera Interamericana, which is four-lane from David to the Costa Rican border.
  • Look for: Resplendent Quetzal, Three-wattled Bellbird, Volcano Junco, Snowcap, Yellow-billed Cotinga
  • National parks:
    • Volcan Baru National Park, The highest ground in Panama at 3,475 m. The Sendero Los Quetzales crosses from Boquete to Cerro Punta.
    • La Amistad International Park, A transboundary park shared with Costa Rica. Enter it at Las Nubes above Cerro Punta.
  • Stops:
    • : The coffee town on the eastern flank, and the usual base for the quetzal trails.
    • : The farming town on the western flank at 2,000 m, at the entrance to La Amistad.
    • : Two highland lakes below Volcan. Search them for waterbirds and work the surrounding oak forest.
    • : The road over the continental divide from Chiriqui. Bird it at every pull-in for cloud forest species.
  • Also covers: Boquete, Cerro Punta, David, Fortuna Road, La Amistad International Park, Volcan Baru National Park, Volcan Lakes
  • Also watch for: Baird tapir in La Amistad, and puma recorded on camera traps above Cerro Punta.

The Darien

The forest at the end of the road, and the only Harpy Eagles most people will see

The Pan-American Highway stops at Yaviza and everything east of it is reached by river or on foot. The Darien holds birds shared with Colombia and nothing else in Central America, and the Harpy Eagle nests here are the reason most people come.

  • Habitat: Lowland forest running to the Colombian border, with the Serrania de Pirre rising behind it, and Cerro Tacarcuna at 1,875 m the highest peak in eastern Panama.
  • When: Good all year. Harpy Eagle, Dusky-backed Jacamar and Crimson-bellied Woodpecker are here in every month.
  • Getting there: The road reaches Yaviza and no further. Beyond that it is boat and light aircraft, to El Real for Pirre Station, or to Cana.
  • Look for: Harpy Eagle, Dusky-backed Jacamar, Speckled Antshrike, Crimson-bellied Woodpecker, Black-crowned Antpitta, Sapayoa, Pheasant Cuckoo
  • National park:
    • Darien National Park, 579,000 ha on the Colombian border, and the largest protected area in Central America.
  • Stops:
    • Canopy Camp: A tented camp near Meteti, and the usual base for the accessible Darien and its Harpy Eagle nests.
    • : The town where the Pan-American Highway ends, and the river port for everything beyond it.
    • : The river town below Pirre, on a boat, and the gateway to Darien National Park.
  • Also covers: Canopy Camp, Darien National Park, El Real, Meteti, Yaviza
  • Also watch for: Jaguar and Baird tapir in the interior, and four monkey species around the camps.

The Pacific Coast and Coiba

Dry forest, shorebird mudflats and an island national park

The Pacific side is drier and more farmed, and what it holds is shorebirds on the Bay of Panama mudflats and dry forest on the Azuero peninsula. Coiba, a former prison island, kept its forest by accident.

  • Habitat: Dry savanna and woodland on the Pacific slope, deforested to cattle pasture across much of the Azuero, with mangrove and dry scrub on the coast and freshwater marsh inland. Isla Coiba, 34 km by 21 km, is the largest island off the Pacific coast of Central America.
  • Getting there: Santiago on the Interamericana is the staging point, and the usual place to overnight before a trip out to Coiba.
  • Look for: Yellow-billed Cotinga, Fiery-billed Aracari, Sunbittern, Spectacled Owl, Keel-billed Toucan
  • National parks:
    • Coiba National Park, An island park off Veraguas. It was a penal colony until 2004, which is why the forest survived.
    • Cerro Hoya National Park, The last forest on the Azuero peninsula, remote and on a rough track.
  • Stops:
    • : A dry-coast town at the tip of Azuero. It works as a base for the peninsula and Isla Iguana.
  • Also covers: Cerro Hoya National Park, Chitre, Coiba National Park, Pedasi, Santiago
  • Also watch for: Humpback whales off Coiba between July and October, and scarlet macaws reintroduced on the island.

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Panama for birding?

December to April is the dry season on the Pacific side and the canal forest, which is where most birding happens. The Caribbean coast runs the opposite calendar and its best window is September and October, which is also when the southbound raptor passage crosses. The three seasons.

Panama or Costa Rica?

Costa Rica has more infrastructure, more lodges and an easier first trip. Panama has the Darien, which holds birds shared with Colombia and nothing else in Central America, and it has lowland rainforest twenty minutes from an international airport. Many people do Costa Rica first and Panama second for exactly that reason.

What is Pipeline Road?

A disused canal service road running 17 km into Soberania National Park, and among the most productive stretches of birding anywhere in the Americas. It is walked from the gate at dawn, it is flat, and it is twenty minutes from Panama City. The Canal Zone.

How many bird species does Panama have?

Just over a thousand on a country the size of Ireland, because the isthmus carries both North and South American families and the two mountain systems add their own. The number above counts the species with a card in the BeakBrain guide rather than a figure typed in by hand.

Can you see Harpy Eagles in Panama?

Panama is the most reliable country in the world for it. Local guides in the Darien track active nests, and a nest with a chick holds the adults in the area for months. It is nest-dependent rather than seasonal, so ask what is active before you book.

Is the Darien safe?

The birding Darien and the Darien Gap crossing are two different things. Operators work the accessible western part around Meteti and El Real with local guides and permits, and that is what every itinerary above means. The border region itself is not visited.

Are there eco lodges in Panama?

The canal-forest towers are the best known: buildings put among the crowns so you stand level with the canopy rather than looking up through it. Several lodges here fund reserve land, and the Bocas island lodges run on their own power and water.

How long do you need?

Four days reaches the Canal Zone properly. A week adds El Valle. Nine days takes in the Darien. Sixteen covers the country from the Costa Rican border to the Colombian one. Five routes.

What is the raptor passage?

Every autumn between two and three million raptors funnel down the isthmus, mostly Turkey Vultures, Broad-winged and Swainson Hawks, and pass over the canal in streams that take hours to clear. October is the peak and any high ground near the city will do.

What other wildlife is there?

Three sloth species and Geoffroy tamarin along Pipeline Road, howler and capuchin monkeys almost everywhere, humpback whales off Coiba from July, and jaguar and Baird tapir in the Darien interior.
