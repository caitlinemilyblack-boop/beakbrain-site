Birds that decide the trip

Karthala Scops Owl on one volcano

The owl lives in the forest on the upper slopes of Karthala and nowhere else on earth. It calls through the night and the walk up from Moroni is the way to hear it.

When All year, calling after dark

Anjouan Scops Owl, found again in 1992

The owl went unrecorded for most of the twentieth century and was widely written off. It was found again in 1992 in the forest above Moya, which is where it still holds on.

Mohéli Scops Owl

The third of the archipelago’s scops owls, one to an island, each separated from the next by open sea the birds do not cross.

Grande Comore and the Karthala forest

Two birds that live only on one volcano: the Karthala Scops Owl and the Karthala White-eye

Karthala is an active volcano covering most of Grande Comore, and the forest on its upper slopes holds two birds found on this mountain and nowhere else. The island is 1,024 square kilometres and the forest that remains sits above the plantations.

  • Habitat: Montane rainforest and heath on an active volcano, with a bare caldera above and plantation and scrub on the lower slopes.
  • When: May to October, when the tracks up the mountain are passable.
  • Getting there: Moroni is the base and the airport. The forest is reached on foot from Boboni or Nkourani above the town, and the walk to the caldera takes a long day.
  • Base yourself in: Moroni
  • All year: Karthala Scops Owl, Karthala White-eye, Grande Comore Brush Warbler, Grande Comore Bulbul, Grande Comore Drongo, Comoro Green Pigeon

Mohéli and its marine park

The smallest island, with its own bulbul, its own scops owl and its own brush warbler

Mohéli is 211 square kilometres and the least disturbed of the three. Three of its birds live on this island alone, and the ridge forest above Fomboni carries all of them. The marine park along the south coast was the country’s first protected area.

  • Habitat: Ridge forest and secondary woodland on a low volcanic island, with mangrove, beach and islets along the south coast.
  • When: May to October for the forest. Turtles nest on the south coast beaches through the year.
  • Getting there: Fomboni is reached by air or by boat from Moroni. The ridge forest is walked from Djoiezi or Ouallah on tracks that need a guide.
  • Base yourself in: Fomboni
  • All year: Moheli Scops Owl, Moheli Bulbul, Moheli Brush Warbler, Comoro Blue Pigeon, Comoro Black Parrot, Comoro Fody, Comoro Blue Vanga, Malagasy White-eye

  • Also watch for: Green turtles nest on the south coast beaches in numbers, and dugongs are recorded in the marine park.

Anjouan and the Moya forest

The Anjouan Scops Owl, which was thought extinct until 1992

Anjouan is 424 square kilometres and the most heavily farmed of the three. The Anjouan Scops Owl was not recorded for most of the twentieth century and was found again in 1992 in the forest remnants above Moya, which is where it still holds on.

  • Habitat: Forest remnants on steep ridges above cultivated slopes, with the highest ground on Mount Ntringui.
  • When: May to October. The owl calls after dark all year.
  • Getting there: Ouani is the airport and Mutsamudu the town. The Moya forest is reached from the south coast road and the walking is steep.
  • Base yourself in: Mutsamudu
  • All year: Anjouan Scops Owl, Anjouan Sunbird, Comoro Thrush, Comoro Olive Pigeon, Comoro Cuckooshrike

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
