When to Go

Madagascar has a hot wet season from November to March and a cool dry one from April to October, and the birding season is the dry. Cyclones cross the east coast between January and March, the western roads to Bemaraha are closed for months, and the eastern rainforest is at its wettest exactly when it is hardest to reach. What the wet does give is breeding activity and green.

Peak

September to November

The end of the dry, and the best few weeks on the island.

Go for Andasibe records its driest month in October at 52 mm, the ground rollers are calling in the spiny forest, lemur births run through September and October, and the Fosa is at Kirindy to breed.

Trade off It is the busiest and dearest window, and by November the west is very hot and the tsingy road is closing.

Typical daily average
  • Andasibe: 15 to 25 °C / 59 to 77 °F
  • Toliara: 19 to 32 °C / 66 to 90 °F
  • Maroantsetra: 20 to 29 °C / 68 to 84 °F

Cool dry

May to August

The cool, empty half of the dry season.

Go for Morondava records 2 mm in June and Toliara 3 mm in August, the roads are at their best, and Humpback Whales are in the Bay of Antongil from July.

Trade off Highland nights fall to 10 C and the rainforest birds are quieter than they are once the calling starts in September.

Typical daily average
  • Antananarivo: 10 to 21 °C / 50 to 70 °F
  • Toliara: 13 to 29 °C / 55 to 84 °F
  • Morondava: 15 to 29 °C / 59 to 84 °F

Wet

December to March

The cyclone season, and the months almost nobody runs.

Go for The forests are green, the chameleons and frogs are at their peak, and the reptile and amphibian tours that do run come now on purpose.

Trade off Maroantsetra takes 454 mm in March, cyclones cross the east coast, and the road to Bemaraha is closed for the whole period.

Typical daily average
  • Andasibe: 17 to 26 °C / 63 to 79 °F
  • Toliara: 22 to 33 °C / 72 to 91 °F
  • Maroantsetra: 22 to 30 °C / 72 to 86 °F

Andasibe-Mantadia and the eastern rainforest

Mid-altitude rainforest on the eastern escarpment three hours from the capital, and the first stop on almost every itinerary

Andasibe is where a Madagascar trip usually opens, and it earns the place twice over. The forest is mid-altitude rainforest on a road good enough to drive at night, the guiding is done by the village association at Mitsinjo, and the birding starts at the reserve gate. Blue Coua and Velvet Asity are on the first morning's trails, Nuthatch Vanga works the trunks in the mixed flocks, and Short-legged Ground Roller is walked to on a known territory in Mantadia up the road.

  • Habitat: Mid-altitude rainforest between 900 and 1,500 m, with montane marshes alongside it adding a second set of birds.
  • When: Best from May to December. Blue Coua, Velvet Asity and Nuthatch Vanga are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Andasibe village and the protected areas around it are reached by road from Antananarivo. Local guides are mandatory in the park.
  • Look for: Blue Coua, Velvet Asity, Nuthatch Vanga, Short-legged Ground Roller, Pitta-like Ground Roller, Collared Nightjar, White-throated Rail, Madagascar Cuckoo-Hawk
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Madagascar Flufftail, Rand's Warbler
  • Parks and reserves: Andasibe-Mantadia National Park, Analamazaotra Special Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The village at the reserve gate. The guide association and the guesthouses are here, with the Indri trails behind them.
    • : The higher and wilder block, twenty kilometres north on a rough road. Search it for the ground rollers and the sifaka.
    • : The town on the main road west of the reserve. It is the junction for the route north.
  • Also covers: Analamazaotra Special Reserve, Andasibe-Mantadia National Park, Moramanga
  • Also watch for: The Indri is here, the largest living lemur, and its call carries for two kilometres through the forest at eight in the morning. Diademed Sifaka is in Mantadia, and the night walks along the road produce Goodman's Mouse Lemur, chameleons and leaf-tailed geckos in numbers.

Ranomafana

Steep montane rainforest in the southeast, made a national park in 1991 for a lemur discovered there five years earlier

Ranomafana is hard walking on steep, wet trails and it holds birds no other park does. Yellow-bellied Sunbird-Asity feeds at flowering trees along the Vohiparara road, Rufous-headed Ground Roller is called in on a slope above the river, and Cryptic Warbler and Brown Mesite both need a guide who knows a territory. The park exists because the Golden Bamboo Lemur was found here in 1986, undescribed until then.

  • Habitat: Rainforest running from 600 to 1,550 m, with high-altitude marshes on the plateau above it, and a separate forest patch at Vohiparara west of the main entrance.
  • When: Best from August to December. Yellow-bellied Sunbird-Asity, Rufous-headed Ground Roller and Pollen's Vanga are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: The town lies about 50 km north-east of Fianarantsoa, effectively a twelve-hour drive from the capital. Local guides are mandatory, and most are proficient general naturalists.
  • Look for: Yellow-bellied Sunbird-Asity, Rufous-headed Ground Roller, Pollen's Vanga, Cryptic Warbler, Brown Mesite, Grey-crowned Tetraka, Forest Rock Thrush, Madagascar Flufftail
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Eleonora's Falcon, Marsh Owl
  • Parks and reserves: Ranomafana National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The village below the park, named for its hot springs. The road climbs through the forest above it.
    • : The higher entrance on the road west. Search it for the sunbird-asity, with the marsh birds behind.
  • Also covers: Ranomafana National Park, Vohiparara
  • Also watch for: Golden Bamboo Lemur was described from this forest and is still watched here, along with Greater Bamboo Lemur, Milne-Edwards's Sifaka and Red-bellied Lemur. The night walks on the road outside the park are among the best in the country for mouse lemurs and chameleons.

Ifaty and the spiny forest

Octopus-tree spiny forest on the southwest coast, a habitat that exists on this island and nowhere else

The spiny forest north of Toliara is unlike any other habitat on earth. Didiereas stand like octopus arms over baobabs and thorn scrub, and they grow on almost no rain. Two birds here are the reason people come. Long-tailed Ground Roller runs on the sand between the thorns and Subdesert Mesite walks the leaf litter, and local guides track both on foot at dawn.

  • Habitat: Spiny forest of multi-stemmed octopus trees and other thorny shrubs under bottle-shaped baobabs, with marine coast and commercial saltpans alongside.
  • When: Best from July to December. Long-tailed Ground Roller, Subdesert Mesite and Running Coua hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Ifaty lies 27 km north of Toliara along a sandy track best driven in a 4WD, though the taxi-brousses run up the coast from Toliara.
  • Look for: Long-tailed Ground Roller, Subdesert Mesite, Running Coua, Sickle-billed Vanga, Lafresnaye's Vanga, Thamnornis, Banded Kestrel
  • Parks and reserves: Reniala Reserve (private reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : The beach village north of Toliara, with the spiny forest reserves inland from it.
    • : The southwestern port and airport. It is the road head for the spiny forest and the route north.
  • Also covers: Ifaty, Reniala Reserve, Toliara
  • Also watch for: Ring-tailed Lemur and Verreaux's Sifaka both use the dry forest, and the spiny forest itself carries an extraordinary reptile fauna with several species of Furcifer chameleon. The coast at Ifaty is a reef lagoon and the whale watching runs from July to September.

Isalo, Zombitse and the southern highlands

Sandstone canyons and grassland at the middle of the southern road, and the driest of the highland stops

Isalo is a break in the long drive south and a landscape worth the stop on its own: eroded sandstone, canyons with gallery forest in the bottom of them and grassland burned every year. The bird everyone wants is the rock thrush of these canyons, long called Benson's and carried here as Forest Rock Thrush, which sits on the rock in the open, and the canyon pools hold the lemurs that come down to drink in the middle of the day. Zombitse, an hour and a half west on the same road, is a dry forest block birded from the roadside and holds Appert's Tetraka, which lives there and nowhere else.

  • Habitat: Open sandstone landscape with rocky cliffs at Isalo, and dry deciduous forest at Zombitse straddling the RN7 about 90 km west.
  • When: Best from August to December. Forest Rock Thrush, Madagascar Partridge and Malagasy Kestrel hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: The Interpretation Centre sits about 10 km south-west of Ranohira, with the cliffs around the nearby Oasis and the roofs of the Relais de la Reine 3 km further west. Zombitse is on the RN7 and needs local guides.
  • Look for: Forest Rock Thrush, Madagascar Partridge, Malagasy Kestrel, Madagascar Cuckoo, Olive Bee-eater, Malagasy Harrier, Broad-billed Roller, Appert's Tetraka
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Marsh Owl, Malagasy Black Swift
  • Parks and reserves: Isalo National Park, Zombitse-Vohibasia National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The town at the park gate on the southern road. The canyon walks start a short drive away.
    • : A dry forest block on the road west towards Toliara. Bird it from the roadside for an hour or a morning.
  • Also covers: Isalo National Park, Ranohira, Zombitse-Vohibasia National Park
  • Also watch for: Ring-tailed Lemur, Verreaux's Sifaka and Red-fronted Brown Lemur all come to the canyon pools in the heat of the day, which is the easiest lemur watching in the country. The sapphire town of Ilakaka down the road is a different kind of sight.

Berenty and the far south

Gallery forest on the Mandrare river in the extreme southeast, a private reserve inside a sisal estate

Berenty is a private reserve of tamarind gallery forest surrounded by sisal, and its lemurs are so habituated that this is where most of the famous footage was shot. The birding is dry-country and easy: Giant Coua walks the tracks, White-browed Owl roosts in the tamarinds and Madagascar Sandgrouse comes to water at dusk. The spiny forest plot at the back of the reserve holds a different set again.

  • Habitat: Tall gallery woodland along the Mandrare River with strips of spiny forest beside it.
  • When: Best from June to November. Giant Coua, White-browed Owl and Madagascar Sandgrouse hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Reached from Amboasary on the road west of Fort Dauphin.
  • Look for: Giant Coua, White-browed Owl, Madagascar Sandgrouse, Madagascar Green Pigeon, Hook-billed Vanga, Madagascar Hoopoe, Littoral Rock Thrush
  • Parks and reserves: Berenty Reserve (private reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : The town on the Mandrare beside the reserve, an hour and a half from the Fort Dauphin airport.
    • : The airport town on the southeast coast. It is the only practical way into the far south.
  • Also covers: Amboasary, Berenty Reserve, Fort Dauphin
  • Also watch for: Ring-tailed Lemur troops walk through the picnic tables, Verreaux's Sifaka does the sideways hop across the open ground, and a colony of Madagascar Flying Fox hangs over the main path. Berenty is the easiest wildlife watching on the island and it looks it.

Ankarafantsika and the northwest

Dry deciduous forest above a lake on the road north from the capital, and the best of the western sites

Ampijoroa is the forestry station inside Ankarafantsika and the birding is done on flat sandy trails straight out of the campsite. Western dry forest holds a different vanga set from the east, with Van Dam's Vanga and Rufous Vanga both here, and Schlegel's Asity displays in the same forest. Lake Ravelobe beside the road carries Madagascar Fish Eagle and the crocodiles that make swimming a poor idea.

  • Habitat: Western deciduous forest around a small lake, with more extensive wetland at Lac d'Amboromalandy nearby.
  • When: Best from July to December. Van Dam's Vanga, Schlegel's Asity and White-breasted Mesite stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Most visitors reach Mahajanga by air and hire transport for the 120 km, about two hours, to Ampijoroa. From Antananarivo it is an eight to ten hour drive.
  • Look for: Van Dam's Vanga, Schlegel's Asity, White-breasted Mesite, Coquerel's Coua, Madagascar Fish Eagle, Rufous Vanga, Red-capped Coua, Malagasy Pond Heron
  • Also recorded in the parks here: African Darter, Sickle-billed Vanga
  • Parks and reserves: Ankarafantsika National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The forestry station and campsite inside the park. Every trail starts at the door.
    • Mahajanga: The northwestern port and airport, two hours further north on the same road.
  • Also covers: Ampijoroa, Ankarafantsika National Park, Mahajanga
  • Also watch for: Coquerel's Sifaka is common around the campsite and Mongoose Lemur is here at the eastern edge of its range. The night walks produce Golden-brown Mouse Lemur, Milne-Edwards's Sportive Lemur and the Fosa's tracks on the sandy trails.

Masoala and the northeast

The largest protected rainforest on the island, running to the sea on a peninsula reached only by boat

Masoala is the wettest and least reached of the great forests, and the trip is a boat across the Bay of Antongil from Maroantsetra to a beach camp with the forest behind it. Helmet Vanga is the bird people come for, with a blue bill the size of its head, and Bernier's Vanga and Scaly Ground Roller are here in the same forest. Nosy Mangabe in the bay is the island the Aye-aye was moved to in 1967.

  • Habitat: Lowland rainforest, and the only remaining large tract of it on the island.
  • When: October and November, before the heavy December to February rains.
  • Getting there: Maroantsetra is the jumping off point. It is reached by air from Antananarivo or Toamasina, or by a hard two day overland journey. Boats for the 30 km crossing are hired there.
  • Look for: Helmet Vanga, Bernier's Vanga, Scaly Ground Roller, Red-breasted Coua, Red Owl, Madagascar Serpent Eagle, Short-legged Ground Roller, Brown Mesite
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Madagascar Pratincole, African Spoonbill
  • Parks and reserves: Masoala National Park, Nosy Mangabe Special Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The town at the head of the bay, with an airstrip. Every boat to the peninsula leaves from here.
    • : The forested island in the bay, an hour out. The Aye-aye was introduced here and the leaf-tailed geckos are easiest on it.
    • : A village on Antongil Bay and the usual landing for the Masoala forest. A boat runs there from Maroantsetra.
  • Also covers: Ambanizana, Maroantsetra, Masoala National Park, Nosy Mangabe Special Reserve
  • Also watch for: Red Ruffed Lemur lives on this peninsula and nowhere else, and Aye-aye was introduced to Nosy Mangabe when the species was thought to be nearly gone. Humpback Whales calve in the Bay of Antongil from July to September, which is the same window the forest is driest.

Amber Mountain and the far north

An isolated rainforest massif above Diego Suarez, wet enough to hold its own thrush and its own chameleons

Amber Mountain rises out of dry country in the far north and catches enough cloud to hold rainforest on its own. It is cool, easy walking on made paths, and it has Amber Mountain Rock Thrush, which lives on this massif alone. Ankarana's limestone tsingy an hour south is a different landscape again, with caves, gorges and dry forest.

  • Habitat: Mid-altitude forest on an isolated massif in the far north, high enough above the surrounding dry country to hold its own endemics.
  • When: September to November, in the austral spring, is the best time to visit Madagascar. Away from the humid eastern escarpment the rainy season usually runs from November to March.
  • Getting there: Reached from Diego Suarez through Joffreville.
  • Look for: Amber Mountain Rock Thrush, Madagascar Ibis, Pitta-like Ground Roller, Dark Newtonia, Madagascar Scops Owl, Madagascar Blue Vanga, Malagasy Green Sunbird
  • Parks and reserves: Montagne d'Ambre National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The hill village at the park gate, an hour from Diego Suarez. The made paths start above it.
    • : The northern port and airport, on one of the largest natural harbours in the world.
  • Also covers: Diego Suarez, Joffreville, Montagne d'Ambre National Park
  • Also watch for: Sanford's Brown Lemur and Crowned Lemur are both easy on the Amber Mountain paths, and the massif holds Brookesia, the leaf chameleons, of which one described here is among the smallest reptiles known. Ankarana's caves hold crocodiles and a large bat colony.

The western wetlands and Tsingy de Bemaraha

Limestone pinnacles, mangrove and lake country on the western coast, reached on roads that close in the wet

The road north from Morondava passes the baobab avenue, the Kirindy dry forest and two river ferries before it reaches the tsingy at Bekopaka, and it is closed for months of the year. Kirindy is the reliable place for Giant Coua and for the Fosa, and the wetlands and mangroves along this coast hold Bernier's Teal and Black-winged Stilt, both of which are hard anywhere else.

  • Habitat: The Tsingy de Bemaraha covers 152,000 hectares and is the largest protected area in Madagascar. Its highlight is a vast forest of eroded karst pinnacles, more rugged even than those at Ankarana in the north. Remoteness has kept the records thin, and 53 birds, eight reptiles and six lemurs have been logged so far, among them Decken's sifaka and the grey mouse, fork-marked, grey bamboo, sportive and brown lemurs. Belo-sur-Tsiribihina, the usual starting point for the reserve, sits among the vast marshes, mangroves and barrier islands of the Tsiribihina delta.
  • When: The reserve can be visited from April to November.
  • Getting there: Reaching Bekopaka and the Tsingy de Bemaraha independently is very difficult, and in the wet season it is close to impossible. Bekopaka is the nearest village to the reserve, and organised trips run in from Belo-sur-Tsiribihina or Morondava, some of them taking a canoe down the Manambolo River from Bekopaka. Check that the road from Belo to Bekopaka is passable before setting off in any vehicle. Taxis-brousse cover the two to three hours between Belo and Morondava frequently. Entry needs a permit arranged in advance through the park authority, and independent visitors should sort that out before they travel.
  • Look for: Bernier's Teal, Black-winged Stilt, Malagasy Sacred Ibis, Sakalava Rail, White-breasted Mesite, Crested Coua, Humblot's Heron, Madagascar Pratincole
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Crab-plover, Giant Coua
  • Parks and reserves: Tsingy de Bemaraha National Park, Kirindy Mitea National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The west coast town with an airstrip. The road north past the baobab avenue starts here.
    • : The dry forest reserve an hour and a half north. It has a research camp and the best night walks in the west.
    • : The village at the tsingy, eight hours and two ferries beyond Kirindy. It is cut off entirely in the wet.
  • Also covers: Bekopaka, Kirindy Mitea National Park, Morondava, Tsingy de Bemaraha National Park
  • Also watch for: Kirindy is where Fosa, the island's largest carnivore, is seen most reliably, and it comes into the camp in October and November when it breeds. Giant Jumping Rat lives here and nowhere else, and the baobab avenue at sunset is the photograph everyone takes.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Madagascar?

September to November. The dry season roads are all open, lemur births run through September and October, the ground rollers start calling in the spiny forest, and Andasibe records its driest month of the year in October at 52 mm. May to August is cooler, emptier and cheaper, with the trade that the rainforest birds are quieter. The three seasons.

How different are the wet and dry sides?

As different as any two places in one country. Maroantsetra on the northeast coast takes 3,464 mm of rain a year; Toliara in the southwest takes 571 mm and records 3 mm in August. That is why the spiny forest and the eastern rainforest share almost no birds, and why a full itinerary has to visit both. When to go, season by season.

What are the endemic bird families?

Five, which is more than any comparable island: the mesites, the ground rollers, the cuckoo-roller, the asities and the vangas. The vangas alone have radiated into more than twenty species with bills from a hooked shrike's to the Helmet Vanga's blue axe, which is the textbook example of an island radiation. The birds.

Do I need a guide?

Yes, and not as a suggestion. Every national park in Madagascar is entered with a local guide by rule, and in practice that is also how the birds are found: the individual territories of the ground rollers and the mesites are known by the men who walk them daily. The fee goes to the local association. Birding community.

How much of the country can one trip cover?

Less than a map suggests. Antananarivo to Toliara is a thousand kilometres of road that takes three days with birding stops, and Masoala, Amber Mountain and Fort Dauphin each need their own internal flight. Two weeks covers the eastern rainforest and the southwest; the rest is an extension. The itineraries.

Is Tsingy de Bemaraha worth the journey?

It depends what you want. The limestone pinnacles are extraordinary and the drive north from Morondava passes the baobab avenue and Kirindy, which is the best Fosa site in the country. But it is eight hours and two river ferries beyond Kirindy on a road that is shut for months, and the bird list is not the reason to go. The western wetlands and Tsingy de Bemaraha.

Do the places to stay in Madagascar hold sustainability certification?

None of them. No international scheme has reached the country, so no Malagasy property appears in a register. That says something about the schemes rather than about the hotels. Certification.

How many bird species does Madagascar have?

Around 290 recorded, which is a modest list for a country this size, and about half of them live nowhere else. That ratio is the point: the reason to come is birds with no close relatives anywhere, and the list stays short. The birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
