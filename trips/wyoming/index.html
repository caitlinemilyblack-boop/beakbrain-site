Birding in Wyoming

Wyoming holds the first national park in the world and the largest unfenced sagebrush country left in the country, and the sagebrush is the harder one to replace.

Birds that decide the trip

Sage Grouse on a Wyoming lek

It needs large unbroken sagebrush, and this state has more of that left than any other. The males inflate throat sacs on traditional leks in April, and the same ground is used year after year.

When April, at first light from a vehicle or an arranged blind.

Where Seedskadee and the Green River, The Red Desert

Trumpeter Swan on the Yellowstone rivers

About thirty pairs breed in the park and as many as seven hundred birds pass through in autumn, which is the largest concentration the species has anywhere in the region.

When All year on open water, with the peak in autumn passage.

Where Yellowstone and the Absarokas, Grand Teton and Jackson Hole

Great Grey Owl at the forest edge

The park holds one of the highest densities in North America. The birds hunt voles from low perches at the edges of meadows in the last hour of light.

When May to September, at meadow edges at dusk.

Where Yellowstone and the Absarokas, Grand Teton and Jackson Hole

Mountain Plover on bare desert ground

It wants short or bare ground rather than tall grass, which the heavily grazed and alkali parts of the Red Desert supply, and it is easy to walk straight past at any range.

When May to July, on bare and heavily grazed ground.

Where The Red Desert

Harlequin Duck in fast water

It nests along tumbling mountain streams and swims up rapids that look impassable, and the park rivers hold a small and closely watched population.

When May to July, on fast rivers in the park.

Where Yellowstone and the Absarokas

Barrow's Goldeneye on the park lakes

The park carries one of the densest populations on the continent. They nest in tree cavities near water and stay through the winter wherever the thermal areas keep it open.

When All year, on lakes and open river.

Where Yellowstone and the Absarokas

Sage Thrasher over the sagebrush

It sings a long unbroken run of phrases from the top of a sagebrush, which is the easiest way to notice a bird that is otherwise the colour of the ground.

When May to July, from sagebrush tops at dawn.

Where The Red Desert, Seedskadee and the Green River

American White Pelican on Yellowstone Lake

The park holds the only breeding colony in the national park system and one of the most significant in the northern Rockies, at about five hundred birds.

When May to September, on the park lakes and rivers.

Where Yellowstone and the Absarokas

When to Go

Wyoming has a short summer and a very long winter, and the useful window for most of the state is about ten weeks. The exceptions are the Sage Grouse leks in April and the elk refuge, which is a winter trip.

The lek season

April

Sage Grouse gather on traditional leks across the sagebrush basins.

Go for Seedskadee and the Red Desert benches at first light.

Trade off The mountain roads are shut and the desert two-tracks are soft.

Typical daily average

The breeding season

Late May to July

Everything that breeds in the state is on song, from riparian cottonwood to alpine.

Go for Yellowstone and the Tetons, Seedskadee, and the Bighorns once the pass clears.

Trade off The parks are at their busiest and accommodation is expensive and booked out.

Typical daily average

The autumn passage

Mid-August to October

Trumpeter Swans build in Yellowstone and the waterfowl move through the desert lakes.

Go for Yellowstone rivers and lakes, and Fontenelle for the waterfowl.

Trade off Snow can close the high roads from mid-October onwards.

Typical daily average

The elk refuge winter

December to March

Several thousand elk hold the refuge below Jackson with eagles and ravens on them.

Go for The National Elk Refuge by sleigh, and the open river below Jackson.

Trade off Yellowstone is closed to cars and the desert is deep in snow.

10 days

Parks, river and desert

Jackson (JAC) 3 nights, Cody 2 nights, Sheridan 2 nights, Rawlins 1 night, Green River 2 nights, out of Salt Lake City (SLC)

An open-jaw circuit in the second half of June that takes the two parks, the island range and the sagebrush. Trumpeter Swan and Great Grey Owl in the north, then across the Bighorns, then south into the desert for Sage Grouse country. About 1,000 miles on empty roads.

  1. 1Grand Teton National Park
  2. 2National Elk Refuge
  3. 3Yellowstone National Park
  4. 4Hayden Valley
  5. 5Shoshone National Forest

6 days

Yellowstone and the Tetons

Jackson (JAC) 2 nights, Yellowstone 3 nights, back to Jackson

Five days in the two parks in late June. Willow flats below the Tetons at dawn for moose and Calliope Hummingbird, then north for Trumpeter Swan on the rivers, Harlequin Duck in the fast water and Great Grey Owl at the forest edges. Book beds many months ahead.

  2. 2Fontenelle Reservoir
  3. 3Adobe Town
  4. 4Chain Lakes Wildlife Habitat Management Area
  5. 5Killpecker Sand Dunes

5 days

Sage Grouse and the Red Desert

Salt Lake City (SLC), Green River 2 nights, Rawlins 2 nights, back to Salt Lake City

Four days of sagebrush in mid-April, arranged around leks. The refuge office sets up viewing, and the rest of the trip is slow driving on two-track looking for Mountain Plover, Sage Thrasher and Ferruginous Hawk. Carry fuel, water and a spare tyre.

  1. 1Bighorn National Forest
  2. 2Yellowtail Wildlife Habitat Management Area
  3. 3Bighorn Canyon

4 days

The Bighorn Mountains

Sheridan (SHR) 3 nights, back to Sheridan

Three days on an island range in July, once the Cloud Peak Skyway is clear. Conifer forest cut off from the main Rockies, a thousand-metre limestone canyon on the east side with wild horses on the rim, and the wetlands at Yellowtail below the dam.

Yellowstone and the Absarokas

The first national park in the world, with Trumpeter Swans on the rivers and wolves on the flats

Yellowstone holds about thirty breeding Trumpeter Swans in the Wyoming corner of the park and as many as seven hundred in autumn passage, one of the largest American White Pelican colonies in the northern Rockies, and among the highest densities of Great Grey Owl and Barrow's Goldeneye anywhere. The wolves get the attention; the birds are the reason to stay.

  • Habitat: Lodgepole pine over eighty per cent of the park, subalpine meadow and montane sagebrush grassland, more than ten thousand thermal features, and large rivers and lakes.
  • When: Late May to early July for the breeding birds, and mid-August to late October for the swan and waterfowl passage.
  • Getting there: Cody or Jackson are the airports and Cody is the base for the east entrance. The park charges a fee, most roads close to cars from November to April, and the summer traffic through Hayden Valley is heavy.
  • Base yourself in: Cody
  • Look for: Trumpeter Swan, American White Pelican, Great Grey Owl, Barrow's Goldeneye, Harlequin Duck, Bald Eagle, Osprey, American Dipper, Clark's Nutcracker, Dusky Grouse, Northern Pygmy Owl, Sandhill Crane
  • Parks and reserves: Shoshone National Forest (Forest or grassland), Yellowstone National Park, Washakie Wilderness (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Hayden Valley, Shoshone National Forest, Washakie Wilderness, Yellowstone National Park
  • Also watch for: Wolves, grizzly and black bear, bison, elk and pronghorn are all in the park, and Hayden Valley is where most people watch them.

Grand Teton and Jackson Hole

A mountain front rising straight out of a valley floor, with a winter elk herd of seven thousand under it

The Tetons rise more than two thousand metres from the valley in a single face, so willow flat, sagebrush, aspen and subalpine forest sit within a few miles of each other. The elk refuge below them holds several thousand animals through the winter with eagles and ravens on the carcasses.

  • Habitat: Willow flat and beaver pond on the valley floor, sagebrush flats, aspen groves, lodgepole and spruce on the slopes, and alpine above the treeline.
  • When: Late May to July for the breeding birds and the Sage Grouse leks, and December to March for the elk refuge and the eagles.
  • Getting there: Jackson has an airport inside the park boundary and is the base. The park charges a fee, the inner road closes in winter, and the elk refuge runs horse-drawn sleigh rides from December to April.
  • Base yourself in: Jackson
  • Look for: Trumpeter Swan, Sandhill Crane, Sage Grouse, Great Grey Owl, American Dipper, Calliope Hummingbird, Broad-tailed Hummingbird, MacGillivray's Warbler, Green-tailed Towhee, Mountain Bluebird, Golden Eagle, Bald Eagle
  • Parks and reserves: Bridger-Teton National Forest (Forest or grassland), Grand Teton National Park, National Elk Refuge (Protected area), Bridger Wilderness (Protected area), Gros Ventre Wilderness (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Bridger Wilderness, Bridger-Teton National Forest, Grand Teton National Park, Gros Ventre Wilderness, National Elk Refuge
  • Also watch for: Moose feed in the willow flats, bison and pronghorn use the sagebrush, and the elk refuge herd runs to several thousand animals.

Seedskadee and the Green River

Thirty-six miles of cottonwood and willow along a river in a sagebrush desert

Seedskadee protects a long riparian strip on the Green River, and in country that is otherwise sagebrush to the horizon that strip carries almost everything. Sage Grouse lek on the benches above it, and the river holds Trumpeter Swans and nesting raptors.

  • Habitat: Cottonwood and willow riparian along a large desert river, sagebrush and greasewood benches, alkali flat and reservoir shoreline.
  • When: April for the Sage Grouse leks, and late May to July for the riparian and sagebrush breeders.
  • Getting there: Salt Lake City is the airport, three hours west, and Green River the base. The refuge has a gravel road along the river with free access, and the lek viewing is arranged through the refuge office.
  • Base yourself in: Green River
  • Look for: Sage Grouse, Sage Thrasher, Sagebrush Sparrow, Brewer's Sparrow, Trumpeter Swan, Ferruginous Hawk, Golden Eagle, Prairie Falcon, Burrowing Owl, Long-billed Curlew, American Avocet, Wilson's Phalarope
  • Parks and reserves: Seedskadee National Wildlife Refuge, Ashley National Forest (Forest or grassland), Flaming Gorge National Recreation Area (National site), Island Park (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Ashley National Forest, Flaming Gorge National Recreation Area, Fontenelle Reservoir, Green River, Island Park, Seedskadee National Wildlife Refuge
  • Also watch for: Pronghorn cross the refuge on one of the longest land mammal migrations on the continent, and moose use the willow.

The Red Desert

The largest unfenced sagebrush country left in the country, with a desert elk herd in it

The Red Desert is high cold sagebrush over a basin that has no outlet to the sea, and it holds the largest migratory pronghorn herd and a desert elk herd found nowhere else. For birds it means Sage Grouse, Mountain Plover and the full sagebrush passerine list at a scale almost nowhere else offers.

  • Habitat: High cold sagebrush steppe in a closed basin, alkali playa and desert lake, sand dune field, and eroded badland at Adobe Town.
  • When: April for the Sage Grouse leks and late May to June for the breeding birds. There is no shade and no water.
  • Getting there: Rawlins or Rock Springs are the bases, with Salt Lake City or Denver the airports. Everything is unsurfaced two-track with no fuel and no signal, and a wet spell makes the clay impassable.
  • Base yourself in: Rawlins
  • Look for: Sage Grouse, Mountain Plover, Sage Thrasher, Sagebrush Sparrow, Brewer's Sparrow, Chestnut-collared Longspur, Thick-billed Longspur, Ferruginous Hawk, Golden Eagle, Burrowing Owl, Long-billed Curlew, Loggerhead Shrike
  • Parks and reserves: Adobe Town Wilderness Study Area (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Adobe Town Wilderness Study Area, Chain Lakes Wildlife Habitat Management Area, Killpecker Sand Dunes
  • Also watch for: The desert elk herd is the only one of its kind, and the pronghorn migration through here is among the longest on the continent.

The Bighorn Mountains and basin

A range that stands alone out of the plains, with a thousand-metre canyon cut into its side

The Bighorns rise as an island out of the surrounding basin, so their conifer forest is cut off from the main Rockies and carries a slightly different list. Bighorn Canyon on the eastern side is a deep limestone gorge with wild horses on the rim.

  • Habitat: Lodgepole and spruce forest on a high plateau, subalpine meadow, limestone canyon and cliff, sagebrush basin below, and reservoir and wetland at Yellowtail.
  • When: Late June to September, once the high road across the range is clear of snow.
  • Getting there: Sheridan or Billings in Montana are the airports and Sheridan the base. The Cloud Peak Skyway crosses the range on tarmac and closes with heavy snow, and Yellowtail has marked wetland access below the dam.
  • Base yourself in: Sheridan
  • Look for: Clark's Nutcracker, American Three-toed Woodpecker, Dusky Grouse, American Dipper, Golden Eagle, Prairie Falcon, Canyon Wren, Rock Wren, Mountain Bluebird, Green-tailed Towhee, Sandhill Crane, White-faced Ibis
  • Parks and reserves: Bighorn National Forest (Forest or grassland), Bighorn Canyon National Recreation Area (National site), Cloud Peak Wilderness (Protected area), Pryor Mountain Wilderness Study Area (Protected area), Bighorn Tack-On Wilderness Study Area (Protected area)
  • Also covers: Bighorn Canyon National Recreation Area, Bighorn National Forest, Bighorn Tack-On Wilderness Study Area, Cloud Peak Wilderness, Pryor Mountain Wilderness Study Area, Yellowtail Wildlife Habitat Management Area
  • Also watch for: Wild horses range the Bighorn Canyon rim and the range holds moose, elk and bighorn sheep.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Wyoming?

The second half of June, when everything from riparian cottonwood to alpine is singing and both mountain ranges are open. April is the answer if Sage Grouse leks are the point, and December to March for the elk refuge below Jackson.

Where do I see Sage Grouse?

Seedskadee arranges lek viewing through its refuge office, and the Red Desert has leks on public land reachable on two-track. April, at first light, from a vehicle. Do not approach a lek on foot: displaying males leave and do not come back that morning.

What are the Red Desert roads like?

Unsurfaced two-track with no fuel, no water, no shade and no mobile signal, and the clay becomes impassable after rain. Carry more than you think you need and tell someone where you are going. That emptiness is exactly why the sagebrush birds are still there.

Is Yellowstone worth it for birds, given the crowds?

Yes, if you get away from the thermal car parks. It holds one of the highest Great Grey Owl densities in North America, the only pelican colony in the park system, Harlequin Duck on the fast rivers and about thirty breeding Trumpeter Swans.

When do the mountain roads open?

Most Yellowstone roads reopen to cars between mid-April and late May and close again in early November, and the Cloud Peak Skyway across the Bighorns is usually clear from June to October. Plan any high-country birding for July or August.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
