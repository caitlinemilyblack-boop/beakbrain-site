Birding in Michigan

Kirtland's Warbler nests in dense young jack pine and nothing else, and almost the entire world population breeds on a few hundred square miles of Michigan sand plain that are planted and cut on a rotation for it.

Birds that decide the trip

Kirtland's Warbler in the young jack pine

It nests on the ground under dense jack pine between about five and twenty years old, and nowhere else. The population came back from 167 singing males, and the habitat is now planted and cut on a rotation to keep making it.

When Mid-May to early July, on a guided tour.

Where The Kirtland's Warbler jack pine

Boreal Owl at Whitefish Point

The observatory nets and counts owls through the spring, and in an irruption year Boreal Owl numbers there are among the highest recorded anywhere in the country.

When Late March to April, at the observatory count.

Where Whitefish Point and the eastern Upper Peninsula

Yellow Rail in the Seney sedge

It calls like two stones tapped together, only after dark, from sedge marsh so dense that seeing one is close to impossible. Seney is the eastern stronghold.

When June, after dark in wet sedge.

Where Seney and the peatlands

Piping Plover on the Great Lakes shore

The Great Lakes population is separate from the Atlantic one and much smaller, a few dozen pairs on open sand and cobble beach, and Michigan holds most of them.

When May to August, on roped beach sections.

Where Saginaw Bay and Tawas Point

Canvasback on the Detroit River

Diving ducks gather in the lower lakes in numbers that are hard to take in, and the river between Lake St. Clair and Lake Erie holds rafts of them through the winter.

When November to March, on the open river and lake.

Where The Detroit River and Lake Erie

Spruce Grouse in the northern forest

It sits still on a branch and lets people walk past, which is why it is missed far more often than it is absent. The Upper Peninsula and Isle Royale both hold it.

When All year, most easily in June on quiet forest tracks.

Where Isle Royale and the Keweenaw, Whitefish Point and the eastern Upper Peninsula, Seney and the peatlands

Connecticut Warbler on passage at Tawas

A skulking bird most people miss everywhere else turns up on the point in late May, because a migrant that has run out of land takes whatever cover it can find.

When The third week of May, in low cover.

Where Saginaw Bay and Tawas Point

Trumpeter Swan on the Seney pools

They were reintroduced to the refuge and now breed on the managed pools, where the dyke roads put a nesting pair within easy view from a bicycle.

When May to September, on the refuge pools.

Where Seney and the peatlands

Where: Seney and the peatlands

When: June, after dark in wet sedge.

When to Go

May and June carry almost everything here. The jack pine warbler sings for about six weeks, the Tawas Point passage lasts a fortnight, and Isle Royale is only open at all from mid-April to October. Winter is for owls and diving ducks and very little else.

The Whitefish counts

Late March to May

Owls at night and raptors by day funnel up the point rather than cross the lake.

Go for Whitefish Point for the daily counts, which peak in April.

Trade off The jack pine warbler has not arrived and the southern marshes are still frozen.

Typical daily average

The May passage

The second and third weeks of May

Tawas Point takes a whole warbler migration onto a small sand spit.

Go for Tawas Point at first light, then Shiawassee inland.

Trade off It is a narrow window and it moves with the weather.

Typical daily average

The jack pine season

Mid-May to early July

Kirtland's Warbler is singing on the breeding blocks and the guided tours are running.

Go for The Grayling and Mio tours, and Seney or Whitefish for the boreal birds.

Trade off The breeding blocks are closed to independent entry through the whole window.

Typical daily average

The duck winter

November to March

Diving ducks and swans fill the lower lakes and the owls come south in irruption years.

Go for The Detroit River and Lake St. Clair, and Whitefish Point in an owl winter.

Trade off The Upper Peninsula is deep in snow and Isle Royale is shut.

The Kirtland's Warbler jack pine

A warbler that nests in one kind of tree, at one age, in one part of one state

Kirtland's Warbler will only nest in dense young jack pine between about five and twenty years old, which used to be created by wildfire and is now created by planting and cutting on a rotation. The whole world population breeds in this corner of the state and a few places nearby, and it came back from 167 singing males.

  • Habitat: Dense young jack pine on outwash sand, planted and cut on rotation, with older pine, oak and open grassy plains between the blocks.
  • When: Mid-May to early July, when the males are singing. The guided tours run through that window only.
  • Getting there: Traverse City is the airport and Grayling the base. The breeding blocks are CLOSED to entry from 1 May to mid-August, and the only legal way in is a free guided tour from the Grayling or Mio offices.
  • Base yourself in: Grayling
  • Look for: Kirtland's Warbler, Upland Sandpiper, Nashville Warbler, Northern Harrier, Black-backed Woodpecker, Eastern Whip-poor-will, Vesper Sparrow, Clay-colored Sparrow, Brown Thrasher, American Goshawk, Merlin, Common Nighthawk
  • Also covers: Grayling, Huron National Forest, Kirtland's Warbler Management Area
  • Also watch for: The jack pine plains hold black bear, elk in the Pigeon River country nearby, and the rare Karner blue butterfly.

Whitefish Point and the Upper Peninsula forest

A sand spit at the top of the lake, funnelling every migrant that will not cross open water

Whitefish Point sticks out into Lake Superior and northbound birds run up it rather than cross the water, so a few acres of dune and jack pine take a whole spring migration. It counts owls at night, raptors by day and waterbirds offshore, all from the same spot.

  • Habitat: Sand spit and dune with jack pine, boreal forest of spruce, fir and aspen inland, bog and beaver flowage, and the open water of the lake.
  • When: Late March to May for the owl and raptor counts, which peak in April, and November to February in an irruption winter.
  • Getting there: Sault Ste. Marie is the nearest airport and Paradise the base. The bird observatory at the point has a car park, a shop and daily counts posted, and the road in is paved.
  • Base yourself in: Paradise
  • Look for: Boreal Owl, Northern Saw-whet Owl, Great Grey Owl, Snowy Owl, Rough-legged Buzzard, American Goshawk, Long-tailed Duck, Red-necked Grebe, Evening Grosbeak, Pine Grosbeak, Two-barred Crossbill, Bohemian Waxwing
  • Also covers: Hiawatha National Forest, Lake Superior State Forest, Whitefish Point
  • Also watch for: Moose have been reintroduced to the peninsula and wolves are established across it.

Seney and the peatlands

A hundred thousand acres of pool, bog and marsh with a road through the middle of it

Seney was drained for farming, failed, and was made a refuge, and the pools and dykes left behind now carry loons, Trumpeter Swans and Sandhill Cranes. Yellow Rail calls from the sedge at night, at the eastern edge of its range.

  • Habitat: Sphagnum bog with black spruce and tamarack, sedge marsh, a system of managed pools and dykes, jack pine ridges and northern hardwood.
  • When: June for the breeding birds. Yellow Rail calls after dark, so the marsh is worth a night as well as a morning.
  • Getting there: Marquette or Escanaba are the airports and Germfask the base. The refuge has a seven-mile auto tour and a visitor centre, and the dyke roads are open to walking and cycling.
  • Base yourself in: Germfask
  • Look for: Yellow Rail, Trumpeter Swan, Common Loon, Sandhill Crane, American Bittern, LeConte's Sparrow, Sedge Wren, Palm Warbler, Rusty Blackbird, Black-backed Woodpecker, Spruce Grouse, Northern Waterthrush
  • Also covers: Manistique, Seney National Wildlife Refuge
  • Also watch for: The refuge holds wolves, moose and beaver, and its otter population is watched from the pool dykes.

Isle Royale and the Keweenaw

An island wilderness forty miles offshore, reached only by boat or floatplane

Isle Royale is a roadless island in the north of Lake Superior with no bridge and a season of a few months, and it holds boreal forest birds along with the wolves and moose it is famous for. The Keweenaw on the mainland is the hawk-watch and the migrant point.

  • Habitat: Boreal spruce and fir, northern hardwood, bog and inland lake, rock shoreline and cliff, with open lake water between island and mainland.
  • When: June and July, which is most of the season the island is open at all: mid-April to the end of October.
  • Getting there: Houghton is the airport and Copper Harbor the base. Isle Royale is reached by a ferry of three to six hours or by floatplane, needs a park fee and a night booked ahead, and closes entirely from November to mid-April.
  • Base yourself in: Copper Harbor
  • Look for: Spruce Grouse, Canada Jay, Boreal Chickadee, Black-backed Woodpecker, American Three-toed Woodpecker, Common Loon, Merlin, Blackburnian Warbler, Black-throated Green Warbler, Mourning Warbler, Winter Wren, Bald Eagle
  • Also covers: Copper Harbor, Isle Royale National Park, Keweenaw Peninsula
  • Also watch for: The island wolf and moose populations have been studied continuously since 1958, the longest predator-prey study anywhere.

The Detroit River and Lake Erie

Coastal marsh at the bottom of the lakes, taking the raptor stream and the diving ducks

The bottom of Lake Huron drains through the Detroit River into Lake Erie, and the marshes on either side hold enormous numbers of diving ducks in winter. Migrating raptors funnel down the same corridor in autumn rather than cross the water, and Pointe Mouillee is one of the great shorebird stops in the interior.

  • Habitat: Coastal Great Lakes marsh, diked management units and mudflat, open lake and river channel, and lakeplain prairie remnants inland.
  • When: August to October for the shorebirds on the drawn-down units, and November to March for the diving ducks and swans.
  • Getting there: Detroit is the airport, forty minutes north, and Monroe the base. Pointe Mouillee is walked or cycled on dykes with no vehicle access, and the marshes are hunted in autumn so check the season.
  • Base yourself in: Monroe
  • Look for: Canvasback, Redhead, Tundra Swan, Black Tern, King Rail, Least Bittern, Common Gallinule, Marsh Wren, Yellow-headed Blackbird, Prothonotary Warbler, Bald Eagle, Caspian Tern
  • Also covers: Erie Marsh Preserve, Lake Erie Where the Detroit River Enters It, Lake St. Clair, Pointe Mouillee State Game Area
  • Also watch for: The marshes hold the eastern fox snake and one of the largest wintering diving duck concentrations on the Great Lakes.

Saginaw Bay and Tawas Point

A hooked sand point on Lake Huron that concentrates a whole spring warbler passage

Tawas Point hooks out into Lake Huron and migrants moving up the shore pile into it, which is why a small state park produces May lists out of proportion to its size. Shiawassee inland is bottomland and managed marsh at the head of the bay.

  • Habitat: Sand spit and dune with low scrub, coastal marsh and mudflat on the bay, bottomland hardwood and managed impoundment inland, and lakeplain prairie remnant.
  • When: The second and third weeks of May for the warbler passage at the point, and October to March for waterfowl on the bay.
  • Getting there: Detroit or Flint are the airports and Bay City the base. Tawas Point is a state park with a small entry fee and a loop trail, and Shiawassee has an auto tour open seasonally.
  • Base yourself in: Bay City
  • Look for: Piping Plover, Cerulean Warbler, Golden-winged Warbler, Connecticut Warbler, Prothonotary Warbler, Henslow's Sparrow, Sandhill Crane, Tundra Swan, Ruddy Turnstone, Hudsonian Whimbrel, Common Tern, Dickcissel
  • Also covers: Saginaw Bay, Shiawassee National Wildlife Refuge, Tawas Point State Park
  • Also watch for: The bay holds one of the larger walleye fisheries in the country and the refuge carries a large deer and turkey population.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Michigan?

The third week of May, which is when the Tawas Point warbler passage peaks and Kirtland's Warbler has just arrived on the breeding blocks. April is the answer if Whitefish Point and the owl count are the point, and June if Isle Royale is.

How do I see Kirtland's Warbler?

On a free guided tour from Grayling or Mio, mid-May to early July. The breeding blocks are closed to independent entry from 1 May to mid-August, so the tour is the only legal way in. Book it, because places are limited.

Why is Whitefish Point so good?

It is a sand spit sticking into Lake Superior, and northbound birds follow the shoreline up it rather than set out over open water. That funnels raptors, owls and passerines into a few acres, and the observatory has been counting them there for decades.

How do I get to Isle Royale?

By ferry from Houghton or Copper Harbor, three to six hours, or by floatplane. There are no roads on the island, a park fee applies, nights must be booked, and it closes completely from November to mid-April.

Where do I look for Piping Plover?

On the Lake Huron and Lake Michigan beaches in summer. The Great Lakes population is separate from the Atlantic one and far smaller, only a few dozen pairs, and Michigan holds most of them behind roped enclosures on open sand.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
