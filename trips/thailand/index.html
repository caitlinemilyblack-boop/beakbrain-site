Birds that decide the trip

Spoon-billed Sandpiper on the Inner Gulf

The salt pans at the head of the Gulf of Thailand are the most reliable wintering site anywhere for Spoon-billed Sandpiper. The species is down to a few hundred pairs, and the pans that hold it are working salt farms rather than a reserve.

When November to March, with numbers building from October.

Where Pak Thale, Laem Pak Bia

Nordmann's Greenshank on the same flats

Nordmann's Greenshank winters on the same Inner Gulf flats and is scarcely less rare. It feeds on the open mud rather than in the pans, so the tide matters more for this one than for the sandpiper.

When November to March, and best on a rising tide.

Where Pak Thale, Laem Pak Bia, Khok Kham

Great Hornbill over Khao Yai

Khao Yai holds four hornbill species and the Great Hornbill is the one most people see, crossing the valleys morning and evening. The park is close enough to Bangkok that this is a short trip rather than an expedition.

When January to April, while the birds are breeding and most active.

Where Khao Yai National Park, Kaeng Krachan National Park

Mangrove Pitta at Krabi

Mangrove Pitta lives in mangrove forest and in no other habitat, which makes the Andaman coast creeks the place to look. It is heard far more often than seen, and a boat gets you to where the calls are.

When February to May, when the birds call most in the early morning.

Where Krabi, Ao Phang Nga

Green-tailed Sunbird on the Doi Inthanon summit

The summit of Doi Inthanon carries mossy forest and a sphagnum bog with a bird list that exists nowhere else in Thailand, and Green-tailed Sunbird is the most visible of them along the Ang Ka boardwalk.

When November to February, when the winter visitors are also in.

Where Doi Inthanon National Park

Giant Nuthatch on the border ridges

Giant Nuthatch reaches Thailand only along the Myanmar border ridges, where it uses old pines. Doi Lang and Doi Ang Khang are where most people look for it.

When December to February, and the tracks need a high-clearance vehicle.

Where Doi Lang, Doi Ang Khang

Ratchet-tailed Treepie at Kaeng Krachan

Kaeng Krachan is the largest national park in Thailand and its forest runs over the border into Myanmar, which is why its list includes birds scarce elsewhere in the country. Ratchet-tailed Treepie is one of the ones people come for.

When November to April, while the park road above Ban Krang is open.

Where Kaeng Krachan National Park, Ban Krang

Helmeted Hornbill at Khao Sok

Helmeted Hornbill is hunted for its solid casque and has collapsed across its range. Khao Sok is one of the Thai forests where it hangs on, and its call carries a long way over the karst.

When December to April in the dry season.

Where Khao Sok National Park, Cheow Lan Lake

When to Go

Thailand runs on the monsoon. The dry cool season from November to February is when every region works at once, and the southwest monsoon closes forest roads from May onward. The far south keeps its own timing and stays wetter for longer.

November to February

Dry, cool and the only window when the whole country is usable at once.

Go for Winter visitors fill the northern ridges, the Inner Gulf pans hold both rare waders, and the Kaeng Krachan upper road is open.

Trade off This is high season for tourism generally, and the northern summits are genuinely cold before dawn.

Doi Inthanon and the northern mountains

The highest ground in Thailand, and a summit bog with its own bird list

Doi Inthanon reaches 2,565 metres, the highest point in Thailand, and the summit carries mossy evergreen forest and a sphagnum bog that holds species found nowhere else in the country. The road runs to the top, so the altitude comes without the climb.

  • Habitat: Montane evergreen forest with rhododendron and moss, a summit sphagnum bog, and pine and dry dipterocarp lower down. The park spans more than 2,000 metres of altitude in a short distance.
  • When: November to February, when the winter visitors are in and the weather is dry and cool. The summit can be close to freezing at dawn in December and January.
  • Getting there: Chiang Mai is the airport and the park entrance is about two hours southwest. The summit road is paved to the top, and the Ang Ka boardwalk at the summit is the standard first stop.
  • Base yourself in: Chiang Mai
  • Look for: Green-tailed Sunbird, Ashy-throated Warbler, Yellow-bellied Fantail, Blue-winged Minla, Silver-eared Laughingthrush, Rufous-winged Fulvetta, Dark-sided Thrush, Eyebrowed Thrush, Pygmy Cupwing, Snowy-browed Flycatcher, Chinese Shortwing, Mrs. Hume's Pheasant
  • National park: Doi Inthanon National Park
  • Also covers: Chiang Mai, Doi Inthanon National Park, Doi Suthep, Mae Chaem
  • Also watch for: Northern treeshrew and Phayre's leaf monkey in the montane forest.

Doi Ang Khang and the Myanmar border

Border ridges that carry Chinese and Himalayan birds into Thailand

The ridges along the Myanmar border north of Chiang Mai take a set of species at the southern edge of a Chinese and Himalayan range. Doi Lang in particular has become the place for several birds that reach Thailand only along this frontier.

Chiang Saen and the northern wetlands

A lake on the Mekong plain that fills with wintering ducks

Nong Bong Khai near Chiang Saen is a shallow lake on the Mekong plain and one of the more reliable wintering waterfowl sites in the north. The river itself carries sandbar-nesting birds where the channel is wide.

  • Habitat: Shallow freshwater lake with floating vegetation and reed fringe, surrounded by paddy and scrub. The Mekong channel has sandbars and shingle islands at low water.
  • When: November to February for the wintering ducks and waders. The lake is at its most productive when water levels are low in the dry season.
  • Getting there: Chiang Rai has an airport about an hour away. Nong Bong Khai is signed off Route 1016 just south of Chiang Saen, and the Mekong is a few minutes further on.
  • Base yourself in: Chiang Rai
  • Look for: Ferruginous Duck, Baer's Pochard, Cotton Pygmy Goose, Pheasant-tailed Jacana, Bronze-winged Jacana, Purple Swamphen, Small Pratincole, River Lapwing, Great Cormorant, Black-headed Ibis
  • National park: Nong Bong Khai Non-hunting Area
  • Also covers: Chiang Rai, Chiang Saen, Mekong River, Nong Bong Khai Non-hunting Area
  • Also watch for: Smooth-coated otter use the Mekong channel where it is undisturbed.

Kaeng Krachan and the western forest

The largest national park in Thailand, on a forest that runs into Myanmar

Kaeng Krachan is the largest national park in the country and its forest continues over the border into Myanmar, which gives it a bird list drawn from both. The park road climbs from lowland forest to a ridge campsite and the hornbills cross it at dawn.

The Inner Gulf mudflats

Salt pans holding one of the rarest waders in the world

The salt pans and mudflats at the head of the Gulf of Thailand are the most reliable wintering site anywhere for Spoon-billed Sandpiper. The same flats hold Nordmann's Greenshank and very large numbers of commoner waders.

  • When: November to March for the wintering waders, including both rarities. Numbers build from October and the birds leave through April.
  • Getting there: Bangkok is about two hours away and Phetchaburi is the nearest town to Pak Thale. The pans are worked from the roadside and a local guide saves a great deal of time with the two rare species.
  • Base yourself in: Phetchaburi

Limestone karst over rainforest, with a reservoir running through it

Krabi and the Andaman coast

Mangrove creeks holding a pitta that lives in them and nowhere else

The mangrove belt along the Andaman coast holds Mangrove Pitta, which lives in that habitat alone, and the estuary flats near Krabi take migrant waders. Boat trips through the creeks are the usual way to see both.

  • Habitat: Extensive mangrove forest on tidal creeks, with intertidal mudflat at the river mouths and limestone karst islands offshore in Phang Nga bay.
  • When: December to April in the dry season, and the pitta calls most in the early morning from February to May.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
