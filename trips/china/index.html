At a glance

Best months
  • May and June for the western mountains
  • August to October for the Yellow Sea flyway
  • December to February for the tropical south and the wintering cranes
Regions
Western Sichuan: Balang Shan and WolongNorthern Sichuan: Tangjiahe, Jiuzhaigou and RuoergaiQinghai and the Tibetan plateauYunnan: Gaoligongshan and the far westYunnan: Lijiang, Zhongdian and the northwestThe Yellow Sea coast: Rudong and the Jiangsu mudflatsThe northeast: Jilin, Heilongjiang and the crane marshesBeidaihe, Beijing and the Bohai coastHainan and the tropical southXinjiang and the Tian Shan
Getting in
Chengdu (CTU) for Sichuan, Xining (XNN) for the plateau, Kunming (KMG) for Yunnan, Beijing (PEK) for the Bohai coast, Shanghai (PVG) for the Jiangsu flats, Haikou (HAK) for Hainan and Urumqi (URC) for Xinjiang
Getting around
Domestic flights between regions, a car and driver within them, and the high-speed rail network for the eastern legs

When to Go

China's birding calendar is set by altitude and latitude rather than by a wet season. The Sichuan and Qinghai passes are under snow until May and shut again in October; the Yellow Sea flyway peaks on passage in April and May and again in August to October; the northeast's cranes are on their breeding marshes in summer and its winter is genuinely dangerous; and Hainan and the tropical south are the only places worth going in January.

Peak

May and June

The western mountain window, and the reason most China tours run when they do.

Go for Balang Shan is clear of snow, the pheasants are displaying at every altitude on the pass, Grandala flocks are over the top, and Qinghai's breeding season is under way at 3,200 m.

Trade off It is the only window, so the Sichuan lodges and guides are booked far ahead, and the eastern coast has nothing on it once the spring passage ends.

Typical daily average
  • Wolong: 8 to 18 °C / 46 to 64 °F
  • Xining: 6 to 21 °C / 43 to 70 °F
  • Baihualing: 17 to 26 °C / 63 to 79 °F

Flyway

August to October

The southbound passage on the Yellow Sea, which is a separate trip from the mountains.

Go for Spoon-billed Sandpiper and Nordmann's Greenshank stage on the Jiangsu flats from August, and Beidaihe's raptor and passerine passage peaks through September.

Trade off The tides govern everything, so the days are shaped by a tide table rather than by daylight, and September in Jiangsu is still hot and humid.

Typical daily average
  • Rudong: 18 to 28 °C / 64 to 82 °F
  • Beidaihe: 15 to 25 °C / 59 to 77 °F
  • Changchun: 10 to 23 °C / 50 to 73 °F

Winter

December to February

The southern and eastern winter, when the north is closed and the cranes are down.

Go for Hainan and the tropical south are warm and green, and the eastern reserves hold wintering cranes, geese and Black-faced Spoonbill in numbers.

Trade off Changchun is at minus twenty, every western pass is shut, and a winter trip is a different country from a May one.

Typical daily average
  • Haikou: 15 to 21 °C / 59 to 70 °F
  • Rudong: 0 to 6 °C / 32 to 43 °F
  • Changchun: -20 to -10 °C / -4 to 14 °F

Western Sichuan: Balang Shan and Wolong

A road that climbs from 1,300 m to a 4,500 m pass in four hours, on the western edge of the province where Sichuan meets the Tibetan plateau

The road over Balang Shan is the most productive single stretch of tarmac in Chinese birding. It leaves the Wolong valley in temperate forest and tops out on alpine scree, and the pheasants change every few hundred metres of altitude: Chinese Monal on the scree at the top, Blue Eared Pheasant in the scrub below it, Temminck's Tragopan and Golden Pheasant in the forest at the bottom. Grandala flocks over the pass in spring like blue hail.

  • Habitat: Wolong was set up for giant pandas, and the habitat inside it is being degraded like most of China's 400-odd reserves. Over 600 of the country's 1,244 recorded species occur in Sichuan.
  • When: Best from April to July. Chinese Monal, Temminck's Tragopan and Golden Pheasant stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Wolong village lies about 100 km west of Chengdu, six hours away on a rough road. Most of the birding is done from the road and the few trails, above all at the meadows and scree slopes beyond the Balang Shan pass at 4,300 m, the headquarters area in the Valley of the Heroes and the Ting Fang Pass at 3,658 m. A track leaving a small suspension bridge 10 km out of the village climbs to the observation station in about two hours on foot.
  • Look for: Chinese Monal, Blue Eared Pheasant, Temminck's Tragopan, Golden Pheasant, Grandala, Snow Partridge, Firethroat, Chinese Fulvetta
  • Parks and reserves: Wolong National Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The valley village and reserve headquarters at about 1,900 m. The pass road starts here.
    • : The town on the far side of Balang Shan at 3,200 m, under the Siguniang peaks.
  • Also covers: Rilong, Wolong National Nature Reserve
  • Also watch for: Wolong is the panda reserve, and while wild Giant Panda is effectively never seen the breeding centre is on the road. Takin, a goat-antelope the size of a cow, uses the mid-altitude forest, and Blue Sheep hold the scree at the top of the pass.

Northern Sichuan: Tangjiahe, Jiuzhaigou and Ruoergai

Bamboo forest, limestone lakes and a high grassland plateau on the Gansu border

North of Chengdu the country changes twice. Tangjiahe is bamboo and broadleaf forest with the tamest large mammals in China, Jiuzhaigou's travertine lakes hold the montane forest birds, and beyond them the road climbs onto the Ruoergai grassland at 3,400 m, which is Black-necked Crane country and the southern edge of the Tibetan plateau.

  • Habitat: Jiuzhaigou in north Sichuan is a wild land of lakes, streams and waterfalls set among forested peaks capped with snow. The road in from the southwest crosses the Zhegu Shan pass at 4,000 m and the eastern edge of the Tibetan Plateau, and high altitude scrub covers the ground between Ruoergai and the reserve.
  • When: Best from March to July. Black-necked Crane, Golden Pheasant and Blood Pheasant are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Jiuzhaigou is about 430 km north of Chengdu by way of Sunpang. A 15 km road runs from the reserve entrance to Nuorilang and a second 15 km road carries on to Swan Lake, and the birding is done on foot from these two roads. A steep yak trail above Zechawa climbs to meadows and a yak shed, and a trail follows the normally dry river bed above the Swan Lake car park into forest.
  • Look for: Black-necked Crane, Golden Pheasant, Blood Pheasant, Sichuan Jay, Przevalski's Nuthatch, Chinese Grouse, Sichuan Treecreeper, White-browed Tit
  • Parks and reserves: Tangjiahe National Nature Reserve, Jiuzhaigou National Nature Reserve, Zoige Wetland National Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The reserve in the far north of the province. A road runs through the valley with mammals along it. It is mapped at Qingchuan, the county town it is reached from, because four unrelated places called Tangjiahe exist across China and the reserve itself is not a named point.
    • : The town on the Ruoergai grassland at 3,400 m. It is the base for the crane marshes.
  • Also covers: Jiuzhaigou National Nature Reserve, Qingchuan, Tangjiahe National Nature Reserve, Zoige Wetland National Nature Reserve
  • Also watch for: Tangjiahe is the reserve where Takin, Tufted Deer and Chinese Serow walk the road in daylight because nobody hunts them. Golden Snub-nosed Monkey holds the higher forest, and the Ruoergai grassland carries Plateau Pika in numbers, which is what the raptors are there for.

Qinghai and the Tibetan plateau

Steppe, salt lake and pass country above 3,000 m, with the Kunlun range along its southern edge

Qinghai is the accessible Tibetan plateau: a paved road from Xining to Golmud crossing steppe, salt lake and 4,800 m passes, with the birds getting stranger as the altitude climbs. Tibetan Sandgrouse and Tibetan Snowcock are on the high ground, Przevalski's Finch is a Chinese endemic in a family of one, and Qinghai Lake holds breeding Bar-headed Goose and Brown-headed Gull in thousands.

  • Habitat: Marshes, meadows and scrubby mountain slopes surround Qinghai Hu, the largest lake in China, which measures 100 km by 60 km and sits at 3,200 m. Xining stands at 2,500 m on the northeastern edge of the Tibetan Plateau below barren hills. Golmud lies at the southern edge of the Zaidam Depression, with reservoirs to the north of the town and sand dunes on its southeastern outskirts.
  • When: Best from April to October. Przevalski's Finch, Bar-headed Goose and Black-necked Crane are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: The wet meadows along the southwest shore of the lake near Heimahe, 200 km west of Xining, are the richest ground, together with the scrubby hillsides and the high plateau behind the town. Bird Island lies in the northwest corner of the lake, about 70 km from Heimahe and 16 km from the nearest accommodation. South of Xining the road to Nangqen climbs onto the plateau and crosses the Er La Pass at 4,482 m.
  • Look for: Tibetan Sandgrouse, Tibetan Snowcock, Przevalski's Finch, Bar-headed Goose, Black-necked Crane, Tibetan Partridge, Ground Tit, Blanford's Snowfinch
  • Parks and reserves: Qinghai Lake National Nature Reserve, Sanjiangyuan National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The provincial capital at 2,300 m. It has the airport and the road west onto the plateau.
    • : The largest lake in China, saline and at 3,200 m. Goose and gull colonies nest on its islands.
    • : The town in the Qaidam basin at the far end of the road. It is the way in to the Kunlun passes.
  • Also covers: Golmud, Qinghai Lake National Nature Reserve, Sanjiangyuan National Park, Xining
  • Also watch for: Tibetan Gazelle, Tibetan Wild Ass and Wild Yak all use the plateau. The Gouli valley south of Golmud has become the most reliable place in the world for Snow Leopard, and you watch from a vehicle with local spotters. Pallas's Cat and Tibetan Fox are on the same ground.

Yunnan: Gaoligongshan and the far west

The eastern end of the Himalaya, where Indo-Burmese forest reaches into China

Western Yunnan is the only place Indo-Burmese lowland forest reaches Chinese territory, and the village of Baihualing has built a birding economy on it: dozens of paid hides at feeding and bathing pools, run by families, where laughingthrushes and pheasants come in at a few metres. Mrs. Hume's Pheasant and Lady Amherst's Pheasant both come to hides here, and the Yingjiang lowlands to the west add hornbills.

  • Habitat: The Gaoligong Shan reserve lies in the mountains of northwest Yunnan. Further south at Ruili, on the border with Burma, excellent deciduous forest grows beside a broad, shallow river.
  • Getting there: At Ruili the birding is done in the forested hills northwest of the town.
  • Look for: Mrs. Hume's Pheasant, Lady Amherst's Pheasant, Rufous-necked Hornbill, Silver-eared Mesia, Rusty-naped Pitta, Grey-lored Broadbill, Beautiful Nuthatch, Blyth's Tragopan
  • Parks and reserves: Gaoligongshan National Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The village on the eastern slope at about 1,500 m, with the hide network. A trail climbs into the reserve.
    • : The town west of the range, with an airport, volcanic hot springs and the road on to the border.
  • Also covers: Baihualing, Gaoligongshan National Nature Reserve, Tengchong
  • Also watch for: Gaoligongshan holds the Skywalker Hoolock Gibbon, described in 2017 and named for Star Wars, with a few hundred animals split across the border with Myanmar. Red Panda is in the same forest and Asian Elephant uses the Yingjiang lowlands.

Yunnan: Lijiang, Zhongdian and the northwest

Snow peaks, alpine meadow and pine forest at 3,000 m, in the Chinese foothills of the Himalaya

Northwest Yunnan is the softer version of the Tibetan plateau: pine forest, alpine meadow and snow peaks with roads and towns among them. Napahai marsh outside Zhongdian holds wintering Black-necked Crane, the Baima Shan pass gives the high pheasants, and the forests around Lijiang carry a laughingthrush and parrotbill list that takes days to work through.

  • Habitat: Remnant pine forests stand around Lijiang in northwest Yunnan. Jade Dragon Snow Mountain rises to the northwest of the town and carries the best of the remaining forest above its temple.
  • Getting there: Lijiang is about half a day by road from Dali. Work the park and the hills beyond it to the north of town, and the low hills 3 km west of town alongside the Dali road. Jade Dragon Snow Mountain is a short drive from town, and the forest above the temple takes four hours of hard hiking to reach.
  • Look for: White Eared Pheasant, Chinese Grouse, Yunnan Nuthatch, Giant Laughingthrush, Chinese White-browed Rosefinch, Grey-headed Parrotbill, Yunnan Fulvetta, Bar-tailed Treecreeper
  • Parks and reserves: Pudacuo National Park, Bitahai Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The old town at 2,400 m with an airport, under the Yulong snow range.
    • : The town formerly called Zhongdian, at 3,200 m, with the Napahai marsh outside it.
  • Also covers: Bitahai Nature Reserve, Lijiang, Pudacuo National Park, Shangri-La
  • Also watch for: Yunnan Snub-nosed Monkey, which lives at higher altitude than any other primate outside the Ethiopian highlands, is watched at a habituated group near Tacheng. Red Panda is in the same forests and Chinese Goral works the cliffs.

The Yellow Sea coast: Rudong and the Jiangsu mudflats

Intertidal mudflat on the Yellow Sea, the narrowest point of the East Asian flyway and the last refuelling stop for two critically endangered waders

The Jiangsu mudflats are where the East Asian-Australasian flyway narrows to a point. Spoon-billed Sandpiper stages here in autumn in numbers that represent a large share of the world population, and Nordmann's Greenshank with it. The flats are watched from sea walls on a rising tide, and the reclamation that has taken most of this coastline is the reason the two species are where they are.

  • Habitat: Grassy marshes run along the coast at Yancheng, north of Shanghai, and a small reserve protects part of them.
  • When: Best from August to November. Spoon-billed Sandpiper, Nordmann's Greenshank and Black-faced Spoonbill are here in every month.
  • Look for: Spoon-billed Sandpiper, Nordmann's Greenshank, Great Knot, Black-faced Spoonbill, Saunders's Gull, Reed Parrotbill, Red-crowned Crane, Far Eastern Curlew
  • Parks and reserves: Yancheng National Nature Reserve, Tiaozini, Yancheng Migratory Bird Sanctuary (World Heritage site)
  • Stops:
    • : The county on the Jiangsu coast. Its sea walls give access to the Tiaozini flats.
    • : The coastal city north of Rudong, with the crane reserve and the Pere David's Deer herd.
  • Also covers: Rudong, Tiaozini, Yancheng Migratory Bird Sanctuary, Yancheng National Nature Reserve
  • Also watch for: Yancheng holds a reintroduced Pere David's Deer herd, a species that survived only in European parks after it was lost in China, and the nature reserve there is also where the Red-crowned Cranes winter.

The northeast: Jilin, Heilongjiang and the crane marshes

Boreal forest, reed marsh and the coldest winters in the country, on the Russian and Korean borders

The northeast is where the Siberian avifauna reaches China. Zhalong and Momoge hold breeding Red-crowned Crane and passage Siberian Crane, the Changbai Shan on the Korean border carries boreal forest with Hazel Grouse and Black Woodpecker, and winter here brings Steller's Sea Eagle to the coast. It is cold beyond most people's expectations, at minus thirty in January.

  • Habitat: Wetlands fill the Zhalong reserve in northeast China. Reedy wetland, grassland and scattered elm woods cover the larger Xianghai reserve, which holds three cranes and the highly localised Rufous-backed Bunting.
  • When: Most of the birds of both reserves are present only in summer, between May and September.
  • Getting there: Zhalong is about 25 km from Qiqihar, which has air and rail links from Beijing. Two lakes either side of the entrance road just before the headquarters are worth checking, and a short track off that road leads to fields that repay long birding. Xianghai is about an hour by road from Kaitong.
  • Look for: Red-crowned Crane, Siberian Crane, White-naped Crane, Steller's Sea Eagle, Hazel Grouse, Baikal Teal, Scaly-sided Merganser, Jankowski's Bunting
  • Parks and reserves: Zhalong National Nature Reserve, Xianghai National Nature Reserve, Momoge National Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The reed marsh reserve outside Qiqihar. It is the best-known Red-crowned Crane breeding site in China.
    • : The provincial capital, with an airport and the road east towards the Changbai range.
  • Also covers: Changchun, Momoge National Nature Reserve, Xianghai National Nature Reserve, Zhalong National Nature Reserve
  • Also watch for: Amur Tiger and Amur Leopard both hold small populations in the border forests, monitored by camera and never met. Siberian Musk Deer and Sable are in the same forest, and the Songhua river carries otters.

Beidaihe, Beijing and the Bohai coast

A migration headland on the Bohai Gulf three hours from the capital, watched since the 1940s

Beidaihe is the oldest migration watchpoint in China and still the easiest way to see the flyway working. Cranes, raptors and passerines funnel down this coast in spring and autumn, with Relict Gull on the mudflats and Happy Island offshore as the passerine trap. Beijing itself has more birding than visitors expect, with the Yeyahu wetland and the hills behind the city.

  • Habitat: The resort sits on the Gulf of Bohai at the northern end of the Yellow Sea, and the range of habitat around it, from wooded gully to paddy and estuary, has drawn over 400 species. Only about ten of them are resident.
  • When: The year splits in two here. Amur Falcon is away from November to March.
  • Getting there: Beidaihe is 280 km east of Beijing. The wooded valleys near the station 85 km northwest of the capital are worth working, and the Great Wall is less crowded at Mutianyu.
  • Look for: Relict Gull, Pallas's Reed Bunting, Yellow-breasted Bunting, Pechora Pipit, Chinese Egret, Oriental Stork, Amur Falcon, Siberian Rubythroat
  • Parks and reserves: Beidaihe Coastal Wetland
  • Stops:
    • : The seaside resort on the Bohai Gulf, with the Lotus Hills and the sandflats. They have been watched since the 1940s.
    • : The capital, with international flights. The wetland and hill sites lie within an hour or two of the ring roads.
  • Also covers: Beidaihe Coastal Wetland, Beijing
  • Also watch for: The Bohai coast has little in the way of mammals and the interest is entirely in the flyway. Beijing's western hills carry Tolai Hare and Siberian Weasel, and Raccoon Dog turns up on the city edges.

Hainan and the tropical south

A tropical island off the southern coast with monsoon rainforest inland and a handful of birds found nowhere else

Hainan is China's tropical corner, and its interior mountains hold a set of endemics split from mainland forms: Hainan Partridge, Hainan Leaf Warbler and Hainan Blue Flycatcher among them. The forest reserves at Jianfengling and Bawangling are worked on made trails, and the island is warm and green when the rest of the country is frozen.

  • Habitat: Hainan is an island off the south coast of China. Its two endemic birds, a partridge and a leaf warbler, occur in the Bawangling, Jianfengling and Wuzhishan reserves.
  • Look for: Hainan Partridge, Hainan Leaf Warbler, Hainan Blue Flycatcher, Silver Pheasant, Ratchet-tailed Treepie, Blue-rumped Pitta, Orange-breasted Trogon, White-bellied Erpornis
  • Parks and reserves: Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park, Yinggeling National Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The town in the island's mountainous interior. It is the base for the forest reserves around it.
    • : The island capital and airport on the north coast, three hours from the interior.
  • Also covers: Haikou, Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park, Wuzhishan, Yinggeling National Nature Reserve
  • Also watch for: Hainan Gibbon at Bawangling is the rarest primate on earth, down to about forty animals from seven in the 1970s, and its recovery is one of the few genuinely encouraging conservation stories in the region. Sambar and Hainan Hare are on the same reserves.

Xinjiang and the Tian Shan

Desert, mountain and lake in the far northwest, closer to Kazakhstan than to Beijing

Xinjiang is Central Asia inside Chinese borders, and its birds are Central Asian: Xinjiang Ground Jay on the Taklamakan gravel, Ibisbill on the mountain rivers, Altai Snowcock on the ridges above Kanas in the far north. The distances are the difficulty, with Urumqi to Kashgar a day and a half by road, and it is the least visited of the birding regions above.

  • Habitat: Xinjiang in the extreme west of China holds poplar stands, barren desert, lakes and pine forest. Semi-desert runs between Kuytun and Altai, forested hills surround Hanas Hu, and Tien Che, the Heavenly Lake near Urumqi, lies below the mountain of Bogda Feng.
  • When: Best from April to September. Ibisbill, Himalayan Snowcock and Pallas's Sandgrouse are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Parts of Xinjiang need a travel permit, and the rules change, so check them when you book. The west end of Tien Che is popular with tourists, so the foothills to the south and east repay the walk. Boshteng Hu lies 12 km from Bofur, and Hanas Hu needs its own permission.
  • Look for: Xinjiang Ground Jay, Ibisbill, Altai Snowcock, Himalayan Snowcock, Pallas's Sandgrouse, Saxaul Sparrow, White-winged Woodpecker, Black-throated Accentor
  • Parks and reserves: Tianshan Tianchi National Park, Bayanbulak Swan Lake National Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The regional capital and the main airport, with the Tian Shan rising directly south of it.
    • : A lake under Bogda Feng, two hours east of Urumqi. The foothills north of the peak hold Himalayan Snowcock, Rufous-backed and Blue-capped Redstarts and Black-throated Accentor, and the west end draws the tourist coaches.
    • : The old Silk Road city on the western edge of the Taklamakan. It is the base for the desert and the Pamir road.
  • Also covers: Bayanbulak Swan Lake National Nature Reserve, Kashgar, Tianchi, the Heavenly Lake, Tianshan Tianchi National Park, Urumqi
  • Also watch for: Xinjiang holds Snow Leopard in the Tian Shan and Argali on the high ridges, with Goitered Gazelle on the desert edge. Przewalski's Horse has been reintroduced to the Kalamaili reserve, the only place it exists in the wild in China.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in China?

There is no one answer, and that is the honest one. May and June for the western mountains, when Balang Shan is clear and the pheasants are displaying. August to October for the Yellow Sea flyway and the Spoon-billed Sandpipers. December to February for Hainan and the wintering cranes. The three are different trips. The three seasons.

How big are the differences within the country?

Changchun in the northeast runs minus twenty to minus ten in January while Haikou on Hainan runs fifteen to twenty-one, a thirty-degree difference. Urumqi takes 234 mm of rain a year and Haikou 1,713 mm. Kashgar to Beijing is 3,400 km. Every region above is a domestic flight from the last. When to go, season by season.

Which region should a first trip take?

Sichuan. The Balang Shan road climbs three thousand metres in a morning and the pheasants change with the altitude, which is the single most concentrated birding experience in the country, and Chengdu is easy to reach. Most operators' China tour is a Sichuan tour for exactly this reason. Western Sichuan.

What are the Yunnan bird hides?

Villages in western Yunnan, Baihualing above all, have built an economy on paid hides at feeding and bathing pools. A family owns the pool, keeps it stocked and charges for a morning in the blind, and laughingthrushes, pheasants and pittas come in at a few metres. It is the most productive photography in Asia and the money goes to the village. Yunnan: Gaoligongshan and the far west.

Can I bird China independently?

With difficulty. The sites are hard to reach, permits vary by province, the village hide networks are arranged locally rather than booked online, and the language barrier is real away from the cities. Almost everybody goes with a guide and a driver, and the tour prices reflect that rather than luxury. Birding community.

Why does the Yellow Sea coast matter so much?

Because the East Asian-Australasian flyway narrows to it. Spoon-billed Sandpiper stages on the Jiangsu flats in numbers that represent a large share of the world population, with Nordmann's Greenshank alongside, and the reclamation that has taken most of this coastline is the direct reason both species are critically endangered. Part of the coast is now a World Heritage site. The Yellow Sea coast.

Why does this guide carry no certification badges?

Because the international schemes reach the big-city international hotel chains and stop there. Every chip on a BeakBrain guide renders from a scheme's own register rather than from a property's claim, and no reserve guesthouse above appears in one. Certification.

How many bird species does China have?

Around 1,450 recorded, which is the largest list of any country outside the tropics, and about ninety live nowhere else. The reason is range: the country holds Siberian taiga, Tibetan plateau, Central Asian desert and Indo-Burmese rainforest inside one border. The birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
