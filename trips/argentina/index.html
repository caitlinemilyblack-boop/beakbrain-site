Argentina runs opposite seasons at its two ends. Tierra del Fuego and Patagonia work from October to March and are shut the rest of the year; the Chaco and the northwest are at their best in the dry winter from May to September, when the south is closed. November is the fairest month for a trip that wants both.

Southern summer

November to March

The far south is open, the days are long, and everything from Patagonia to Tierra del Fuego is breeding.

Go for Magellanic Woodpecker in the lenga, penguins ashore at Punta Tombo, and the puna lagoons full of flamingos.

Trade off Hot and wet in the Chaco and the northwest, and the yungas roads can wash out.

Typical daily average
  • Ushuaia: 5 to 15 °C / 41 to 59 °F
  • Buenos Aires: 18 to 30 °C / 64 to 86 °F
  • Salta: 17 to 29 °C / 63 to 84 °F

Northern dry

May to September

The dry season in the north. The Chaco is passable, the yungas are clear, and the right whales are at Valdes.

Go for The Chaco endemics, the northwest cloud forest, and southern right whales inshore from June.

Trade off Tierra del Fuego is cold, dark and largely shut, and the high puna passes can close in snow.

Typical daily average
  • Ushuaia: -1 to 6 °C / 30 to 43 °F
  • Buenos Aires: 8 to 18 °C / 46 to 64 °F
  • Salta: 5 to 23 °C / 41 to 73 °F

Shoulder

April and October

The two months when both ends of the country are workable at once, which is why most long routes use them.

Go for Migrants moving in both directions, the north drying out or wetting up, and the south open.

Trade off Neither end is at its absolute best, and the weather is less predictable than either season proper.

Typical daily average
  • Ushuaia: 2 to 11 °C / 36 to 52 °F
  • Buenos Aires: 12 to 23 °C / 54 to 73 °F
  • Salta: 11 to 26 °C / 52 to 79 °F

Tierra del Fuego and the Beagle Channel

Southern beech forest, peat bog and the last land before Antarctica

The island at the end of the continent, where Nothofagus forest meets the Beagle Channel and the seabirds of the Southern Ocean come inshore. Most trips reach it on the way to or from Antarctica.

  • Habitat: Nothofagus forest, lakes and wild coast make up the southern extremity of the country.
  • When: Good all year. Magellanic Woodpecker, Kelp Goose and Flying Steamer Duck stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Ushuaia is the base. Tierra del Fuego National Park lies 10 km west of the town and the rubbish dump 7 km west is worth a stop on the way. Turn north before the bridge over the Lago Roca outlet and pass the park headquarters for the Lago Roca campsite and cafe, and take the track south just inside the park, before Lago Roca, to reach the bay on the channel shore. The scree slopes around the Martial glacier sit 7 km behind town and are reached by chairlift or a 3 km walk, and Garibaldi pass on Route 3 lies 50 km northeast of Ushuaia. Cruises along the Beagle Channel to the penguin colony near Harberton are run by an agency in Ushuaia.
  • Look for: Magellanic Woodpecker, Kelp Goose, Flying Steamer Duck, Austral Pygmy Owl, Magellanic Penguin, Ruddy-headed Goose
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Blackish Cinclodes, Fuegian Steamer Duck, Gentoo Penguin, Magellanic Diving Petrel
  • National park:

Patagonia and the Valdes Peninsula

Steppe, Andean lakes and a peninsula that whales calve against

Patagonia is two birding countries: the dry steppe that runs to the Atlantic, and the Andean lakes and forest along the Chilean border. Valdés is where the right whales come in, and Punta Tombo holds a million Magellanic Penguins.

  • Habitat: Arid, lake-dotted, windswept Patagonian steppe covers over a third of the country, bounded to the west by glaciated mountains, with the Valdes peninsula standing out on the east coast.
  • When: Good all year. Hooded Grebe, Elegant Crested Tinamou and Rufous-tailed Plantcutter are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Puerto Madryn and Trelew both make good bases, with the Valdes peninsula to the north and Punta Tombo to the south. The peninsula is a headland 100 km long, and the junction between the main peninsula road and the road to Puerto Piramides repays birding. The observatory sits 5 km from Puerto Piramides, with Punta Norte and Punta Cantor further round. Punta Tombo lies 130 km south of Trelew, and Dos Pozos, 95 km south of Trelew on the way to it, is worth a stop around the two derelict buildings. Glacier National Park is 50 km west of Calafate.
  • Look for: Hooded Grebe, Elegant Crested Tinamou, Rufous-tailed Plantcutter, Black-necked Swan, Magellanic Penguin, Andean Condor
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Great Shrike-Tyrant, White-browed Ground Tyrant, Chilean Flicker, Diuca Finch
  • National parks:
    • Los Glaciares National Park, The Perito Moreno glacier and the lenga forest around El Calafate, with Andean Condor over the ice.
    • Nahuel Huapi National Park, Andean lakes and araucaria at Bariloche, and the northern end of the Patagonian forest.
  • Stops:
    • : A World Heritage peninsula with right whales inshore, elephant seals, and orca that beach themselves at Punta Norte.
    • : The largest Magellanic Penguin colony on the continent, walked through on boardwalks.
    • : The base for Valdés, on the Golfo Nuevo, where whales come within sight of the promenade.
  • Also covers: Bariloche, El Calafate, Los Glaciares National Park, Nahuel Huapi National Park, Peninsula Valdes, Puerto Madryn, Punta Tombo, Trelew
  • Also watch for: Southern right whales at Valdés from June to December, orca at Punta Norte, elephant seals and guanaco throughout.

The Chaco and the Northern Dry Forest

Thorn scrub and quebracho, and the birds nobody else has

The Gran Chaco is the largest dry forest in South America and among the least visited. It is hot, flat and thorny, and it holds a set of birds that reach nowhere else, three of them effectively Chaco endemics.

  • Habitat: Mesquite and quebracho dominate the chaco, though years of degradation have turned much of it to thick thorn scrub that goes by the name El Impenetrable. The east stays wet and relatively wild while the west is dry, and the eastern chaco ends at the Ibera wetlands.
  • When: Good all year. Quebracho Crested Tinamou, Chaco Owl and Black-bodied Woodpecker stay through the year.
  • Getting there: From Salta head east 43 km to Route 34 and then turn south. For Palomitas turn east onto a 2 km track just north of the Montenegro sign, then work the main track and the side tracks beyond the village, with the ground 14 km east of Palomitas the best of it. For Joaquin V. Gonzalez continue south past the Palomitas turning for about 50 km, turn east onto Route 16 and carry on roughly 100 km to the town. Just south of it turn northeast onto Route 41 towards Santo Domingo and bird the tracks east after 14 km and 17 km.
  • Look for: Quebracho Crested Tinamou, Chaco Owl, Black-bodied Woodpecker, Black-legged Seriema, Crested Gallito, Solitary Eagle
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Cinereous Tyrant, Golden-green Woodpecker, Little Thornbird, Saffron-billed Sparrow
  • National parks:
    • Copo National Park, The largest protected block of quebracho forest, on the Santiago del Estero and Chaco border.
    • Chaco National Park, Humid Chaco on the Rio Negro. It is easier to reach than Copo and good for the woodpeckers.
  • Stops:
    • : The western Chaco town, on the road from Salta into the dry forest.
    • : A Formosa road town, and the base for the northern Chaco.
    • : The provincial town nearest Chaco National Park.
  • Also covers: Chaco National Park, Copo National Park, Joaquin V. Gonzalez, Las Lomitas, Resistencia, Saenz Pena
  • Also watch for: Giant anteater and Chacoan peccary in the deeper forest, and jaguar tracks on the sand roads.

The Northwest: Jujuy, Salta and the Yungas

Cloud forest on the eastern Andean slope, an hour above the desert

The yungas are a ribbon of subtropical cloud forest running down the eastern slope, and in the northwest they sit within an hour of dry high valleys. That compression is why the northwest carries the longest day lists in Argentina.

  • Habitat: Calilegua covers 76,320 hectares and runs through savanna, subtropical yungas and temperate forest up into alder woodland. El Rey holds much the same range on the edge of the chaco at the foot of the Andes.
  • When: Good all year. Rufous-throated Dipper, Andean Condor and Red-legged Seriema are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Tucuman and Salta both have flights from most Argentine cities and are the bases for the whole northwest. El Rey lies 190 km east of Salta, and Calilegua near Jujuy is the easier of the two to reach.
  • Look for: Rufous-throated Dipper, Yungas Pygmy Owl, Andean Condor, Red-legged Seriema, Toco Toucan
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Andean Slaty Thrush, Black-and-chestnut Eagle, Black-and-white Hawk-Eagle, Black-banded Woodcreeper, Black-capped Antwren
  • National park:
    • Calilegua National Park, The largest yungas park. A road climbs through every forest band in one morning.
  • Stops:
    • : A dry Andean valley of coloured rock. The road runs from Jujuy up to the puna (the dry high grassland of the central Andes), and the valley is the transition to high country.
    • : The valley town most northwest routes stay in, at 2,460 m.
    • : The lagoons and cloud forest just above San Salvador de Jujuy, and the dipper river.
    • : The salt flat on the road west from Purmamarca, at 3,450 m.
  • Also covers: Calilegua National Park, Libertador General San Martin, Quebrada de Humahuaca, Salinas Grandes, Salta, San Salvador de Jujuy, Tilcara, Yala
  • Also watch for: Spectacled bear at the northern limit of its range, and taruca in the high valleys.

The Puna and the High Andes

Salt flats and flamingo lagoons above 3,500 m

The puna is the high Andes at their driest: cold grassland above 3,500 m with brine lagoons in its hollows, and three flamingo species share them. The road from Jujuy climbs to it in a morning, which is why altitude is the thing to plan around here.

  • Habitat: Lago Pozuelos is a high-altitude Andean lake covering 10,000 hectares at 3,670 metres, and Laguna Larga lies 3 km west of the village of Lagunilla nearby. Ravines, cactus scrub and stands of Polylepis woodland break up the puna around them. Further south the road over the Cuesta del Obispo climbs cacti-clad slopes past the Cumbres del Obispo at 3,500 metres.
  • When: Good all year. Andean Flamingo, Giant Coot and Horned Coot are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Head west from Abra Pampa, roughly halfway between Humahuaca and La Quiaca, on the road to Rinconada. For the southern end of Lago Pozuelos turn north onto a 6 km track just past the ranger's house and bridge. Continue west and turn north to Lagunilla on the road to Yoscaba at the next junction, from where a 5 km track runs east to the western shore. Take food, warm clothes and a good sleeping bag to Lagunilla, and beware of soft mud on the approach to the lakes.
  • Look for: James's Flamingo, Andean Flamingo, Giant Coot, Horned Coot, Puna Ibis, Puna Teal, Diademed Sandpiper-Plover
  • National park: Laguna de los Pozuelos Natural Monument
  • Stops:
    • : A Ramsar brine lagoon at 3,600 m. It holds all three southern flamingos.
    • : The puna town on the northern highway, and the base for Pozuelos.
    • : The pass road from Salta to Cachi. It climbs through cardon cactus to 3,300 m.
    • : A mining town at 3,775 m on the Tren a las Nubes line, with high puna either side.
  • Also covers: Abra Pampa, Cachi, Cuesta del Obispo, La Quiaca, Laguna de los Pozuelos Natural Monument, Laguna de Pozuelos, San Antonio de los Cobres
  • Also watch for: Vicuña on the open puna and viscacha on every rock pile.

Iguazu and the Atlantic Forest

The last of the Atlantic forest, and a waterfall in it

Misiones holds the largest surviving block of Atlantic forest in Argentina, a habitat that has lost most of its extent across the border in Brazil. The falls are the reason people come and the forest around them is the reason to stay.

  • Habitat: Subtropical forest with large stands of bamboo surrounds the falls, 492 square kilometres of it inside the national park, and remnant Araucaria woodland survives in the northeast finger of the country.
  • When: Good all year. Black-fronted Piping Guan, Ochre-collared Piculet and Blond-crested Woodpecker are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: The Argentine side of the falls is the better of the two for birds. Four days should produce over a hundred species.
  • Look for: Black-fronted Piping Guan, Ochre-collared Piculet, Blond-crested Woodpecker, Toco Toucan, Sickle-winged Nightjar
  • National park:
    • Iguazu National Park, Subtropical forest around the falls. A forest trail here is worth more than the walkways.
  • Stops:
    • : The largest Misiones reserve, on the road south from Puerto Iguazú.
    • : Jesuit ruins in grassland and forest, and a different set from the falls.
    • : The border town at the falls, with its own airport.
  • Also covers: El Dorado, Iguazu National Park, Puerto Iguazu, San Ignacio, Urugua-i Provincial Park, Wanda
  • Also watch for: Coati at the falls, and jaguar and tapir in the Urugua-i corridor.

The Ibera Marshes

A wetland the size of a small country, and the rewilding in it

Iberá is 13,000 square kilometres of marsh, lagoon and floating island in Corrientes, and it is the site of the largest rewilding project in South America. Jaguar, giant otter and macaw have all been returned to it.

  • Habitat: Vast wet grassland covers Corrientes, and it holds a number of endangered species.
  • Getting there: Colonia Carlos Pellegrini is best reached from Mercedes to the south, which has much the better road. The 123 km stretch between Route 14 and the town floods easily. At Pellegrini bird the marsh next to the Centro Turistico on the north side of the road west of the Bailey bridge, the ground opposite on the south side, and the woodland at the end of a track north of the road just east of the bridge. Ituzaingo, west of Posadas, is another base for the fringes of this largely inaccessible region.
  • Look for: Strange-tailed Tyrant, Yellow Cardinal, Black-necked Swan, Toco Toucan
  • National park:
    • Ibera National Park, Marsh, lagoon and grassland. Watch it from boats and horseback out of Carlos Pellegrini.
  • Stops:
    • : The village on Laguna Iberá, and the base every visit works from.
    • : The road town on the way in, with grassland either side holding Strange-tailed Tyrant.
    • : The northern access to the reserve, off the main road.
  • Also covers: Colonia Carlos Pellegrini, Ibera National Park, Ituzaingo, Mercedes, San Nicolas
  • Also watch for: Capybara everywhere, marsh deer, and reintroduced jaguar and giant otter.

Buenos Aires and the Pampas

Grassland, coastal lagoon and a reserve inside the capital

The pampas grassland has been almost entirely ploughed, and what survives holds some of the most threatened birds in the country. The city itself has a reserve on the river that makes a first morning worth having.

  • Habitat: Rolling grassland and marshy flatland cover much of central Argentina. The east is wet and still relatively wild, and the west is dry and largely given over to agriculture.
  • When: Punta Rasa is good for migrants in the austral spring through October and November and again in autumn through March and April, and seabirds pass offshore when strong onshore winds blow.
  • Getting there: The Costanera Sur reserve lies on the riverside southeast of the city centre, about half an hour on foot from it, and the entrances are off the avenue along its western edge. San Clemente lies about 330 km southeast of Buenos Aires, and the birding concentrates on the Punta Rasa peninsula just north of the town. Two km inside the reserve the left fork runs 3 km to the biological station, observatory and lighthouse, and the right fork runs 4 km to the point. The Campos del Tuyu entrance is on the north side of the road about 12 km west of San Clemente, with the reserve itself a further 9 km on, and a visit is arranged in advance. The canal bridge west of General Lavalle lies some 28 km west of San Clemente.
  • Look for: Pampas Meadowlark, Hudsonian Godwit, Olrog's Gull, Black-necked Swan, Yellow Cardinal

    • Otamendi Natural Reserve, Delta marsh and talar woodland north of the city. A train reaches it.

Cordoba and the Central Sierras

High granite grassland and a salt lake with a million flamingos

The Sierras de Cordoba rise out of the pampas to 2,800 m in the central belt of the country and carry their own endemic. North of them Mar Chiquita is the largest salt lake in South America and holds flamingos and Nearctic shorebirds in six figures.

  • Habitat: West of Cordoba the road to San Luis crosses a grey granite plateau known as the Pampa de Achala, in the Sierra de los Comechingones, a range of rugged hills isolated from the Andes. Laguna Mar Chiquita is a huge salt lake some 200 km northeast of Cordoba and it holds thousands of shorebirds along with masses of waterfowl. Chaco scrub runs alongside the Rio San Antonio at Icho Cruz and north out of La Para.
  • When: Good all year. Andean Condor, Olive-crowned Crescentchest and Andean Flamingo are on site in all twelve months. Cordoba Cinclodes is away from May to August.
  • Getting there: Olrog's Cinclodes occurs along the creek beside the Cordoba to Villa Dolores road opposite the Parador El Condor cafe, 84 km southwest of Cordoba, and the old dirt road north from there runs past a radio station with a rocky area of tussock grass 1 km along. At Icho Cruz, walk north through the town and past the campsite to reach 3 km of chaco alongside the Rio San Antonio. Miramar is the best base for Mar Chiquita: turn west 8 km south of the town beside the school and head for the river mouth.
  • Look for: Cordoba Cinclodes, Andean Condor, Olive-crowned Crescentchest, Hudsonian Godwit, Andean Flamingo
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Andean Swift, Andean Tinamou, Black-and-chestnut Warbling Finch, Brown-capped Tit-Spinetail, Cordilleran Canastero
  • National parks:
    • Quebrada del Condorito National Park, High granite grassland at 2,000 m, with a gorge the condors nest in and roost along.
    • Ansenuza National Park

Mendoza and the Monte Desert

Creosote scrub under the highest mountain in the Americas

The Monte is Argentina's warm desert. It runs along the Andean foot and carries a set of scrub birds shared with nowhere else. Above it the cordillera reaches 6,960 m at Aconcagua, and condors work the ridges.

  • Habitat: Mendoza sits at the foot of the Andes, 1,060 km west of Buenos Aires. Potrerillos is a resort 58 km north of the city on the road to Uspallata and Puente del Inca, with an escarpment above the side road to Chacritas. Laguna Horcones lies inside Aconcagua National Park, 166 km west of Mendoza and below Cerro Aconcagua, the highest mountain in South America at 6,960 metres.
  • When: Good all year. Elegant Crested Tinamou, Andean Condor and Rufous-tailed Plantcutter are here in every month.
  • Getting there: For Potrerillos, drive out from Mendoza and turn left towards Chacritas just after the river, then work the escarpment on the right about 2.5 km along that road from the Mendoza to Uspallata road. Laguna Horcones sits 6 km northwest of Puente del Inca at 2,720 metres. Take the track north off the main road a few km west of Puente del Inca, past the bridge over the Rio Horcones, and walk the flat ground west of the track about 1 km north of the lake.
  • Look for: Sandy Gallito, Salinas Monjita, Elegant Crested Tinamou, Andean Condor, Rufous-tailed Plantcutter
  • National park:
    • Aconcagua Provincial Park, The highest peak in the Americas. Watch for condors from the road at Puente del Inca.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Argentina for birding?

November is the fairest single month for a whole-country trip, because it is the only part of the year when both ends work. The far south runs October to March and the Chaco and northwest are at their best in the dry winter from May to September, and those two do not overlap. The three seasons.

How many bird species does Argentina have?

Just over a thousand, spread across more habitats than any other country in the southern cone: Atlantic forest, Chaco, yungas, puna, pampas, Monte desert, Patagonian steppe and southern beech forest. The number above counts the species with a card in the BeakBrain guide rather than a figure typed in by hand.

Where do you go birding in Argentina?

The northwest gives the longest day lists, the Chaco has the birds nobody else has, Tierra del Fuego has the southern specialities, and Iguazu has the Atlantic forest. The ten regions.

Do you have to fly between regions?

For anything past a week, yes. The country is 3,500 km long and the domestic network is good and cheap, so most published routes fly between blocks and drive within them. Five routes.

Can you combine Argentina with Antarctica?

Almost every Antarctic voyage sails from Ushuaia, and a few days in Tierra del Fuego either side is the normal shape of it. The lenga forest, the Beagle Channel and the peat bogs are all within a short drive of the port.

What is the Ibera rewilding project?

Corrientes gave up 700,000 hectares of marsh to a national park, and jaguar, giant otter, Red-and-green Macaw and Pampas deer have all been returned to it. It is the largest restoration of its kind on the continent and a visitor can watch it working.

What other wildlife is there?

Southern right whales at Valdes from June, orca that beach themselves at Punta Norte, elephant seals, guanaco and vicuña, and reintroduced jaguar and giant otter at Ibera.

Is the Chaco worth the effort?

If the list matters, yes. It is hot, flat and thorny with long drives on sand, and it holds several birds that occur nowhere outside the dry Chaco. Most operators sell it bolted to the northwest, which is the sensible way to do it.

How long do you need?

Six days does Tierra del Fuego. Nine covers the northwest properly. Eleven takes Patagonia and the Valdes coast. Nineteen runs the length of the country. Five routes.

Do you need a guide?

The pampas, Patagonia and Iguazu can be self-driven with a good book. The Chaco and the high puna reward someone who knows the tracks and the calls, and the Hooded Grebe project is reached through the people who run it.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
