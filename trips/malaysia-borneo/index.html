At a glance

Best months
March to August, with April the driest at Danum and March the clearest on Kinabalu
Regions
Mount KinabaluDanum ValleyThe Kinabatangan riverTabin and the eastern lowlandsSarawak: the Kelabit Highlands and Bako
Getting in
Kota Kinabalu (BKI) for Kinabalu and the Sabah circuit, Sandakan (SDK) or Lahad Datu (LDU) for the east, and Kuching (KCH) or Miri for Sarawak
Getting around
A car and driver in Sabah, boats on the Kinabatangan, and a small plane to Bario because there is no road

When to Go

Borneo is equatorial and there is no dry season, only a wetter northeast monsoon from November to February that Sarawak feels far more than Sabah does. Sabah's eastern lowlands, which is where most of a Bornean itinerary sits, are workable in every month, and what actually decides the timing is fruiting: the mast years when the dipterocarps fruit together bring everything down to feed and cannot be predicted more than a few months ahead.

Peak

March to August

The drier half of the Bornean year, and when every operator runs.

Go for Kota Kinabalu records 72 mm in March and Danum 142 mm in April, the mountain trails are least cloudy, and the Kinabatangan sits at a level that keeps the wildlife on the banks.

Trade off It is also when the lodges are full, and Borneo Rainforest Lodge in particular books a year ahead.

Typical daily average
  • Kinabalu Park: 17 to 25 °C / 63 to 77 °F
  • Danum Valley: 22 to 31 °C / 72 to 88 °F
  • Sukau: 23 to 32 °C / 73 to 90 °F

Shoulder

September and October

The build-up, with the lowlands still working.

Go for Sabah's interior is fine and the lodges are quieter and cheaper than in the peak. The rising river changes what the boats find rather than stopping them.

Trade off Kota Kinabalu takes 329 mm in October and the mountain is often in cloud all day.

Typical daily average
  • Kinabalu Park: 17 to 24 °C / 63 to 75 °F
  • Danum Valley: 22 to 31 °C / 72 to 88 °F
  • Kuching: 23 to 32 °C / 73 to 90 °F

Monsoon

November to February

The northeast monsoon, which Sarawak feels much harder than Sabah.

Go for Sabah's eastern lowlands work through it, and a flooded Kinabatangan pushes the mammals onto the remaining dry banks where they are easier to find.

Trade off Kuching takes 707 mm in January, the Sarawak legs are effectively off, and Sukau's river can run too high to land at the usual places.

Typical daily average
  • Danum Valley: 22 to 30 °C / 72 to 86 °F
  • Sukau: 23 to 31 °C / 73 to 88 °F
  • Kuching: 23 to 31 °C / 73 to 88 °F

Mount Kinabalu

A granite massif rising to 4,095 m, with a montane endemic list found on this mountain and nowhere else

Kinabalu's park headquarters sit at 1,560 m and the birding is done on the road and the trails around them. This is where the Bornean montane endemics live: Whitehead's Trogon, Whitehead's Broadbill and Whitehead's Spiderhunter, all named for the same nineteenth-century collector, plus Mountain Serpent Eagle and Bornean Whistling Thrush. The Poring hot springs at the foot add a lowland list on the same ticket.

  • Habitat: Forest covers the highest mountain between the Himalayas and New Guinea at 4,101 metres, and it holds most of Borneo's montane endemics. Island Thrush, Flavescent Bulbul and Friendly Bush-Warbler generally start above 2,150 metres.
  • When: Good all year. Whitehead's Trogon, Whitehead's Broadbill and Whitehead's Spiderhunter are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Carson's Camp at 2,650 metres is a there-and-back day walk from the power station with time to bird on the way. Walking to the bare granite summit needs a permit and a guide, and both should be arranged well in advance. Any Bornean itinerary should allow several days here.
  • Look for: Whitehead's Trogon, Whitehead's Broadbill, Whitehead's Spiderhunter, Mountain Serpent Eagle, Bornean Whistling Thrush, Crimson-headed Partridge, Bornean Green Magpie, Golden-naped Barbet
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Kinabalu Park (national park), Park headquarters at 1,560 m. The Silau Silau and Liwagu trails start here, and the summit road climbs above.
  • Stops:
    • : The hot springs at the mountain's foot at 500 m. There is a canopy walkway, and the lowland list comes with it.
  • Also covers: Kinabalu Park, Kundasang, Poring
  • Also watch for: Kinabalu is a botanical site of the first order, with Rafflesia and dozens of pitcher plant species, several of which grow on this mountain alone. Mountain Treeshrew works the rubbish bins at headquarters and Bornean Ferret-badger comes out after dark.

Danum Valley

A conservation area of primary lowland dipterocarp forest with no settlement inside it

Danum is 438 square kilometres of primary forest that has never been logged, two hours by road from Lahad Datu, and it is the best lowland birding in Borneo. Bornean Bristlehead moves through in noisy parties, Blue-headed Pitta and Bornean Banded Pitta hold the floor, and the canopy walkway at Borneo Rainforest Lodge puts you level with the fig flocks.

  • Habitat: Luxuriant lowland dipterocarp forest, which is what sustains most of Borneo's birdlife and where most of the island's remaining lowland forest survives.
  • When: Good all year. Bornean Bristlehead, Blue-headed Pitta and Bornean Banded Pitta are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Danum lies about 90 km west of Lahad Datu by rough road, with the lodge at the end of it and the Field Centre some 35 km short of the lodge. Permits, transport and accommodation should all be arranged well in advance. Around the lodge, walk the short Danum Trail behind the buildings, the Segama Trail across the suspension bridge and the canopy walkway. From the Field Centre the Rhino Ridge Trail runs 6.4 km, the Elephant Ridge Trail holds two dancing grounds of Great Argus, and the Perwina Trail leads on from it.
  • Look for: Bornean Bristlehead, Blue-headed Pitta, Bornean Banded Pitta, Bornean Crested Fireback, Great Argus, Helmeted Hornbill, Bornean Ground Cuckoo, White-crowned Shama
  • Parks and reserves: Danum Valley Conservation Area
  • Stops:
    • : The conservation area and its field centre, two hours by road from Lahad Datu. There is no public access.
    • : The town with the airstrip. It is the transfer point for both Danum and Tabin.
  • Also covers: Danum Valley Conservation Area, Lahad Datu
  • Also watch for: Bornean Orangutan is wild here rather than at a feeding station, which is a different experience entirely. Bornean Gibbon calls at dawn, Red Leaf Monkey and Bearded Pig are constant, and the night drives produce Sunda Clouded Leopard often enough to be worth doing.

The Kinabatangan river

A floodplain river with forest along its banks, watched from a boat at dawn and dusk

The Kinabatangan is a corridor of forest along a muddy river with oil palm behind it, and that concentration is exactly why the wildlife watching is so good. Boats out of Sukau at first light find all eight Bornean hornbill species over the season, Storm's Stork on the snags and Oriental Darter on the oxbows. It is the easiest big-wildlife river in Asia and its context is uncomfortable.

  • Habitat: Ox-bow lakes and secondary forest line the Kinabatangan. Near Sandakan Bay the river runs out through mangrove-lined creeks and mud flats.
  • When: Good all year. Storm's Stork, Rhinoceros Hornbill and Wrinkled Hornbill are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: The bridge over the Kinabatangan is an hour by road from Sandakan towards Lahad Datu, and the jungle camp lies an hour downstream from the bridge. The tributaries above Sukau are worked by boat.
  • Look for: Storm's Stork, Rhinoceros Hornbill, Wrinkled Hornbill, Wreathed Hornbill, Oriental Darter, Western Hooded Pitta, Black-and-red Broadbill, Buffy Fish Owl
  • Parks and reserves: Lower Kinabatangan Wildlife Sanctuary
  • Stops:
    • : The village on the lower river, with the lodges. Every boat leaves from here.
    • : The coastal city with the airport, two and a half hours from the river. The Sepilok orangutan centre is next to it.
  • Also covers: Lower Kinabatangan Wildlife Sanctuary, Sandakan, Sukau
  • Also watch for: Proboscis Monkey lines the river at dusk in numbers found nowhere else, and Bornean Pygmy Elephant swims the channels in herds. Bornean Orangutan is regular from the boat, and the Gomantong caves nearby hold two million bats that leave in a column at sunset.

Tabin and the eastern lowlands

A wildlife reserve of regenerating and primary lowland forest, quieter than Danum and easier to reach

Tabin is a 1,200 square kilometre wildlife reserve east of Lahad Datu, most of it regenerating forest with a primary core, and it holds the same list as Danum with more of the mammals showing. The road through it is birded from a vehicle and the mud volcano at Lipad brings everything in to the mineral licks.

  • When: Birding is good at any time outside the rainy season of November to January, when flooding can limit access to some sites.
  • Getting there: Tabin is four hours by shuttle bus from Lahad Datu, and the bus runs when accommodation in the reserve has been prearranged.
  • Look for: Bornean Bristlehead, Bornean Ground Cuckoo, Black-crowned Pitta, Bushy-crested Hornbill, White-fronted Falconet, Rufous-collared Kingfisher, Scarlet-rumped Trogon, Blue-eared Kingfisher
  • Parks and reserves: Tabin Wildlife Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The reserve east of Lahad Datu. A lodge sits inside it, and a road runs to the mud volcano.
  • Also covers: Tabin Wildlife Reserve
  • Also watch for: The Lipad mud volcano is a mineral lick that draws Bearded Pig, Sambar and Bornean Pygmy Elephant, and the night drives here are the best in Sabah for civets and Leopard Cat. Sumatran Rhinoceros was last seen in Malaysia in this reserve.

Sarawak: the Kelabit Highlands and Bako

An interior plateau reached only by air, and a coastal national park of heath forest

Sarawak is the half of Malaysian Borneo almost no birding itinerary reaches. Bario in the Kelabit Highlands sits at 1,000 m on a plateau with no road to it, and it holds montane birds that in Sabah need Kinabalu. Bako near Kuching is the opposite: an hour by boat from the city, heath forest and cliff, with proboscis monkeys on the beach.

  • Habitat: Bako is a small rugged headland of 27 sq km, 37 km northeast of Kuching, and it carries a variety of habitats where shore and lowland forest birds occur side by side. Gunung Mulu to the northeast is a land of limestone rising to 2,376 m, with pinnacles standing high above the forest and the most extensive cave system on earth beneath it.
  • When: Birding is good at any time outside the rainy season of November to January, when flooding can limit access to some sites.
  • Getting there: The Bako headquarters is at Telok Assam, 30 minutes by boat from Kampung Bako, which is 45 minutes by road from Kuching. Permits and accommodation are booked in advance in Kuching. An elevated boardwalk runs just north of Telok Assam, Telok Delima is an hour's walk south, and the 5 km Lintang Loop is the best of the 30 km of marked trails because it crosses most of the habitats.
  • Look for: Bornean Barbet, Dulit Frogmouth, Hose's Broadbill, Blue-banded Pitta, Black Oriole, Bornean Bulbul, Bornean Whistler, Mountain Barbet
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Black-backed Dwarf Kingfisher, Blue-and-white Flycatcher
  • Parks and reserves: Bako National Park, Pulong Tau National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The settlement on the Kelabit plateau at 1,000 m. Only a small plane from Miri reaches it.
    • : The coastal park an hour by boat from Kuching. It has heath forest, sandstone cliffs and beach.
    • : The state capital and airport. It is the base for Bako and the western sites.
  • Also covers: Bako National Park, Bario, Kuching, Pulong Tau National Park
  • Also watch for: Bako's proboscis monkeys come down to the beach in the late afternoon and Bornean Bearded Pig walks through the park headquarters. Mulu, further north, holds the largest cave chamber in the world and a bat exodus of several million every evening.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to bird Malaysian Borneo?

March to August. Kota Kinabalu records 72 mm in March and Danum 142 mm in April, the mountain is least cloudy in March, and the Kinabatangan sits at a level that keeps the wildlife on the banks. Sabah's eastern lowlands work in every month, which is more than can be said for Sarawak. The three seasons.

How wet is Sarawak?

Kuching takes 4,164 mm a year and 707 mm in January, which makes it one of the wettest inhabited places in Asia and is why almost no birding itinerary goes there. Sabah at 2,194 mm is a different proposition and is where the whole industry sits. When to go, season by season.

Danum or Tabin?

Danum for the birds and Tabin for the mammals, roughly. Danum is primary forest that has never been logged and has the better list, the bristlehead and the pittas among it. Tabin is regenerating forest with a mud volcano that draws elephants and pigs, and its night drives are the best in Sabah. Many itineraries take both. Danum Valley.

Is the Kinabatangan as good as it looks?

Yes, and the reason is uncomfortable. It is a corridor of forest along a river with oil palm behind it, and the wildlife is concentrated into that corridor because there is nowhere else to be. That is what makes the boat trips extraordinary and it is also the conservation problem. The Kinabatangan river.

What are the Whitehead's birds?

Whitehead's Trogon, Whitehead's Broadbill and Whitehead's Spiderhunter, all named for John Whitehead, who collected on Kinabalu in the 1880s. All three are Bornean montane endemics, all three are on the mountain, and getting the set in one trip is the traditional measure of a good Kinabalu visit. Mount Kinabalu.

How does this compare with the peninsula?

Borneo is harder, dearer and much better. Some fifty birds live here and not on the peninsula, the primary forest at Danum is a different order of experience, and the mammals are unmatched in Asia. The peninsula is easier and closer to an airport. Most people who bird one end up doing both. Peninsular Malaysia.

Do the lodges carry certifications?

Some do, and each card says which. Bornean wildlife accommodation is the best on this whole surface and several properties have gone through a real scheme rather than making a claim, which is unusual in the region. Every chip renders from the scheme's own register. Certification.

How many bird species does Malaysia have?

Around 800 recorded across the whole country, and Borneo holds roughly fifty that live nowhere else. The card wall carries every Malaysian species with a BeakBrain card, which is coded at country level, so the same wall appears on both Malaysian guides. The birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
