At a glance

Best months
May for the country as a whole, April for the Strait of Messina raptor crossing, June and July for the Alps and the Dolomites, and November to February for the largest wildfowl counts on the Adriatic wetlands
Getting around
A hire car for the wetlands, the parks and the islands. The rail network is good between cities and reaches none of the birding
The one thing to know
The hunting season is a real planning factor from autumn through winter across much of the country, and the national and regional parks are where to be in those months

When to Go

The Italian year is really two years. The lowlands and the islands run early and hard through spring, and the Alps and the Apennines only open once the passes clear, which pushes the mountain list into summer.

spring

March to May

The season most itineraries run. Raptors cross the Strait of Messina, the lagoons fill with passage waders and everything on the peninsula is singing.

Go for The Messina passage, the Po Delta herons, and the Sardinian scrub warblers.

Trade off The Alpine passes stay shut into late spring, so a north to south route needs May at the earliest.

Typical daily average Milano 8 to 18 °C / 46 to 64 °F, Roma 9 to 19 °C / 48 to 66 °F, Cagliari 10 to 19 °C / 50 to 66 °F

summer

June to August

The mountain season. Gran Paradiso and the Dolomites are open and the Alpine breeders are at their most visible, while the plains run hot.

Go for Lammergeier, Ptarmigan and Snowfinch in the Alps, Eleonora's Falcon colonies on the islands.

Trade off The Po plain and the south are heavy going in July and August, and the coast is at its busiest.

Typical daily average Milano 17 to 28 °C / 63 to 82 °F, Roma 18 to 29 °C / 64 to 84 °F, Cagliari 18 to 30 °C / 64 to 86 °F

autumn

September to October

Autumn passage through the lagoons and the islands, with the Eleonora's Falcon colonies still active into October.

Go for Wader and passerine passage, the last of the falcon colonies, wildfowl arriving.

Trade off The Italian hunting season opens in autumn and it is a genuine factor in where a visitor can walk.

Typical daily average Milano 12 to 20 °C / 54 to 68 °F, Roma 14 to 24 °C / 57 to 75 °F, Cagliari 16 to 26 °C / 61 to 79 °F

winter

November to February

The wetland season. The Po Delta, Orbetello and the Circeo lakes hold their largest wildfowl counts, and Sardinia keeps its flamingos.

Go for Wintering wildfowl, Greater Flamingo, and the resident southern raptors.

Trade off The Alps and the Apennine high roads are closed or snowbound, so the mountain list is out of reach.

Typical daily average Milano 1 to 8 °C / 34 to 46 °F, Roma 5 to 14 °C / 41 to 57 °F, Cagliari 7 to 16 °C / 45 to 61 °F

Po Delta and Comacchio

The largest wetland complex in the country, a working landscape of salt pans, fish lagoons and flooded wood at the river mouth

The Po Delta is the largest wetland complex in Italy and the reason the country holds Pygmy Cormorant and Glossy Ibis in numbers. Abruzzo and the Apennines

Apennine beech and open limestone within two hours of Rome, the one range where the mountain birds come with bears and wolves

The Apennine high country, and the one Italian region where the mountain birds come with wolves and Marsican bears. Rock Partridge on the open slopes at dawn, Alpine Chough over Camosciara, Wallcreeper on the Maiella rock, and White-backed Woodpecker in the old beech.

  • Look for: Rock Partridge, Alpine Chough, Wallcreeper, White-backed Woodpecker, Golden Eagle, Eurasian Dotterel, Common Rock Thrush
  • Stops:
    • , the oldest national park in the Apennines, beech forest and limestone, holding Marsican Brown Bear, wolf and White-backed Woodpecker
    • , a cirque of cliffs reached by a closed road walked from the car park, and the reliable place for Alpine Chough and chamois
    • , a beech valley off the main road with a stream through it, worked at first light for White-backed Woodpecker and Red-backed Shrike
    • , the park's main village, with its visitor centre and the trailheads for the surrounding valleys
    • , a bare limestone massif east of Abruzzo, rising above 2,700 m, with Alpine Accentor, Snowfinch and Rock Partridge on the tops
    • , high pasture and beech between Abruzzo and L'Aquila, and the surest Apennine ground for Golden Eagle and Rock Thrush
  • Also covers: Abbateggio, Caramanico Terme, Cassino, Civitella Alfedena, Fara San Martino, L'Aquila, PESCARA, Pescasseroli, San Martino sulla Marrucina, Torino di Sangro
  • Also watch for: This is the range that still holds Marsican Brown Bear, fewer than a hundred animals and found nowhere else, along with Apennine Wolf and the Abruzzo Chamois recovered from near extinction. Red Deer and Roe Deer are common in the beech.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Italy?

May, if you want one answer. Passage is still running, the breeding season is at full stretch and the Alpine passes are opening. April is better for the Strait of Messina raptor crossing, and the winter months hold the largest wildfowl counts on the Adriatic wetlands.

Where do I go for the Alpine specialities?

Gran Paradiso for Lammergeier, which is the centre of the Alpine reintroduction, and the Dolomites for Black Grouse, Hazel Grouse and Rock Ptarmigan without the glacier work. Valsavarenche and Cogne are the two working bases in Gran Paradiso.

Is Sardinia worth a separate trip?

Yes. Marmora's Warbler, Moltoni's Warbler and the Eleonora's Falcon colonies on San Pietro make it a different list from the mainland, and Molentargius holds one of the largest flamingo colonies in the Mediterranean inside Cagliari's city limits.

Does hunting affect birding in Italy?

It does, more than in most of Europe, and it is worth planning around. The season opens in autumn and runs through winter across much of the country. National parks and regional reserves are the places to be in those months.

What about the Strait of Messina?

It is one of the great raptor bottlenecks of the western Palearctic, with Honey Buzzard the headline in spring. No operator checked currently sells it as a package stop, so it is a place to organise independently from the Sicilian side.

What certification should I look for in Italian accommodation?

Green Key has almost no presence in Italy, so it is the wrong thing to look for. The Carta Europea del Turismo Sostenibile inside protected areas is the scheme that carries the country, and the EU Ecolabel is the one that certifies individual hotels.

Do I need a car in Italy?

For the wetlands, the mountains and the islands, yes. The rail network is good between cities, and the valli, the park valleys and the lagoon tracks all sit well beyond it.

Who are the birding institutions in Italy?

LIPU is the BirdLife partner and the largest conservation membership body. ISPRA is the government research institute and runs the national ringing scheme, and Federparchi coordinates the parks and the CETS network.
