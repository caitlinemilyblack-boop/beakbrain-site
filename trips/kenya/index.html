Getting around
Nairobi is the hub and most routes drive. The Mara, Samburu and the coast all have airstrips, and a short internal flight saves a long day on the road.

Birds that decide the trip

Lesser Flamingos on the soda lakes

Three quarters of the world population lives on the East African soda lakes, and when the algae bloom is right Bogoria or Nakuru carries them in six figures. Which lake holds them moves with the water chemistry, so ask before you drive.

When All year somewhere on the chain, with the largest gatherings usually from July to October.

Where Nakuru, Marigat

Great Blue Turaco at Kakamega

A turaco the size of a small goose, blue with a yellow and red bill. It crashes about the canopy in noisy parties. Kakamega is the only Kenyan forest that has it, because it is the only Kenyan piece of the Guinea-Congo belt.

When All year, and the parties are loud enough to find by ear.

Where Kakamega

Vulturine Guineafowl in Samburu

The largest guineafowl, cobalt-breasted with a bare vulture-like head. It walks the dry acacia in parties of dozens. It is a northern dryland bird and it stops where the dry country stops.

When All year, and the parties come to water in the heat of the day.

Where Archers Post, Isiolo

Taita Thrush in the last fragments

A thrush confined to a few hundred hectares of cloud forest in three fragments on the Taita Hills, with a world population estimated in the low thousands. Ngangao is the fragment most people find it in.

When All year, on the forest floor, and the walk in is steep.

Where Wundanyi, Mwatate

Sokoke Scops Owl at Arabuko

A tiny owl found in the Cynometra woodland of Arabuko-Sokoke and one forest in Tanzania, and nowhere else. It is searched for at dusk on the forest tracks with a local guide who knows the territories.

When All year, at dusk, and the dry months from January to March are easiest underfoot.

Where Gede, Watamu

Secretarybirds on the plains

A terrestrial raptor that walks grassland on stilt legs and kills snakes by stamping on them. The Mara and Amboseli hold them in the open where a vehicle can follow at a distance.

When All year, and the short-grass months from July to October make them easiest to see.

Where Narok, Amboseli

Sharpe's Longclaw on the Kinangop

A grassland pipit relative confined to high tussock grassland in central Kenya, and almost all of that grassland has gone to smallholdings. The Kinangop plateau holds what is left, in fields that are being ploughed.

When All year on the remaining tussock, and a local guide is effectively required to find the right field.

Where Nyahururu, Nyeri

Grey Crowned Cranes in the Rift

The national bird of Uganda and a familiar sight across the Kenyan Rift, and it has fallen far enough to be listed as endangered. Pairs display on wet grassland and roost communally in the shallows.

When All year, with display and breeding through the rains from March to June.

Where Naivasha, Narok

When to Go

Kenya sits on the equator and runs two rainy seasons: the long rains from March to May and the short rains from October to December. The dry months between them concentrate the game, and the wet months bring the birds. October and November hold the largest Palearctic arrival of the year.

Dry and busy

July to October

The wildebeest are in the Mara, the country is dry, and birds and game concentrate on the remaining water.

Go for The migration river crossings, the northern drylands at their most workable, and the first Palearctic waders.

Trade off The busiest and priciest months, and the Mara camps book many months ahead.

Typical daily average
  • Nairobi: 11 to 24 °C / 52 to 75 °F
  • Samburu: 21 to 34 °C / 70 to 93 °F
  • The coast: 22 to 30 °C / 72 to 86 °F

The green months

November to February

Short rains then the hot dry spell, and the largest Palearctic arrival of the year sitting on top of the residents.

Go for The longest bird lists of the year, resident breeding, and green country with few vehicles in it.

Trade off Afternoon storms in November, and the migration has left the Mara.

Typical daily average
  • Nairobi: 13 to 26 °C / 55 to 79 °F
  • Samburu: 22 to 35 °C / 72 to 95 °F
  • The coast: 23 to 32 °C / 73 to 90 °F

The long rains

March to June

The wettest stretch, the country at its greenest, and the lowest rates of the year.

Go for Everything breeding, the forests in full song, and empty parks.

Trade off Black-cotton tracks in the Mara turn impassable and some camps close through April.

Typical daily average
  • Nairobi: 13 to 25 °C / 55 to 77 °F
  • Samburu: 21 to 33 °C / 70 to 91 °F
  • The coast: 23 to 31 °C / 73 to 88 °F

The Maasai Mara and the Southern Plains

Open savanna, and the migration that crosses it

The Mara is the northern end of the Serengeti ecosystem, and from July to October the wildebeest cross into it. The birding is raptors and open-country species on grassland you can see across, and almost every Kenyan trip includes it.

  • Habitat: Open red-oat grassland on volcanic soil, with riverine forest along the Mara and Talek and scattered balanites and acacia on the rises.
  • When: July to October for the crossings and the raptors that follow them. November to March adds the Palearctic migrants, and the grass is short enough to see through after the short rains.
  • Getting there: Five to six hours from Nairobi through Narok, which is the last fuel. Airstrips at Keekorok, Ol Kiombo and Musiara take the scheduled flights from Wilson.
  • Look for: Secretarybird, Kori Bustard, Martial Eagle, Grey Crowned Crane, Southern Ground Hornbill
  • National parks:
    • Maasai Mara National Reserve, 1,500 square kilometres of grassland and riverine forest, and the Kenyan end of the Serengeti system.

    • Naboisho Conservancy
    • Mara North Conservancy

The Rift Valley Lakes

Soda lakes, flamingos and freshwater in the same afternoon

The Rift floor carries a chain of lakes within two hours of each other. They alternate soda and freshwater, and each holds a different set. Nakuru and Bogoria take flamingos in six figures when the algae bloom is right.

  • Habitat: Soda and freshwater lakes on the rift floor, ringed by acacia woodland and fever tree forest, with cliffs and lava at Baringo and Bogoria.
  • When: Year round. Flamingo numbers move between Nakuru, Bogoria and Elementaita with water level rather than with season, so ask before choosing one.
  • Getting there: The main road north from Nairobi reaches Naivasha in under two hours and Nakuru in three. Bogoria and Baringo are a further two hours through Marigat.
  • Look for: Lesser Flamingo, Greater Flamingo, Great White Pelican, Grey Crowned Crane, Rufous-bellied Heron
  • Also recorded in the parks here: African Grey Woodpecker, Anteater Chat, Black-lored Babbler, Coqui Francolin, Arrow-marked Babbler
  • National parks:
    • Lake Nakuru National Park, A fenced soda lake with acacia and yellow-bark woodland. It has rhino as well as the flamingos.
    • Hell's Gate National Park, A gorge and cliffs by Naivasha, walked or cycled, with Verreaux's Eagle on the walls.
  • Stops:
    • : The freshwater lake. Watch it from a boat for fish eagles, herons and the papyrus edge.
    • : A hot-spring soda lake north of Nakuru. The flamingos come here when Nakuru is too full or too fresh.
    • : Freshwater with cliffs behind it, and the best of the Rift dry-country birding.
  • Also covers: Elmenteita, Hell's Gate National Park, Lake Baringo, Lake Bogoria, Lake Naivasha, Lake Nakuru National Park, Marigat
  • Also watch for: Rothschild giraffe and white rhino at Nakuru, and hippo throughout the freshwater lakes.

Kakamega and the Western Forests

The last Kenyan piece of the Guinea-Congo rainforest

Kakamega is the eastern edge of the great equatorial forest belt and the last western rainforest Kenya has, isolated from the rest of it for thousands of years. It holds around forty bird species that occur nowhere else in the country, which is why trips make the drive west.

  • Habitat: The only remnant of Guineo-Congolian rainforest in Kenya, 240 square kilometres of it. It holds over 120 tree species, with glades and regrowth between the blocks.
  • When: Any month. The forest birds are resident and vocal, and the drier stretches from December to February make the trails easier walking.
  • Getting there: Seven hours by road from Nairobi, or fly to Kisumu and drive an hour and a half north. The forest is entered at Isecheno in the south or Buyangu in the north.
  • Look for: Great Blue Turaco, Black-billed Turaco, Blue-headed Bee-eater, Turner's Eremomela, Chapin's Flycatcher, Grey-crested Helmetshrike
  • Also recorded in the parks here: African Blue Flycatcher, African Spotted Creeper, Black-and-white-casqued Hornbill, Black-billed Weaver
  • National parks:
    • Kakamega Forest National Reserve
    • Mount Elgon National Park, Moorland and montane forest on the Ugandan border, with elephants that mine the salt caves.
  • Stops:
    • : The last Kenyan rainforest. Walk in from Isecheno or the Rondo side.
    • Saiwa Swamp National Park : The smallest park in Kenya, a papyrus swamp with walkways and sitatunga.
    • : The Lake Victoria city, with papyrus and the Dunga swamp on its edge.
    • : The cane-country town north of the forest, on the road to Mount Elgon.
  • Also covers: Kakamega Forest National Reserve, Kisumu, Mount Elgon National Park, Mumias, Saiwa Swamp National Park, Webuye
  • Also watch for: Blue and red-tailed monkeys in the canopy, and the potto on night walks.

Samburu and the Northern Drylands

Dry acacia country, and a set of birds and mammals found only north of the equator

North of Mount Kenya the land drops into hot dry acacia along the Ewaso Ng'iro, and it carries northern species that reach nowhere else in Kenya. The mammals do the same, which is why the northern reserves feel like a different country.

  • Habitat: Acacia-Commiphora thornbush and bare rock on red soil, with doum palm and riverine forest along the Ewaso Ng'iro.
  • When: June to October and January to February. The river holds the birds together in the dry months, and the roads north are hard going in the long rains.
  • Getting there: Five hours north of Nairobi through Nanyuki and Isiolo. Airstrips at Samburu and Buffalo Springs take the Wilson flights.
  • Look for: Vulturine Guineafowl, Somali Ostrich, Golden-breasted Starling, Somali Bee-eater, Kori Bustard
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Boran Cisticola, Bristle-crowned Starling, Brown-tailed Rock Chat, Donaldson Smith's Sparrow-Weaver, Fan-tailed Raven
  • National parks:
    • Samburu National Reserve, Doum palm and acacia along the Ewaso Ng'iro, and the core of the northern dry-country list.
    • Marsabit National Park, A forested volcano rising out of the desert, five hours further north and rarely birded.
  • Stops:
    • Buffalo Springs National Reserve : Across the river from Samburu, with springs and open plain.
    • Shaba National Reserve : The third of the trio, drier and more volcanic, and the quietest.
    • : The road town at the reserve gates, on the highway north.
  • Also covers: Archers Post, Buffalo Springs National Reserve, Isiolo, Maralal, Marsabit National Park, Samburu National Reserve, Shaba National Reserve
  • Also watch for: Grevy zebra, reticulated giraffe, beisa oryx and gerenuk, the northern five that are seen nowhere further south.

Mount Kenya, the Aberdares and Laikipia

Montane forest, moorland and the highland endemics

Two mountain blocks stand either side of the Rift with forest, bamboo and giant heath on them, and they carry the Kenyan highland endemics. Laikipia below is a plateau of conservancies where much of the country's rhino now lives.

  • Habitat: Montane forest to bamboo to giant heath and afro-alpine moorland with the height, and open ranchland with scattered acacia on the Laikipia plateau.
  • When: January to March and June to October for clear mountain weather. The Laikipia list is steady year round.
  • Getting there: Three hours from Nairobi to Nanyuki. The Aberdares are entered from Nyeri or Naro Moru, and the Laikipia conservancies have their own airstrips.
  • Look for: Jackson's Spurfowl, Aberdare Cisticola, Hunter's Cisticola, Sharpe's Longclaw, Abbott's Starling, Hartlaub's Turaco

    • Aberdare National Park, Moorland at 3,500 m with giant lobelia, and forest lodges with waterholes below it.
    • Mount Kenya National Park, Forest, bamboo and moorland on the second-highest mountain in Africa.
    • Ol Pejeta Conservancy, A Laikipia conservancy with rhino, a chimpanzee sanctuary and good open-country birding.

    • : The high farmland plateau west of the Aberdares, and the only ground Sharpe's Longclaw has left.

Amboseli, Tsavo and the Southern Parks

Dust, elephants and Kilimanjaro behind them

Amboseli is a small park under Kilimanjaro with swamps fed by the mountain, which concentrates everything. Tsavo next door is one of the largest protected areas in the world and the red dust it is famous for is in the birds too.

  • Habitat: Alkaline dust pan fed by permanent swamps under Kilimanjaro, and red-soil Commiphora bush with lava ridges and the Mzima springs through Tsavo.
  • When: June to October for the concentrations, and November to April for the Palearctic waders on the Amboseli swamps and the resident breeding.
  • Getting there: Four hours from Nairobi to Amboseli through Namanga. Tsavo is entered from the Mombasa road at Mtito Andei or Voi, both of which have rail stations on the Nairobi to Mombasa line.
  • Look for: Kori Bustard, Secretarybird, Martial Eagle, Grey Crowned Crane, Golden-breasted Starling
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Black-bellied Sunbird, D'Arnaud's Barbet, Fischer's Starling, Grey Wren-Warbler, Von der Decken's Hornbill
  • National parks:
    • Amboseli National Park, Swamps fed by Kilimanjaro meltwater, with elephants and pelicans on the same water.
    • Tsavo East National Park, Red dust, the Galana river and the Yatta plateau, and open country birding at scale.
    • Chyulu Hills National Park, Green volcanic hills between the two, with cloud forest on the summit ridges.
  • Stops:
    • Tsavo West National Park : Hillier and greener, with Mzima Springs and lava flows.
    • : The highway town at the Tsavo East gate, on the Nairobi to Mombasa road.
  • Also covers: Amboseli National Park, Chyulu Hills National Park, Loitokitok, Mtito Andei, Tsavo East National Park, Tsavo West National Park, Voi

The Taita Hills and the Eastern Arc

Three forest fragments holding three birds and nothing else

The Taita Hills are the northernmost of the Eastern Arc mountains, and their cloud forest is down to a few hundred hectares in three fragments. Three birds live in them and in no other place on earth, which makes this the most concentrated endemism in Kenya.

  • Habitat: Fragments of cloud forest on isolated hilltops above cultivated slopes, the northernmost of the Eastern Arc blocks and none of them large.
  • When: Any month. The three endemics are resident, and the fragments are small enough to work in a morning whatever the weather.
  • Getting there: Leave the Mombasa road at Voi, then climb to Wundanyi. It is four hours from Mombasa and five from Nairobi, and the fragments are reached on foot from the ridge road.
  • Look for: Taita Thrush, Taita Apalis, Taita White-eye, Hartlaub's Turaco
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Ngangao Forest (forest reserve), The largest surviving fragment, about 120 hectares, and the most reliable for all three endemics.
  • Stops:
    • : A smaller and more disturbed fragment, and the harder of the two for the thrush.
    • : The hill town the forests are reached from, an hour up from the Mombasa road.
    • : The border town west of the hills, with Lake Chala and Jipe nearby.
  • Also covers: Chawia Forest, Mwatate, Ngangao Forest, Taveta, Wundanyi
  • Also watch for: Taita blade-horned chameleon and a caecilian, both as restricted as the birds.

The Coast: Arabuko-Sokoke and the Sabaki

The largest coastal forest in East Africa, and an estuary beside it

Arabuko-Sokoke is 420 square kilometres of coastal forest, the biggest left in East Africa, and it holds six globally threatened birds. The Sabaki estuary a few kilometres north takes Palearctic waders and terns in thousands.

  • Habitat: 420 square kilometres of coastal forest in three distinct types, brachystegia, cynometra and mixed, with mangrove and open estuary sand at the Sabaki mouth.
  • When: October to April, when the Palearctic waders are on the Sabaki and the estuary counts peak. The forest birds hold all year and call best at first light.
  • Getting there: Fly to Malindi or Mombasa. The forest headquarters at Gede sits on the coast road, and the Sabaki mouth is signed north of Malindi.
  • Look for: Sokoke Scops Owl, Sokoke Pipit, Amani Sunbird, East Coast Akalat, Malindi Pipit
  • National park: Arabuko-Sokoke National Park
  • Stops:
    • : Three forest types in one block. Watch from the Gede gate at dawn for the owl and the pipit.
    • : An estuary and dune system north of Malindi, with waders and terns in thousands on passage.
    • : A mangrove creek in the biosphere reserve, with a boardwalk and roosting waders.

Nairobi and the Central Highlands

A national park inside a capital, and a good first morning

Nairobi National Park runs to the city boundary, so lion and rhino are visible with tower blocks behind them, and the bird list runs past 500. The central highlands begin immediately behind it, and it is the most useful first morning of any African trip.

  • Habitat: Black-cotton grassland and acacia on the park side of the city, with highland forest remnants at Karura and Ngong Road and papyrus at the Limuru ponds.
  • When: Year round, and it is the one region worth birding on an arrival or departure morning. November to March brings the migrants to the dams and ponds.
  • Getting there: Nairobi National Park's main gate is twenty minutes from the city centre and ten from Wilson airport. Limuru and the highland forests are within an hour north.
  • Look for: Secretarybird, Grey Crowned Crane, Hartlaub's Turaco, Hinde's Babbler, Martial Eagle
  • Also recorded in the parks here: African Cuckoo, Black-bellied Bustard, Black-winged Lapwing, Brown-backed Honeybird, Cuckoo-finch
  • National park:
    • Nairobi National Park, 117 square kilometres of plains and gorge against the city, with a bird list past 500.
  • Stops:
    • : City forest with trails, and Hartlaub's Turaco within a taxi ride of a hotel.
    • : A grassy ridge west of the city, walked for highland grassland species.
    • : The town north-east of the city, and the accessible ground for Hinde's Babbler.
    • : Tea country on the escarpment above the Rift, with forest patches and ponds.
  • Also covers: Karura Forest, Limuru, Machakos, Nairobi National Park, Ngong Hills, Thika
  • Also watch for: Black rhino, lion and giraffe within sight of the skyline, and the park is unfenced on its southern side.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Kenya for birding?

October and November bring the largest Palearctic arrival of the year on top of the residents, which is the best window for a bird list. July to October is dry, has the wildebeest in the Mara, and is what most people book. The three seasons.

How many bird species does Kenya have?

Over 1,100 in a country the size of France, because it holds rainforest, savanna, desert, montane forest, coastal forest and soda lakes within one day's drive of each other. A day list past 200 is an ordinary thing here.

Can you combine birding and a safari?

They are the same trip. Nairobi National Park has a bird list past 500 with rhino in it, and the Mara holds the raptors and the wildebeest on the same grass. Nothing above asks you to choose.

Where do you go for the endemics?

Kakamega for the Guinea-Congo forest species, the Taita Hills for three birds that live in three forest fragments, Arabuko-Sokoke on the coast for six globally threatened species, and the Kinangop for Sharpe's Longclaw. The nine regions.

When is the wildebeest migration in the Mara?

Usually July to October, with the river crossings concentrated in August and September. The timing moves with the rain, so anyone booking on it should expect a window rather than a date.

Do you need a guide?

For the parks a driver-guide is the normal arrangement and worth it. For the endemics it is close to necessary: the Taita fragments, the Kinangop grasslands and the Sokoke Scops Owl territories are all found through people who know them.

Are there eco lodges in Kenya?

The conservancy model is the strongest version here: community-owned land leased for tourism, with the fees going back to the landowners. Several of the Mara and Laikipia conservancies work that way and hold more wildlife per hectare than the reserves.

What is Arabuko-Sokoke?

The largest coastal forest left in East Africa, 420 square kilometres north of Mombasa, and it holds six globally threatened birds including an owl and a pipit found almost nowhere else. It is a morning's work from Watamu.

How long do you need?

Four days does Nairobi and the Rift. Eight covers the classic circuit with the Mara. Ten is enough for the endemic run west and north. Seventeen crosses the country from Kakamega to the coast. Five routes.

What other wildlife is there?

The full East African set: lion, leopard, cheetah, elephant, black and white rhino, and the northern species that reach nowhere further south, Grevy zebra, reticulated giraffe and gerenuk among them.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
