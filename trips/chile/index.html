At a glance

Best months
  • October to December for the whole country
  • January to March if Patagonia is the point
  • April to September for the altiplano
Regions
Santiago, Farellones and the Cajon del MaipoThe central coast and the Humboldt pelagicsLauca, Surire and the northern altiplanoArica, the Azapa valley and the AtacamaLa Serena, Fray Jorge and the Norte ChicoThe Araucania: Conguillio and the araucaria forestsChiloe and the Valdivian rainforestTorres del Paine and PatagoniaTierra del Fuego and the Strait of Magellan
Getting in
Santiago (SCL) for everything, with internal flights to Arica (ARI), La Serena (LSC), Temuco (ZCO), Puerto Montt (PMC) and Punta Arenas (PUQ)
Getting around
A hire car, which Chile makes easier than anywhere else in South America, plus internal flights between the far north, the centre and Patagonia

When to Go

Chile is in the southern hemisphere and the birding season is its summer, roughly October to March. What complicates that is length: the altiplano in the far north has a summer WET season, the January and February altiplanic winter, exactly when central and southern Chile are dry. So the north and the south want opposite months and a full itinerary compromises.

Peak

October to December

Southern spring, and the one window that suits the whole length of the country.

Go for Punta Arenas records its driest month in October, the Farellones road is clear of snow, the sandpiper-plover is on its bogs, and the altiplano has not yet turned wet.

Trade off It is the busiest and dearest period in Patagonia, and Paine in particular needs booking months ahead.

Typical daily average
  • Santiago: 10 to 26 °C / 50 to 79 °F
  • Putre: 1 to 16 °C / 34 to 61 °F
  • Punta Arenas: 4 to 13 °C / 39 to 55 °F

Southern summer

January to March

The warmest months in the far south, and the wettest in the far north.

Go for Patagonia is at its warmest with very long days, Chiloe and Temuco are at their driest of the year, and Blue Whales are in the Gulf of Corcovado.

Trade off Putre takes 88 mm in January with afternoon storms above 4,000 m, so the altiplano is the wrong half of the country to be in.

Typical daily average
  • Punta Arenas: 6 to 15 °C / 43 to 59 °F
  • Castro: 10 to 18 °C / 50 to 64 °F
  • Putre: 4 to 17 °C / 39 to 63 °F

Northern dry

April to September

The altiplano's clear season, and the only sensible time to be at 4,500 m.

Go for Putre records 0 mm in May and the high roads to Chungara and Surire are open and dry. Arica on the coast below is warm and rainless in every month.

Trade off Central Chile is having its winter rain, the passes above Santiago are under snow, and Patagonia is closed for practical purposes with July nights at minus one.

Typical daily average
  • Putre: -4 to 13 °C / 25 to 55 °F
  • Arica: 13 to 17 °C / 55 to 63 °F
  • Santiago: 3 to 14 °C / 37 to 57 °F

Santiago, Farellones and the Cajon del Maipo

Andean foothills, high cordillera and matorral, the drought-adapted scrub of the Chilean lowlands, within two hours of the capital's airport

Almost every Chilean trip starts here, because the road from Santiago to Farellones climbs from matorral scrub to three thousand metres in an hour. Moustached Turca and Crag Chilia hold the rocky slopes on the way up, Grey-flanked Cinclodes works the boulder fields at the top, and the Yeso valley in the Cajon del Maipo is the classic site for Diademed Sandpiper-Plover on its bog.

  • Habitat: Santiago stands in the Andean foothills in the middle of the country and opens onto several excellent Andean sites. The road up to the El Yeso reservoir reaches 2,286 metres, and gravelly plains and a mass of streams spread out beyond the reservoir. This is one of the few places in South America, and the only one below 3,048 metres, where Diademed Sandpiper-Plover occurs. Farellones ski resort sits at 2,134 metres, 51 km northeast of the city.
  • When: Good all year. Moustached Turca, Crag Chilia and Grey-flanked Cinclodes hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Chile runs 4,329 km north to south on an average width of 200, so the distances between sites are larger than the map suggests and the internal air network is what makes a short trip work.
  • Look for: Moustached Turca, Crag Chilia, Diademed Sandpiper-Plover, Grey-flanked Cinclodes, Chilean Tinamou, Dusky Tapaculo, White-throated Tapaculo, Mountain Parakeet
  • Parks and reserves: Monumento Natural El Morado (nature reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : The ski village at 2,400 m, an hour of hairpins above the city, with the scrub below it and the boulder fields above.
    • : A reservoir and bog at 2,500 m in the Cajon del Maipo. It is the standard site for the sandpiper-plover.
    • : The capital and the arrival point for the whole country. The cordillera is visible from the runway.
  • Also covers: El Yeso, Farellones, Monumento Natural El Morado, San Jose de Maipo, Santiago
  • Also watch for: Guanaco holds the high valleys above the ski villages, and Culpeo, the Andean fox, works the roadside verges at dusk. Viscacha sits out on the rocks in the late afternoon on the way up to Valle Nevado.

The central coast and the Humboldt pelagics

Cold upwelling water an hour from the capital, and one of the best day boats for albatrosses anywhere

The Humboldt Current runs cold water up this coast and the shelf edge is close enough to reach on a day trip. Boats out of Quintero and Valparaiso find Black-browed Albatross, Salvin's Albatross and Pink-footed Shearwater within a couple of hours, with Peruvian Diving Petrel on the way out and Humboldt Penguin on the rocks. The coastal wetlands at Con Con and Algarrobo add the shorebirds.

  • Habitat: The nutrient-rich Humboldt current runs a short distance offshore from Valparaiso and Vina del Mar and feeds thousands of seabirds, among them Humboldt Penguin, Royal Albatross, Pink-footed Shearwater, Peruvian Diving-Petrel and Inca Tern. North of the city the road to Zapallar passes rocky headlands suited to seawatching, crosses an estuary with reedbeds beside it and reaches the Zapallar valley. Lago Penuelas is a large lake beside the Santiago to Valparaiso road.
  • When: Good all year. Black-browed Albatross, Salvin's Albatross and Peruvian Diving Petrel hold the ground through the year. Pink-footed Shearwater is away from June to August.
  • Getting there: Boat trips are best arranged by asking at the hotels or approaching local fishermen in Valparaiso, and such a trip is likely to be expensive. Punta Concon lies just south of Concon, 6 km north of Vina del Mar, and the Rio Aconcagua estuary is just north of Concon, where the reedy pools and channels on the north side repay exploring. Punta Liles at Quintero is 17 km north of Concon and is also good for seawatching, with a Humboldt Penguin colony just offshore at Isla Cachagua. Lago Penuelas sits opposite the Quintay junction on the north side of the Santiago to Valparaiso road, and a narrow road runs right around the water.
  • Look for: Black-browed Albatross, Salvin's Albatross, Pink-footed Shearwater, Peruvian Diving Petrel, Humboldt Penguin, Westland Petrel, Inca Tern, Peruvian Booby
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Black-headed Duck, Andean Goose
  • National park: La Campana National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The fishing port most pelagic day trips leave from, an hour and a half from Santiago.
    • : The historic port city. Sea lions haul out on the harbour walls and a second set of boats runs from here.
    • : The coastal wetland and lagoon south of the port. Search it for shorebirds, with the Humboldt Penguin islet offshore.
    • Lago Penuelas National Reserve : A reservoir and sclerophyll woodland reserve on the road between Santiago and Valparaiso.
  • Also covers: Algarrobo, La Campana National Park, Lago Penuelas National Reserve, Quintero, Valparaiso
  • Also watch for: South American Sea Lion hauls out on the harbour walls and follows the boats, and Peale's Dolphin and Common Dolphin are both regular on the crossing. Marine Otter works the rocky shore between Valparaiso and Quintero.

Lauca, Surire and the northern altiplano

High Andean plateau above 4,000 m on the Bolivian border in the far northern cordillera, with three flamingo species on the salt lakes

Putre at 3,500 m is where you sleep for a night before going higher, and the road above it reaches Lake Chungara at 4,500 m under two volcanoes. All three South American flamingos are on the salt lakes here, James's Flamingo the scarcest, and the bofedales, high Andean peat bogs, hold Giant Coot building its floating nests and Puna Tinamou on the tola scrub.

  • Habitat: Putre sits at 3,048 metres among bird-filled hillsides carrying the scarce Andean species, and Lauca stands another 1,500 metres above it on the altiplano proper.
  • When: Good all year. James's Flamingo, Andean Flamingo and Chilean Flamingo are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Putre is 120 km east of Arica and worth a full day and a night on the way up, because it is how you acclimatise before climbing the last 1,500 metres into the park.
  • Look for: James's Flamingo, Andean Flamingo, Chilean Flamingo, Giant Coot, Horned Coot, Puna Tinamou, Grey-breasted Seedsnipe, Puna Miner
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Andean Avocet, Andean Hillstar
  • National park: Lauca National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The village at 3,500 m where everybody acclimatises, three hours up from Arica.
    • : The altiplano hamlet at 4,400 m inside the park, with the bofedales and the lake beyond it.
    • : A lake at 4,500 m under the Payachata volcanoes, with the flamingos and the coots on it.
  • Also covers: Lake Chungara, Lauca National Park, Parinacota, Putre
  • Also watch for: Vicuna is everywhere on the bofedales and Guanaco lower down, with Viscacha on every rock pile around Parinacota. The altitude is the real hazard here: Chungara is 4,500 m and a day trip from sea level at Arica is a poor idea.

Arica, the Azapa valley and the Atacama

The driest desert on earth, with irrigated valleys cutting through it to the sea

Arica sits where the Atacama meets the sea and the two irrigated valleys behind it, Azapa and Lluta, are green lines through absolute desert. That is where Chilean Woodstar survives, a hummingbird down to a few hundred birds and one of the most threatened in the Americas, and where Tamarugo Conebill works the tamarugo groves. The Lluta river mouth is a shorebird site in its own right.

  • Habitat: The Azapa valley runs inland from Arica and holds flowering shrubs along the roadside, the Lluta valley beside it is dry farmland and scrub, and the Alacran Peninsula south of town is a rocky causeway.
  • When: Good all year. Peruvian Pelican, Slender-billed Finch and Oasis Hummingbird stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Head north out of Arica and turn east after 9 km to bird the fields and scrub along the Putre road. The Alacran Peninsula is a short run south of town and doubles as the seawatching point.
  • Look for: Chilean Woodstar, Tamarugo Conebill, Peruvian Pelican, Slender-billed Finch, Oasis Hummingbird, Peruvian Sheartail, Grey Gull, Croaking Ground Dove
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Black-hooded Sierra Finch, Greenish Yellow Finch
  • National parks: Llullaillaco National Park, Pan de Azúcar National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The northernmost city, on the Peruvian border. It is the base for the valleys and for the road up to the altiplano (the high plateau between the Andean ranges).
    • : The irrigated valley behind the city, olive groves and tamarugo. It is the last stronghold of the woodstar.
    • : The second valley north of the city, with the river mouth wetland at its seaward end.
  • Also covers: Arica, Azapa, Llullaillaco National Park, Lluta, Pan de Azúcar National Park
  • Also watch for: There is very little to see in the desert itself, which is the point of it: parts of the Atacama have no recorded rainfall at all. The Lluta river mouth and the coast hold South American Sea Lion and a resident group of Bottlenose Dolphin.

La Serena, Fray Jorge and the Norte Chico

Semi-desert scrub and a fog forest on the coast, where the desert begins to give way to matorral

Fray Jorge is a patch of Valdivian rainforest sitting on a coastal ridge seven hundred kilometres north of the nearest other one, kept alive entirely by fog that rolls in off the Humboldt. The trees are the same species as those at Chiloe and the birds around it are desert birds, which is a boundary you can stand on. The scrub inland holds the endemic canasteros.

  • When: Good all year. Dusky-tailed Canastero, Chilean Mockingbird and Band-tailed Sierra Finch are here in every month. Giant Hummingbird is away from April to May.
  • Look for: Dusky-tailed Canastero, Chilean Mockingbird, Band-tailed Sierra Finch, Chilean Seaside Cinclodes, Rufous-tailed Plantcutter, Moustached Turca, Giant Hummingbird, Peruvian Pelican
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Black-winged Ground Dove, White-throated Tapaculo
  • National parks: Bosque Fray Jorge National Park, Llanos de Challe National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The coastal city with the airport, and the base for the Norte Chico.
    • : The fog forest on a coastal ridge. A dirt road off the Panamericana reaches it.
    • : The inland town in the Limari valley, and the road towards the scrubland sites.
  • Also covers: Bosque Fray Jorge National Park, La Serena, Llanos de Challe National Park, Ovalle
  • Also watch for: Culpeo and Chilla, the two Chilean foxes, both use the Fray Jorge scrub, and the coast at Coquimbo carries South American Sea Lion and Bottlenose Dolphin. Humboldt Penguin breeds on the islands off Punta de Choros an hour north.

The Araucania: Conguillio and the araucaria forests

Monkey puzzle forest under a live volcano, in the country's lake district

Conguillio's araucaria stands grow on lava flows under the Llaima volcano and look like nothing else on the continent. The birding is austral forest at its most accessible: Magellanic Woodpecker on the monkey puzzle trunks, Chestnut-throated Huet-huet and Chucao Tapaculo on the forest floor, and Austral Pygmy Owl answering in the middle of the afternoon.

  • When: Good all year. Magellanic Woodpecker, Chucao Tapaculo and Austral Pygmy Owl are on site in all twelve months.
  • Look for: Magellanic Woodpecker, Chestnut-throated Huet-huet, Chucao Tapaculo, Austral Pygmy Owl, Chilean Pigeon, Thorn-tailed Rayadito, White-throated Treerunner, Patagonian Sierra Finch

    • : The town at the southern park entrance, with rooms and the road up to the araucaria stands.
    • : The regional capital with an airport, two hours from the park.

Chiloe and the Valdivian rainforest

Temperate rainforest and a big island coast, wet enough to be green in every month

Chiloe is a big wooded island with a national park on its Pacific side and a mudflat coast on the other, and the Valdivian forest on the mainland opposite is the same habitat with more of it. Slender-billed Parakeet moves through in noisy flocks, Ochre-flanked Tapaculo and Black-throated Huet-huet hold the bamboo understorey, and the Chiloe mudflats carry Hudsonian Godwit wintering from Alaska.

  • Habitat: Chiloe is 250 km long and 50 wide, often misty and wet, and its tidal creeks, marshes, forest and bamboo hold the endemic Slender-billed Parakeet. Vicente Perez Rosales and Puyehue protect extensive forest on the mainland behind it.
  • When: Good all year. Slender-billed Parakeet, Ochre-flanked Tapaculo and Black-throated Huet-huet hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Puerto Montt is 1,080 km south of Santiago and is the gateway to the island and to both mainland parks. For Puyehue head 105 km north to Osorno, then east past Lake Puyehue to the park headquarters. The Refugio Antillanca inside the park is the best birding and the best base.
  • Look for: Slender-billed Parakeet, Ochre-flanked Tapaculo, Black-throated Huet-huet, Hudsonian Godwit, Des Murs's Wiretail, Magellanic Tapaculo, Black-faced Ibis, Flying Steamer Duck
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Chucao Tapaculo, Patagonian Tyrant
  • National parks: Chiloé National Park, Alerce Andino National Park, Puyehue National Park, Vicente Pérez Rosales National Park, Corcovado National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The island's main town, with the palafito houses on stilts, an hour from the national park at Cucao.
    • : The village at the Pacific park entrance, with the forest trails and the dune coast behind it.
    • : The mainland city and airport, and the ferry port for the island.
  • Also covers: Alerce Andino National Park, Castro, Chiloé National Park, Corcovado National Park, Cucao, Puerto Montt, Puyehue National Park, Vicente Pérez Rosales National Park
  • Also watch for: Blue Whale feeds in the Gulf of Corcovado off Chiloe's east coast from December to April, which is one of very few known feeding grounds in the southern hemisphere. Marine Otter works the rocky shore and Pudu holds the island's forest.

Torres del Paine and Patagonia

Granite towers over Patagonian steppe and lake at the southern end of the road, with condors over both

Paine is a national park most people come to for the mountains, and the birding is done between them. Andean Condor uses the cliffs and is seen every day, Lesser Rhea runs on the steppe outside the park, and the lakes hold Black-necked Swan and Chilean Flamingo together, which is a combination that surprises people. Magellanic Woodpecker is in the lenga forest on the eastern side.

  • Habitat: Lakes, meadows and rushing rivers lie below the glaciers and granite towers of the park.
  • When: Good all year. Andean Condor, Lesser Rhea and Black-necked Swan are on site in all twelve months. Austral Negrito joins them from September to February.
  • Getting there: The park is 392 km north of Punta Arenas and 145 km northwest of Puerto Natales, and it is possible to stay inside it.
  • Look for: Andean Condor, Lesser Rhea, Black-necked Swan, Austral Parakeet, Chilean Flamingo, Upland Goose, Rufous-tailed Hawk, Austral Negrito
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Austral Canastero, Cinnamon-bellied Ground Tyrant
  • National park: Torres del Paine National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The town on the fjord two hours from the park, with the rooms and the guides.
    • : The park itself, with the steppe, the lakes and the lenga forest between the towers.
  • Also covers: Puerto Natales, Torres del Paine National Park
  • Also watch for: Guanaco is abundant on the steppe inside the park, and with it the Puma population that has made Paine the best place in the world to see the cat. The tracking is done at dawn and dusk with guides who know individual animals.

Tierra del Fuego and the Strait of Magellan

Steppe, bog and shoreline at the end of the continent, with a king penguin colony on the island

The strait is crossed by ferry from Punta Arenas and the island beyond it is flat steppe and peat bog. Magellanic Plover feeds on the lagoon margins here and belongs to a family with one member, and the colony at Bahia Inutil is the only King Penguin colony on the American mainland side. Ruddy-headed Goose winters here in what is left of its Chilean population.

  • Habitat: Sea, lake-dotted steppe grassland and Nothofagus forest run together around Punta Arenas and across the Chilean half of Tierra del Fuego, and between them they carry a superb set of seabirds.
  • When: Good all year. Ruddy-headed Goose, Magellanic Penguin and Dolphin Gull hold the ground through the year. Chilean Skua is away from May to September.
  • Getting there: Punta Arenas is 2,140 km south of Santiago and is the gateway to the island. Porvenir on Tierra del Fuego also takes visitors.
  • Look for: Magellanic Plover, King Penguin, Ruddy-headed Goose, Magellanic Penguin, White-bellied Seedsnipe, Chilean Skua, Dolphin Gull, Kelp Goose
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Magellanic Oystercatcher, Ashy-headed Goose
  • National parks: Pali-Aike National Park, Alberto de Agostini National Park, Yendegaia National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The southernmost city on the mainland, with the airport and the ferry across the strait.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Chile?

October to December if you want the whole country: Patagonia opens, the snow has gone from the cordillera above Santiago, and the altiplano has not yet turned wet. January to March is warmer and longer in the far south. April to September is the altiplano's clear season and the only sensible time to be at 4,500 m. The three seasons.

Why do the north and the south want different months?

Because the altiplano's rain falls in the southern summer, the altiplanic winter, exactly when Patagonia is at its best. Putre takes 88 mm in January and 0 mm in May, while Punta Arenas is at its driest in October. Any full-country itinerary compromises on one end or the other. When to go, season by season.

Is Arica really that dry?

Yes. The climate model above computes 2 mm of rain a year there and the published normal is under a millimetre, which makes it the driest inhabited place on earth. That is why the Azapa and Lluta valleys matter so much: they are the only green in several hundred kilometres and everything concentrates in them. Arica, the Azapa valley and the Atacama.

Can I do this as a self-drive?

Yes, more easily than anywhere else in South America. The roads are good, the hire cars are reliable and the Panamericana runs the length of the country. The two things worth a guide are the altiplano, where the altitude is a real hazard, and puma tracking at Torres del Paine. Birding community.

How much of the country can one trip cover?

Two of the three blocks. Arica to Punta Arenas is 4,300 km, further than Lisbon to Moscow, and each region above is a separate flight. A fortnight does central Chile and the south, or central Chile and the north, and not both. The itineraries.

What are the endemic birds?

Chile's endemics are few and concentrated: Crag Chilia, Moustached Turca, Chilean Tinamou, Dusky Tapaculo, White-throated Tapaculo, Chilean Mockingbird and the Chilean Woodstar in the north, with the Juan Fernandez birds offshore. Most are in the matorral within two hours of Santiago, which is why the capital region opens every trip. The birds.

Is Torres del Paine worth it for birds?

Not on its own. The bird list at Paine is good rather than exceptional, and the condors, rheas and Black-necked Swans are the draw. What makes the park worth the flight is that it is the best place in the world to see a puma, tracked at dawn and dusk with guides who know individual animals. Torres del Paine and Patagonia.

How many bird species does Chile have?

Around 550 recorded, which is modest for South America because the Andes and the desert cut the country off from the Amazon. What it has instead is a set of habitats found nowhere else on the continent: the Atacama, the Valdivian rainforest and the Magellanic steppe. The birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
