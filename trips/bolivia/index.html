Birding in Bolivia

Bolivia runs from a lake at 3,810 metres to Amazon forest at 180, and holds two macaws whose entire world ranges sit inside its borders.

At a glance

Best months
May to September for the country as a whole, and June to October if the Beni and the Blue-throated Macaw are the reason for the trip. The altiplano is dry all year and merely colder in winter.
Regions
The Yungas: Coroico and the Cotapata roadLake Titicaca and the northern altiplanoCochabamba and the Siberia cloud forestSamaipata and the dry inter-Andean valleysSanta Cruz, Amboro and the eastern lowlandsThe Beni llanos and the Blue-throated MacawNoel Kempff Mercado and the Huanchaca plateau
Routes
La Paz and the Yungas6 daysSanta Cruz, Amboro and the dry valleys8 daysThe altiplano and Titicaca7 daysCochabamba to Santa Cruz9 daysEnd to end18 days
Getting in
Santa Cruz (VVI) takes most of the international flights and is the practical entry for the lowlands and the dry valleys. La Paz (LPB) sits at 4,061 metres, which is the highest international airport in the world and needs a day before any walking.
Getting around
A hired four-wheel drive covers the roads on this guide, and internal flights save days: Santa Cruz to Trinidad is an hour against two on the road, and Noel Kempff and Barba Azul are charters rather than drives. Altitude is the real constraint rather than distance.

When to Go

Bolivia runs a dry season from May to October and a wet one from November to April, and the difference matters far more in the lowlands than in the mountains. The Beni floods and closes, the Yungas road becomes dangerous, and Noel Kempff is unreachable. The altiplano is dry all year and merely colder.

Peak

May to September

The dry season. The Beni plain is passable, the Yungas road is open, Noel Kempff can be flown into, and the altiplano air is clear enough to see a hundred kilometres.

Go for Blue-throated Macaws gathering at the palm islands, the full Yungas transect in one morning, and the flamingo lagoons at their most reliable.

Trade off Nights on the altiplano fall well below freezing, and June and July are the coldest.

Shoulder

October to November

The rains have not arrived and the breeding season has started. Red-fronted Macaws are at the nest cliffs and the dry valleys are at their most active.

Go for Breeding activity in the valleys and the lowlands, with the roads still dry.

Trade off The hottest weeks in the lowlands, and the first storms can arrive early.

Wet

December to April

The wet season. The Beni floods, the unsurfaced lowland roads close and the Yungas road runs in cloud and landslide.

Go for The Andes are green and the cloud forest is at its most active, and rates are at their lowest.

Trade off Most of the lowland half of this guide is simply unreachable, and Noel Kempff closes.

The Yungas: Coroico and the Cotapata road

A road that falls from a 4,650 metre pass to 1,200 metres in four hours, crossing every height band the eastern Andes has

The old road from La Paz to Coroico drops through puna, elfin forest, cloud forest and subtropical forest in a single morning, and the birds change every few hundred metres of descent. Cotapata protects the upper half and the Chuspipata to Yolosa stretch is the classic transect.

  • Habitat: Humid upper tropical to elfin forest on the eastern Andean scarp, from puna grassland at the pass down through cloud forest to subtropical forest at Yolosa.
  • When: May to September, the dry season, when the road is open and the cloud lifts by mid-morning. Mixed flocks move through all year.
  • Getting there: The old Yungas road leaves La Paz at the pass above the city, an hour out, and reaches Coroico in three to four hours. The new highway carries the traffic, which is what makes the old road birdable.

Lake Titicaca and the northern altiplano

The highest large lake in the world at 3,810 metres, with puna grassland and salt lakes on the plain south of it

Titicaca holds a grebe found on it and nowhere else, flightless and confined to the lake system. South of it the northern altiplano runs through Oruro to shallow salt lakes that take flamingos, and the puna birds hold ground above 4,000 metres.

  • Habitat: High puna grassland and totora reed lake at 3,810 metres, with salt pan, brine lagoon and polylepis woodland patches on the ranges above.
  • When: May to September for dry roads and clear air, though nights fall well below freezing. The lake birds hold all year.
  • Getting there: Copacabana is four hours from La Paz around the southern shore of the lake. Oruro is three and a half hours south of La Paz on a surfaced road.
  • Look for: Titicaca Grebe, Chilean Flamingo, Lesser Rhea, Andean Condor, Torrent Duck, Giant Conebill
  • Stops:

  • Also watch for: Vicuna on the puna and the Avaroa lagoons, and the largest salt flat on earth at Uyuni.

Cochabamba and the Siberia cloud forest

Cloud forest on the ridge between two valleys, holding birds described from it and found in a handful of ridges either side

Siberia is a stretch of cloud forest on the old Cochabamba to Santa Cruz road, high and permanently wet, and several Bolivian endemics were described from it. The valleys either side are dry and hold a different set again, and the Villa Tunari road drops north into the Chapare.

  • Habitat: Upper montane cloud forest on a permanently wet ridge at 2,300 to 3,000 metres, between dry inter-Andean valleys to the west and the Chapare foothills to the north.
  • When: April to October, the dry season. Siberia sits in cloud most afternoons whatever the month, and the roads are difficult in the wet.
  • Getting there: Cochabamba has an airport with flights from La Paz and Santa Cruz. The old road east through Comarapa reaches Siberia in four hours, and the Villa Tunari road north drops into the Chapare in three.
  • Look for: Bolivian Recurvebill, Hooded Mountain Toucan, Grey-breasted Mountain Toucan, Wattled Guan, Andean Cock-of-the-rock, Cochabamba Mountain Finch, Black-hooded Sunbeam

Samaipata and the dry inter-Andean valleys

Dry valleys and cactus scrub on the eastern Andean front, holding the whole world range of a macaw

The Red-fronted Macaw lives in dry river valleys between Cochabamba and Santa Cruz and nowhere else, nesting in cliffs above the Rio Mizque. Samaipata sits where these inter-Andean valleys meet the humid Amboro slope, so a short drive crosses between two entirely different bird communities.

  • Habitat: Dry inter-Andean valley scrub with columnar cactus and thorn woodland on the rain shadow side, running up to humid montane forest on the Amboro front.
  • When: April to October, the dry season. The macaws are at the cliff colonies through the year and breed from October.
  • Getting there: Samaipata is two and a half hours west of Santa Cruz on a surfaced road. The macaw valleys are a further three to four hours on unsurfaced roads and are usually visited with a local guide.
  • Look for: Red-fronted Macaw, Bolivian Earthcreeper, Military Macaw, Andean Condor, Greyish Mourner, Whooping Motmot

Santa Cruz, Amboro and the eastern lowlands

Where the Amazon, the Andes, the Chaco and the cerrado all meet, which is why the bird list here is the longest in the country

Amboro sits at the junction of four biomes and the road from Santa Cruz to Buena Vista reaches its northern front in two hours. Lowland forest, foothill forest and cerrado are all within a short drive of the city, and the eastern lowlands beyond it run into the largest tropical dry forest on earth.

  • Habitat: Humid lowland and foothill forest on the eastern Andean front, meeting Chiquitano dry forest, cerrado and Chaco thorn scrub on the plains around Santa Cruz.
  • When: April to October, the dry season, when the lowland tracks are passable. The foothill forest is good all year.
  • Getting there: Santa Cruz has the country's main international airport. Buena Vista is two hours northwest on a surfaced road and is the usual base for the northern Amboro front.
  • Look for: Horned Curassow, Blue-throated Macaw, Toco Toucan, Amazonian Motmot, Crested Eagle, Bolivian Recurvebill

The Beni llanos and the Blue-throated Macaw

Seasonally flooded savanna with forest islands, holding a macaw whose entire world population is a few hundred birds

The Beni is a plain of grassland and palm island that floods each wet season, and the Blue-throated Macaw lives on it and nowhere else. Barba Azul is a private reserve set up for the bird, reached by light aircraft, and the Trinidad road crosses the same habitat for anyone driving.

  • Habitat: Seasonally flooded savanna with motacu palm islands and gallery forest along the rivers, on an alluvial plain that floods from December to May.
  • When: June to October, the dry season, when the plain is passable and the macaws gather at the palm islands. The wet season closes most of it.
  • Getting there: Trinidad has an airport with daily flights from Santa Cruz and La Paz. Barba Azul is reached by chartered light aircraft from Trinidad, and the Beni Biological Station on the Yucumo road is a long drive from either.

Noel Kempff Mercado and the Huanchaca plateau

A sandstone plateau above Amazon forest on the Brazilian border, with cerrado on top and waterfalls off the edge

Noel Kempff protects the Huanchaca plateau and the forest around it, and the change from Amazon lowland to cerrado over a five hundred metre escarpment puts two bird communities in one park. Access is by light aircraft or a very long drive, which is why the visitor numbers are counted in the hundreds.

  • Habitat: Amazon lowland forest and seasonally flooded savanna below a sandstone escarpment, with cerrado grassland and gallery forest on the plateau above it.
  • When: May to October, the dry season. Access is difficult in any month and impossible in parts of the wet.
  • Getting there: Light aircraft from Santa Cruz to the Flor de Oro or Los Fierros camps, which is how almost everyone arrives. The road from Santa Cruz takes two days and needs a four-wheel drive.
  • Look for: Harpy Eagle, Hyacinth Macaw, Greater Rhea, Toco Toucan, Crested Eagle, Jabiru

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Bolivia?

May to September, the dry season, when the Beni plain is passable, the Yungas road is open and Noel Kempff can be flown into. June to October if the Blue-throated Macaw is the reason for the trip. The altiplano is dry all year and simply colder in the middle of it.

How many bird species does Bolivia have?

Around 1,440 species, which puts it in the top ten countries on earth despite having no coast. The reason is height and position: the country runs from Amazon forest at 180 metres to puna above 4,500, and takes in Amazon, Andes, Chaco, cerrado and dry inter-Andean valley within its borders.

What are the two endemic macaws?

The Blue-throated Macaw, whose entire world population of a few hundred birds lives on the Beni savanna, and the Red-fronted Macaw, which lives in dry river valleys between Cochabamba and Santa Cruz. Armonia runs conservation programmes and reserves for both, and both can be seen.

How bad is the altitude?

La Paz airport is at 4,061 metres, the highest international airport in the world, and arriving there means a day of doing nothing before any walking. Santa Cruz at 400 metres is the gentler entry, and a route that works upward rather than starting at the top is far more comfortable.

Is the old Yungas road safe?

The through traffic moved to the new highway in 2006, which is what made the old road birdable. It is narrow, unsurfaced and exposed, and it is driven slowly with a local driver rather than a hire car. Cyclists use it in numbers, so expect them on blind corners.

Can I reach Noel Kempff independently?

Not easily. It is a charter flight from Santa Cruz to Flor de Oro or Los Fierros, or two days of driving with a four-wheel drive. Visitor numbers are in the hundreds a year, which is both the difficulty and the point.

Do I need four-wheel drive?

For the Beni, the macaw valleys, the Avaroa lagoons and anything unsurfaced in the wet season, yes. Santa Cruz to Samaipata, La Paz to Copacabana and the new Yungas highway are surfaced.

Is Bolivia expensive?

It is among the cheapest countries in South America for food, transport and simple rooms. What costs money is the flying: Barba Azul and Noel Kempff are charters, and internal flights save days that a road would eat.

Can I combine Bolivia with Peru?

Titicaca is the obvious join. Copacabana is on the Bolivian shore and Puno on the Peruvian one, three hours apart by road around the lake, and the two guides cover the same high Andean set from either side.

What field guide should I take?

Birds of Bolivia by Herzog, Terrill, Jahn and others, published in 2016, is the first guide written for the country alone and covers the whole national list. Before it, visitors carried the Peru and Brazil guides together.
