At a glance

Best months
November to April for the dry season and the winter birds, September and October for the Veracruz raptor passage
Regions
Oaxaca and the Southern HighlandsThe Yucatan PeninsulaVeracruz and the Gulf SlopeChiapas and the Southern BorderBaja California and the Sea of CortezSan Blas and the West CoastThe Sierra Madre Occidental and Copper CanyonThe Huasteca and the Eastern Sierra
Routes
Oaxaca in a Week6 daysThe Yucatan Loop7 daysVeracruz and the River of Raptors8 daysBaja and the Sea of Cortez10 daysThe Southern Endemics17 days
Getting around
Mexico City is the hub but most regions have their own airport and a domestic flight beats a long drive. Roads are good in the Yucatan and the Bajio, and rough on the tracks into El Cielo and El Triunfo.

When to Go

Mexico runs a summer wet season and a dry winter, which is the opposite of the European pattern and means the best birding months are the cool dry ones from November to April. The exception is Veracruz, where the reason to come is the autumn raptor passage in September and October.

Peak

November to April

The dry season. Cool, clear and comfortable, with every northern migrant in the country on top of the residents.

Go for The full Oaxaca endemic list, grey whales calving in Baja from January, monarchs in the Michoacan forests, and the Yucatan at its most pleasant.

Trade off The busiest and priciest months, and highland mornings are genuinely cold.

Typical daily average Oaxaca 10 to 28 °C / 50 to 82 °F, The Yucatan 17 to 33 °C / 63 to 91 °F, Baja 10 to 29 °C / 50 to 84 °F

Passage

September and October

The largest raptor migration on earth crosses the Veracruz plain, and counts have exceeded five million birds in a season.

Go for The river of raptors from a hotel roof at Cardel, and the lowlands at their greenest.

Trade off The wettest weeks of the year elsewhere, and hurricane season on both coasts.

Typical daily average Veracruz 18 to 28 °C / 64 to 82 °F, The Yucatan 22 to 33 °C / 72 to 91 °F, Oaxaca 14 to 27 °C / 57 to 81 °F

Green season

May to August

The summer rains, afternoon storms and the whole country green. Residents breeding and in song.

Go for Endemics on territory and singing, the lowest rates of the year, and empty sites.

Trade off Heat, daily rain, and rough going on the unpaved tracks to El Cielo and El Triunfo.

Typical daily average Oaxaca 15 to 30 °C / 59 to 86 °F, The Yucatan 22 to 36 °C / 72 to 97 °F, Baja 20 to 37 °C / 68 to 99 °F

Pine-oak, dry scrub and cloud forest inside an hour of a colonial city

Veracruz and the Gulf Slope

The largest raptor migration on earth, and cloud forest above it

Every autumn the whole eastern flyway funnels between the Gulf and the Sierra Madre Oriental and passes over a coastal plain twenty kilometres wide. Counts from Cardel and Chichicaxtle have exceeded five million birds in a season, which is more raptors than are counted anywhere else, and the Gulf slope above the plain holds cloud forest within half an hour of the count site.

  • Look for: Bat Falcon, Crimson-collared Grosbeak, Military Macaw, Turquoise-browed Motmot, Bearded Wood Partridge
  • National parks: Los Tuxtlas Biosphere Reserve, Cofre de Perote National Park

Chiapas and the Southern Border

Cloud forest on the Sierra Madre, and the birds shared with Guatemala

The Sierra Madre de Chiapas carries cloud forest that continues into Guatemala, and the birds in it belong to the Central American highlands rather than to Mexico. El Triunfo is walked into over two days and holds the Horned Guan.

  • Look for: Horned Guan, Pink-headed Warbler, Azure-rumped Tanager, Resplendent Quetzal, Belted Flycatcher, Buffy-crowned Wood Partridge

Desert peninsula endemics, and a sea that feeds them

A 1,200 km desert peninsula with its own endemic birds at the southern tip and one of the most productive seas on earth alongside it. The birding and the whale watching are the same trip, and the boat is how you reach both.

A small port on the Nayarit coast with mangrove channels behind it and thorn forest on the hills above, and it has been a birding destination since the 1960s. The west Mexican endemic list is longer here than anywhere else on the coast.

Pine-oak highlands and a canyon system deeper than the Grand Canyon

The Sierra Madre Occidental runs the length of northern Mexico and carries the largest pine-oak forest in North America. Copper Canyon cuts through it, and the train that crosses it is how most people arrive.

Cloud forest at the northern limit of the tropics

The eastern sierra, the Sierra Madre Oriental, catches the Gulf moisture and holds cloud forest further north than anywhere else in the Americas. El Cielo is the northernmost of it, and the Huasteca below carries lowland species at the edge of their range.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Mexico for birding?

November to April is the dry season, cool and comfortable, with every northern migrant in the country on top of the residents. The exception is Veracruz, where the reason to come is the autumn raptor passage in September and October. The three seasons.

How many bird species does Mexico have?

Over 1,100, and more than a hundred of them live in Mexico and nowhere else, which is the highest endemic count of any country in the Americas outside the Andes. The number above counts the species with a card in the BeakBrain guide rather than a figure typed in by hand.

Where do you go birding in Mexico?

Oaxaca holds more endemics than any other state and most Mexican trips include it. The Yucatan is a separate set again, Chiapas shares its birds with Guatemala, and Baja has its own. The eight regions.

What is the Veracruz river of raptors?

The whole eastern flyway funnels between the Gulf and the Sierra Madre Oriental onto a coastal plain twenty kilometres wide. Counts from the roof at Cardel have exceeded five million birds in a season, mostly Broad-winged and Swainson Hawks and Turkey Vultures. October is the peak.

Can you see whales and birds on the same trip?

In Baja that is the normal shape of a trip. Grey whales calve in Magdalena Bay and the lagoons from January to March, the Sea of Cortez holds blue whales in the same window, and the Cape endemics are an hour from the same base.

What other wildlife is there?

Grey and blue whales off Baja in winter, monarch butterflies overwintering in the Michoacan fir forests from November, spider and howler monkeys in the southern rainforest, and jaguar in Calakmul and the Lacandon.

Are there eco lodges in Mexico?

The strongest are community-run: several of the Yucatan and Oaxaca reserves are held by ejidos and the lodges on them are owned by the villages. Mexico has no national eco-certification register, so this guide lists birding lodges rather than badges.

Is it safe?

The birding regions above are ordinary tourist Mexico: Oaxaca, the Yucatan, Veracruz, Chiapas and Baja are visited in normal numbers and the operators here run them year after year. Advice varies by state rather than by country, so check the state rather than the map.

How long do you need?

Six days does Oaxaca properly from one base. A week covers the Yucatan or Veracruz. Ten days takes Baja. Seventeen crosses the whole endemic-rich south. Five routes.

Do you need a guide?

For Oaxaca and the Yucatan a car and a good book go a long way. Chiapas needs one: El Triunfo is a permitted reserve reached on a two-day walk with arranged mules. The northern sierra also rewards someone who knows which barranca is holding what.
