At a glance

Best months
  • September to November for cerrado breeding and song
  • June to August for Lear's Macaw with the caatinga bare
Regions
Serra da CanastraSerra do Cipo and the Caraca sanctuaryChapada dos Veadeiros and BrasiliaEmas and the Goias plateauThe Sao Francisco valley and Lapa GrandeChapada DiamantinaBoa Nova and the mata de cipoCanudos and the Raso da CatarinaThe Ceara chapadas: Araripe and UbajaraPernambuco and the Alagoas brejos
Getting in
Belo Horizonte (CNF) for the Minas cerrado, Brasilia (BSB) for the plateau, Salvador (SSA) for Bahia, Recife (REC) or Fortaleza (FOR) for the north-east
Getting around
A hired vehicle with clearance. The distances are long, several parks are unmade road inside, and two sites need a permit arranged in advance

When to Go

Two calendars, in opposition. The cerrado has a hard wet season from October to March and is brown by August. The north-eastern coast runs the other way: Murici takes 46 mm in January and 143 mm in August. A trip that covers both is always compromising on one of them, which is why operators sell the two separately.

Peak

September to November

The end of the cerrado dry season and the start of the rains, which is when the interior breeds.

Go for Cerrado breeding at Canastra, Cipo and Emas, the grassland tyrants displaying, and the first storms bringing the caatinga into leaf within days.

Trade off It is the hottest stretch of the year in the caatinga, and roads in the far interior can be difficult once the rain really starts.

Typical daily average
  • Serra do Cipo: 13 to 27 °C / 55 to 81 °F
  • Canudos: 17 to 32 °C / 63 to 90 °F
  • Murici: 20 to 30 °C / 68 to 86 °F

Shoulder

March to June

The cerrado drying out and the north-eastern coast at its wettest, which are two different arguments.

Go for Comfortable travelling in the interior with the grass still green, and the Pernambuco brejos at their most active.

Trade off The caatinga is closing down through this period and Canudos is at its harshest.

Typical daily average
  • Serra do Cipo: 10 to 28 °C / 50 to 82 °F
  • Canudos: 17 to 31 °C / 63 to 88 °F
  • Murici: 20 to 30 °C / 68 to 86 °F

Quiet

July and August

The deep dry season. Canudos takes 15 mm in August and the caatinga is leafless.

Go for Lear's Macaw at the roost with the trees bare, which is the easiest viewing of the year, and cool comfortable nights on the cerrado plateaus.

Trade off The cerrado is brown, fire is a real risk on the plateaus, and very little is singing.

Typical daily average
  • Serra do Cipo: 10 to 25 °C / 50 to 77 °F
  • Canudos: 16 to 26 °C / 61 to 79 °F
  • Murici: 19 to 26 °C / 66 to 79 °F

Serra da Canastra

High cerrado grassland at the head of the Sao Francisco, and clear streams that hold a duck

Canastra is high open cerrado on a plateau above 1,200 m, and the Sao Francisco rises inside it. Its clear streams hold Brazilian Merganser, down to a few hundred birds, and the grassland above holds a set of tyrants and finches that live nowhere else in Brazil in any number.

  • Habitat: Cerrado, gallery forest and wild rivers, and the rivers are why the park is famous: it is about the only place in the country where Brazilian Merganser can still be found.
  • When: Good all year. Brazilian Merganser, Cock-tailed Tyrant and Streamer-tailed Tyrant stay through the year.
  • Getting there: The park lies 330 km west of Belo Horizonte.
  • Look for: Brazilian Merganser, Cock-tailed Tyrant, Streamer-tailed Tyrant, Black-masked Finch, Blue Finch, Grey-backed Tachuri, Collared Crescentchest, Cinnamon Tanager
  • National park:
    • Serra da Canastra National Park, 200,000 hectares of high cerrado. The upper plateau is reached from Sao Roque de Minas and the lower part from Vargem Bonita.
  • Stops:
    • : The Sao Francisco falls 186 m off the plateau edge. Gallery forest stands at the foot of it and swifts cross the lip.
    • : The town at the upper gate. It is the base for the plateau roads where the merganser streams are.
    • : The town below the escarpment, and the way to the lower falls and the gallery forest.
  • Also covers: Casca d'Anta waterfall, Delfinopolis, Sao Roque de Minas, Serra da Canastra National Park, Vargem Bonita
  • Also watch for: Maned Wolf is seen on the park roads at dusk more often here than anywhere else in Brazil, and Giant Anteater is regular on the open grassland in the early morning.

Serra do Cipo and the Caraca sanctuary

Campo rupestre on quartzite above Belo Horizonte, and wolves at a monastery door

The Espinhaco range runs north out of Belo Horizonte and carries campo rupestre, a rocky high grassland with a plant and bird list of its own. Serra do Cipo National Park is the accessible piece of it, and an hour east the Caraca sanctuary puts food out for Maned Wolf on the monastery steps every night.

  • Habitat: Serra do Cipo National Park lies 120 km north of Belo Horizonte and holds cerrado and gallery forest, with rocky outcrops beside the road above the Chapeu de Sol Hotel. Seven endemics occur here, among them Cipo Canastero, which is known only from this site and was discovered in 1985.
  • When: Good all year. Hyacinth Visorbearer, Cipo Canastero and Grey-backed Tachuri are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: From Belo Horizonte head north to Lagoa Santa and follow the road north into the park. Roadside forest stands just before the administration building and the bridge, and the flowering trees there repay checking. From the bridge the road climbs about 9 km, a rough track runs northwest 3 km beyond that, and the rocky outcrops on the right-hand side of the road a few kilometres higher up hold the endemics.
  • Look for: Hyacinth Visorbearer, Cipo Canastero, Grey-backed Tachuri, Cinnamon Tanager, Horned Sungem, Blue Finch, Collared Crescentchest, Streamer-tailed Tyrant
  • National park:
    • Serra do Cipo National Park, 31,000 hectares of campo rupestre and gallery forest. Bird the park road slowly and walk the Farofa trail.
  • Stops:
    • Santuario do Caraca : A nineteenth century monastery in 11,000 hectares of its own reserve. Maned Wolves are fed on the steps after dark, and forest trails run behind it.
    • : The village on the park road, ninety minutes from Belo Horizonte. It is the base for the plateau.
  • Also covers: Catas Altas, Santana do Riacho, Santuario do Caraca, Serra do Cipo National Park
  • Also watch for: The Maned Wolves at Caraca come to the monastery steps after dark and are watched from the terrace, which is a set piece rather than a chance encounter. Cipo has a high rate of plant endemism that the birds are only part of.

Chapada dos Veadeiros sits at the highest point of the central plateau and is a UNESCO world heritage site for the cerrado it protects. Brasilia itself is worth a day on the way through: the Brasilia National Park is inside the city and holds the tapaculo that carries its name.

  • Habitat: Brasilia National Park sits 9 km north of the capital and protects 28,000 hectares of some of the best remaining cerrado in Brazil. Forest surrounds the swimming pool at the entrance and scrub covers the ground around it.
  • When: Dawn and dusk at the park entrance are when Small-billed Tinamou, Bare-faced Curassow and Curl-crested Jay show themselves.
  • Getting there: Most visitors bird the entrance area, where the forest around the pools is excellent. A road runs along the northern perimeter of the park and turns off the road east out of town. Permission for the parts of the park that stay closed to visitors comes from the park authority.
  • Look for: Brasilia Tapaculo, Pfrimer's Parakeet, Cock-tailed Tyrant, Collared Crescentchest, Horned Sungem, Grey-backed Tachuri, Black-masked Finch, Cinnamon Tanager
  • National parks:
    • Chapada dos Veadeiros National Park, Quartzite plateau and gallery forest at the head of the cerrado, a UNESCO world heritage site. Marked trails start from Alto Paraiso.
    • Brasilia National Park, 42,000 hectares of cerrado inside the federal capital. It is the type locality of the Brasilia Tapaculo.
  • Stops:
    • : The town at the park gate, three hours north of Brasilia. It is the base for the plateau.
    • : The quieter northern approach. Quilombola communities run the access to several of the waterfalls.
  • Also covers: Alto Paraiso de Goias, Brasilia National Park, Cavalcante, Chapada dos Veadeiros National Park
  • Also watch for: Maned Wolf and Giant Anteater are both on the Veadeiros grassland, and the plateau streams hold Giant Otter in places, which is a surprise this far from any large river.

Emas and the Goias plateau

Open grassland, termite mounds that glow at night, and the best mammals in the cerrado

Emas National Park is 132,000 hectares of open cerrado grassland surrounded by soya, and it is what the whole plateau looked like. The draw is the combination: the grassland tyrants and seedeaters by day, Giant Anteater and Maned Wolf on the open ground, and the bioluminescent termite mounds after dark.

  • Habitat: Emas National Park covers 130,000 hectares roughly halfway between Brasilia and Cuiaba and protects some of the best remaining cerrado in Brazil along with gallery forest. The rolling tree-dotted savanna grassland is somewhat reminiscent of Africa, and the park carries probably the best variety of large mammals in South America.
  • When: Best from April to December. Cock-tailed Tyrant, Streamer-tailed Tyrant and Greater Rhea stay through the year.
  • Getting there: The park entrance lies about 130 km southwest of Mineiros off the BR 359, a very long drive from Brasilia or Cuiaba, though it is possible to fly in from Goiania or Campo Grande. The walk beside the river inside the park is worth taking. Visiting and staying both need permission arranged in advance with the park authority, and anyone staying inside should bring all their own food.
  • Look for: Cock-tailed Tyrant, Black-masked Finch, Grey-backed Tachuri, Streamer-tailed Tyrant, Greater Rhea, Red-legged Seriema, Blue Finch, Collared Crescentchest
  • National park:
    • Emas National Park, 132,000 hectares of open cerrado on the Goias and Mato Grosso do Sul border. It has an internal road network and controlled entry.
  • Stops:
    • : The town the park is reached from, on the main highway. The guides and the permits are here.
    • : The smaller town on the park's southern side, closer to the grassland the anteaters use.
  • Also covers: Chapadao do Ceu, Emas National Park, Mineiros
  • Also watch for: Giant Anteater, Giant Armadillo and Maned Wolf are all more visible here than anywhere else in the cerrado, because the ground is open. The termite mounds carry click beetle larvae that light up in the first wet weeks and turn the plain green at night.

The Sao Francisco valley and Lapa Grande

Dry forest and limestone caves along the river that made the interior

The middle Sao Francisco runs through dry forest and limestone, a transition between cerrado and caatinga that holds birds from both and a few of its own. Lapa Grande State Park sits on the edge of Montes Claros with caves, dry forest and gallery forest in one place, and the valley works end to end with the Caraca sanctuary.

  • When: Good all year. Silvery-cheeked Antshrike, Spotted Piculet and Caatinga Cacholote stay through the year.
  • Look for: Blue-eyed Ground Dove, Great Xenops, Silvery-cheeked Antshrike, Spotted Piculet, Caatinga Cacholote, Stripe-backed Antbird, Broad-tipped Hermit, White-browed Guan
  • National park:
    • Cavernas do Peruacu National Park, Limestone canyon and caves on the Sao Francisco north of Januaria, with dry forest above and rock art inside.
  • Stops:
    • : 16,000 hectares of dry forest, caves and springs on the edge of Montes Claros. It is the water supply for the city.
    • : The city in the north of Minas Gerais, with the airport. It is the base for the valley.
    • : The river town upstream of the Peruacu canyon, and the way onto the water.
  • Also covers: Cavernas do Peruacu National Park, Jaiba, Januaria, Lapa Grande State Park, Montes Claros
  • Also watch for: This is the driest ground Maned Wolf regularly uses, and the limestone caves along the Peruacu hold rock art that is among the oldest in South America.

Chapada Diamantina

Flat-topped sandstone tablelands in central Bahia where three biomes meet

Chapada Diamantina National Park stands in the middle of Bahia as a block of tablelands and canyons, and it is where Atlantic Forest, caatinga and cerrado meet: 359 species are recorded inside it, 21 of them threatened. A single base gives you three faunas.

  • Habitat: Diamantina National Park protects a mountain range near Lencois. On its northern side around Morro do Chapeu there is low vegetation at the Cachoeira waterfall and roadside forest running south towards Utinga.
  • When: Good all year. Sincora Antwren, Broad-tipped Hermit and Caatinga Antwren are here in every month.
  • Getting there: Covering the range fully would take a major expedition, though some roads skirt the edge of the park. Cachoeira waterfall lies about 19 km east of Morro do Chapeu on the main road east, and people in the town will give precise directions.
  • Look for: Hyacinth Visorbearer, Diamantina Sabrewing, Sincora Antwren, Broad-tipped Hermit, Caatinga Antwren, Scarlet-throated Tanager, Pectoral Antwren, Grey-backed Tachuri
  • National park:
    • Chapada Diamantina National Park, 152,000 hectares of tableland, canyon and campo rupestre. It has 359 bird species recorded, 21 of them threatened.
  • Stops:
    • : The old diamond town on the eastern side, with an airstrip. Most visitors use it as their base.
    • : The flat-topped hill above the road, a short climb to the top. It gives the standard first view of the whole chapada.
    • : The town on the southern side, closer to the Sincora range and the antwren that carries its name.
  • Also covers: Chapada Diamantina National Park, Lencois, Morro do Pai Inacio, Mucuge, Palmeiras
  • Also watch for: Maned Wolf holds on the higher grassland and the canyons carry Brown Capuchin. The Pati valley is walked over several days and is the way most visitors see the interior of the park.

Boa Nova and the mata de cipo

Vine forest and cactus within a kilometre of each other, in southern Bahia

Boa Nova is where the Atlantic Forest and the caatinga meet, and the transition is abrupt enough to walk across in an afternoon: humid vine forest on one side of the ridge, cactus and thorn on the other. The draw is Slender Antbird, which lives in the vine forest and nowhere else.

  • Habitat: Caatinga sits west of the town and dry and wet forest to the east, and that variety is why Boa Nova carries more endemics per square kilometre than anywhere else in the country.
  • When: Good all year. Slender Antbird, Narrow-billed Antwren and Silvery-cheeked Antshrike hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Boa Nova is 230 km west of Ilheus by way of Ibicarai and Pocoes. Head north from Pocoes towards Jequie on the BR 116, then turn right for Boa Nova and Dario Meira. The village is 20 km further on, and the fazenda that takes birders lies a few kilometres northeast of it.
  • Look for: Slender Antbird, Narrow-billed Antwren, Silvery-cheeked Antshrike, Great Xenops, Spotted Piculet, Stripe-backed Antbird, Ochraceous Piculet, Scarlet-throated Tanager
  • National park:
    • Boa Nova National Park, 12,000 hectares declared in 2010 to protect the vine forest. Access runs along the old railway line and the roads out of town.
  • Stops:
    • : The small town the region is reached from, four hours inland of Ilheus, with the vine forest on one side and caatinga on the other.
    • : The disused railway grade running west out of town through the vine forest. It is the classic morning walk here.
  • Also covers: Boa Nova National Park, Estrada de Ferro, Manoel Vitorino
  • Also watch for: The mata de cipo is a vegetation type found only in this part of Bahia, and it is what makes the birding here unlike anywhere else above. Very little of it is protected.

Canudos and the Raso da Catarina

Sandstone canyons in the driest caatinga, and a macaw that roosts on the cliffs

The Raso da Catarina is the harshest caatinga there is, and Lear's Macaw roosts and nests on its sandstone cliffs. The Canudos Biological Station holds most of what is left of the species, and the birds leaving the roost at dawn are the reason to come to this corner of Bahia.

  • Habitat: Caatinga is thorny woodland and scrub built on cacti and terrestrial bromeliads, and burning, grazing and clearance have made it one of the most threatened habitats in South America. The red-rock escarpment of the Raso da Catarina holds the world's only population of Lear's Macaw.
  • When: Good all year. Lear's Macaw, Red-shouldered Spinetail and Caatinga Cacholote are here in every month.
  • Getting there: The macaws are near Canudos, also called Cocorobo, 320 km northwest of Aracaju on the rough road to Juazeiro, and seeing them is arranged through the local wardens.
  • Look for: Lear's Macaw, Caatinga Antwren, Scarlet-throated Tanager, Pygmy Nightjar, Red-shouldered Spinetail, Caatinga Cacholote, Spotted Piculet, White-browed Guan
  • Parks and reserves:
    • Canudos Biological Station (protected area), A reserve run by Fundacao Biodiversitas. It holds the main Lear's Macaw roost, visited at dawn by arrangement.
  • Stops:
    • Raso da Catarina Ecological Station : 99,000 hectares of the driest caatinga. It is a federal reserve closed to general visits and watched from its edges.
    • : The town rebuilt after the war of 1897. It has the only accommodation for a long way and is the base for the macaw roost.
  • Also covers: Canudos Biological Station, Jeremoabo, Paulo Afonso, Raso da Catarina Ecological Station
  • Also watch for: This is dry enough that the licuri palm the macaws feed on is the limiting factor for the whole population, so the birds and the palm groves are managed together.

The Ceara chapadas: Araripe and Ubajara

Wet forest islands standing above the dry north-east

Where the north-eastern chapadas rise high enough to catch rain they carry wet forest, and those forest islands sit in a sea of caatinga. The Araripe escarpment above Crato is one, and it holds the Araripe Manakin, described in 1996 and confined to a strip of springs at the foot of the cliff.

  • When: Good all year. Ochraceous Piculet, Great Xenops and Silvery-cheeked Antshrike hold the ground through the year.
  • Look for: Ochraceous Piculet, Great Xenops, Silvery-cheeked Antshrike, Yellow-faced Siskin, Broad-tipped Hermit, Pygmy Nightjar, White-browed Guan, Golden-capped Parakeet
  • National park:
    • Ubajara National Park, The smallest national park in the north-east, wet forest on the Ibiapaba escarpment. A cable car runs down to the caves.
  • Stops:
    • Araripe National Forest : The plateau forest above Crato and Barbalha. The escarpment springs below it are what the manakin depends on.
    • : The town at the foot of the Araripe escarpment. It is the base for the manakin sites.
  • Also covers: Araripe National Forest, Barbalha, Crato, Ubajara National Park
  • Also watch for: The Araripe Manakin's whole world is about 30 square kilometres of spring-fed forest below the escarpment, and those springs are also the water supply for Crato and Barbalha, which is the whole conservation problem in one sentence. Ubajara's wet forest holds Brown Capuchin and the caves below it a resident bat colony.

Pernambuco and the Alagoas brejos

The most threatened forest in the Americas, in fragments above the caatinga

North of the Sao Francisco the Atlantic Forest is in fragments, and the Pernambuco centre of endemism has lost more of its forest than anywhere else in the Americas. Murici in Alagoas is the largest surviving piece and holds birds found nowhere else. What survives is measured in hundreds of hectares.

  • Habitat: Forest inland from Maceio holds many of the northeastern endemics. The Pedra Talhada state park, 160 km west of the city, is a forested reserve carrying Pinto's Spinetail, Alagoas Foliage-gleaner, Alagoas Antwren, Alagoas Tyrannulet, Seven-coloured Tanager and Forbes's Blackbird, all of which are known from this site and only a few others. Excellent forest also stands north of the village of Bititinga.
  • When: Good all year. Orange-bellied Antwren, Seven-colored Tanager and Willis's Antbird hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Pedra Talhada is 25 km northeast of Quebrangulo, a small town 150 km west of Maceio. Side trails leave the main trail from the warden's house, and the best of them goes left at the first major crossing. For the forest inland of the Murici junction, take the rough track known as Usina Bititinga II, 10 km north of the junction on the BR 101, which runs west for 10 km to Bititinga, and from there a very rough track leads 11 km north.
  • Look for: Alagoas Antwren, Orange-bellied Antwren, Seven-colored Tanager, Willis's Antbird, White-collared Foliage-gleaner, Scarlet-throated Tanager, Yellow-faced Siskin, Golden-capped Parakeet
  • Parks and reserves: Estação Ecológica de Murici (protected area)
  • Stops:
    • Murici Ecological Station : 6,100 hectares in Alagoas, the largest remaining fragment of the Pernambuco centre of endemism. Several of these birds are confined to it.
    • : A private reserve in the Pernambuco hill forest at Jaqueira, in sugar-cane country. It is one of the few pieces with any protection.
    • : The coastal town south of Recife, with the Saltinho reserve inland of it and the mangrove behind the beach.
  • Also covers: Caruaru, Estação Ecológica de Murici, Frei Caneca Reserve, Murici Ecological Station, Tamandare
  • Also watch for: The brejos are hill forests fed by orographic rain standing above dry caatinga, which is why they hold Atlantic Forest species hundreds of kilometres from the coast. Almost all of them are unprotected.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to bird the cerrado and the caatinga?

September to November for the cerrado, when the first storms bring the whole plateau into breeding at once. June to August for the caatinga, when the trees are bare and Lear's Macaw at the Canudos roost is at its most visible. The two do not overlap neatly, so a trip usually picks one. The three seasons.

What is the difference between cerrado and caatinga?

The cerrado is savanna: fire-adapted grassland with twisted trees, on deep old soils, from Minas Gerais north to Tocantins. The caatinga is dry thorn scrub and cactus on shallow soil in the north-east, and it drops its leaves entirely in the dry season. Canudos takes 491 mm of rain a year and Alto Paraiso 1,662 mm, which is most of the story.

Is Brazil one page or four?

Four, because nobody books Brazil. The Pantanal, the Amazon, the Atlantic Forest and the cerrado and caatinga are different months, different airports and almost entirely different birds. This guide covers the dry interior; the other three link from the foot of it.

Where do I see Lear's Macaw?

At the Canudos Biological Station in northern Bahia, at dawn, from a viewpoint above the sandstone cliff the birds roost on. It is arranged in advance with the reserve, and almost the whole world population uses those cliffs. No trip to the caatinga leaves it out.

Do I need a guide?

For Canudos and the Raso da Catarina, yes: access is arranged with the reserve. For Boa Nova and the Pernambuco fragments, effectively yes, because the sites are unmarked and several are on private land. Canastra, Cipo, Veadeiros and Diamantina are national parks and can be birded from their own roads.

Why is the Araripe Manakin missing from the card wall?

Because the guide's range data carries no country code for it at all, and the wall is built from species coded to Brazil. It has a page and the link above works; it simply does not count towards the 1,790. Two hundred and fifty-five species are in the same position, mostly recent splits and genus renames, and it is worth knowing when a count looks lower than it should.

How much of this is protected?

Far less than the Amazon or the Atlantic Forest, and protection is thin across both biomes. About eight per cent of the cerrado is in protected areas against roughly half of it cleared for soya and cattle, the caatinga has less again, and the Pernambuco forest fragments are mostly unprotected private land.

Is there anywhere certified to stay?

No. Nothing in the Brazilian interior meets the certification rule this site applies. The listings here are a handful of places inside reserves or run by them, and each card says which. How a certification is judged.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
