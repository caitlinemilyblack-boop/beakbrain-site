Birding in Hawaii

Every native Hawaiian forest bird lives above the line mosquitoes cannot cross, which is roughly four thousand feet, and that one fact decides where a birder goes on all five islands.

Birds that decide the trip

Akiapolaau at Hakalau

A honeycreeper with a straight lower bill for hammering and a long curved upper one for probing, so it does the work of a woodpecker and a nuthatch at once. Hakalau is the reliable place for it.

When All year, in koa on the refuge weekend openings.

Where Hakalau and the Big Island forests

Palila on the Mauna Kea mamane

It eats mamane seed pods and lives only in the dry woodland on one side of one mountain, which is as narrow a range as any bird in the country has.

When All year, in mamane along the Mauna Kea access road.

Where Hakalau and the Big Island forests

Iiwi in flowering ohia

Scarlet with a long curved bill built for ohia flowers, and the bird most visitors remember. It has vanished from the low ground because malaria kills it, so the high forest is the only place left.

When All year, best when the ohia is in heavy flower from January.

Where Hakalau and the Big Island forests, Volcanoes and the Kau district, Haleakala and the Maui uplands

Akikiki in the Alakai

A small grey creeper that works ohia bark, down to a handful of birds in the wild on one plateau. Seeing one is unlikely and looking for one carefully is the point.

When All year, in the wettest Alakai forest.

Where Kokee and the Alakai on Kauai

Akohekohe on windward Haleakala

The crested honeycreeper, black with orange flecking and a tuft over the bill, aggressive at flowering ohia and confined to the windward rainforest of one volcano.

When All year, on guided access into Waikamoi.

Where Haleakala and the Maui uplands

Nene on the park lawns

The Hawaiian goose came back from about thirty birds and now grazes the lawns and the crater cinder in both national parks, which is the most visible conservation success in the islands.

When All year, on open ground in the parks.

Where Volcanoes and the Kau district, Haleakala and the Maui uplands

Laysan Albatross at Kilauea Point

They nest on the clifftop lawns and dance in pairs through the winter, which puts an albatross within a few metres of a car park. Kaena Point on Oahu has a smaller fenced colony.

When November to July, on the clifftop colonies.

Where Kokee and the Alakai on Kauai, Oahu: the uplands and the coast

Hawaiian Petrel over the crater

It nests in burrows high on Haleakala and comes and goes only at night, so the way to meet one is to stand on the crater rim after dark and listen for the calls overhead.

When March to October, after dark on the summit.

Where Haleakala and the Maui uplands, Volcanoes and the Kau district

When to Go

The forest birds are resident and there is no wrong month for them, which makes Hawaii unusual. What varies is the ohia bloom, which pulls honeycreepers to accessible flowering trees, and the seabird seasons, which are the only firm dates on this page.

The albatross season

November to July

Laysan Albatross hold the Kauai and Oahu colonies. They court from November and fly their young in July.

Go for Kilauea Point on Kauai, and Kaena Point on Oahu.

Trade off The north shore surf makes some coastal access difficult in midwinter.

The heaviest ohia bloom

January to June

Flowering ohia concentrates Apapane and Iiwi into trees a visitor can stand under.

Go for Hakalau, Kipuka Puaulu and Hosmer Grove.

Trade off The windward slopes are at their wettest and the unsealed roads are worst.

The petrel season

March to October

Hawaiian Petrels are back at the Haleakala crater burrows and calling over the summit at night.

Go for The Haleakala crater rim after dark, and the Kau coast for shearwaters.

Trade off The summit needs a booked slot and is genuinely cold before dawn.

The refuge winter

October to February

James Campbell opens to visitors and Pacific Golden Plovers are on every lawn in the islands.

Go for James Campbell on a guided visit, Kealia Pond, and Kawainui Marsh.

Trade off It is the wet season and the forest tracks are heavy going.

12 days

Four islands, four endemic sets

Honolulu (HNL) 2 nights, Hilo 4 nights, Kula 3 nights, Waimea on Kauai 3 nights, out of Lihue (LIH)

Each island has its own honeycreepers, so seeing the group properly means visiting four of them. Oahu for its amakihi and elepaio, the Big Island for Akiapolaau and Palila, Maui for Akohekohe, and Kauai for the Alakai birds. Book Hakalau and Waikamoi weeks ahead, and hire four-wheel drive on the Big Island.

Hakalau and the Big Island forests

Ohia and koa forest above four thousand feet, holding honeycreepers that exist on one island

Mosquitoes carry avian malaria and cannot survive the cold above about four thousand feet, which is why almost every surviving Hawaiian forest bird is now confined to high ground. Hakalau on the windward slope of Mauna Kea is the best of it, and the dry mamane forest higher up is the only place Palila still lives.

  • Habitat: Wet ohia and koa forest on the windward slope, dry mamane and naio woodland on the mountain above it, pasture and fenced restoration planting on the lower boundary.
  • When: All year for the resident forest birds. January to June is when the ohia flowers most heavily and the honeycreepers are easiest.
  • Getting there: Hilo or Kona are the airports and Hilo the base. Hakalau needs four-wheel drive on an unsealed road and is open at weekends only, and the Palila ground on Mauna Kea Access Road needs the same vehicle.
  • Base yourself in: Hilo
  • Look for: Apapane, Iiwi, Hawaii Amakihi, Hawaii Creeper, Akiapolaau, Hawaii Akepa, Palila, Omao, Hawaii Elepaio, Hawaiian Hawk, Short-eared Owl, Nene
  • Parks and reserves: Hakalau Forest National Wildlife Refuge, Mauna Kea Forest Reserve, Puʻu Waʻawaʻa Cooperative Game Management Area (Protected area), Kaʻū Forest Reserve
  • Also covers: Hakalau Forest National Wildlife Refuge, Kaʻū Forest Reserve, Kau Forest Reserve, Mauna Kea Forest Reserve, Puʻu Waʻawaʻa Cooperative Game Management Area, Puu Waawaa Wildlife Sanctuary
  • Also watch for: Feral pigs and cattle are the main threat to the forest understorey, and the refuge is fenced against them.

Volcanoes and the Kau district

Forest islands left standing between lava flows, reachable on a paved road

A kipuka is a patch of older forest that a lava flow went around, and Kipuka Puaulu in the park is one of the easiest places in the islands to see native forest birds without a permit or a four-wheel drive. The park runs from sea level to the summit of Mauna Loa.

  • Habitat: Wet and dry ohia forest, koa kipuka standing in lava, bare pahoehoe and aa flow, alpine desert on the summit, and coastal cliff.
  • When: All year. The park is open every day and the birds are resident, and dawn is best in the kipuka.
  • Getting there: Hilo is the airport, forty minutes away, and Volcano village the base. The park charges an entrance fee, and Kipuka Puaulu has a short loop trail from a small car park on Mauna Loa Road.
  • Base yourself in: Volcano
  • Look for: Apapane, Iiwi, Hawaii Amakihi, Omao, Hawaii Elepaio, Nene, Hawaiian Petrel, Newell's Shearwater, White-tailed Tropicbird, Hawaiian Hawk, Short-eared Owl, Pacific Golden Plover
  • Parks and reserves: Hawaii Volcanoes National Park Kahuku-Pōhue Parcel, Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park, Kaʻu Desert Wilderness (Protected area), Mauna Loa Wilderness (Protected area), Upper Waiākea Forest Reserve
  • Also covers: Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park, Hawaii Volcanoes National Park Kahuku-Pōhue Parcel, Kaʻu Desert Wilderness, Kipuka Puaulu, Mauna Loa Wilderness, Upper Waiākea Forest Reserve
  • Also watch for: Nene were brought back from 30 birds and now graze the park lawns, and the coast has nesting hawksbill turtles.

Kokee and the Alakai on Kauai

The wettest plateau on earth, holding the rarest honeycreepers left anywhere

The Alakai is a rain-soaked plateau on top of Kauai, and the last Akikiki and Akekee live in it. Both are down to very small numbers. Kokee State Park on its edge is where a visitor can reach that forest on foot, and Kilauea Point on the north shore is the seabird cliff.

  • Habitat: Montane rainforest and open bog on the Alakai plateau, ohia and koa on the Kokee ridges, and sea cliff with seabird colonies on the north shore.
  • When: All year for the forest birds, and November to July for the albatross at Kilauea Point.
  • Getting there: Lihue is the airport and Waimea the base. The Kokee road is paved to the lookouts, the Alakai boardwalk beyond it is often deep mud, and Kilauea Point is a paid refuge with limited opening hours.
  • Base yourself in: Waimea
  • Look for: Akikiki, Akekee, Puaiohi, Anianiau, Kauai Amakihi, Kauai Elepaio, Apapane, Iiwi, Laysan Albatross, Red-tailed Tropicbird, Red-footed Booby, Great Frigatebird
  • Parks and reserves: Kīlauea Point National Wildlife Refuge, Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary (Reserve), Na Pali-Kona Forest Reserve, Alakaʻi Wilderness Preserve, Kōkeʻe State Park (State or provincial park)

Haleakala and the Maui uplands

A ten thousand foot volcano with a rainforest on one flank and a crater on top

Haleakala climbs to ten thousand feet and its windward rainforest holds the Maui honeycreepers, Kiwikiu and Akohekohe among them, both of which live nowhere else. Hosmer Grove near the park entrance is the accessible edge of that forest, and Kealia Pond on the coast below is the wetland.

  • Habitat: Windward ohia rainforest, subalpine shrubland of mamane and pukiawe, bare cinder in the crater, and coastal saline pond and mudflat.
  • When: All year for the forest birds. The Hawaiian Petrels return to the crater burrows from March to October.
  • Getting there: Kahului is the airport and Kula the base. Haleakala charges a park fee and needs a booked slot for sunrise, Hosmer Grove is just inside the gate, and Waikamoi is entered only on a guided walk.
  • Base yourself in: Kula
  • Look for: Akohekohe, Maui Parrotbill, Maui Alauahio, Apapane, Iiwi, Nene, Hawaiian Petrel, Black-necked Stilt, Common Gallinule, Hawaiian Coot, Hawaiian Duck, Pacific Golden Plover
  • Parks and reserves: Keālia Pond National Wildlife Refuge, Haleakalā National Park, Haleakalā Wilderness (Protected area), Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary (Reserve), Koolau Forest Reserve
  • Also covers: Haleakala and Its Forests, Haleakalā National Park, Haleakalā Wilderness, Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary, Koolau Forest Reserve, Waikamoi Preserve
  • Also watch for: The crater holds silversword found nowhere else, and humpback whales fill the channel below from December to April.

Oahu: the uplands and the coast

The most visited island, still holding its own honeycreeper and four endangered waterbirds

Oahu has lost more than the other islands but keeps its own amakihi and elepaio in the mountain forest, and the lowland marshes at James Campbell and Kawainui hold the four endangered Hawaiian waterbirds within reach of the city. Kaena Point at the western tip is a fenced seabird reserve.

  • Habitat: Ridge and valley rainforest in the Koolau and Waianae ranges, coastal marsh and impoundment, dune and rocky point at the western tip.
  • When: All year. James Campbell opens to the public only from October to February, which is also the albatross season at Kaena.
  • Getting there: Honolulu is the airport and the base for everything. James Campbell is guided-visit only in its open season, and Kaena Point is a level two-mile walk in from either end with a predator fence at the reserve.
  • Base yourself in: Honolulu
  • Look for: Oahu Amakihi, Oahu Elepaio, Apapane, Black-necked Stilt, Hawaiian Coot, Common Gallinule, Hawaiian Duck, Laysan Albatross, Wedge-tailed Shearwater, Brown Booby, Great Frigatebird, Bristle-thighed Curlew
  • Parks and reserves: Pearl Harbor National Wildlife Refuge, U.S. Naval Base, Pearl Harbor (Protected area), Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary (Reserve), Round Top Forest Reserve, Honolulu Forest Reserve
  • Also covers: Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary, Honolulu Forest Reserve, James Campbell National Wildlife Refuge, Kaena Point State Park, Kawainui Marsh Regional Park, Pearl Harbor National Wildlife Refuge, Round Top Forest Reserve, U.S. Naval Base, Pearl Harbor, Uplands of Oahu
  • Also watch for: Kaena Point is fenced against predators and Hawaiian monk seals haul out on the beach inside it.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Hawaii?

Any month works for the forest birds, which are resident. January to June is marginally better because the ohia flowers most heavily then and the honeycreepers concentrate in it. November to July is the albatross season on Kauai and Oahu.

Why are all the native birds up mountains?

Introduced mosquitoes carry avian malaria, to which Hawaiian forest birds have almost no resistance, and mosquitoes cannot complete their cycle in the cold above roughly four thousand feet. That line is why every remaining forest bird site in the islands is high ground.

How do I visit Hakalau Forest?

On its public open weekends, with four-wheel drive: the access is a long unsealed road that an ordinary hire car will not manage and most rental agreements forbid. Check the refuge schedule before booking flights, because the openings are limited.

Can I see all the honeycreepers on one island?

No. Each island evolved its own set, so Oahu, the Big Island, Maui and Kauai each hold birds the others do not. A trip that wants the group properly needs four islands and about twelve days.

Which of these birds might I not see at all?

Akikiki and Akekee on Kauai are down to very small numbers and a sighting is unlikely. Kiwikiu on Maui is similar. That is worth knowing before the trip rather than after, and the guided access into Waikamoi is the honest best chance for the Maui birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
