At a glance

Best months
  • November to April for the whole country
  • March for quetzals at Montecristo before the upper forest closes in May
  • The wintering shorebird counts at Jiquilisco peak between December and February
Regions
Montecristo and the northern cloud forestThe volcanic chain: Cerro Verde and Santa AnaEl Imposible and the western hillsThe Pacific coast and the mangrove estuariesThe eastern highlands: Perquin and the Rio Sapo
Routes
The volcanoes and the coast4 daysThe classic west7 daysNorth to the cloud forest6 daysEnd to end11 days
Getting in
San Salvador (SAL) takes all the international flights and sits on the coast road rather than in the capital, which puts the Pacific reserves closer than the city is.
Getting around
Everything here sits within five hours of the airport on surfaced main roads, and a hired vehicle with clearance handles the park tracks, with Montecristo and El Imposible both needing a permit arranged before arrival.

When to Go

El Salvador runs one calendar. The dry season is November to April and the wet one May to October, and because the country has no Caribbean coast there is no half of it that behaves differently. Height is what varies instead: Montecristo is in cloud when the coast is clear.

Peak

November to April

The dry season across the whole country. The forest tracks are firm, the dry forest at El Imposible is open, and the wintering waders are on the Jiquilisco mudflats.

Go for Quetzals calling at Montecristo from March, the coastal counts at their fullest, and clear weather on the volcanic chain.

Trade off The busiest months, and the Montecristo upper forest closes to visitors in May.

May to July

The rains arrive and the country greens within a fortnight. Afternoon storms rather than all-day rain.

Go for Breeding activity everywhere, the lowest rates of the year, and the rivers running clear again.

Trade off The Montecristo cloud forest is closed for breeding from May to October, which is most of what the north offers.

Montecristo and the northern cloud forest

Cloud forest on the summit where three countries meet, holding birds that reach El Salvador nowhere else

Montecristo is the only real cloud forest left in the country, on the northern border where Guatemala and Honduras meet El Salvador. Resplendent Quetzal and Highland Guan are both here and nowhere else in the country, which is why the upper forest is closed for part of the year to let them breed.

  • Habitat: Montane cloud forest of oak, cypress and tree fern above a pine belt, on a limestone massif reaching 2,418 metres.
  • When: January to April, and the upper forest is closed from May to October for the breeding season. Quetzals are calling by March.
  • Getting there: Metapan is three hours northwest of San Salvador on the CA-12, and the park road climbs from there. The last stretch needs clearance and a permit arranged in advance.
  • Look for: Resplendent Quetzal, Highland Guan, Blue-and-white Mockingbird, Bushy-crested Jay, Spotted Wood-Quail, Green-breasted Mountaingem
  • National parks: Parque Nacional Montecristo, Area Natural Protegida San Diego y San Felipe Las Barras
  • Stops:
    • Los Planes and the summit trail : The campsite at 1,900 metres, with the trail to the tri-border point above it. The cloud forest birds are up there.
    • : The highest point in the country at 2,730 metres. A rough road climbs to it from La Palma through cypress and pine.
    • San Diego y San Felipe Las Barras : Dry forest and lake shore below Metapan. It is a different community entirely from the massif above it.
  • Visitor centres: Montecristo Visitor Centre, Los Planes
  • Also covers: Area Natural Protegida San Diego y San Felipe Las Barras, Citala, El Pital, La Palma, Los Planes and the summit trail, Metapan, Montecristo Visitor Centre, Los Planes, Parque Nacional Montecristo, Santa Ana
  • Also watch for: The Trifinio massif is a trinational biosphere reserve, and the Salvadoran side still holds spider monkeys and margay.

The volcanic chain: Cerro Verde and Santa Ana

Three volcanoes and a crater lake in one view, with cloud forest on the dormant cone between the two active ones

Cerro Verde is a dormant cone whose crater has filled with forest, and it stands in a chain between Santa Ana, the highest volcano in the country, and Izalco, which erupted continuously for two centuries. Lake Coatepeque fills a caldera below all three, and the forest at the top holds the highland birds within an hour of the city.

  • Habitat: Montane forest in a filled crater, with pine and oak on the surrounding cones, lava fields on Izalco and a caldera lake below.
  • When: November to April for the clearest cone weather. The Santa Ana summit walk goes in a group with a guide, and departures are morning only.
  • Getting there: An hour and a half west of San Salvador on the CA-8, then the park road up from El Congo. Buses run from Santa Ana city to the park gate.
  • Look for: Blue-and-white Mockingbird, Bushy-crested Jay, Turquoise-browed Motmot, Long-tailed Manakin, Collared Aracari, White-bellied Chachalaca
  • National park: Parque Nacional Los Volcanes
  • Stops:
    • Cerro Verde crater trail : A level loop inside the filled crater. It is the easiest highland forest walking in the country.
    • : A caldera lake below the cones, with a wooded shore. Wintering wildfowl sit on the water.
    • : A road through shade coffee between Sonsonate and Ahuachapan. Forest fragments and cloud run along its length.
  • Visitor centres: Cerro Verde Visitor Centre
  • Also covers: Cerro Verde crater trail, Cerro Verde Visitor Centre, El Congo, Juayua, Lake Coatepeque, Parque Nacional Los Volcanes, Santa Ana, Sonsonate, The Ruta de las Flores
  • Also watch for: The Apaneca-Ilamatepec range behind is the country's coffee heartland, and the shade plantations hold much of what the forest fragments do.

El Imposible and the western hills

The largest national park in the country, on a gorge road that gave it its name, holding the last big block of dry and premontane forest

El Imposible protects five thousand hectares on the Pacific slope of the western hills, which is the largest single block of forest left in El Salvador. The height range from 250 to 1,450 metres is what gives it the country's longest bird list, and the trails leave from two entrances on opposite sides.

  • Habitat: Premontane and dry tropical forest on a steep Pacific slope cut by eight rivers, from 250 to 1,450 metres.
  • When: November to April, when the dry forest is open and the tracks are firm. The park needs a permit and entry is arranged the day before.
  • Getting there: Two and a half hours west of San Salvador. The San Benito entrance is reached from Cara Sucia on the coast road and the Mixtepe entrance from Tacuba, and the two do not connect by vehicle.
  • Look for: King Vulture, Turquoise-browed Motmot, Long-tailed Manakin, Lesser Ground Cuckoo, Yellow-naped Amazon, White-throated Magpie-Jay
  • National park: Parque Nacional El Imposible
  • Stops:
    • San Benito entrance : The main way in from the coast road. The visitor centre is here and the mirador trails climb above it.
    • : The northern way in, higher and cooler. A coffee town on the ridge is the route through.
    • Barra de Santiago : A mangrove estuary and sand bar below the park. Work it by boat for herons and waders.
  • Visitor centres: El Imposible Visitor Centre, San Benito
  • Also covers: Ahuachapan, Apaneca, Barra de Santiago, Cara Sucia, El Imposible Visitor Centre, San Benito, Parque Nacional El Imposible, San Benito entrance, Tacuba and the Mixtepe entrance
  • Also watch for: The park holds the country's last confirmed puma population, and the eight rivers that rise in it supply much of western El Salvador.

The Pacific coast and the mangrove estuaries

The Jiquilisco bay, a Ramsar site and biosphere reserve holding the largest mangrove system in the country

Jiquilisco is a bay of islands and mangrove channels behind a long sand peninsula, and it is where the shorebird counts are done. Walter Deininger sits west along the coast road with dry forest above the beach, and the smaller estuaries between the two take waders on a falling tide.

  • Habitat: Mangrove channel and mudflat behind barrier beaches, with remnant coastal dry forest and salt pans on the plain behind.
  • When: October to March for the wintering waders and terns, which is the reason the bay is listed. The dry forest is most open from January.
  • Getting there: Puerto El Triunfo is two hours southeast of San Salvador and the bay is worked by boat from there. Walter Deininger is forty minutes from the capital on the coast road near La Libertad.
  • Look for: Roseate Spoonbill, Wood Stork, Boat-billed Heron, Bare-throated Tiger Heron, White-throated Magpie-Jay, Turquoise-browed Motmot
  • National parks: Bahia de Jiquilisco Biosphere Reserve, Parque Nacional Walter Thilo Deininger, Area Natural Protegida Nancuchiname
  • Stops:
    • Bahia de Jiquilisco : Mangrove channels and islands, worked by boat from Puerto El Triunfo. It is the main shorebird count site in the country.
    • Walter Thilo Deininger : Coastal dry forest above the beach, forty minutes from the capital. It has marked trails and a camping area.
    • Nancuchiname : Gallery forest on the lower Lempa river, one of the last stands of it. Reach it from the coast road.
  • Also covers: Area Natural Protegida Nancuchiname, Bahia de Jiquilisco Biosphere Reserve, La Libertad, Parque Nacional Walter Thilo Deininger, Puerto El Triunfo, Usulutan, Zacatecoluca
  • Also watch for: Hawksbill turtles nest on the Jiquilisco beaches in one of the largest remaining eastern Pacific populations, and the bay is a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

The eastern highlands: Perquin and the Rio Sapo

Pine and oak on the Honduran border in Morazan, the least visited corner of the country

The Rio Sapo rises in pine forest above Perquin and runs clear through a valley that was the heart of the guerrilla territory in the war. The forest here is oak and pine rather than cloud forest, and it holds a northern set the volcanic chain does not.

  • Habitat: Pine and oak forest on the eastern highlands at 1,000 to 1,600 metres, with clear river valleys cutting north towards the Honduran border.
  • When: November to April for the roads, which are unsurfaced beyond Perquin. The river is at its clearest late in the dry season.
  • Getting there: Perquin is four hours east of San Salvador through San Miguel, and the last hour is a mountain road. The Rio Sapo is reached on tracks from the town.
  • Look for: Bushy-crested Jay, Blue-and-white Mockingbird, Spotted Wood-Quail, Montezuma Oropendola, Collared Aracari, Long-tailed Manakin
  • National park: Area Natural Protegida Rio Sapo
  • Stops:
    • The Rio Sapo valley : Pine and gallery forest along a clear river. A community campsite sits at the crossing.
    • : A pine-forest town on the border road. It is the base for the whole eastern highland block.
    • : A coffee-and-pine massif above Gotera. A track climbs to the summit, with long views into Honduras.
  • Also covers: Arambala, Area Natural Protegida Rio Sapo, Cerro Cacahuatique, Perquin, San Francisco Gotera, San Miguel, The Rio Sapo valley
  • Also watch for: The Rio Sapo is one of the cleanest rivers in the country, and the valley is managed by the communities that returned to it after the war.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in El Salvador?

November to April, and the country runs one calendar rather than two because it has no Caribbean coast. March is the month for Montecristo, since quetzals are calling by then and the upper forest closes to visitors in May.

How many bird species does El Salvador have?

Around 570 species, which is the shortest list on the isthmus. The country is small, it has no Caribbean slope and it has lost most of its forest, so what remains is concentrated in a handful of protected blocks rather than spread across a landscape.

Is it worth a trip on its own?

For a week, yes, and it is easy in a way its neighbours are not: everything on this guide is within five hours of the airport on surfaced roads. For a longer trip it pairs naturally with Guatemala or Honduras, which share the highland avifauna and are a few hours away.

Do I need a permit?

For Montecristo and El Imposible, yes, and both are arranged in advance rather than at the gate. MARN issues them and the co-managers can help. Walter Deininger and the coastal reserves are walk-in.

Where do I see a quetzal?

Montecristo, and nowhere else in the country. The whole Salvadoran population is on that massif. Go between January and April, because the upper forest closes from May to October for the breeding season.

Is El Salvador safe for birding?

The security situation has changed markedly since 2022 and the reserves are rural and managed. As anywhere, check current government travel advice before booking, and note that most birding sites are reached with a guide or a co-manager rather than alone.

What happened to the forest?

Most of it was cleared for coffee and cotton in the twentieth century, and around two per cent of the original cover remains as closed forest. That is why El Imposible matters out of proportion to its size and why the shade coffee on the Ruta de las Flores carries as many birds as it does.

Is Jiquilisco worth the drive?

For waders and for the scale of it. The bay is the largest mangrove system on the Central American Pacific, a Ramsar site and a biosphere reserve, and the counts done there are the country's contribution to the flyway record.

Do I need four-wheel drive?

For the Montecristo park road, the Mixtepe entrance above Tacuba and the tracks in Morazan, yes. The main highways and the coast road are surfaced and a normal car handles them.

What field guide should I take?

Birds of Central America by Vallely and Dyer covers the whole isthmus in one volume and is the current standard. There is no guide to El Salvador alone.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
