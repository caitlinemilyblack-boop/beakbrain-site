Atlas / Kentucky

Birding in Kentucky

Kentucky has the longest cave system in the world, fifty thousand acres of reclaimed strip mine that now holds one of the larger Henslow's Sparrow populations anywhere, and a mountain on the Virginia line with red spruce on top of it.

Birds that decide the trip

Henslow's Sparrow on the reclaimed mines

Fifty thousand acres of former strip mine at Peabody are now managed as grassland, and they hold one of the larger populations anywhere of a bird that has been in long decline.

When May to July, from tall grass on still mornings.

Where The Ohio River wetlands

Cerulean Warbler on the gorge rim

It sings from the tops of tall hardwoods on the steep sides of the sandstone gorges, and the rim roads bring that canopy down to eye level.

When April to July, from rim roads above the gorges.

Where The Red River Gorge and Daniel Boone, Mammoth Cave and the karst country

Canada Warbler on Black Mountain

It breeds in wet thickets under northern hardwood near the summit of the highest ground in the state, and it occurs nowhere else in Kentucky in the breeding season.

When Late May to July, near the summit.

Where Black Mountain and the eastern coalfields

Northern Raven over Bad Branch

The state has very few nesting pairs and the cliff line above the Bad Branch gorge holds one of them, which is why the preserve is watched as closely as it is.

When All year, over the gorge cliffs.

Where Black Mountain and the eastern coalfields

Bald Eagle over the western lakes

The two reservoirs and the sloughs along the Ohio hold a large wintering population, and the shoreline drives put them in view without any walking.

When December to February, along the reservoir shorelines.

Where Land Between the Lakes, The Ohio River wetlands

Prothonotary Warbler in the sloughs

It nests in holes above standing water, and the cypress sloughs along the Ohio give it exactly the flooded timber it needs.

When April to July, over standing water.

Where The Ohio River wetlands, Land Between the Lakes

Short-eared Owl over the mine grassland

The reclaimed grassland at Peabody holds hunting owls through the winter. They work the fields on long floppy wings from mid-afternoon onwards.

When December to February, from mid-afternoon.

Where The Ohio River wetlands

Louisiana Waterthrush on a gorge creek

It needs a clear fast stream under closed canopy and sings from the water's edge before most other birds start, which the hemlock ravines below the arches provide in quantity.

When April to June, along shaded rocky streams.

Where The Red River Gorge and Daniel Boone, Mammoth Cave and the karst country

When to Go

Kentucky is a spring state. Late April into June gives the whole breeding list, and the western wetlands are the exception, holding their waterfowl through the winter when the forest has gone quiet.

The waterfowl winter

December to February

The western sloughs and reservoirs fill with geese, ducks and eagles.

Go for The Sloughs and Ballard areas, and the Land Between the Lakes shoreline.

Trade off The forest breeders are all gone and the mountain roads are icy.

Typical daily average

The spring passage

April

Migrants move up the river valleys and the first breeders arrive.

Go for Mammoth Cave and the Red River Gorge, and the lakes for late waterfowl.

Trade off The high mountain breeders have not settled yet.

Typical daily average

The breeding season

May to July

Every region here holds its full breeding list, from the gorge warblers to the mountain thrushes.

Go for The Red River Gorge, Black Mountain, and Peabody for the grassland birds.

Trade off It is hot and humid by June, and the gorge fills with climbers at weekends.

Typical daily average

The autumn passage

September and October

Migrants move back down the plateau and the first waterfowl reach the western lakes.

Go for The Daniel Boone ridges and the Ohio River sloughs.

Trade off It is a slower and more scattered movement than the spring.

8 days

The lakes to the mountains

Nashville (BNA), Cadiz 2 nights, Cave City 2 nights, Slade 2 nights, Cumberland 1 night, out of Lexington (LEX)

An open-jaw run east in the second half of May, from two enormous reservoirs to a mountain with red spruce on it. The peninsula and its bison first, then the karst and the Green River, the sandstone arches of the gorge, and a finish on the highest ground in the state.

  1. 1Red River Gorge Geological Area
  2. 2Daniel Boone National Forest
  3. 3Cumberland Falls State Resort Park

4 days

The Red River Gorge

Lexington (LEX), Slade 3 nights, back to Lexington

Three days in May among sandstone arches and hemlock ravines. Cerulean and Worm-eating Warbler on the ridge roads, Louisiana Waterthrush along the creeks below, and the whole thing free apart from petrol. Go on a weekday: the gorge is busy with climbers.

  1. 1Sloughs Wildlife Management Area
  2. 2Ballard Wildlife Management Area
  3. 3Peabody Wildlife Management Area

4 days

The western wetlands in winter

Evansville (EVV), Henderson 3 nights, back to Evansville

Three January days on cypress slough and reclaimed strip mine. The Sloughs and Ballard take the geese and eagles, and Peabody is fifty thousand acres of grassland with Short-eared Owls hunting it in the afternoons. Free access and gravel roads throughout.

  1. 1Black Mountain
  2. 2Kingdom Come State Park
  3. 3Bad Branch State Nature Preserve

4 days

Black Mountain and the coalfields

Lexington (LEX), Cumberland 3 nights, back to Lexington

Three days in late June on the highest ground in the state, for birds that breed nowhere else in it. Canada Warbler and Veery near the summit, ravens over the Bad Branch gorge, and a hemlock waterfall at the bottom of it. The summit road is rough.

Land Between the Lakes

A forty-mile peninsula between two very large reservoirs, with bison and elk on it

Two dams turned the Tennessee and Cumberland rivers into reservoirs and left a long forested Kentucky peninsula between them. The open water on both sides takes wintering waterfowl and eagles, and the restored prairie in the middle holds a bison and elk herd.

  • Habitat: Oak and hickory forest on a long peninsula, restored tallgrass prairie in the centre, two very large reservoirs with shallow bays, and bottomland along the creeks.
  • When: April to June for the breeding birds, and December to February for the eagles and waterfowl on the lakes.
  • Getting there: Nashville in Tennessee is the airport, ninety minutes south, and Cadiz the base. The recreation area charges a small day-use fee, has a paved road down its length, and the elk and bison prairie has a driving loop.
  • Base yourself in: Cadiz
  • Look for: Bald Eagle, American White Pelican, Snow Goose, Common Loon, Red-breasted Merganser, Horned Grebe, Prothonotary Warbler, Cerulean Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Summer Tanager, Barred Owl, Wild Turkey
  • Also covers: Kentucky Lake, Lake Barkley, Land Between the Lakes National Recreation Area
  • Also watch for: A bison and elk herd runs on the restored prairie in the middle of the peninsula, and the lakes carry a large osprey population.

The Ohio River wetlands

Cypress sloughs and reclaimed mine land along the Ohio, holding waterfowl and grassland birds together

The Sloughs and Ballard areas along the Ohio hold cypress swamp and flooded bottomland that take very large numbers of ducks and geese, and Peabody is fifty thousand acres of reclaimed strip mine now managed as grassland, which is where the state's grassland birds are.

  • Habitat: Cypress slough and flooded bottomland hardwood, moist-soil impoundment, reclaimed strip mine grassland and shallow ponds, and farmed refuge fields.
  • When: December to February for the waterfowl, and May to July for the grassland breeders on the reclaimed mines.
  • Getting there: Evansville in Indiana is the airport, thirty minutes north, and Henderson the base. All three areas are free with gravel roads, and parts close for the winter hunting season.
  • Base yourself in: Henderson
  • Look for: Snow Goose, Greater White-fronted Goose, Bald Eagle, Prothonotary Warbler, Anhinga, Wood Duck, Henslow's Sparrow, Grasshopper Sparrow, Dickcissel, Northern Harrier, Short-eared Owl, Least Bittern
  • Also covers: Ballard Wildlife Management Area, Peabody Wildlife Management Area, Sloughs Wildlife Management Area
  • Also watch for: The reclaimed mine grassland holds one of the larger Henslow's Sparrow populations anywhere, and the sloughs carry river otter.

Mammoth Cave and the karst country

The longest cave system in the world, with a river-bottom forest above it

Mammoth Cave is more than four hundred mapped miles of passage, and above ground the park protects the Green River and the hardwood forest on its bluffs. The karst limestone means sinkholes, springs and no surface streams over much of it.

  • Habitat: Oak and hickory forest on limestone bluffs, sinkhole plain with no surface streams, river bottomland along the Green, and cave passage underground.
  • When: April to June for the breeding birds. The park is busiest in summer for the cave tours rather than for the surface.
  • Getting there: Nashville in Tennessee or Louisville are the airports and Cave City the base. The park is free to enter, cave tours are booked separately, and the surface trails along the Green River are quiet even in high season.
  • Base yourself in: Cave City
  • Look for: Cerulean Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Worm-eating Warbler, Louisiana Waterthrush, Acadian Flycatcher, Hooded Warbler, Wood Thrush, Scarlet Tanager, Yellow-throated Warbler, Barred Owl, Pileated Woodpecker, Wild Turkey
  • Also covers: Barren River Lake, Green River, Mammoth Cave National Park
  • Also watch for: The Kentucky cave holds eyeless fish and shrimp found nowhere else, and the park has one of the larger Indiana bat hibernation sites.

The Red River Gorge and Daniel Boone

Sandstone arches and cliffs cut into the western edge of the Cumberland Plateau

The Red River Gorge has more than a hundred natural sandstone arches and long cliff lines, with hemlock and rhododendron in the cool ravines below them. The forest around it runs the length of the plateau edge and holds the state's fullest woodland breeding list.

  • Habitat: Sandstone cliff, arch and rockhouse, hemlock and rhododendron in the ravines, oak and pine on the ridges, and clear gravel-bed rivers.
  • When: Late April to June for the breeding birds. The gorge is busy with climbers and hikers at weekends from April onwards.
  • Getting there: Lexington is the airport, an hour west, and Slade the base. The national forest is free with a scenic byway through the gorge, and Cumberland Falls charges nothing to visit the waterfall.
  • Base yourself in: Slade
  • Look for: Cerulean Warbler, Worm-eating Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Hooded Warbler, Louisiana Waterthrush, Acadian Flycatcher, Black-and-white Warbler, Ovenbird, Wood Thrush, Ruffed Grouse, Barred Owl, Eastern Whip-poor-will
  • Also covers: Cumberland Falls State Resort Park, Daniel Boone National Forest, Red River Gorge Geological Area
  • Also watch for: Black bear have recolonised the Kentucky gorge from the east, and the cliff lines hold rare ferns and the Virginia big-eared bat.

Black Mountain and the coalfields

The highest ground in the state, holding a northern breeding list at four thousand feet

Black Mountain reaches 4,145 feet on the Kentucky and Virginia line, and its upper slopes carry northern hardwood and a handful of red spruce, which is enough for breeding birds that occur nowhere else in the state. Bad Branch below it is a hemlock gorge with a waterfall and the state's only nesting ravens.

  • Habitat: Northern hardwood with scattered red spruce near the summit, hemlock and rhododendron gorge, mixed mesophytic forest on the slopes, and reclaimed surface mine on the ridges.
  • When: Late May to July, when the high-elevation breeders are singing. The summit road is rough and the weather turns fast.
  • Getting there: Knoxville in Tennessee or Lexington are the airports and Cumberland the base. Kingdom Come and Bad Branch are free state areas, and the Black Mountain summit is reached on a rough road with permission needed for the very top.
  • Base yourself in: Cumberland
  • Look for: Northern Raven, Blackburnian Warbler, Canada Warbler, Black-throated Blue Warbler, Chestnut-sided Warbler, Veery, Winter Wren, Golden-crowned Kinglet, Ruffed Grouse, Northern Saw-whet Owl, Ovenbird, Wood Thrush
  • Also covers: Bad Branch State Nature Preserve, Black Mountain, Kingdom Come State Park
  • Also watch for: The gorge at Bad Branch holds rare plants left from the ice age, and black bear are common through the coalfields.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Kentucky?

The second half of May, when every region holds its full breeding list at once, from the sandstone gorge warblers to the Black Mountain thrushes. January is the alternative, for waterfowl and eagles on the western lakes and sloughs.

Where do I find Henslow's Sparrow?

Peabody Wildlife Management Area, fifty thousand acres of reclaimed strip mine now managed as grassland. It holds one of the larger populations anywhere of a bird in long decline, which is an unexpected outcome for surface mining.

Which birds breed on Black Mountain and nowhere else in the state?

Canada Warbler, Veery, Winter Wren, Golden-crowned Kinglet and Northern Saw-whet Owl all reach their Kentucky limit on its upper slopes, which carry northern hardwood and a handful of red spruce at over four thousand feet.

Is the Red River Gorge crowded?

At weekends from April onwards, yes: it is one of the best-known rock climbing areas in the country. Go on a weekday, start early, and the ridge roads above the gorge are quiet even when the trailheads are full.

Do I need to book cave tours at Mammoth Cave?

For the cave itself, yes, and well ahead in summer. For the birds you do not need to go underground at all: the surface trails along the Green River and the bluff forest above it are free and much less visited.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
