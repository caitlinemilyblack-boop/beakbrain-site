Atlas / Oklahoma

Birding in Oklahoma

Oklahoma runs from a lava-capped mesa where the panhandle almost touches four other states to oak and pine hills that get twice the rain, and the largest protected tallgrass prairie left anywhere sits in between.

At a glance

Best months
  • April to June for the breeding birds everywhere, late March and April for the prairie chicken leks
  • October and November for the crane passage at Salt Plains

Oklahoma City (OKC) for the centre and the west, Tulsa (TUL) for the tallgrass and the east, and Amarillo in Texas for Black Mesa
Getting around
A hire car and long distances: Boise City to Poteau is 600 miles, which crosses the whole state and about four different landscapes

Birds that decide the trip

Greater Prairie Chicken on the Osage tallgrass

Forty thousand acres of unploughed prairie, burned in patches and grazed by bison, still carries booming grounds that the birds return to every spring.

When Late March and April, at dawn.

Where The tallgrass prairie

Snowy Plover on the salt crust

It nests on thirty square miles of bare salt left by an evaporated sea. No vegetation grows there and no other nesting bird will use it.

When May to July, on the salt flat.

Where Salt Plains and the north-west

Pinyon Jay on Black Mesa

Noisy flocks work the pinyon and juniper on the Oklahoma mesa slopes at the eastern edge of the range, in a corner of the state that has more in common with New Mexico than with Tulsa.

When All year, on the mesa slopes.

Where Black Mesa and the panhandle

Painted Bunting in the oak draws

The state is close to the centre of its range, and males sing from oak and brush edges in the Wichita draws where the grass runs up against the rock.

When May to July, from brushy edges.

Where The Wichita Mountains, The eastern forests

Whooping Crane at Salt Plains

The Aransas flock crosses the state twice a year and the salt flats are a traditional stop, which puts a bird of a few hundred individuals within reach of a refuge road.

When October and November, and again in late March.

Where Salt Plains and the north-west

Scissor-tailed Flycatcher on a wire

The state bird. On any May roadside it sits on the fence wires with a tail twice the length of its body, twisting in flight to take insects.

When April to September, on roadside wires and fence posts.

Where Salt Plains and the north-west, The tallgrass prairie

Red-cockaded Woodpecker in the Ouachita pine

The shortleaf pine and bluestem restoration in the Oklahoma part of the Ouachitas holds a small managed population at the western edge of the species range.

When April to June, at first light around the marked clusters.

Where The eastern forests

Upland Sandpiper on a fence post

A wader that lives in dry grass rather than on water. It calls a long bubbling whistle from fence posts, and the Osage prairie holds it in good numbers.

When May to July, on the open prairie.

Where The tallgrass prairie

When to Go

The Oklahoma season is spring, and it is short before the heat arrives. April and May carry the leks, the breeding birds and the last of the passage at once, and by July the western half of the state is too hot to work after mid-morning.

The lek season

Late March and April

Greater Prairie Chickens boom on the Osage tallgrass before the grass has greened.

Go for The Tallgrass Prairie Preserve at dawn, and Salt Plains for the first cranes.

Trade off The forest breeders have not arrived and the panhandle is still cold.

Typical daily average

The breeding season

April to June

All five Oklahoma regions hold their full breeding lists at the same time.

Go for The Wichitas, Black Mesa, the Ouachitas and the tallgrass.

Trade off The state is 600 miles across, so covering all of it takes real driving.

Typical daily average

The tern and plover season

May to July

Snowy Plover and Least Tern nest on the bare salt crust at the Great Salt Plains.

Go for Salt Plains refuge from the open areas, with the nesting ground closed.

Trade off It is intensely hot and there is no shade at all on the flat.

Typical daily average

The crane passage

October and November

Sandhill Cranes move through the west and Whooping Cranes stop at Salt Plains.

Go for Salt Plains and the western reservoirs.

Trade off The breeding birds have gone and the eastern forest is quiet.

The tallgrass leks

Tulsa (TUL), Pawhuska 3 nights, back to Tulsa

Three April dawns on the largest protected tallgrass left anywhere, with two and a half thousand bison grazing it. Prairie chickens boom on traditional leks, Upland Sandpipers call from fence posts, and the burns running through the same weeks are worth seeing themselves.

The Wichita Mountains

Granite domes standing out of mixed-grass prairie, with bison and free-ranging longhorn on it

The Wichitas are ancient granite worn down to rounded domes and boulder fields, sticking out of prairie in the south-west of the state. The refuge around them has held bison since 1907, and its mix of rock, oak cross timbers and grass carries a bird list that belongs to none of its neighbours.

  • Habitat: Granite dome and boulder field, mixed-grass prairie between the ridges, oak cross timbers in the draws, and small lakes and creeks.
  • When: April to June for the breeding birds. It is very hot from July, and the refuge roads are open all year.
  • Getting there: Oklahoma City is the airport, ninety minutes north, and Lawton the base. The refuge is free with a paved road to the top of Mount Scott, and parts close when the bison are being handled.
  • Base yourself in: Lawton
  • Look for: Rock Wren, Canyon Wren, Say's Phoebe, Golden Eagle, Prairie Falcon, Wild Turkey, Greater Roadrunner, Painted Bunting, Blue Grosbeak, Bell's Vireo, Bewick's Wren, Lark Sparrow
  • Also covers: Charons Garden Wilderness, Mount Scott, Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge
  • Also watch for: Bison, Rocky Mountain elk and a free-ranging longhorn herd all use the Oklahoma refuge, and prairie dog towns sit beside the road.

The Great Salt Plains

A bare salt flat left by an inland sea, with terns and plovers nesting on it

The Great Salt Plains is thirty square miles of bare salt crust left where an ancient sea evaporated. Snowy Plover and Least Tern nest on it because no vegetation will grow there. Whooping Cranes stop on the same refuge on passage.

  • Habitat: Bare salt flat and shallow saline lake, mudflat and alkali marsh, mixed-grass prairie and sand sage, and cottonwood along the Salt Fork.
  • When: April to June for the nesting terns and plovers, and October and November for the crane passage.
  • Getting there: Oklahoma City or Wichita in Kansas are the airports and Jet the base. The refuge has gravel roads and a free salt flat area open from April to mid-October, and the nesting areas are closed through the summer.
  • Base yourself in: Jet
  • Look for: Snowy Plover, Least Tern, American Avocet, Whooping Crane, Sandhill Crane, Franklin's Gull, White-faced Ibis, Snow Goose, Mississippi Kite, Scissor-tailed Flycatcher, Burrowing Owl, Cassin's Sparrow
  • Also covers: Great Salt Plains State Park, Optima National Wildlife Refuge, Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge
  • Also watch for: Visitors can dig for selenite crystals on the flat, which form nowhere else in this shape, and the refuge holds a large deer herd.

Black Mesa and the panhandle

The highest point in the state, on a lava-capped mesa where the Rockies reach out onto the plains

Black Mesa is a basalt flow at the far end of the panhandle, closer to four other states than to most of Oklahoma, and it carries pinyon, juniper and a western bird list found nowhere else in the state. Its species list reads like New Mexico.

  • Habitat: Basalt-capped mesa with pinyon and juniper on the slopes, shortgrass prairie and sand sage below, and cottonwood along the Cimarron.
  • When: Late April to June for the breeding birds. Summer is very hot and the panhandle has almost no shade.
  • Getting there: Amarillo in Texas is the nearest airport, two and a half hours south, and Boise City the base. The nature preserve has a four-mile trail to the summit and the state park has a small campground, and services are very thin.
  • Base yourself in: Boise City
  • Look for: Pinyon Jay, Juniper Titmouse, Rock Wren, Canyon Wren, Say's Phoebe, Golden Eagle, Prairie Falcon, Scaled Quail, Curve-billed Thrasher, Black-throated Sparrow, Lark Bunting, Cassin's Sparrow
  • Also covers: Black Mesa Nature Preserve, Black Mesa State Park, Cimarron County
  • Also watch for: Pronghorn range the shortgrass around the mesa, and the preserve holds dinosaur trackways in the creek beds.

The eastern forests

Oak and pine hills in the wet corner of the state, with eastern birds that reach no further west

The eastern third of Oklahoma gets twice the rain of the panhandle, and its Ouachita and Ozark hills carry hardwood and shortleaf pine with an eastern breeding list. Sequoyah refuge on the Arkansas River takes the waterfowl.

  • Habitat: Shortleaf pine and bluestem on the Oklahoma ridges, oak and hickory on the north slopes, limestone cave country in the Ozark corner, and bottomland and open water on the Arkansas River.
  • When: April to June for the breeding birds, and November to February for the waterfowl and eagles at Sequoyah.
  • Getting there: Fort Smith in Arkansas or Tulsa are the airports and Poteau the base. The forest roads are free and gravel, the Ozark refuge caves are closed to entry, and Sequoyah has an auto tour.
  • Base yourself in: Poteau
  • Look for: Red-cockaded Woodpecker, Brown-headed Nuthatch, Prairie Warbler, Worm-eating Warbler, Kentucky Warbler, Louisiana Waterthrush, Acadian Flycatcher, Painted Bunting, Bald Eagle, Snow Goose, Wood Duck, Barred Owl
  • Also covers: Ouachita National Forest, Ozark Plateau National Wildlife Refuge, Sequoyah National Wildlife Refuge
  • Also watch for: The Ozark refuge protects caves holding several endangered bat species, and black bear have returned to the Ouachitas.

The tallgrass prairie

Forty thousand acres of unploughed tallgrass with bison on it and prairie chickens booming in April

The Osage hills hold the largest protected tallgrass prairie left anywhere, burned and grazed by a free-ranging bison herd to keep it as prairie. Greater Prairie Chicken lek on it, and the grassland songbird list is the fullest in the state.

  • Habitat: Unploughed tallgrass prairie on rolling hills, burned in patches and grazed by bison, with gallery woodland in the draws and shallow reservoirs.
  • When: Late March and April at dawn for the prairie chicken leks, and May to July for the grassland breeders.
  • Getting there: Tulsa is the airport, an hour south-east, and Pawhuska the base. The preserve has a free gravel road through the bison range with a visitor centre, and vehicles must give way to the herd.
  • Base yourself in: Pawhuska
  • Look for: Greater Prairie Chicken, Upland Sandpiper, Dickcissel, Grasshopper Sparrow, Henslow's Sparrow, Eastern Meadowlark, Bobolink, Northern Bobwhite, Scissor-tailed Flycatcher, Bell's Vireo, Short-eared Owl, Northern Harrier
  • Also covers: Hulah Wildlife Management Area, Osage County, Tallgrass Prairie Preserve
  • Also watch for: The bison herd runs to about two and a half thousand animals and grazes the burns, which is what keeps the prairie in condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Oklahoma?

The second half of May, when all five regions hold their breeding birds at once. April is better if the prairie chicken leks are the point, and October and November for the crane passage through the west.

What is the Great Salt Plains?

Thirty square miles of bare salt crust left where an ancient sea evaporated. Snowy Plover and Least Tern nest on it because nothing grows there, and visitors can dig for selenite crystals on part of the flat from April to mid-October.

Is Black Mesa worth the drive?

If you want a western bird list, yes. It is closer to four other states than to most of Oklahoma, carries pinyon and juniper, and holds Pinyon Jay, Juniper Titmouse and Scaled Quail that reach nowhere else in the state. It is a long way from anything.

Can I drive through the bison herd at the Tallgrass Prairie?

Yes, on the gravel road through the preserve, and the animals have right of way. About two and a half thousand of them graze the burns, which is the management that keeps the prairie as prairie rather than letting it turn to scrub.

How different is the eastern third of the state?

Completely. It gets roughly twice the rainfall of the panhandle, and its Ouachita and Ozark hills carry hardwood, shortleaf pine, limestone caves and an eastern breeding bird list. The two ends of Oklahoma have almost nothing in common.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
