Birds that decide the trip

Cyprus Wheatear in the Troodos

Cyprus Wheatear breeds on the island and nowhere else, and winters in Ethiopia and Sudan. It uses open rocky ground and forest clearings through the Troodos and is easy to find once the birds are back.

When March to September, and the whole population leaves the island for the winter.

Where Troodos, Mount Olympus

Cyprus Warbler in the phrygana

The second Cyprus endemic lives in low phrygana scrub across the island, and a proportion of the population stays through the winter rather than crossing to the Middle East.

When Present year round, and most obvious from February when the males begin singing.

Where Cape Greco, Akamas Peninsula, Akrotiri Salt Lake

Greater Flamingo on the salt lakes

Both the Larnaca and Akrotiri salt lakes fill in winter and take Greater Flamingo in thousands. Both dry out completely through the summer, so the season is fixed by the water rather than by the birds.

When November to March, while the lakes hold water.

Where Larnaca Salt Lake, Akrotiri Salt Lake

Eleonora's Falcon over the headlands

Eleonora's Falcon breeds late in the year so that its young are fed on autumn migrant songbirds. The Cypriot headlands are where they hunt them, and the birds are most visible over Cape Greco and the sea cliffs.

When April to October, with the breeding season running late into the autumn.

Where Cape Greco, Akamas Peninsula

Demoiselle Crane over the Karpaz

Cranes cross Cyprus on passage between the Middle East and their breeding grounds, and the Karpaz peninsula channels them. Visible migration along the peninsula in spring includes cranes, herons, egrets and ibis moving northeast and out to sea.

When Late March to early May, and again in autumn on a wider front.

Where Karpaz Peninsula, Dipkarpaz

Masked Shrike in the olive groves

Masked Shrike breeds through the wooded and cultivated parts of the island and is one of the easier summer visitors to find. It hunts from exposed perches in olive groves, orchards and open woodland.

When April to September, and it winters in northeast Africa.

Where Troodos, Cape Greco, Paphos

Black Francolin on the farmland edges

Black Francolin is widespread on Cypriot farmland and scrub and is heard far more often than seen. The call carries a long way across open ground in the early morning.

When Present year round, and calling most through spring.

Where Paphos, Asprokremmos Dam, Kyrenia

Spur-winged Lapwing on the wetlands

Spur-winged Lapwing has spread through the Cypriot wetlands and is now a common breeder on the marshes and lake margins, where it is noisy and easy to see.

When Mainly March to September, with some birds staying through the winter.

Where Oroklini Marsh, Akrotiri Salt Lake, Zakaki Marsh

When to Go

Cyprus works on water. The salt lakes fill with the autumn rains and dry out completely by midsummer, so the wetland season and the migration season only partly overlap, and the Troodos stays usable when the lowlands are too hot.

Spring passage

March to May

Migrants cross the island from the south and funnel along the headlands and the Karpaz.

Go for Visible migration along both coasts, both endemics back and breeding, and the mountain flowers at their best.

Trade off The salt lakes are already dropping by May, and the flamingos have largely gone.

Typical daily average

Summer

June to August

The lowlands are hot and the wetlands dry, and the mountains are where the birding is.

Go for The Troodos stays cool and holds both endemics, and Eleonora's Falcons are hunting the coasts.

Trade off The salt lakes are bare, and the middle of the day on the coast is unusable.

Typical daily average

Autumn and winter

September to February

Autumn passage runs on a broad front, then the rains refill the lakes and the winter birds arrive.

Go for Flamingos in thousands on both salt lakes, wintering waterfowl and waders, and thrushes through the Karpaz.

Trade off Autumn migration is less concentrated than spring, and the mountain roads can close in snow.

Akrotiri and the Limassol salt lakes

Greater Flamingos in thousands on a lake that dries every summer

The Akrotiri peninsula holds the largest wetland in Cyprus, and the salt lake behind it fills each winter with Greater Flamingo. The reedbeds and marshes around its edge take migrants in both passages, and the whole peninsula funnels raptors and passerines moving through the island.

  • Habitat: A seasonal salt lake that dries out completely by late summer, ringed by reedbed, marsh, eucalyptus plantation and citrus groves. The gravel spit at Ladies Mile carries beach and open shingle.
  • When: November to March for the flamingos and wintering waterfowl, and March to April for spring passage. The lake is dry and largely birdless from July to September.
  • Getting there: Limassol is the base and Larnaca and Paphos airports are each about an hour away. The Ladies Mile track runs south from Zakaki along the lake shore, and the Phasouri reedbeds are reached from the Kolossi road.
  • Base yourself in: Limassol
  • Look for: Greater Flamingo, Black-winged Stilt, Spur-winged Lapwing, Glossy Ibis, Squacco Heron, Little Bittern, Pallid Harrier, Red-footed Falcon, Eleonora's Falcon, Great Spotted Cuckoo, Cyprus Wheatear, Cyprus Warbler
  • National park: Akrotiri Wetlands Ramsar Site
  • Also covers: Akrotiri Salt Lake, Akrotiri Wetlands Ramsar Site, Ladies Mile, Limassol, Phasouri Reedbeds, Zakaki Marsh
  • Also watch for: Green and loggerhead turtles nest on the sandy beaches, and the fox is common but almost entirely nocturnal.

The Troodos mountains

Two endemic birds and a pine forest that stays cool through the summer

The Troodos rises to nearly 2,000 metres and carries black pine and cedar forest that holds both Cyprus endemics. Cyprus Wheatear breeds through the open ground and Cyprus Coal Tit and the endemic jay forms live in the pines.

  • Habitat: Black pine and golden oak forest on igneous rock, with cedar in the Cedar Valley and open rocky ground above the treeline on Mount Olympus. Orchids are abundant on the picnic-area slopes in spring.
  • When: April to June, when the endemics are breeding and the flowers are out. Winter brings snow to the summit and the roads can close.
  • Getting there: Platres and Troodos village are the mountain bases, about an hour from Limassol on the B8. Mount Olympus is reached by the road above Troodos square, and Cedar Valley is a rough forest road best driven slowly.
  • Base yourself in: Platres
  • Look for: Cyprus Wheatear, Cyprus Warbler, Coal Tit, Eurasian Jay, Short-toed Treecreeper, Alpine Swift, Peregrine Falcon, Bonelli's Eagle, Red Crossbill, Masked Shrike
  • National park: Troodos National Forest Park
  • Also covers: Cedar Valley, Kykkos, Mount Olympus, Platres, Troodos National Forest Park
  • Also watch for: The Cyprus mouflon survives in the high Troodos, and the site guide puts the population at around 200 animals. Troodos lizards live among the old monastery ruins.

Cape Greco and the southeast

A headland that has produced the island's only Bateleur, among much else

Cape Greco is the southeastern corner of the island and migrants funnel onto it from both directions. The records here include a Bateleur that had been seen eight days earlier at the other end of the island, which is a fair measure of what the headland can produce.

  • Habitat: Low limestone headland with sea cliff, phrygana scrub and juniper, backed by the seasonal Paralimni Lake and open farmland.
  • When: March to May and September to November for the passage, which is what this region is for. Winter holds thrushes, Black Redstart and Stonechat in numbers.
  • Getting there: Larnaca airport is about forty minutes away and Ayia Napa is the nearest town. The headland is reached by the coast road east from Ayia Napa, and the sea caves car park is the usual starting point.
  • Base yourself in: Ayia Napa
  • Look for: Eleonora's Falcon, Red-footed Falcon, Pallid Harrier, European Roller, European Bee-eater, Masked Shrike, Cyprus Warbler, Isabelline Wheatear, Black-headed Bunting, Cretzschmar's Bunting, Rüppell's Warbler, Blue Rock Thrush
  • National park: Cape Greco National Forest Park
  • Also covers: Ayia Napa, Cape Greco National Forest Park, Paralimni Lake, Protaras
  • Also watch for: Green sea turtles are visible offshore from mid May as they gather before coming ashore to lay.

The Karpaz peninsula

A long finger of land that channels everything moving along the island

The Karpaz runs northeast for eighty kilometres and works as a migration corridor along its whole length. Visible migration is common here in spring, with flocks of herons, egrets and ibis passing along the shore and then heading out to sea to the northeast.

  • Habitat: Open phrygana and maquis on limestone, with sand dune and beach along both coasts and small seasonal wetlands. Farmland and olive groves fill the wider middle section.
  • When: Late March to early May for spring passage, which is the strongest season here. Autumn passage runs from late August to October and is less concentrated.
  • Getting there: Ercan is the nearest airport in the north. The peninsula road runs from Yeni Erenkoy through Dipkarpaz to the monastery at Cape Apostolos Andreas, and the last stretch is slow.
  • Base yourself in: Dipkarpaz
  • Look for: Audouin's Gull, Scopoli's Shearwater, Greater Sand Plover, Black-headed Bunting, Cretzschmar's Bunting, Isabelline Wheatear, Masked Shrike, European Roller, Pallid Harrier, Demoiselle Crane, Glossy Ibis, Squacco Heron
  • National park: Karpaz National Park
  • Also covers: Cape Apostolos Andreas, Dipkarpaz, Karpaz National Park, Karpaz Peninsula, Yeni Erenkoy
  • Also watch for: Wild donkeys roam the peninsula, and green turtles nest on the northern beaches.

The Kyrenia range and the northern coast

A limestone ridge above the sea, with raptors riding it in spring

The Kyrenia range runs along the northern coast as a narrow limestone wall, and spring raptor passage rides the ridge lift along it. The castles on the crags give the height to watch from without a climb.

  • Habitat: Steep limestone ridge with pine and cypress, dropping to a narrow coastal plain with citrus and carob. Alagadi on the coast is a nesting beach.
  • When: March to May for raptor passage and the breeding birds of the ridge. Summer is hot and quiet, and winter brings passerines to the coastal plain.
  • Getting there: Ercan airport is about forty minutes from Kyrenia. St Hilarion sits above the pass on the Nicosia road, and the coast road east reaches Alagadi in about twenty minutes.
  • Base yourself in: Kyrenia
  • Look for: Bonelli's Eagle, Long-legged Buzzard, Peregrine Falcon, Alpine Swift, Blue Rock Thrush, Cyprus Wheatear, Sardinian Warbler, Chukar Partridge, Black Francolin, Eurasian Crag Martin
  • National park: Alagadi Turtle Beach protected area
  • Also covers: Alagadi Turtle Beach protected area, Besparmak, Kyrenia, St Hilarion
  • Also watch for: Loggerhead and green turtles nest at Alagadi, which is monitored through the summer.

Paphos and the western headlands

Reservoirs, a wild peninsula and the driest corner of the island

The west is drier and less developed than the rest of Cyprus, and the Akamas peninsula at its tip is largely undeveloped scrub and coast. The reservoirs inland hold wintering waterfowl and draw raptors, and Paphos headland takes migrants.

  • Habitat: Phrygana and maquis on the Akamas with sea cliff and sandy bays, and open juniper scrub inland. Asprokremmos is a large reservoir in dry farmland and river valley.
  • When: March to May for passage and the breeding scrub birds, and November to February for the reservoir waterfowl. Summer is hot and largely quiet.
  • Getting there: Paphos has an international airport. The Akamas is reached through Polis and Neo Chorio, and the last section to Lara Bay needs a high-clearance vehicle. Asprokremmos Dam is signed off the B6 east of the city.
  • Base yourself in: Paphos
  • Look for: Cyprus Warbler, Cyprus Wheatear, Spur-winged Lapwing, Black Francolin, Chukar Partridge, Griffon Vulture, Bonelli's Eagle, Little Owl, Cretzschmar's Bunting, Rüppell's Warbler, Spectacled Warbler, Common Hoopoe
  • National park: Akamas Peninsula National Forest Park
  • Also covers: Agia Varvara, Akamas Peninsula National Forest Park, Asprokremmos Dam, Paphos
  • Also watch for: Green turtles nest at Lara Bay on the Akamas, and the peninsula holds one of the last Cyprus mouflon areas outside the Troodos.

Larnaca salt lake and the eastern plain

Flamingos within sight of the runway

Larnaca salt lake sits beside the airport on the eastern plain and fills each winter with Greater Flamingo and waterfowl. Oroklini Marsh nearby is a small managed wetland that takes waders and herons, and both are reachable within minutes of landing.

  • Habitat: A shallow seasonal salt lake that dries in summer, with saltmarsh and halophytic scrub around it. Oroklini is reedbed and open water on the coastal plain.
  • When: November to March for the flamingos and wintering waterfowl. March and April for spring waders. Both wetlands are dry and empty by midsummer.
  • Getting there: Larnaca airport is beside the lake and the walking track starts at the Hala Sultan Tekke mosque. Oroklini Marsh is about ten minutes northeast with a hide on its southern edge.
  • Base yourself in: Larnaca
  • Look for: Greater Flamingo, Black-winged Stilt, Spur-winged Lapwing, Marsh Sandpiper, Little Stint, Ruff, Glossy Ibis, Squacco Heron, Ferruginous Duck, Cyprus Warbler, Spanish Sparrow, Great Spotted Cuckoo
  • National parks: Larnaca Salt Lake Ramsar Site, Oroklini Marsh Natura 2000 site
  • Also covers: Kiti, Larnaca Salt Lake Ramsar Site, Oroklini Marsh Natura 2000 site
  • Also watch for: The lake bed cracks into salt polygons through the summer, and foxes work its edges at dusk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.

Every reserve above with its own link carries its access detail there, and a warden answers what a page cannot.
