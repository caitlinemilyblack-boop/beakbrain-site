At a glance

Best months
  • September to November for a trip that wants the Arfak and the islands together
  • June to August if the Baliem highlands are the point
Regions
The Arfak MountainsWaigeo and central Raja AmpatKofiau, Misool and the outer islandsThe Klasow Valley and the Sorong lowlandsBiak and NumforNimbokrang and the Jayapura lowlandsThe Baliem Valley and the Snow MountainsCenderawasih Bay and the Kaimana coast
Getting in
Sorong (SOQ) for Raja Ampat and the Klasow valley, Manokwari (MKW) for the Arfak, Biak (BIK) and Sentani (DJJ) for the north coast, and Wamena (WMX) only by air from Sentani
Getting around
Domestic flights between the airport towns, four-wheel drive on the Arfak road, and boats for everything in Raja Ampat and Cenderawasih Bay

When to Go

There is no dry season here in the sense a temperate reader means. Sorong takes rain in every month of the year and the difference between the wettest and the driest is a matter of degree. What moves is the sea: the crossings to the outer islands are made or refused by swell, and the display season for most of the birds-of-paradise runs through the middle of the year.

Peak

September to November

The one window when the Arfak, the Klasow and the islands are all reasonable at once.

Go for Manokwari at its driest of the year, the Arfak trails walkable, the Sorong lowlands off their mid-year peak, and the liveaboards coming back into Raja Ampat from October.

Trade off It is the shoulder that everyone else has also worked out, so homestays in Raja Ampat and the Arfak guides book further ahead than at any other time.

Typical daily average
  • Sorong: 24 to 30 °C / 75 to 86 °F
  • Manokwari: 22 to 30 °C / 72 to 86 °F
  • Wamena: 14 to 26 °C / 57 to 79 °F

Highlands

June to August

The highland season, and the months most operators sell.

Go for Wamena and the Baliem are at their driest, the Arfak is off its wet peak, and the display grounds are being worked hard by the village guides.

Trade off Sorong is having its wettest months of the year at up to 365 mm, so the Klasow valley walk in is mud from end to end and the open crossings carry swell.

Typical daily average
  • Sorong: 23 to 30 °C / 73 to 86 °F
  • Manokwari: 22 to 30 °C / 72 to 86 °F
  • Wamena: 14 to 25 °C / 57 to 77 °F

Islands

December to April

The calm water half of the year, when Raja Ampat is at its best and the Arfak is at its wettest.

Go for The flattest sea of the year for the crossings to Kofiau and Misool, February at 184 mm the driest month Sorong records, and the liveaboard fleet all in the archipelago.

Trade off Manokwari is taking 270 to 340 mm a month and the road to Mokwam is at its worst, so an Arfak leg in these months is a gamble on the weather.

Typical daily average
  • Sorong: 24 to 31 °C / 75 to 88 °F
  • Manokwari: 21 to 29 °C / 70 to 84 °F
  • Wamena: 15 to 26 °C / 59 to 79 °F

The Arfak Mountains

A limestone range climbing to 2,900 m behind Manokwari, with village-owned hides built on the display grounds themselves

The range most people come for, and the one where the birding is run by the families who own the forest. Above Mokwam the villages of Syoubri and Kwau build the blinds, keep the display courts swept and walk visitors in before first light. Western Parotia dances on a cleared arena with the light coming through the canopy behind it, Vogelkop Bowerbird builds a thatched hut with a mown lawn of moss and fruit in front of it, and Magnificent Bird-of-paradise strips its own saplings bare so the sun reaches the perch. Higher on the ridge road the fruiting trees bring in Arfak Astrapia and Long-tailed Paradigalla, and the Anggi lakes on the plateau behind add open country the forest trips miss.

  • Habitat: Montane rain forest covers the range behind Manokwari, and nine birds-of-paradise display in it. The coastal forest at Maruni and Warkapi below holds the lowland birds.
  • When: Best from June to January. Western Parotia, Vogelkop Bowerbird and Magnificent Bird-of-paradise are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Reaching the good forest means camping on the ridges, so food and porters travel with you, and three days or more is what the mountain repays.
  • Look for: Western Parotia, Vogelkop Bowerbird, Magnificent Bird-of-paradise, Black Sicklebill, Arfak Astrapia, Long-tailed Paradigalla, Vogelkop Melidectes, Feline Owlet-nightjar
  • National park: Pegunungan Arfak Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The village at about 1,500 m, where most of the display-ground blinds sit. A guide walks you in at four in the morning and the bird arrives with the light.
    • : The second hide village on the same slope. Search it for bowerbird courts and for the sicklebill's calling post.
    • : A lake on the plateau at 1,900 m, with grassland and pine ridge around it. The road in is slow in any weather.
    • : The airport town and the start of the four-hour climb to Mokwam. The coastal scrub behind it is worth an hour on arrival.
  • Also covers: Anggi Gigi, Anggi Lakes, Kwau village, Manokwari, Pegunungan Arfak Nature Reserve, Syoubri and the Mokwam hides
  • Also watch for: Vogelkop Tree-kangaroo lives in this forest and is very rarely seen. Common Spotted Cuscus is the mammal a night walk usually produces, and the Arfak is a type locality for several birdwing butterflies, which are farmed under licence in the villages below.

Waigeo and central Raja Ampat

Karst islands off the tip of the Vogelkop where two birds-of-paradise display within a boat ride of the reef

Raja Ampat sells itself on coral, and the birding sits inside the same trip. Wilson's Bird-of-paradise displays on Waigeo and nowhere else on earth, on a cleared patch of forest floor a walk inland from the beach, and Red Bird-of-paradise holds lek trees on the ridges above. Both are reached before dawn from a homestay or a liveaboard, and both are back at the boat by mid-morning. The karst itself carries Western Crowned Pigeon on the bigger islands and Palm Cockatoo along the shore, and Beach Kingfisher sits on the mangrove edge in plain view.

  • Habitat: On Batanta the forest meets the sea along the shore east of Wai Lebed, and lowland forest holds Red Bird of paradise a few hundred metres inland. The Wilson's Bird of paradise lek stands on a ridge on the flank of Mount Batanta.
  • When: Good all year. Wilson's Bird-of-paradise, Red Bird-of-paradise and Beach Kingfisher stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Boats for Batanta are hired at the jetty in Sorong, the regional capital of the Vogelkop peninsula, which has daily flights from Ambon, Biak, Jayapura and Jakarta. The crossing to Yenenes takes three to three and a half hours, and Wai Lebed is a further hour and a half east along the coast. A village guide leads the tough hour and a half climb to the lek, and you start at dawn.
  • Look for: Wilson's Bird-of-paradise, Red Bird-of-paradise, Western Crowned Pigeon, Palm Cockatoo, Beach Kingfisher, Common Paradise Kingfisher, Hook-billed Kingfisher, Papuan Eclectus
  • Parks and reserves: Cagar Alam Pulau Waigeo Barat (nature reserve)
  • Stops:
    • : The district town on Waigeo and the ferry arrival from Sorong. The hide trails lie an hour or two beyond it.
    • : The mainland airport and the ferry port for the whole archipelago. Every itinerary starts here.
    • : The homestay village on Gam opposite Waisai. The walk up to a Red Bird-of-paradise lek starts here.
    • : The southern island of the four, forested to the shoreline. Search it for kingfishers and Western Crowned Pigeon.
    • : The island closest to Sorong. Its lowland forest shares the mainland's list rather than Waigeo's.
  • Also covers: Batanta, Cagar Alam Pulau Waigeo Barat, Salawati, Sorong, Waisai, Yenwaupnor, on Gam
  • Also watch for: The reefs below hold more coral species than anywhere else measured, and Reef Manta Ray aggregates at cleaning stations off Mansuar through the season. Waigeo has its own Spotted Cuscus, and Dugong feeds in the seagrass in the sheltered bays.

Kofiau, Misool and the outer islands

The scattered western and southern islands of Raja Ampat, each holding a bird found on it and nowhere else

The outer islands are a different trip from Waigeo: a boat, a long crossing and a bird that exists on one island. Kofiau has both a paradise kingfisher and a monarch of its own, and reaching them means a chartered crossing of about a hundred kilometres of open water. Misool in the south is the diving island, with limestone lagoons and rock art, and its forest carries the same crowned pigeon and cockatoos with far less traffic than Waigeo.

  • Habitat: Coastal mangrove and lowland forest on the outer Raja Ampat islands, with hill forest behind. Kofiau is rarely visited and keeps two birds of its own, the Kofiau Paradise Kingfisher and the Kofiau Monarch.
  • When: October to April, the dry season, when the birds are most active and the crossings most dependable.
  • Getting there: By boat from Sorong or Waisai, the outer islands taking considerably longer than Waigeo and needing a chartered crossing.
  • Look for: Kofiau Paradise Kingfisher, Kofiau Monarch, Northern Variable Pitohui, Great-billed Parrot, Nicobar Pigeon, Beach Stone-curlew, Spice Imperial Pigeon
  • Stops:
    • : A low forested island west of the main group, on a charter. It is the whole world range of two birds.
    • : The main settlement on Misool, with the limestone lagoons and the long-standing marine reserve around it.
  • Also covers: Kofiau, Waigama, Misool
  • Also watch for: Misool's lagoons hold a stingless jellyfish lake and the private reserve around the island is one of the longest-running no-take zones in Indonesia, with reef shark numbers measurably higher inside it. Green Turtle and Hawksbill Turtle both nest on the outer beaches.

The Klasow Valley and the Sorong lowlands

Flat lowland rainforest an hour inland from the airport, where the lowland birds-of-paradise hold their leks

The Klasow valley is the lowland half of a Vogelkop trip and it sits close enough to Sorong that it usually opens or closes the itinerary. The walk in to Malagufuk village crosses swamp forest on a log path, and the leks are watched from there: King Bird-of-paradise calling from the mid-storey, Lesser Bird-of-paradise on a traditional tree, and Twelve-wired Bird-of-paradise on a dead snag above the swamp. Northern Cassowary leaves prints on the trail more often than it shows itself.

  • Habitat: The Bird's Head Peninsula carries lowland alluvial forest, and Bintuni Bay beside it holds some of the most extensive mangrove swamp in the world.
  • When: Best from June to December. King Bird-of-paradise, Lesser Bird-of-paradise and Twelve-wired Bird-of-paradise stay through the year.
  • Getting there: Sorong is the regional capital of the Vogelkop peninsula and has daily flights from Ambon, Biak, Jayapura and Jakarta. Boats for the offshore islands are hired at the town jetty.
  • Look for: King Bird-of-paradise, Lesser Bird-of-paradise, Twelve-wired Bird-of-paradise, Northern Cassowary, Blyth's Hornbill, Pale-billed Sicklebill, Brown-headed Crow, Golden Myna
  • Stops:
    • : The village on the Sorong road where the track into the Klasow valley leaves the tarmac. A guide meets you here.
    • : A village inside the forest, three hours on foot from Klayili. It has simple guest huts and the leks lie within an hour of them.
    • : The airport city, an hour and a half from the roadhead. It is the arrival point for this whole page.
  • Also covers: Klayili, Malagufuk, Sorong
  • Also watch for: Northern Common Cuscus and Sugar Glider both turn up on a night walk from the village, and the swamp forest holds Papuan Frogmouth and enormous flying foxes moving over at dusk.

Biak and Numfor

Two flat coral islands in Cenderawasih Bay, each with an endemic list out of all proportion to its size

Biak is an island of raised coral and secondary forest with an airport on it, which makes an extraordinary endemic list unusually easy to reach. Biak Scops Owl answers tape from roadside scrub, Biak Paradise Kingfisher calls from the forest edge at dawn, and Biak Monarch and Biak Coucal are worked in the remnant forest in the north of the island. Numfor is a short flight or a long boat ride away and holds its own paradise kingfisher, which is the reason anyone makes the crossing.

  • Habitat: Biak itself is largely deforested, and the endemics hold on in mixed degraded and primary forest at the far eastern tip of the island. Supiori next door is a nature reserve of steep terrain that makes for difficult walking, with primary forest on the slopes behind Korido village.
  • When: October to April in the drier half. Biak is the one most birders take, for the number of endemics reachable in a short stay.
  • Getting there: Biak is the air gateway. Hire a vehicle in Biak town for the hour long drive east along the southern coast road through Mokmer and Bosnik to Warafri, and bird from the road and the logging tracks. For Supiori, a powered longboat makes the five hour sea trip to Korido on the southwest coast, and the crossing can be wet, so allow two to three days there. A steep trail climbs into primary forest behind the village, and other trails run inland and parallel to the coast in both directions.
  • Look for: Biak Scops Owl, Biak Paradise Kingfisher, Biak Monarch, Biak Coucal, Numfor Paradise Kingfisher, Geelvink Pygmy Parrot, Biak White-eye, Long-tailed Starling
  • National park: Biak Utara Nature Reserve
  • Stops:
    • : The airport island. Forest patches lie in the north and the scops owl is found along the roads between them.
    • : A low island west of Biak with a small airstrip. People visit it for one kingfisher and a handful of subspecies.
    • : The hilly island joined to Biak by a bridge. It holds the largest block of forest in the group.
  • Also covers: Biak Utara Nature Reserve, Numfor, Supiori
  • Also watch for: Cenderawasih Bay south of the islands is the place whale sharks feed at the fishing platforms year round, which is unusual anywhere and part of why the cruises call here. The islands themselves carry little beyond bats and introduced deer.

Nimbokrang and the Jayapura lowlands

Lowland forest and logging track two hours from Sentani airport, and the classic north-coast birding base

Nimbokrang has been the northern lowland base for thirty years, and the birding is done on old logging tracks off the transmigration road. Pale-billed Sicklebill displays in swamp forest reached by canoe, Victoria Crowned Pigeon walks the trails at dawn in the blocks that are still hunted lightly, and Shovel-billed Kookaburra is heard far more often than seen. The village guides here know individual display trees and work them on a rotation.

  • Habitat: Bird-rich lowland alluvial forest runs out from the base of the Cyclops Mountains, and the roadside is lined with pools and swamp. The forest edge along the road is the place for birds-of-paradise.
  • When: Best from June to November. Pale-billed Sicklebill, Brown Lory and Papuan Pitta hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Nimbokrang is a transmigration settlement 65 km and about three hours west of Sentani. Clearance for agriculture has taken the edges, and huge areas stay intact with roads and side paths running into them.
  • Look for: Victoria Crowned Pigeon, Pale-billed Sicklebill, Jobi Manucode, Shovel-billed Kookaburra, Brown Lory, Blue Jewel-babbler, Papuan Pitta, Double-eyed Fig Parrot
  • Stops:
    • : The village on the transmigration road. It has guesthouse rooms, and the km-marked logging tracks run north from it.
    • : The airport for Jayapura, on a lake under the Cyclops Mountains. It is the arrival point for the whole north coast.
    • : The small town west of Nimbokrang, and the road on to the swamp forest where the sicklebill displays.
  • Also covers: Genyem, Nimbokrang, Sentani
  • Also watch for: Northern Common Cuscus and Long-beaked Echidna both occur, the echidna very rarely. Lake Sentani below holds its own endemic fish and a rainbowfish found nowhere else.

The Baliem Valley and the Snow Mountains

A highland valley at 1,600 m under the only permanent ice in the tropics outside the Andes and Africa

Wamena is a flight from Jayapura into a valley with no road out of it, and the birding runs upslope from there. The forest above Ibele holds Splendid Astrapia and Brown Sicklebill in the moss, and the high grassland beyond the treeline is the one accessible ground for Snow Mountain Quail and Snow Mountain Robin. This is the hardest leg on any Papuan itinerary and the one that adds the birds no other region can.

  • Habitat: The valley sits at about 1,600 metres among alpine swamp grassland, moss forest, montane forest and cultivation. The Snow Mountains behind it are well forested between 1,800 and 3,000 metres, with alpine grassland and snow above.
  • When: Best from June to November. Splendid Astrapia, Common Smoky Honeyeater and Western Crested Berrypecker hold the ground through the year.
  • Getting there: Shelters along the trail are where you camp.
  • Look for: Snow Mountain Quail, Snow Mountain Robin, Splendid Astrapia, Brown Sicklebill, MacGregor's Honeyeater, Common Smoky Honeyeater, Western Crested Berrypecker, Alpine Pipit
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Amboyna Cuckoo-Dove, Bar-tailed Cuckoo-Dove
  • National park: Lorentz National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The valley town. Only a flight reaches it, and it is the base for everything above.
    • : The village at the top of the valley road. The moss forest and the ridge trail to the high grassland begin here.
  • Also covers: Ibele, Lorentz National Park, Wamena
  • Also watch for: Dingiso, a black and white tree kangaroo the Moni people traditionally do not hunt, lives in this range and is almost never seen. Long-beaked Echidna occurs in the same forest and is equally elusive.

Cenderawasih Bay and the Kaimana coast

The great bay on the north coast and the karst coast south of it, both watched from a boat rather than a road

This is cruise ground rather than lodge ground. Cenderawasih Bay is the largest marine national park in the country and its fishing platforms hold whale sharks all year, which is why the sailing itineraries call here. Yapen island inside the bay carries the crowned pigeon and its own manucode, and the Kaimana coast to the south, Triton Bay, adds limestone islands, rock art and a reef the divers rate with Raja Ampat.

  • Habitat: Well-kept lowland rainforest running down to the bay, which is the forest the birds of paradise need and the first thing lost where it is not protected.
  • When: October to April, the drier half of the year on this coast.
  • Getting there: Nabire and Manokwari are the airports on the bay, with Kaimana further west along the coast; the forest is reached by road and boat from them.
  • Look for: Papuan Eagle, Great-billed Heron, Yellow-billed Kingfisher, Rufous-bellied Kookaburra, Black-capped Lory, Dusky Lory, Lesser Frigatebird, Boyer's Cuckooshrike
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Black Berrypecker, Black Monarch
  • National park: Teluk Cenderawasih National Park
  • Stops:
    • : The airport town at the head of Cenderawasih Bay, and the road access to the whale shark platforms.
    • : The village whose bagan fishing platforms hold whale sharks in every month of the year.
    • : The small airport for Triton Bay. The karst coast and its rock art lie an hour out by boat.
    • : The long forested island inside the bay, on a ferry from Nabire. It has the best forest on this leg.
  • Also covers: Kaimana, Kwatisore, Nabire, Teluk Cenderawasih National Park, Yapen
  • Also watch for: Whale sharks feed under the bagan fishing platforms at Kwatisore through the year, the only place in the world where the aggregation is not seasonal. Triton Bay holds its own walking shark, described in 2013, and Dugong is regular in both bays.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Papua?

September to November if you want the Arfak Mountains and the Raja Ampat islands on one trip: Manokwari is at its driest of the year, down to 128 mm in September, and the liveaboards come back into the archipelago from October. June to August is the highland season, when Wamena and the Baliem are driest, but those are also Sorong's wettest months at up to 365 mm. The three seasons.

Is there a dry season?

Not in the sense a temperate reader means. The lowest monthly rainfall anywhere above is 122 mm, at Wamena in June, and Sorong takes over 180 mm in every month of the year. What changes is which end of the Vogelkop is wet: Sorong peaks in the middle of the year and Manokwari peaks at the turn of it. When to go, season by season.

How do the bird-of-paradise hides work?

The forest above Mokwam belongs to the villages of Syoubri and Kwau, and the families who own each display ground build the blind, keep the court swept and take visitors in. You walk up in the dark, sit from about four thirty, and the bird arrives with the light. The fee goes to the family. It is the arrangement worth supporting here, and it is why the guiding is arranged through an operator rather than booked online. The Arfak Mountains.

Can I combine Raja Ampat diving with the birding?

Yes, and most people do. Wilson's Bird-of-paradise displays on Waigeo within a walk of the homestays on Gam, and Red Bird-of-paradise holds lek trees on the ridges above them. Both are done before breakfast and back at the boat by mid-morning, which leaves the day for the reef. Waigeo and central Raja Ampat.

Do I need to go to the highlands?

Only if you want the birds that live nowhere else. Wamena is a flight from Sentani into a valley with no road out, and the walking above Ibele is the hardest leg on any Papuan itinerary. What it adds is the Snow Mountain Quail, the Snow Mountain Robin and Splendid Astrapia, none of which occur in the Vogelkop. The Baliem Valley and the Snow Mountains.

Where do people stay?

Village homestays in Raja Ampat, forest huts in the Klasow valley and above Mokwam, guesthouses in Sorong, Manokwari and Biak, and liveaboard boats for the island itineraries. The Raja Ampat homestay network is community-owned and is listed island by island by the network itself. Where to stay.

Do the places to stay in Papua and Raja Ampat hold sustainability certification?

None of them. No scheme that certifies accommodation has reached this coast, so no Papuan property appears in a register. That says something about the schemes rather than about the homestays. Certification.

How many bird species does Indonesia have?

More than any country except Colombia and Peru, and about a fifth of them live nowhere else. The card wall carries every Indonesian species with a BeakBrain card, which is coded at country level, so the same wall appears on all four Indonesian guides. The birds.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Often yes, and it varies by site. A good deal of the birding here happens from roadsides, hides and lodge grounds rather than on long walks, and some reserves keep flat, surfaced trails. Ask a lodge or reserve directly about step free access, surfaces and seating before you book. The Birdability Map records those details for birding sites, and their Access Considerations guide lists the questions worth asking. Coverage is thin outside the United States, so read a blank area as unrecorded rather than unsuitable.
