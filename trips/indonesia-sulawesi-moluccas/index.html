At a glance

Best months
August to October for Sulawesi and the Banda Sea, October to December if Seram and Buru are the point
Regions
Tangkoko and the Minahasa peninsulaBogani Nani Wartabone and the Maleo groundsGunung Ambang and the northern volcanoesLore Lindu and the Anaso trackThe southeast: Rawa Aopa and ButonHalmahera and TernateSeram and AmbonBuruThe Banda Sea, Tanimbar and KaiSangihe, Talaud and the northern arcTogian, Banggai and the Sula islands
Getting in
Manado (MDC) for the north, Palu (PLW) for Lore Lindu, Kendari (KDI) for the southeast, Ternate (TTE) for Halmahera and Ambon (AMQ) for Seram, Buru and the Banda Sea
Getting around
Domestic flights between the island airports, a hired car and driver on Sulawesi, and ferries or a chartered schooner for everything east of it

When to Go

There is no one season for Wallacea, because the islands do not share one. The north of Sulawesi is wettest at the turn of the year and driest in August; the southern Moluccas are the other way round; and the Tanimbars, closer to Darwin than to Jakarta, have a genuine dry season from August to November. What an itinerary actually plans around is the sea, because half of Wallacea is reached by ferry or charter.

Peak

August to October

The one window when north Sulawesi, the highlands and the Banda Sea are all workable at once.

Go for Manado at its driest of the year at 146 mm, Wuasa down to 92 mm so the Anaso track is walkable, and Saumlaki at 12 mm with the calmest water in the Banda Sea arc.

Trade off It is also when the schooners are booked, and the Moluccan islands are still coming out of the southeast monsoon at the start of it.

Typical daily average Manado 22 to 31 °C / 72 to 88 °F, Wuasa 16 to 25 °C / 61 to 77 °F, Ambon 22 to 28 °C / 72 to 82 °F

Moluccas

October to December

The southern Moluccan window, when Ambon, Seram and Buru are at their driest.

Go for Ambon down to 118 mm in November against 410 mm in June, so Manusela and the Buru highlands are reachable rather than theoretical.

Trade off North Sulawesi is going back into its wet, and by December Manado is at 350 mm a month.

Typical daily average Ambon 22 to 28 °C / 72 to 82 °F, Ternate 23 to 30 °C / 73 to 86 °F, Manado 22 to 31 °C / 72 to 88 °F

Quiet

January to April

The northwest monsoon, and the half of the year most operators avoid.

Go for Palu is at its driest in March, 116 mm, and Lore Lindu's western approach is passable when the north coast is not. Prices and pressure on the Tangkoko guides are both at their lowest.

Trade off Manado takes 300 to 390 mm a month, and the ferry and charter crossings that half the region depends on are the least reliable of the year.

Typical daily average Manado 22 to 31 °C / 72 to 88 °F, Palu 22 to 32 °C / 72 to 90 °F, Ambon 22 to 29 °C / 72 to 84 °F

Tangkoko and the Minahasa peninsula

A coastal reserve under two volcanoes at the tip of the Minahasa peninsula, and the easiest endemic birding in the country

Tangkoko is where most Sulawesi trips open, and it earns the place. The lowland forest behind Batuputih village runs from black sand up to the Dua Saudara volcanoes, the trails are short and level, and the guiding is done by men from the village who know individual roost holes. Knobbed Hornbill comes in to fruiting figs in numbers, Green-backed Kingfisher and Sulawesi Lilac Kingfisher sit still on low perches through the morning, and Ochre-bellied Boobook is walked to at a known day roost.

  • Look for: Knobbed Hornbill, Sulawesi Hornbill, Green-backed Kingfisher, Sulawesi Lilac Kingfisher, Ochre-bellied Boobook, Sulawesi Pitta, Ashy Woodpecker, Yellow-billed Malkoha
  • Stops:
    • , the village at the reserve gate, with the guesthouses and the guide association, and the trails starting behind it
    • , the airport city, an hour and a half from the reserve, and the arrival point for the whole north of the island
    • , the port town between the two, and the ferry for the Lembeh strait
  • Also watch for: This is the reserve for the Celebes Crested Macaque, an all-black monkey down to a few thousand animals, habituated here and watched at a few metres. Spectral Tarsier roosts in strangler figs and is walked to at dusk, and Sulawesi Bear Cuscus feeds high in the canopy.

Bogani Nani Wartabone and the Maleo grounds

The largest national park in Sulawesi, and the nesting beaches where a megapode buries its eggs in volcanic sand

The park runs across the neck of the northern peninsula and holds the birds Tangkoko does not. The reason to come is Maleo: a megapode the size of a chicken that walks in from the forest to lay a single enormous egg in sand warmed by geothermal heat, then leaves it. The nesting ground at Tambun is warden-protected and watched from a hide at first light. The forest at Toraut behind it adds the interior species.

  • Look for: Maleo, Purple-winged Roller, Purple-bearded Bee-eater, Sulawesi Nightjar, Bay Coucal, Isabelline Bush-hen, Yellow-sided Flowerpecker
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Blue-eared Kingfisher, Citrine Canary-flycatcher, Green-backed Kingfisher
  • National park: Bogani Nani Wartabone National Park
  • Stops:
    • , the warden-run Maleo nesting ground in the Dumoga valley, with a hide overlooking the sand, and birds arriving through the early morning
    • , the town most people stay in for the park, four hours from Manado
    • , the forest entrance on the Dumoga side, with the interior trails and a research station
  • Also watch for: Babirusa, the pig with tusks that curve back through its own snout, uses the salt licks in this park and is occasionally seen from the Toraut trails. Anoa, the dwarf buffalo, is present and very rarely met.

Gunung Ambang and the northern volcanoes

Montane forest on a volcano rim at 1,700 m, holding two birds the lowland reserves cannot give

Gunung Ambang is a short, steep morning above the village of Modayag and it exists on an itinerary for two birds. Matinan Blue Flycatcher holds the mossy ridge forest and is found nowhere else, and Cinnabar Boobook is a small rufous owl of the same altitude. The walk up is on a farm track through clove and vegetable plots before the forest begins.

  • Look for: Matinan Blue Flycatcher, Cinnabar Boobook, Sulawesi Myzomela, Scaly-breasted Kingfisher, Sulawesi Thrush, Fiery-browed Starling, Indonesian Serin
  • Stops:
    • , the village below the ridge, where the walk up the farm track begins before dawn
  • Also watch for: The forest here is thin and the mammals with it. What the ridge does hold is Sulawesi Bear Cuscus, and the clove plantations below carry an exceptional butterfly fauna in the middle of the day.

Lore Lindu and the Anaso track

A mountain national park in the island's centre, with a ridge track that is the whole world range of several birds

Lore Lindu is the hard leg and the one that produces birds no other site can. The Anaso track climbs from about 1,700 m to over 2,200 m on a broken logging road, and it is walked in the dark for Satanic Nightjar and again at dawn for Geomalia, a large ground-dwelling thrush that walks the trail edge and is seen by very few people. Purple-bearded Bee-eater hunts from bare branches over the road and Great Shortwing sings from the undergrowth.

  • Look for: Geomalia, Satanic Nightjar, Malia, Great Shortwing, Sulawesi Woodcock, Sulawesi Blue Flycatcher, Sulawesi Streaked Flycatcher, Sulawesi Leaf Warbler
  • Stops:
    • , the village in the Napu valley on the eastern side, and the base most groups use for the Anaso track
    • , the provincial capital and airport, three to four hours from the park by road
    • , the park entrance on the western side, lower and hotter, with the lowland species
  • Also watch for: Anoa and Babirusa both use the park and both are essentially never seen from the track. What a night walk does give is Sulawesi Bear Cuscus and Sulawesi Dwarf Cuscus, and the megaliths scattered through the Bada and Napu valleys are worth a morning of their own.

The southeast: Rawa Aopa and Buton

A wetland and savanna national park on the southeastern peninsula, with a forested island offshore

The southeastern peninsula is the least travelled part of Sulawesi and Rawa Aopa Watumohai is the reason to go: swamp, savanna and lowland forest in one park, which is a combination the rest of the island does not offer. The wetland holds waterbirds in numbers and the savanna edge carries raptors. Buton island offshore adds the Lambusango forest and its own subspecies.

  • Look for: Sulawesi Serpent Eagle, Spotted Kestrel, Sulawesi Babbler, Pale-blue Monarch, Black-ringed White-eye, White-necked Myna, Grey-sided Flowerpecker
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Isabelline Bush-hen, Lemon-bellied White-eye, Milky Stork
  • National park: Rawa Aopa Watumohai National Park
  • Stops:
    • , the provincial capital and airport, and the road head for the park
    • , the town on Buton island, reached by ferry or a short flight, with the Lambusango forest inland
  • Also watch for: Lambusango on Buton is one of the better places for Anoa, which is monitored there, and the same forest holds Buton Macaque. The Rawa Aopa wetland carries an unusually large crocodile population, which shapes where anybody walks.

Halmahera and Ternate

The largest of the northern Moluccas, and the display trees of a bird-of-paradise that lives west of New Guinea

Halmahera is the Moluccan island every itinerary reaches, and the bird it is sold on is Standardwing, a bird-of-paradise whose males gather at dawn in a tall tree and hang from the branches with two long white flags off each wing. The lek above Sidangoli or Foli is walked to before four in the morning. The rest of the list is as good: Ivory-breasted Pitta on the forest floor, Halmahera Paradise-crow, and Moluccan Owlet-nightjar on a night walk.

  • Look for: Standardwing, Ivory-breasted Pitta, Halmahera Paradise-crow, Moluccan Owlet-nightjar, Chattering Lory, White Cockatoo, Halmahera Boobook, Azure Dollarbird
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Beach Kingfisher, Blue-and-white Kingfisher
  • National park: Aketajawe-Lolobata National Park
  • Stops:
    • , the ferry landing from Ternate, with the standardwing lek in forest above it and simple rooms in the village
    • , the volcanic island with the airport, an hour by speedboat from Halmahera, and a spice-trade town worth a morning
    • , the bay on the southeast coast, with a resort built for birders and the forest tracks behind it
  • Also watch for: The northern Moluccas hold very little in the way of mammals beyond bats and cuscus, which is what makes the birds so distinctive. Common Spotted Cuscus is the one a night walk finds, and the island's giant Ornithoptera birdwings are on the wing through the middle of the day.

Seram and Ambon

The largest of the southern Moluccas, forested to 3,000 m, with the old spice port as its gateway

Seram is a big forested island with a national park across its spine and a bird list that includes some of the most striking parrots in Asia. Salmon-crested Cockatoo is a Seram endemic in serious trouble from the cage-bird trade and is still met in the Manusela foothills, and Purple-naped Lory moves through the canopy in noisy groups. Ambon next door holds the airport and the ferry, and its remnant forest is worth a morning on arrival.

  • Look for: Salmon-crested Cockatoo, Purple-naped Lory, Moluccan King Parrot, Lazuli Kingfisher, Seram Boobook, South Moluccan Pitta, Moluccan Megapode, Wallace's Fruit Dove
  • Also recorded in the parks here: Amboyna Cuckoo-Dove, Ashy Flowerpecker
  • National park: Manusela National Park
  • Stops:
    • , the airport island and the ferry port for Seram, with a small patch of forest at Wai Ela worth an early morning
    • , the town on Seram's south coast, and the road north over the spine towards the national park
    • , the fishing village on the north coast under the Manusela limestone, with homestays over the water
  • Also watch for: Seram has almost no native mammals beyond bats, cuscus and a rat or two, and the introduced deer are now part of the landscape. The island's freshwater has its own fish and the coastal caves hold enormous bat colonies that stream out at dusk.

Buru

A large, little-visited island west of Seram with a highland forest and three birds found nowhere else

Buru is the island most Moluccan itineraries treat as the difficult extension, and it is difficult: a ferry from Ambon, a long drive and a walk into highland forest with camping at the end of it. What it holds is a set of birds that exist on this island alone, including a lorikeet that went unrecorded for most of a century.

  • Look for: Buru Boobook, Buru Flowerpecker, Buru Racket-tail, Buru Monarch, Buru Cuckooshrike, Tawny-breasted Parrotfinch, Buru White-eye
  • Stops:
    • , the port and airstrip on the north coast, and the start of the drive inland
  • Also watch for: Like Seram, Buru is a Wallacean island with a thin mammal list, and the introduced deer and wild pig are the animals most often met. The highland forest is the interest here and it is under pressure from a gold rush that began in 2011.

The Banda Sea, Tanimbar and Kai

The southeastern island arc, worked from a chartered schooner, the only boat that goes

This is the leg that needs a boat. The Tanimbar and Kai groups sit closer to Australia than to Java and carry their own endemics on each cluster, and the schooner voyages that reach them also call at Banda Neira, the nutmeg island the Dutch traded Manhattan for. The birding is short, sharp landings rather than days in one forest.

  • Look for: Tanimbar Corella, Tanimbar Megapode, Tanimbar Eclectus, Tanimbar Boobook, Banda Sea Pitta, Banda Myzomela, Elegant Pitta, Wallace's Owlet-nightjar
  • Stops:
    • , the town on Yamdena in the Tanimbars, with an airstrip and the forest tracks north of it
    • , the town in the Kai islands, with white sand beaches and the forest on Kai Besar behind
    • , the nutmeg island under its volcano, a schooner stop rather than a birding site, and one of the best-preserved colonial towns in the country
  • Also watch for: The Banda Sea is deep, and the crossings between islands produce cetaceans no land site does: Sperm Whale, Melon-headed Whale and Spinner Dolphin are all regular. The Kai islands hold their own set again.

Sangihe, Talaud and the northern arc

A chain of volcanic islands running north towards the Philippines, each with birds that exist on it alone

North of Manado the sea carries a line of volcanic islands towards Mindanao, and each of them has kept something. Sangihe's remnant forest on Mount Sahendaruman is one of the most threatened bird habitats on earth, a few hundred hectares holding several birds and nothing else anywhere. Talaud further north is larger and greener, with a rail and a kingfisher of its own.

  • Look for: Talaud Rail, Sangihe Lilac Kingfisher, Sangihe Hanging Parrot, Elegant Sunbird, Talaud Kingfisher, Sangihe Scops Owl, Sangihe Whistler
  • Stops:
    • , the town on Sangihe, reached by overnight ferry from Manado, and the road to the Sahendaruman forest above it
    • , the airstrip town on Karakelang, the largest of the Talaud islands, with the forest reserve inland
  • Also watch for: Sangihe Tarsier was described as a separate species in 2008 and lives on this island alone. The islands are otherwise thin in mammals, and the volcanoes are the other draw: Karangetang on Siau is one of the most active in Indonesia.

Togian, Banggai and the Sula islands

The islands scattered off Sulawesi's eastern arm, several with an endemic described only in the last thirty years

The eastern arm of Sulawesi trails off into island groups that were birded seriously only recently, and the results were startling. The Banggai Crow was known from two specimens taken in 1900 and was rediscovered on Peleng in 2007. The Togian islands have their own boobook and a white-eye, and the Sula group carries a megapode and a pitta. These are pioneering legs and the logistics are the hard part.

  • Look for: Banggai Crow, Togian Boobook, Sula Megapode, Sula Pitta, Banggai Fruit Dove, Taliabu Myzomela, Sula Hanging Parrot, Togian White-eye
  • Stops:
    • , the main settlement in the Togian islands, reached by ferry from Ampana, with the forest on Batudaka behind it
    • , the airport town on the eastern arm and the ferry port for Peleng, where the crow was found again
    • , the town on Sanana island, the arrival point for the Sula group and its megapode
  • Also watch for: The Togian islands hold a marine park with some of the least damaged reef in the country, and Dugong feed in the seagrass between the islands. Peleng and Taliabu have their own tarsiers, both described recently.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in Sulawesi and the Moluccas?

August to October covers the most ground: Manado is at its driest of the year, the Anaso track in Lore Lindu is walkable, and the Banda Sea is calm enough for the schooner routes. If Seram and Buru matter more, go October to December, when Ambon drops from 410 mm in June to 118 mm in November. The three seasons.

Why does the weather advice differ by island?

Because Wallacea does not share one season. North Sulawesi is wettest at the turn of the year and driest in August; the southern Moluccas run the opposite way; and the Tanimbars, closer to Darwin than to Jakarta, have a genuine dry season from August to November with 12 mm in September. Any single sentence about the best time to visit Indonesia is wrong somewhere above. When to go, season by season.

What is Wallace's Line and does it matter here?

It is the deep-water channel between Bali and Lombok and between Borneo and Sulawesi that Asian animals never crossed and Australian ones never crossed the other way. It matters because it is why Sulawesi's birds are unlike Borneo's despite the islands being visible from one another, and why Wallacea is separated from the Greater Sundas here. The birds.

Where do I see the Standardwing?

At a lek in forest above Sidangoli or at Foli on Halmahera, walked to in the dark and watched from below as the males gather at first light. It is a bird-of-paradise several hundred kilometres west of New Guinea, which is the whole reason it is famous, and it is on every Moluccan itinerary. Halmahera and Ternate.

Is the Maleo hard to see?

No, if you go to Tambun. The nesting ground is warden-protected, the birds walk in from the forest through the early morning to bury a single egg in geothermally warmed sand, and there is a hide overlooking it. Away from a protected ground it is a different story, which is why the species is endangered. Bogani Nani Wartabone.

How much of this needs a boat?

About half. Sulawesi itself is a road trip between airports. The Moluccas are reached by flight to Ternate or Ambon and then by ferry, and the Banda Sea arc, Tanimbar and Kai, is only realistically done on a chartered schooner. That is why those legs cost what they do. The Banda Sea, Tanimbar and Kai.

Why does this guide carry no certification badges?

Because none of the accommodation schemes have reached these islands. Every chip on a BeakBrain guide renders from a scheme's own register rather than a property's claim, and Indonesia's certified properties are almost all Bali resorts. That says something about the schemes rather than about the guesthouses. Certification.

How many bird species does Indonesia have?

More than any country except Colombia and Peru, and about a fifth of them live nowhere else. Sulawesi and the Moluccas hold a large share of that endemism, several of them confined to a single island.
