When to Go

The Canary year barely has a winter. The endemics are there every month, the bustards display in the cool half of the year, and the one genuinely seasonal thing is the pelagic window, which is short.

winter

December to March

The endemic season, and the one time Houbara Bustard is displaying. Mild and dry on every island, with the high ground on Tenerife cold enough for snow.

Go for Houbara display, both Blue Chaffinches, the laurel forest pigeons.

Trade off Teide can close its road in snow, and the pelagic trips are out of season.

Typical daily average
  • Santa Cruz de Tenerife: 14 to 20 °C / 57 to 68 °F
  • Puerto del Rosario: 14 to 22 °C / 57 to 72 °F
  • Santa Cruz de La Palma: 14 to 19 °C / 57 to 66 °F

spring

April to May

The best all round window. Every endemic is singing, and spring passage runs through the eastern islands on top of them.

Go for The full endemic list plus passage migrants and the first seabirds.

Trade off Easter fills the island accommodation and the ferries.

Typical daily average
  • Santa Cruz de Tenerife: 16 to 22 °C / 61 to 72 °F
  • Puerto del Rosario: 16 to 24 °C / 61 to 75 °F
  • Santa Cruz de La Palma: 14 to 20 °C / 57 to 68 °F

summer

June to August

The pelagic season. The Banco de la Concepción trips run in this narrow window and need settled weather to sail at all.

Go for White-faced Storm-petrel, Bulwer's Petrel and the shearwater colonies.

Trade off Hot and dusty on Fuerteventura and Lanzarote, and the trade wind can cancel a sailing.

Typical daily average
  • Santa Cruz de Tenerife: 19 to 27 °C / 66 to 81 °F
  • Puerto del Rosario: 19 to 28 °C / 66 to 82 °F
  • Santa Cruz de La Palma: 18 to 24 °C / 64 to 75 °F

autumn

September to November

Autumn passage and the rarity season, when the eastern islands take African and Nearctic vagrants.

Go for Passage waders and passerines, rarities, and the endemics still in place.

Trade off The laurel forest is at its wettest and the pigeon viewpoints can be in cloud.

Typical daily average
  • Santa Cruz de Tenerife: 19 to 26 °C / 66 to 79 °F
  • Puerto del Rosario: 19 to 26 °C / 66 to 79 °F
  • Santa Cruz de La Palma: 18 to 23 °C / 64 to 73 °F

Teno and Masca

The least developed corner of Tenerife, laurel forest on the ridge and a gorge cutting down to a seabird watchpoint

The northwest corner of Tenerife, and the least developed part of the island. Monte del Agua holds Bolle's Pigeon and Laurel Pigeon in the laurel forest, the Masca gorge cuts down to the sea, and Punta de Teno is the seabird watchpoint with Barbary Falcon on the cliffs above.

  • Habitat: Thermophilous woodland covers the lower ground on Olea, Dracaena, Sideroxylon, Phoenix, Juniperus and Pistacia. It is the sole habitat of the Laurel Pigeon, and the sea cliffs below hold breeding seabirds.
  • When: Good all year. Bolle's Pigeon, Laurel Pigeon and Peregrine Falcon are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: The Monte del Agua forest is entered at Erjos, where a dirt track beside the visitor centre runs to a barrier that closes the forest to cars. Follow the main track on foot to the wooden viewpoints and watch for pigeons flying over the canopy. Teno Bajo is reached by road and its lighthouse is the point to watch the sea from, best in the first hour of light and the last two before dark. Barolo Shearwater passes even in winter.
  • Base yourself in: Buenavista del Norte
  • Look for: Bolle's Pigeon, Laurel Pigeon, Peregrine Falcon, Plain Swift, Canary Islands Chiffchaff, Atlantic Canary, African Blue Tit
  • Parks and reserves: Parque Rural de Teno (rural park)
  • Stops:
    • : Laurel forest on Teno's northern slope, on a forestry track. It is one of the two Tenerife stands holding both Bolle's and Laurel Pigeon.
    • : A hamlet on a knife ridge with a gorge falling to the sea below it. It gives a vantage over the pigeon flight lines at dawn.
    • : The island's western tip below the cliffs, on a shuttle bus. Barbary Falcon hold the crags and seabirds pass off the lighthouse.
    • : The high pasture plateau of Teno Alto, open and dry above the forest. Berthelot's Pipit and Rock Sparrow live on it.
    • : The town on the coast below Teno, and the base for the massif's roads and the Punta de Teno shuttle.
  • Also covers: Buenavista del Norte, Las Lagunas de Teno, Masca gorge, Monte del Agua, Parque Rural de Teno, Punta de Teno
  • Also watch for: Bottlenose Dolphin and Short-finned Pilot Whale are resident in the strait below Punta de Teno. Tenerife Lizard and Tenerife Wall Lizard are on every warm rock, and Barbary Ground Squirrel has yet to reach this corner.

Teide and Las Cañadas

A caldera at two thousand metres inside a national park, ringed by the pine belt one chaffinch lives in

Tenerife Blue Chaffinch holds the Canary pine around the crater rim, and the barbecue site at Las Lajas, 11 km from Vilaflor on the park road, is the standard place to look for it. Berthelot's Pipit works the open pumice inside the park and Plain Swift crosses it all day.

  • Habitat: Summit scrub sits on the highest ground of Tenerife, built on Spartocytisus and Adenocarpus, and several lowland birds come up into it, Spectacled Warbler and Great Grey Shrike among them. Izaña at 2,367 metres records 514 mm of rain and a mean of 9.7 °C.
  • When: Good all year. Tenerife Blue Chaffinch, Berthelot's Pipit and Eurasian Sparrowhawk are on site in all twelve months.
  • Getting there: Las Lajas is a picnic site 11 km above Vilaflor on the road into the national park, and it is the reliable stop in the pine belt at any time of year. It fills with people at weekends, so go on a weekday.
  • Base yourself in: Vilaflor
  • Look for: Tenerife Blue Chaffinch, Berthelot's Pipit, Rock Sparrow, Eurasian Sparrowhawk, Common Kestrel, Great Spotted Woodpecker, Trumpeter Finch
  • National park: Parque Nacional del Teide
  • Stops:
    • Teide National Park : Teide National Park covers the Las Cañadas caldera at 2,000 m, under the highest peak in the Canary Islands. Bare lava and Teide broom fill the crater floor, and Tenerife Blue Chaffinch holds the Canary pine around the rim.
    • : The rock stacks on the caldera floor, with a loop trail from the parador. It is the standard place for Berthelot's Pipit and Canary Islands Chiffchaff.

The petrel pelagic grounds

The water between the islands, worked by ferry crossing and by dedicated trips north to a seamount

Ferry crossings between the islands give Bulwer's Petrel and Cory's Shearwater from the deck. Dedicated boats run north to the Banco de la Concepción seamount, and the grounds over it hold White-faced Storm-petrel on a narrow summer window.

  • Habitat: The seabirds breed on rocky islets around the archipelago. Band-rumped Storm Petrel nests on Roque del Este, Alegranza, Montaña Clara and Los Roques de Anaga, fewer than 300 pairs in all, and White-faced Storm Petrel holds 24 to 30 pairs on Montaña Clara alone.
  • When: Barolo Shearwater is on the crossing in every month and Bulwer's Petrel from May to August. Band-rumped Storm Petrel is the scarcest of the three and turns up most in August and September and again in April and May.
  • Getting there: The scheduled ferry between Los Cristianos on Tenerife and San Sebastián on La Gomera is the seawatching platform, and it needs no charter. The lava coastline at Órzola on Lanzarote is reached by road and repays a short stop.
  • Base yourself in: Los Cristianos on Tenerife
  • Look for: Bulwer's Petrel, Cory's Shearwater, White-faced Storm Petrel, Band-rumped Storm Petrel, European Storm Petrel, Barolo Shearwater, Long-tailed Jaeger
  • Stops:

    • : The northern Lanzarote harbour for the La Graciosa boat, which crosses the Chinijo Archipelago waters.
    • : The Gran Canaria ferry port for Tenerife, and the other crossing regularly searched for petrels and shearwaters.
  • Also covers: Banco de la Concepción seamount, Chinijo Archipelago waters, Gran Canaria-Tenerife ferry crossing, Los Cristianos, Órzola, Puerto de las Nieves, Tenerife-La Gomera ferry crossing
  • Also watch for: The same trips that find the petrels find Short-finned Pilot Whale, Sperm Whale, Atlantic Spotted Dolphin and Loggerhead Turtle, and the archipelago has recorded around thirty cetacean species in all.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to go birding in the Canary Islands?

April, if you want one answer. Every endemic is singing and spring passage is running through the eastern islands. February and March are better for Houbara Bustard display, and the summer months are the only time the seamount pelagics sail.

How many islands do I need to visit?

Two covers most of the list. Tenerife gives the laurel forest pigeons and one Blue Chaffinch, Fuerteventura gives the desert set and the stonechat. Adding Gran Canaria brings the second Blue Chaffinch, and La Palma or La Gomera deepens the laurel forest.

Why are there two Blue Chaffinches?

The Tenerife and Gran Canaria birds were split into separate species in 2016 on integrative taxonomic evidence. The Gran Canaria bird is confined to the Inagua pines in numbers counted in the hundreds, which makes it one of the rarest songbirds in Europe.

Do I need a hire car?

Yes, on every island. The plains tracks on Fuerteventura, the Anaga ridge roads and the Inagua approach are all well away from a bus route, and several of them want decent ground clearance.

How do I see the laurel forest pigeons?

From a ridge viewpoint at first light. Watch across the canopy while the birds move between roost and feeding trees. Cruz del Carmen and El Pijaral on Tenerife, the Garajonay trails on La Gomera, and Los Tilos on La Palma.

Are the islands good for seabirds?

Very. The inter-island ferries cross deep water and give Bulwer's Petrel and Cory's Shearwater for the price of a ticket, and the dedicated trips run north to the Banco de la Concepción seamount for White-faced Storm-petrel in summer.

What certification should I look for?

Biosphere, run by the Responsible Tourism Institute, which certifies whole destinations as well as businesses. Lanzarote was the first Biosphere certified destination in the world. Travelife is the second scheme with real presence in island hotels.

Can I add the Canary Islands to a mainland Spain trip?

Direct flights from the mainland reach the five larger islands, and El Hierro and La Gomera are reached from Tenerife or Gran Canaria. The birds are the reason to give the archipelago its own week: both Blue Chaffinches, both endemic pigeons, Canary Islands Houbara Bustard and Fuerteventura Stonechat live here and nowhere on the mainland.

Can I bird here with limited mobility?

Sites on this page differ from each other more than any rating could carry, so this is the framework the standard British guides use, and the reserve's own page and warden hold the answers.

  • Permission · some reserves open ground to disabled visitors that is closed to everyone else, and it is granted by asking.
  • Priority · parking bays, hides and the nearest paths are often held for disabled visitors. Ask what is reserved and how to claim it.
  • Parking · how close a car reaches the birds shapes the whole day. Ring ahead for the nearest hard standing to the water or the hide.
  • Paths · surfaces, gradients and gate widths vary inside a single reserve. Ask which trail is boarded, surfaced or level.
  • Perches · a hide with a low window, or a bench where the view opens, turns a viewpoint into a morning.
