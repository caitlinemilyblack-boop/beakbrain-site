Step one

Pick your country

Choose where you are birding, and every bird is ranked by how often it is actually seen there, so you start with the ones outside your window and work up to the rarities. All 10,927 species are there when you want them.

Step two

Train your eye and ear

Real photographs and real recordings, with slow playback for a difficult call and lookalikes side by side for the birds people mix up most. Then recall each one from a single photo or call, the way real birding works.

Spaced repetition brings the tricky birds back at the right moment, quick games turn a spare minute into practice, and a daily streak keeps the habit alive until you know them without looking them up.

BeakBrain is built by Dr Cat Black, a behavioural ecologist and conservation scientist who earned her PhD at the University of Oxford. She has studied house finches and swallow-tailed kites in the US, penguins in the Antarctic, and backyard biodiversity in Amsterdam, using bioacoustics, camera trapping, and traditional field methods.

She made BeakBrain to share the joy of birding and help people learn the birds they will meet wherever they travel.

Common questions

Good to know

Which birds does BeakBrain cover?

10,927 species, which is close to every bird you can see in the wild anywhere on Earth. For any country you pick, the birds are ranked by how common each one actually is there, from the everyday garden birds right up to the rarities and endemics. Each species comes with real photos, a call where one exists, its family and lookalikes, and a plain-language identification tip. Seasonality is built in too, so a pack can show only the birds present in the month you travel.

How does it actually help me learn?

You learn in short rounds. First you review lookalike groups side by side to train your eye, then you recall each bird from a single photo or call. Spaced repetition brings the birds you find hard back more often until they stick, and a daily streak keeps the habit going. This is the same approach that makes language apps work, applied to birds.

Is BeakBrain free?

Yes. You can start with no account and no payment. Every quick game and a starter pack for every country are always free. A Pro upgrade opens the full country packs, season packs, national park packs, rarities and endemics, and offline use. You only pay if you want the full library.

Can I use it to prepare for a trip?

Yes, and this is where it shines. Set your destination and the month you travel, and BeakBrain drills exactly the birds you will meet, down to individual national parks. You arrive already knowing the birds you are about to see and hear.

Do I need an internet connection?

You can learn online right away with no setup. Offline use is part of Pro, so you can download your packs and keep learning on a plane or out in the field with no signal.

Is it on iPhone and Android?

The beta is arriving on both. Until it lands you can play the full thing in any browser, on a phone or a laptop, with nothing to install.

Is there a dark mode?

Dark mode is on the way, and it sits high on the list. BeakBrain opens in one warm cream theme today, picked so that photographs and painted plates hold their true colour on any screen. Dawn and dusk are the best hours to be out with the birds, so a proper dark theme belongs here and it is coming.

Where do the photos and sounds come from?

Taxonomy comes from AviList, and distribution and seasonality from GBIF. Bird calls come from the Xeno-canto community, and photos from Wikimedia Commons and iNaturalist contributors, all under open licences. Every photo and recording shows the person who made it and its licence, so credit goes where it is due.
