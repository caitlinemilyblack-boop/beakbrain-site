# Website translations — parked 2026-08-31, with the ground already surveyed

Cat asked for French, Spanish, Portuguese, German, Dutch and Italian, and asked what else.
This is what the site actually looks like underneath that question, what is already built
and free, and the order the work has to happen in. **Nothing has been started.**

---

## 1. The blocker, and it is not translation

The site is **29,069 files**. Cloudflare Pages refuses a deployment over **20,000**. The
site is **9,069 files over the ceiling today** and cannot deploy at all; that is why
`beakbrain.com/` is currently served by the `beakbrain-home-patch` Worker rather than by
Pages, and why the App Store link fix is live on the homepage alone. See
`HANDOVER-2026-08-31-deploy-ceiling.md` and the `project_beakbrain_file_budget` note.

**Workers Paid does not lift the ceiling. The zone plan does (~$20/month), and migrating
Pages to Workers static assets removes it.** Until one of those happens, no translation can
ship no matter how good it is. Phase 0 below is not optional.

## 2. The shape of the site decides the whole approach

| Surface | HTML files | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `birds/` species, family, order, country | **21,672** | 99.1% |
| `trips/` country guides and hub | **68** | 0.3% |
| root, `cams/`, `symbols/` | 9 | — |

**99% of the file count is species pages, and ~0% of the editorial prose is.** All 66
country guides together are 68 files. That asymmetry is the single most useful fact here:

- Translating the guides is **cheap**: 68 pages, and six languages of them is ~470 files,
  which is noise against the ceiling once it is lifted.
- Translating the species pages the same way is **impossible**: 21,672 × 6 = **130,032
  files**, against a ceiling of 20,000. It is not a budgeting problem, it is a wrong shape.

So the two surfaces need two different answers. Do not write a plan that treats them alike.

## 3. What already exists and is free

The **app** solved most of this in the session of 2026-08-23, and the work is reusable.
See `~/Developer/Birding-Quiz-App/birding-app/I18N-PROGRESS.md`.

**Species names in 19 languages, done.** IOC Multilingual v15.2, CC BY 3.0, generated by
`pipeline/step9_multiling_names.py` into `birding-app/assets/names/*.json` (~330 KB each):
ca cs da de es fi fr hr it lt nb nl pl pt ru sk sv tr uk. Measured coverage on the six Cat
named: **fr 100%, pt 99.8%, nl 99.9%, es 98.5%, de 98.1%**, it is shipped too. The pipeline
gates at 90% and refuses Serbian (73%), Hungarian (59%), Estonian (51%) and everything else
under 20% — **do not ship those from this source**.

**UI-string machinery, done.** i18next with a catalogue per language keyed on the English
source text, `scripts/i18n-extract.mjs` (a codemod that retrofits `t()` by byte splice so
comments survive), and `scripts/i18n-check.mjs`, a ledger reporting missing keys, orphans
and per-language coverage. 391 keys, all seven original languages at 391/391.

One trap recorded there and worth carrying over: the codemod wrapped three strings that
must never be translated — `'T00:00:00'` (a date suffix; translating it yields Invalid
Date), `'DD'` (an IUCN code compared with `===`) and `'G'` (the Google logo glyph). Any
extractor pointed at this site needs the same SKIP rule.

## 4. Recommended phases

**Phase 0 — lift the ceiling.** Zone plan, or migrate Pages to Workers static assets.
Nothing else can start. This is already on the list for its own reasons.

**Phase 1 — chrome and the country guides.** The nav, footer, homepage, `/trips/` hub and
the 66 guides. This is where the writing is and where a French or Dutch birder actually
reads something. ~470 files at six languages. URL shape `/fr/trips/costa-rica/` with
`hreflang` and a self-referencing `canonical`.

**Phase 2 — species pages get NAMES, not new URLs.** Serve the local common name from the
IOC pack rather than building a page per language. Either a client-side swap on a stored
preference, or edge rewriting in a Worker. This keeps 21,672 pages at 21,672 pages. If SEO
later demands indexed per-language species pages, that is a Worker-rendered route, never
130,032 static files.

**Phase 3 — decide whether guide prose is machine translated at all.** See the risk below.

## 5. The risk nobody will raise later

Cat's writing rules are specific and enforced: no dashes, no "not X" framing, no fragments,
no participles tacked on a comma, no buzzwords, sourced text preferred over written prose,
and "cut copy that explains our method". See `~/.claude/commands/ai-writing-signs.md` and
the `feedback_voice_style`, `feedback_copy_is_for_the_reader` and
`feedback_minimise_ai_writing` notes.

**A machine translation of a guide is new prose in a language nobody on the team proofs.**
Six languages of 66 guides is a large body of unreviewed writing published under her name,
and the existing quality gates (`frags.js`, `stale-access.js`, the voice rules) are all
English-only. Options, in increasing cost: ship translated chrome and English prose;
translate prose and label it machine translated; or translate and have a native speaker
read each language once. **This is Cat's call and should be asked before Phase 1 ships,
not after.**

## 6. Which languages

Cat named fr, es, pt, de, nl, it. All six have IOC name packs above 98% and app catalogues
already written. Worth considering on the same free basis, in rough order of birding
audience: **sv, da, nb, fi** (Nordic birders are numerous and travel), **pl, cs** (large
and growing birding communities). Everything else the app ships is available at no extra
data cost. Anything NOT in that list of 19 needs a new name source and a new gate.
